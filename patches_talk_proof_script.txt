Slide 18: Patch positivity and the meaning of p-star
----------------------------------------------------
15:00--15:35 (35 seconds)

Here is the meaning of the two conditions we stated earlier. Patch positivity is the condition that C(P) is nonnegative for every bulk patch P. The profile p-star gives the smallest densities at which the end contributions are nonnegative.

It defines the class M-star, for which the averaged product of end contributions is also nonnegative. All the factors in the patch factorization are nonnegative, so the sigma-algebra G-t captures all the cancellations we wanted.

Slide 19: Splitting by late interactions
----------------------------------------
15:35--16:30 (55 seconds)

The proof now follows the structure of the classical duality argument. We split (mu P_t)(chi_A) into two terms: one with weak dependence on the initial law, and one whose contribution is negligible.

The extinction event A_t is empty is no longer the right event to split by: it is not G-t-measurable, because the process A is hidden behind the patches. But we have a good proxy that is G-t-measurable: L_t, the event that there is no interaction between t over two and t.

On L_t, we show weak dependence on mu. On its complement, we show that the whole contribution is small.

Slide 20: Late interaction term
-------------------------------
16:30--18:00 (90 seconds)

To control the late-interaction term, we make the following observation. A spin system and its less noisy version, obtained by removing the independent one-to-zero transitions at rate epsilon, have exactly the same dual process. The less noisy system has a larger Feynman-Kac potential.

They therefore share the random patches, but the less noisy system has larger patch contributions. We use primes for that system. For a patch ending with an outgoing interaction, the ratio is exactly exp(-epsilon times the patch length).

A late interaction at u > t/2 has an ancestry trail of patches ending with outgoing interactions, from time zero to u. Multiplying the ratios along the trail gives exp(-epsilon u) <= exp(-epsilon t/2).

This gives the displayed chain of inequalities. The primed expectation, after dropping the event, is (mu P_t')(chi_A), where P_t' is the semigroup of the less noisy system. It is at most one, because chi_A is bounded by one. The late-interaction term is therefore negligible.

Slide 21: No-late-interaction term
----------------------------------
18:00--18:45 (45 seconds)

On L_t, there is no interaction after t over two, so every end patch extends for at least t over two. These are the only patches that carry dependence on the initial law.

In the contribution from this event, the dependence on the spin z in C(z,P) decays exponentially with the added patch length. All the end patches are long, so we can compare the contributions from two initial laws and obtain the displayed estimate.

The errors accumulate over the number of end patches, |E_t|. This factor is controlled using polynomial growth of the lattice. This is where that assumption enters.

Slide 22: The common invariant limit
------------------------------------
18:45--19:20 (35 seconds)

The two estimates give exponential loss of dependence on the initial law and hence convergence from M-star to the same invariant law pi. We identify the common limit by comparing with an invariant law in M-star. Such a law exists because M-star is preserved by the spin-system semigroup.

A simple linearity trick extends the convergence from M-star to the larger class M-minus. This proves the theorem.

Slide 23: From convergence to ergodicity
----------------------------------------
19:20--20:00 (40 seconds)

Finally, the theorem gives full ergodicity in the two regimes stated earlier. The first case, p_i-star is at most one half for every i, is immediate because M-minus-1 contains every probability law. We get uniform exponential ergodicity from the theorem.

On an orientable neighbourhood graph, a separate burn-in argument shows that every initial law approaches M-star, in the sense of the displayed bound on centered moments. We already know convergence to the common invariant law from that class, which gives uniform ergodicity. Thank you.
