# Paper style

## LaTeX

- Write inline mathematics with dollar delimiters. When prose runs directly into a mathematical expression, insert a nonbreaking space: `site~$i$`, `for~$t\geq0$`, `the set~$A$`.
- Do not use `\(...\)` in the paper source.
- Use `equation*`, `align*`, `gather*`, or another appropriate display environment for unnumbered mathematics.
- Use numbered and labeled display environments only when the displayed formula is cited later. Do not number a formula merely because it is important locally.
- Avoid isolated short displays when the expression reads naturally inline or can be combined with an adjacent display.

## Terminology

- The two generic spin states are called **calm** and **facilitating**.
- In a specific model, use its established terminology when available: vacant/occupied for KCSM, infected/healthy for the contact process, and particle/empty site for BABP.
- The word **activity** is informal. It may be used in the introduction as a collective term for births, refreshes, flips, and their propagation, but it is not a state or a defined mathematical object.
- The signed dual may have an active set or active source. When there is a risk of confusion with the spin states, write **dual-active**.
- Use **bulk patch**, not closed patch.
- The recovered comparison is centered-moment or monomial-moment order, not stochastic domination.

## Prose and paragraph structure

- Paragraphs should correspond to complete mathematical ideas. Avoid a sequence of one- or two-sentence paragraphs unless the argument genuinely changes direction.
- Prefer a small number of developed paragraphs to frequent visual breaks.
- Avoid administrative transitions such as “we now explain,” “the next theorem states,” or “the central condition is” when the mathematical statement can begin directly.
- Do not repeat an interpretation immediately before and after its formal statement.
- Keep setup sections compact, but include the sentence that explains why a definition or coefficient dictionary has its stated form.
