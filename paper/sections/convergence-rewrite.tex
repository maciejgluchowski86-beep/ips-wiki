\section{Proof of the convergence theorem}
\label{sec:convergence}

% The previous version of this section is preserved verbatim in
% paper/sections/convergence.tex while the section is rewritten.

This section proves Theorem~\ref{thm:common-invariant-limit}. The patch
representation suggests a direct route to the limit. At time~$t$, every bulk
patch is already complete, whereas each end patch still carries the terminal
spin through the factor~$C(\eta(i(P)),P)$. In the limiting expression from
Theorem~\ref{thm:common-invariant-limit}, only completed patch contributions
remain, on realizations for which the full patch family is finite. We therefore
have to control two effects: successful interactions that continue to occur at
late times, and the remaining dependence of the end contributions on the
terminal spins once the interactions have stopped.

Fix an intermediate time~$0\leq T<t$. If a successful interaction occurs after
$T$, trace the first such interaction backwards through its ancestry. The
resulting patches end in outgoing interactions and their lifetimes add up to
more than~$T$. The uniform pure-death component then gives a factor at most
$e^{-\varepsilon T}$. If no successful interaction occurs after~$T$, no new
patch boundary is created after~$T$: the end patches present at time~$T$ simply
continue to time~$t$. Their dependence on the terminal spins decays at rate at
least~$\varepsilon$, giving an error of order~$e^{-\varepsilon(t-T)}$.

A separate, standard issue is the spatial spread of the interaction cone before
time~$T$. We restrict it to a finite region~$R$. On this event there are at most
$\abs R$ end patches, while the complementary contribution is controlled by a
finite-propagation estimate. Thus the proof reduces to three estimates: spatial
escape before~$T$, a successful interaction after~$T$, and relaxation of the
end contributions when there is no successful interaction after~$T$. The
argument is first carried out for~$\mu\in\mathcal M_*$, where patch positivity
allows these contributions to be compared term by term. The extension to
$\mathcal M_-$ is made after the three estimates have been combined.
