\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:common-invariant-limit}}
\label{sec:convergence}

% The previous version of this section is preserved verbatim in
% paper/sections/convergence.tex while the section is rewritten.

Fix~$A\Subset\Lambda$ and~$\mu\in\mathcal M_*$. The patch representation gives
\begin{equation*}
(\mu P_t)(\chi_A)
=
\E_A\Cb{
\prod_{P\in\mathcal B_t}C(P)\,
\mu\bb{
\prod_{P\in\mathcal E_t}C\bb{\eta\bb{i(P)},P}
}
},
\end{equation*}
while the limiting moment in Theorem~\ref{thm:common-invariant-limit} is
\begin{equation*}
\E_A\Cb{
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P}C(P)
\ind\bb{\abs{\mathcal P}<\infty}
}.
\end{equation*}
Remove the pure deaths at rate~$\varepsilon$. This leaves the patch decomposition
unchanged and increases the Feynman--Kac potential by~$\varepsilon$ for every
dual-active site. Since the patch contributions are nonnegative, the resulting
less noisy system gives a pointwise upper bound. If a successful interaction
occurs at time~$u$, tracing its ancestry backwards gives patches ending in
outgoing interactions whose lifetimes add up to~$u$. Along these patches the
comparison contributes a factor~$e^{-\varepsilon u}$. Hence realizations with a
successful interaction after time~$T$ have total contribution of order
$e^{-\varepsilon T}$.

On the complementary event there is no successful interaction after~$T$, so the
end patches present at time~$T$ simply continue to time~$t$. Their dependence on
the terminal spin decays at rate at least~$\varepsilon$, and therefore so does
the dependence on the initial law. The resulting bound is proportional to the
number of end patches. We control this number by restricting the interaction
cone up to time~$T$ to a finite region~$R$: on this event there are at most
$\abs R$ end patches, while the complementary contribution is controlled by
finite propagation. These are the three estimates proved below.
