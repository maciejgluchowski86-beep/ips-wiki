\section{Spin systems}
\label{sec:setup}

Let~$\Lambda$ be a countable site set. For each~$i\in\Lambda$, fix a finite
neighbourhood~$N(i)\subseteq\Lambda\setminus\cb{i}$ and assume
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}\abs{N(i)}<\infty.
\end{equation*}

The neighbourhood relation may be directed. Write~$N_*(i)=N(i)\cup\cb{i}$
and, for~$A\subseteq\Lambda$,

\begin{equation*}
N_*(A)=\bigcup_{i\in A}N_*(i),
\qquad
B(A,0)=A,
\qquad
B(A,n+1)=N_*\bb{B(A,n)}.
\end{equation*}

Thus~$B(A,n)$ is the directed ball reached from~$A$ in at most~$n$ steps. The
polynomial-growth assumption used only for
Theorem~\ref{thm:common-invariant-limit} is that, for some~$K,D<\infty$,

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:polynomial-growth}
\abs{B(i,n)}\leq K(1+n)^D
\qquad
\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }n\in\N.
\end{equation}

Write~$A\Subset\Lambda$ when~$A$ is finite and put
$\Omega=\cb{0,1}^{\Lambda}$. A local function depends on finitely many
coordinates. For~$A\Subset\Lambda$, set

\begin{equation*}
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i),
\qquad
\chi_\vn=1.
\end{equation*}

The monomials supported on a finite set form a basis for the functions of the
corresponding coordinates. For a profile
$\mb a=(a_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$, let~$\mu_{\mb a}$ be the
Bernoulli product measure with one-density~$a_i$ at site~$i$. For a constant
profile, write~$\mu_a$.

A spin system has generator

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-generator}
\Lcal f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}c_i(\eta)
\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)},
\end{equation}

where~$c_i\geq0$ depends only on the spins in~$N(i)\cup\cb{i}$ and
$\sup_{i,\eta}c_i(\eta)<\infty$. Here~$\eta^{i,a}$ denotes the configuration
obtained by setting the spin at~$i$ equal to~$a$, and~$\eta^i$ is obtained by
flipping it. Put~$c_i^x(\eta)=c_i(\eta^{i,x})$ for~$x\in\cb{0,1}$. Then

\begin{equation*}
c_i(\eta)
=
\bb{1-\eta(i)}c_i^0(\eta)+\eta(i)c_i^1(\eta),
\end{equation*}

and~$c_i^x$ depends only on~$N(i)$. Its multilinear expansion is

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate-expansion}
c_i^x(\eta)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}c_i^x(S)\chi_S(\eta).
\end{equation}

Thus~$c_i^0$ is the~$0\to1$ rate and~$c_i^1$ the~$1\to0$ rate. The same
symbol is used for a function and its M\"obius coefficients; the argument
$\eta$ or~$S$ distinguishes them. Under the assumptions above, the standard
Harris construction gives a Feller process with finite speed of information
\cite{Harris1978,Liggett1985}. Its semigroup is~$(P_t)_{t\geq0}$, and for an
initial law~$\mu$ we write~$(\mu P_t)(f)=\mu(P_tf)$.

For~$p\in[0,1]$, write~$q=1-p$. The Bernoulli refresh operator at~$i$ is

\begin{align*}
E_{i,p}f(\eta)
&=
pf(\eta^{i,1})+qf(\eta^{i,0}),
\\
\bb{E_{i,p}-I}f(\eta)
&=
\bb{p\bb{1-\eta(i)}+q\eta(i)}
\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)}.
\end{align*}

Here~$p$ is the density of~$1$'s and~$q$ the density of~$0$'s. For
$\mb s=(s_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$, define

\begin{equation*}
\Ncal^{\mb s}f
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bb{E_{i,s_i}-I}f,
\qquad
\Lcal^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\Lcal+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb s}.
\end{equation*}

The constant profile~$\mb 0$ gives independent transitions~$1\to0$. We say
that~$\Lcal$ contains a pure-death component of rate~$\varepsilon>0$ when

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pure-death-component}
\Lcal=\Lcal^\varepsilon+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb 0}
\end{equation}

for another spin-system generator~$\Lcal^\varepsilon$; equivalently,
$c_i^1(\eta)\geq\varepsilon$ for every~$i$ and~$\eta$. The name refers to
the dual process. In the spin system these updates create the facilitating
state.

A probability measure~$\pi$ is invariant when~$\pi P_t=\pi$ for every~$t$.
We call the process ergodic when~$\mu P_t\Rightarrow\pi$ for every initial
law~$\mu$; the limit is then the unique invariant measure. Uniform exponential
ergodicity means that, for some~$\gamma>0$ and every local~$f$,

\begin{equation*}
\sup_{\eta\in\Omega}\abs{P_tf(\eta)-\pi(f)}
\leq C_fe^{-\gamma t}.
\end{equation*}

Since~$\Omega$ is compact in the product topology, convergence against local
functions determines weak convergence.
