\section{Spin systems}
\label{sec:setup}

This section fixes the spin-system setup and notation used throughout the paper.
We describe the dynamics through a neighbourhood-dependent update rate and
refresh parameter, which directly extends the usual KCSM formulation.

Let~$\Lambda$ be a countable site set. For each~$i\in\Lambda$, fix a finite
neighbourhood~$N(i)\subseteq\Lambda\setminus\cb{i}$ and assume
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}\abs{N(i)}<\infty.
\end{equation*}
The neighbourhood relation may be directed. Put
$\Omega=\cb{0,1}^{\Lambda}$. For~$x\in\cb{0,1}$, let~$\eta^{i,x}$ be the
configuration obtained from~$\eta$ by setting the spin at~$i$ equal to~$x$,
and let~$\eta^i$ be obtained by flipping it. For~$p\in[0,1]$, the Bernoulli
refresh operator at~$i$ is
\begin{align*}
E_{i,p}f(\eta)
&=
pf(\eta^{i,1})+(1-p)f(\eta^{i,0}),
\\
\bb{E_{i,p}-I}f(\eta)
&=
\bb{p\bb{1-\eta(i)}+(1-p)\eta(i)}
\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)}.
\end{align*}
Thus a refresh sets the spin to~$1$ with probability~$p$ and to~$0$ with
probability~$1-p$.

A spin system is specified by functions
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_i:\Omega\to[0,\infty),
\qquad
\rho_i:\Omega\to[0,1],
\end{equation*}
each depending only on the spins in~$N(i)${\color{red} This should have been~$N_*(i)$}, with
$\sup_{i,\eta}\lambda_i(\eta)<\infty$. Its generator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-generator}
\Lcal f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\lambda_i(\eta)\bb{E_{i,\rho_i(\eta)}-I}f(\eta).
\end{equation}
At rate~$\lambda_i(\eta)$, the spin at~$i$ is refreshed to~$1$ with
probability~$\rho_i(\eta)$ and to~$0$ with probability~$1-\rho_i(\eta)$.
The value of~$\rho_i(\eta)$ is irrelevant when~$\lambda_i(\eta)=0$. For a
standard KCSM with Bernoulli refresh parameter~$p$, one has
$\rho_i\equiv p$, while~$\lambda_i$ is the kinetic constraint. Thus the
parametrization in~\eqref{eq:spin-generator} keeps the KCSM separation between
the rate at which updates occur and the law used to refresh the spin, while
allowing both to depend on the neighbourhood.

This parametrization is equivalent to the usual flip-rate description. If
$c_i$ denotes the flip rate in the conventional generator
$\Lcal f(\eta)=\sum_i c_i(\eta)\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)}$, the two descriptions are
related by {\color{red} formatting looks really bad}
\begin{align*}
c_i(\eta)
&=
\lambda_i(\eta)
\bb{
\bb{1-\eta(i)}\rho_i(\eta)
+
\eta(i)\bb{1-\rho_i(\eta)}
},
\\
\lambda_i(\eta)
&=
c_i(\eta^{i,0})+c_i(\eta^{i,1}),
&
\rho_i(\eta)
&=
\frac{c_i(\eta^{i,0})}{\lambda_i(\eta)}
\quad\text{when }\lambda_i(\eta)>0.
\end{align*}
When~$\lambda_i(\eta)=0$, the value of~$\rho_i(\eta)$ may be chosen
arbitrarily.

Write~$A\Subset\Lambda$ when~$A$ is finite. A local function depends on
finitely many coordinates. For~$A\Subset\Lambda$, set
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i),
\qquad

\end{equation*}
The monomials supported on a finite set form a basis for the functions of the
corresponding coordinates. For a density profile
$\mb p=(p_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$, let~$\mu_{\mb p}$ be the
Bernoulli product measure with
$\mu_{\mb p}\bb{\eta(i)=1}=p_i$ for every~$i$. For a constant profile,
write~$\mu_p$.

For any function~$g$ depending only on the spins in~$N(i)$, write its unique

\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate-expansion}

\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}\widehat g(S)\chi_S(\eta).
\end{equation}

$A\subseteq\Lambda$,
\begin{equation*}

\qquad

\qquad

\end{equation*}
Thus~$B(A,n)$ is the directed ball reached from~$A$ in at most~$n$ steps.
When polynomial growth is required, we assume that, for some~$K,D<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:polynomial-growth}
\abs{B(i,n)}\leq K(1+n)^D
\qquad
\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }n\in\N.
\end{equation}

The standard Harris construction gives a Feller process under the assumptions
above~\cite{Harris1978,Liggett1985}. Its semigroup is~$(P_t)_{t\geq0}$, and for
an initial law~$\mu$ we write~$(\mu P_t)(f)=\mu(P_tf)$. A probability
measure~$\pi$ is invariant when~$\pi P_t=\pi$ for every~$t$. We call the
process ergodic when~$\mu P_t\Rightarrow\pi$ for every initial law~$\mu$; the
limit is then the unique invariant measure. Uniform exponential ergodicity
means that there is~$\gamma>0$ such that, for every local~$f$, some~$C_f<\infty$
satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{\eta\in\Omega}\abs{P_tf(\eta)-\pi(f)}
\leq C_fe^{-\gamma t}.
\end{equation*}
Since~$\Omega$ is compact in the product topology, convergence against local
functions determines weak convergence.

For~$A\Subset\Lambda$,~$P_t\chi_A(\eta)$ is the expected product of the spins
in~$A$ at time~$t$ when the process starts from~$\eta$. Section~\ref{sec:signed-dual}
constructs a signed set-valued process~$(A_s,\sigma_s)$ and a potential~$V$
that represent this quantity. Writing~$\E_A$ for expectation for the dual
started from~$(A,+)$, we assume throughout that
\begin{equation}
\tag{FK}
\label{eq:fk-integrability}
\E_A\Cb{\exp\bb{\int_0^t V(A_s)\,ds}}<\infty
\qquad
(A\Subset\Lambda,\ t\geq0).
\end{equation}
We refer to~\eqref{eq:fk-integrability} as \emph{Feynman--Kac
integrability}; see~\cite[p.~62]{JansenKurt2014}.

We also use independent Bernoulli refreshes to perturb the dynamics. For
$\mb s=(s_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$ and~$\varepsilon\geq0$, define
\begin{equation*}
\Ncal^{\mb s}f
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bb{E_{i,s_i}-I}f,
\qquad
\Lcal^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\Lcal+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb s}.
\end{equation*}
This is again a spin system. If~$\lambda_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}$ and
$\rho_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}$ denote its update rate and refresh parameter, then
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\lambda_i+\varepsilon,
\qquad
\lambda_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}\rho_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\lambda_i\rho_i+\varepsilon s_i.
\end{equation*}
Hence, whenever~$\lambda_i(\eta)+\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation*}
\rho_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}(\eta)
=
\frac{\lambda_i(\eta)\rho_i(\eta)+\varepsilon s_i}
{\lambda_i(\eta)+\varepsilon}.
\end{equation*}

The constant profile~$\mb 0$ gives independent transitions~$1\to0$. We say
that~$\Lcal$ contains a pure-death component of rate~$\varepsilon>0$ when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pure-death-component}
\Lcal=\Lcal^\varepsilon+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb 0}
\end{equation}
for another spin-system generator~$\Lcal^\varepsilon$. Equivalently,
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_i(\eta)\bb{1-\rho_i(\eta)}
\geq\varepsilon
\qquad\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }\eta\in\Omega,
\end{equation*}
since the left-hand side is the~$1\to0$ transition rate at~$i$.
