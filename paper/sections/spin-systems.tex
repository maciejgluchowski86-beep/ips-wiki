\section{Spin systems}
\label{sec:setup}

This section fixes the basic setting and notation used throughout the paper. We
also record the assumptions on the interaction graph and introduce an
elementary way of adding independent single-site noise.

Let~$\Lambda$ be a countable site set. For each~$i\in\Lambda$, fix a finite
neighbourhood~$N(i)\subseteq\Lambda\setminus\cb{i}$ and assume
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}\abs{N(i)}<\infty.
\end{equation*}
The neighbourhood relation may be directed. Write~$N_*(i)=N(i)\cup\cb{i}$
and, for~$A\subseteq\Lambda$,
\begin{equation*}
N_*(A)=\bigcup_{i\in A}N_*(i),
\qquad
B(A,0)=A,
\qquad
B(A,n+1)=N_*\bb{B(A,n)}.
\end{equation*}
Thus~$B(A,n)$ is the directed ball reached from~$A$ in at most~$n$ steps. The polynomial-growth assumption is that, for some~$K,D<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:polynomial-growth}
\abs{B(i,n)}\leq K(1+n)^D
\qquad
\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }n\in\N.
\end{equation}

Write~$A\Subset\Lambda$ when~$A$ is finite and put
$\Omega=\cb{0,1}^{\Lambda}$. A local function depends on finitely many
coordinates. For~$A\Subset\Lambda$, set
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i),
\qquad
\chi_\vn=1.
\end{equation*}
The monomials supported on a finite set form a basis for the functions of the
corresponding coordinates. For a density profile
$\mb p=(p_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$, let~$\mu_{\mb p}$ be the
Bernoulli product measure with
$\mu_{\mb p}\bb{\eta(i)=1}=p_i$ for every~$i$. For a constant profile,
write~$\mu_p$.

For~$x\in\cb{0,1}$, let~$\eta^{i,x}$ be the configuration obtained from~$\eta$
by setting the spin at~$i$ equal to~$x$, and let~$\eta^i$ be obtained by
flipping it. For~$p\in[0,1]$, the Bernoulli refresh operator at~$i$ is
\begin{align*}
E_{i,p}f(\eta)
&=
pf(\eta^{i,1})+(1-p)f(\eta^{i,0}),
\\
\bb{E_{i,p}-I}f(\eta)
&=
\bb{p\bb{1-\eta(i)}+(1-p)\eta(i)}
\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)}.
\end{align*}
Thus a refresh sets the spin to~$1$ with probability~$p$ and to~$0$ with
probability~$1-p$.

A spin system is specified by functions
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_i:\Omega\to[0,\infty),
\qquad
\rho_i:\Omega\to[0,1],
\end{equation*}
each depending only on the spins in~$N(i)$, with
$\sup_{i,\eta}\lambda_i(\eta)<\infty$. Its generator is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-generator}
\Lcal f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\lambda_i(\eta)\bb{E_{i,\rho_i(\eta)}-I}f(\eta).
\end{equation}
At rate~$\lambda_i(\eta)$, the spin at~$i$ is refreshed to~$1$ with
probability~$\rho_i(\eta)$ and to~$0$ with probability~$1-\rho_i(\eta)$.
The value of~$\rho_i(\eta)$ is irrelevant when~$\lambda_i(\eta)=0$. For a
standard KCSM with Bernoulli refresh parameter~$p$, one has
$\rho_i\equiv p$, while~$\lambda_i$ is the kinetic constraint. Thus~\eqref{eq:spin-generator} keeps the usual KCSM
separation between the rate at which updates occur and the law used to refresh
the spin, while allowing both to depend on the neighbourhood.

This is equivalent to the usual flip-rate description of a spin system. Given
$\lambda_i$ and~$\rho_i$, define
\begin{equation*}
c_i(\eta)
=
\lambda_i(\eta)
\bb{
\bb{1-\eta(i)}\rho_i(\eta)
+
\eta(i)\bb{1-\rho_i(\eta)}
}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation*}
\Lcal f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}c_i(\eta)
\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)}.
\end{equation*}
Conversely, given a flip rate~$c_i$, set
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_i(\eta)
=
c_i(\eta^{i,0})+c_i(\eta^{i,1}),
\end{equation*}
and, whenever~$\lambda_i(\eta)>0$,
\begin{equation*}
\rho_i(\eta)
=
\frac{c_i(\eta^{i,0})}{\lambda_i(\eta)}.
\end{equation*}
When~$\lambda_i(\eta)=0$,~$\rho_i(\eta)$ may be chosen arbitrarily. We use the
refresh parametrization because it separates the frequency of updates from the
law of the refreshed spin.

For any function~$g$ depending only on the spins in~$N(i)$, write its unique
multilinear expansion as
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate-expansion}
g(\eta)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}\widehat g(S)\chi_S(\eta).
\end{equation}
We call~$\widehat g(S)$ the M\"obius coefficient of~$g$ at~$S$. A hat applies
to the whole function underneath it; in particular,
$\widehat{\lambda_i\rho_i}(S)$ is the coefficient of the product function
$\lambda_i\rho_i$, not the product of two coefficients. The two coefficient
families used below are
\begin{align*}
\lambda_i(\eta)
&=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}\widehat{\lambda_i}(S)\chi_S(\eta),
\\
\lambda_i(\eta)\rho_i(\eta)
&=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}\widehat{\lambda_i\rho_i}(S)\chi_S(\eta).
\end{align*}
We do not need M\"obius coefficients of~$\rho_i$ itself. This is also natural
when~$\lambda_i(\eta)=0$, where the value of~$\rho_i(\eta)$ does not affect the
dynamics.

Under the assumptions above, the standard Harris construction gives a Feller
process with finite speed of information~\cite{Harris1978,Liggett1985}. Its
semigroup is~$(P_t)_{t\geq0}$, and for an initial law~$\mu$ we write
$(\mu P_t)(f)=\mu(P_tf)$.

For~$A\Subset\Lambda$,~$P_t\chi_A(\eta)$ is the expected product of the spins
in~$A$ at time~$t$ when the process starts from~$\eta$. We represent the
dependence of this observable on the initial configuration by a signed
set-valued process~$(A_s,\sigma_s)$, called the signed monomial dual. Starting
from~$A$ at the observation time,~$A_s$ traces backward through the
interactions that carry this dependence to earlier sites. The sign~$\sigma_s$
records the accumulated sign, and a potential~$V(A_s)$ supplies the
Feynman--Kac correction. The process and potential are constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:signed-dual}. Writing~$\E_A$ for expectation for this process
started from~$(A,+)$, we impose throughout the absolute-integrability condition
\begin{equation}
\tag{FK}
\label{eq:fk-integrability}
\E_A\Cb{\exp\bb{\int_0^t V(A_s)\,ds}}<\infty
\qquad
(A\Subset\Lambda,\ t\geq0).
\end{equation}
We refer to~\eqref{eq:fk-integrability} as \emph{Feynman--Kac
integrability}. Since~$\abs{\sigma_t\chi_{A_t}(\eta)}\leq1$, it is precisely
the absolute-integrability condition for the Feynman--Kac correction; see
\cite[p.~62]{JansenKurt2014}.

We will also use independent Bernoulli refreshes to perturb the dynamics. For
$\mb s=(s_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$ and~$\varepsilon\geq0$, define
\begin{equation*}
\Ncal^{\mb s}f
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bb{E_{i,s_i}-I}f,
\qquad
\Lcal^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\Lcal+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb s}.
\end{equation*}
This is again a spin system. If~$\lambda_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}$ and
$\rho_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}$ denote its update rate and refresh parameter, then
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\lambda_i+\varepsilon,
\qquad
\lambda_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}\rho_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\lambda_i\rho_i+\varepsilon s_i.
\end{equation*}
Hence, whenever~$\lambda_i(\eta)+\varepsilon>0$,
\begin{equation*}
\rho_i^{\varepsilon,\mb s}(\eta)
=
\frac{\lambda_i(\eta)\rho_i(\eta)+\varepsilon s_i}
{\lambda_i(\eta)+\varepsilon}.
\end{equation*}

The constant profile~$\mb 0$ gives independent transitions~$1\to0$. We say
that~$\Lcal$ contains a pure-death component of rate~$\varepsilon>0$ when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pure-death-component}
\Lcal=\Lcal^\varepsilon+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb 0}
\end{equation}
for another spin-system generator~$\Lcal^\varepsilon$. Equivalently,
\begin{equation*}
\lambda_i(\eta)\bb{1-\rho_i(\eta)}
\geq\varepsilon
\qquad\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }\eta\in\Omega.
\end{equation*}
Indeed, the left-hand side is the rate of the transition~$1\to0$ at~$i$.

A probability measure~$\pi$ is invariant when~$\pi P_t=\pi$ for every~$t$.
We call the process ergodic when~$\mu P_t\Rightarrow\pi$ for every initial
law~$\mu$; the limit is then the unique invariant measure. Uniform exponential
ergodicity means that, for some~$\gamma>0$ and every local~$f$,
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{\eta\in\Omega}\abs{P_tf(\eta)-\pi(f)}
\leq C_fe^{-\gamma t}.
\end{equation*}
Since~$\Omega$ is compact in the product topology, convergence against local
functions determines weak convergence.
