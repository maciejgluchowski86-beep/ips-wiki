\section{Spin systems and motivating examples}
\label{sec:spin-systems}

\subsection{State space and local observables}
\label{subsec:lattices-observables}

Let \(\Lambda\) be a countable set. For each \(i\in\Lambda\), fix a finite
neighbourhood \(N(i)\subseteq\Lambda\setminus\{i\}\), and assume
\[
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}|N(i)|<\infty.
\]
The neighbourhoods may be directed: \(j\in N(i)\) need not imply
\(i\in N(j)\). We write \(A\Subset\Lambda\) when \(A\) is finite, and set
\(\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}\). Following the KCSM convention, state \(0\) is
facilitating and state \(1\) is calm.

\begin{definition}[Local functions]
\label{def:local-function}
A function \(f:\Omega\to\R\) is \emph{local} if there is
\(A\Subset\Lambda\) such that \(f(\eta)=f(\xi)\) whenever
\(\eta|_A=\xi|_A\). Such an \(A\) is called a dependence set of \(f\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Monomials]
\label{def:monomials}
For \(A\Subset\Lambda\), define
\[
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i),
\qquad
\chi_\varnothing=1.
\]
If a local function depends on \(A\), it has a unique expansion in the basis
\(\{\chi_B:B\subseteq A\}\).
\end{definition}

\subsection{Spin-system rates and coefficient expansions}
\label{subsec:rates-coefficients}
\label{subsec:calm-facilitating-rates}

For \(i\in\Lambda\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), let \(\eta^{i,a}\) be obtained from
\(\eta\) by setting the spin at \(i\) equal to \(a\). We write \(\eta^i\) for
the configuration obtained by flipping that spin.

\begin{definition}[Spin system]
\label{def:spin-system}
A spin system on \(\Omega\) is a continuous-time Markov process whose
generator acts on local functions by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-generator}
\Lcal f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}c_i(\eta)
\bigl(f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(c_i(\eta)\geq0\) is the flip rate at \(i\). We assume that \(c_i\)
depends only on \(\eta|_{N(i)\cup\{i\}}\) and that
\[
\sup_{i\in\Lambda, \eta\in\Omega}c_i(\eta)<\infty.
\]
\end{definition}

For \(x\in\{0,1\}\), set \(c_i^x(\eta)=c_i(\eta^{i,x})\). Then \(c_i^x\)
depends only on \(\eta|_{N(i)}\), and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:statewise-rates}
c_i(\eta)
=
(1-\eta(i))c_i^0(\eta)+\eta(i)c_i^1(\eta).
\end{equation}
Thus \(c_i^0\) is the rate of the flip \(0\to1\), while \(c_i^1\) is the
rate of the flip \(1\to0\).

Each \(c_i^x\) has the unique multilinear expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate-expansion}
c_i^x(\eta)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}c_i^x(S)\chi_S(\eta).
\end{equation}
The same symbol denotes the function and its coefficients; the argument
\(\eta\) or \(S\) distinguishes them. The coefficients are given by M\"obius
inversion on the subsets of \(N(i)\).

Under these assumptions, the standard Harris construction gives a Feller
process; we use this without further comment. See Liggett,
\emph{Interacting Particle Systems}, Chapter~I. Its semigroup is denoted by
\((P_t)_{t\geq0}\), with \(P_tf(\eta)=\E_\eta[f(\eta_t)]\). For a probability
measure \(\mu\), we write \((\mu P_t)(f)=\mu(P_tf)\).

Nonpositive interaction coefficients give one simple form of the
calm--facilitating tendency. For fixed \(i\) and \(x\), if
\[
c_i^x(S)\leq0
\qquad\text{for every nonempty }S\subseteq N(i),
\]
then \(c_i^x\) is coordinatewise nonincreasing on \(\{0,1\}^{N(i)}\):
replacing facilitating spins by calm spins can only decrease the flip rate.
The converse is false. Patch positivity, introduced in
Section~\ref{sec:patch-positivity}, instead relates the coefficients of
\(c_i^0\) and \(c_i^1\).

\subsection{Bernoulli refreshes and motivating examples}
\label{subsec:motivating-models}

\phantomsection\label{def:bernoulli-refresh}
For \(p\in[0,1]\), write \(q=1-p\). The Bernoulli refresh operator at site
\(i\) is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bernoulli-refresh}
E_{i,p}f(\eta)
=
pf(\eta^{i,1})+qf(\eta^{i,0}),
\qquad
(E_{i,p}-I)f(\eta)
=
\bigl(p(1-\eta(i))+q\eta(i)\bigr)
\bigl(f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Here \(p\) is the density of calm spins and \(q\) the density of facilitating
spins. In the KCSM and BABP literature, the prefactor multiplying
\(E_{i,p}-I\) is often denoted by \(c_i\) and called the constraint or refresh
rate. In this paper, \(c_i\) always denotes the resulting flip rate; refresh
rates are written explicitly.

\subsubsection{Contact process}
\label{subsubsec:contact-process}

The contact process is usually written with state \(1\) infected and state
\(0\) healthy. We reverse these labels, so that \(0\) is active and \(1\) is
healthy, hence calm. With recovery rate one and infection parameter
\(\lambda\geq0\), its generator is
\[
\Lcal_{\mathrm{CP}}f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\left(
1-\eta(i)
+
\lambda\eta(i)\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j))
\right)
\bigl(f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)\bigr).
\]
Thus
\[
c_i^0(\eta)=1,
\qquad
c_i^1(\eta)=\lambda\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j)),
\]
and the only nonzero nonconstant coefficients are
\(c_i^1(\{j\})=-\lambda\), \(j\in N(i)\). Since the contact process is
attractive, classical coupling gives stronger comparisons than those developed
here.

\subsubsection{FA-1f}
\label{subsubsec:fa-1f-model}

In FA-1f, state \(0\) is called vacant and state \(1\) occupied. A site may
refresh when at least one neighbour is vacant. On the present neighbourhood
system,
\[
\Lcal_{\mathrm{FA}}f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\bigl(1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta)\bigr)
(E_{i,p}-I)f(\eta).
\]
The flip rates are
\[
c_i^0(\eta)=p\bigl(1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta)\bigr),
\qquad
c_i^1(\eta)=q\bigl(1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta)\bigr).
\]
When \(N(i)\neq\varnothing\), their only nonzero coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_i^0(\varnothing)&=p,
&c_i^0(N(i))&=-p,\\
c_i^1(\varnothing)&=q,
&c_i^1(N(i))&=-q.
\end{aligned}
\]
The standard FA-1f model uses an undirected graph. The East model is the
oriented case with one prescribed facilitating neighbour.

\subsubsection{Biased annihilating branching process}
\label{subsubsec:babp-model}

BABP uses the same refresh law, with refresh rate equal to the number of vacant
neighbours:
\[
\Lcal_{\mathrm{BABP}}f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\left(\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j))\right)
(E_{i,p}-I)f(\eta).
\]
Hence
\[
c_i^0(\eta)
=
p\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j)),
\qquad
c_i^1(\eta)
=
q\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j)),
\]
with nonzero coefficients
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_i^0(\varnothing)&=p|N(i)|,
&c_i^0(\{j\})&=-p,\\
c_i^1(\varnothing)&=q|N(i)|,
&c_i^1(\{j\})&=-q,
\end{aligned}
\qquad j\in N(i).
\]
In the complementary particle variables \(\xi(i)=1-\eta(i)\), if \(p>0\)
and time is rescaled by \(p^{-1}\), a particle is born at an empty site at rate
\(\lambda=q/p\) for each neighbouring particle, while each neighbouring
particle removes the particle at the target at rate one. BABP is not attractive
in the usual product order.

\subsection{Independent noise}
\label{subsec:noise-ergodicity}
\label{def:bernoulli-noise}

Let \(\mathbf p=(p_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda\). Independent Bernoulli
noise with profile \(\mathbf p\) has generator
\[
\Ncal^{\mathbf p}f
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}(E_{i,p_i}-I)f.
\]
Adding this noise at level \(\varepsilon\geq0\) gives
\[
\Lcal^\varepsilon
=
\Lcal+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mathbf p}.
\]
The perturbation adds the environment-independent rates \(\varepsilon p_i\)
to \(0\to1\) and \(\varepsilon(1-p_i)\) to \(1\to0\). We say that the spin
system has a \emph{uniform independent \(1\to0\) component} of level
\(\delta>0\) if
\[
c_i^1(\eta)\geq\delta
\qquad
\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }\eta\in\Omega.
\]
Equivalently, an independent transition \(1\to0\) at rate \(\delta\) can be
removed while leaving nonnegative flip rates. The perturbation above has such
a component of level \(\varepsilon\inf_i(1-p_i)\) whenever this quantity is
positive. After introducing the monomial dual in Section~\ref{sec:signed-dual},
we call this a uniform pure-death component.

\subsection{Invariant measures and convergence}
\label{subsec:invariant-ergodicity}

\begin{definition}[Invariant measure and ergodicity]
\label{def:invariant-measure}
\label{def:ergodicity}
A probability measure \(\nu\) on \(\Omega\) is \emph{invariant} if
\[
\nu P_t=\nu
\qquad
\text{for every }t\geq0.
\]
The spin system is \emph{ergodic} if there is a probability measure \(\nu\)
such that
\[
\mu P_t\Rightarrow\nu
\qquad
\text{as }t\to\infty
\]
for every probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\Omega\). The limiting measure is
then the unique invariant measure.
\end{definition}

Since \(\Omega\) is compact in the product topology, weak convergence is
determined by local functions.

\begin{definition}[Uniform exponential ergodicity]
\label{def:uniform-exponential-ergodicity}
The spin system is \emph{uniformly exponentially ergodic} if it has an
invariant measure \(\nu\), and there is \(\gamma>0\) such that for every local
function \(f\) there is \(C_f<\infty\) satisfying
\[
\sup_{\eta\in\Omega}
\left|P_tf(\eta)-\nu(f)\right|
\leq
C_f e^{-\gamma t}
\qquad
\text{for every }t\geq0.
\]
\end{definition}

Some results below are uniform only over specified classes of initial laws.
Such a restricted estimate is not called ergodicity unless it holds for every
point mass, and hence, by integration, for every initial law.
