\section{Spin systems}
\label{sec:setup}

This section fixes the basic setting and notation used throughout the paper. We
also record the assumptions on the interaction graph and introduce an
elementary way of adding independent single-site noise.

Let~$\Lambda$ be a countable site set. For each~$i\in\Lambda$, fix a finite
neighbourhood~$N(i)\subseteq\Lambda\setminus\cb{i}$ and assume
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}\abs{N(i)}<\infty.
\end{equation*}
The neighbourhood relation may be directed. Write~$N_*(i)=N(i)\cup\cb{i}$
and, for~$A\subseteq\Lambda$,
\begin{equation*}
N_*(A)=\bigcup_{i\in A}N_*(i),
\qquad
B(A,0)=A,
\qquad
B(A,n+1)=N_*\bb{B(A,n)}.
\end{equation*}
Thus~$B(A,n)$ is the directed ball reached from~$A$ in at most~$n$ steps.

The polynomial-growth assumption is that, for some~$K,D<\infty$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:polynomial-growth}
\abs{B(i,n)}\leq K(1+n)^D
\qquad
\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }n\in\N.
\end{equation}

Write~$A\Subset\Lambda$ when~$A$ is finite and put
$\Omega=\cb{0,1}^{\Lambda}$. A local function depends on finitely many
coordinates. For~$A\Subset\Lambda$, set
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i),
\qquad
\chi_\vn=1.
\end{equation*}
The monomials supported on a finite set form a basis for the functions of the
corresponding coordinates. For a density profile
$\mb p=(p_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$, let~$\mu_{\mb p}$ be the
Bernoulli product measure with
$\mu_{\mb p}\bb{\eta(i)=1}=p_i$ for every~$i$. For a constant profile,
write~$\mu_p$.

A spin system has generator
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-generator}
\Lcal f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}c_i(\eta)
\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)},
\end{equation}
where~$c_i\geq0$ depends only on the spins in~$N(i)\cup\cb{i}$ and
$\sup_{i,\eta}c_i(\eta)<\infty$. Here~$\eta^{i,x}$ denotes the configuration
obtained by setting the spin at~$i$ equal to~$x$, and~$\eta^i$ is obtained by
flipping it. Put~$c_i^x(\eta)=c_i(\eta^{i,x})$ for~$x\in\cb{0,1}$. Then
\begin{equation*}
c_i(\eta)
=
\bb{1-\eta(i)}c_i^0(\eta)+\eta(i)c_i^1(\eta),
\end{equation*}
and~$c_i^x$ depends only on~$N(i)$. Its multilinear expansion is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate-expansion}
c_i^x(\eta)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}c_i^x(S)\chi_S(\eta).
\end{equation}
Thus~$c_i^0$ and~$c_i^1$ are the restrictions of the same flip rate~$c_i$
to configurations with the spin at~$i$ equal to~$0$ and~$1$, respectively;
they are the~$0\to1$ and~$1\to0$ rates. The same symbol is used for a
function and its M\"obius coefficients; the argument~$\eta$ or~$S$
distinguishes them. Under the assumptions above, the standard Harris
construction gives a Feller process with finite speed of
information~\cite{Harris1978,Liggett1985}. Its semigroup is~$(P_t)_{t\geq0}$,
and for an initial law~$\mu$ we write~$(\mu P_t)(f)=\mu(P_tf)$.

Later we represent the evolution of each monomial~$\chi_A$ {\color{red} bullshit about monomial evolving} by a signed
set-valued process~$(A_t,\sigma_t)$, called the signed monomial dual. Starting
from~$A$, the set~$A_t$ records the sites on which the dual monomial depends {\color{red} bullshit about monomial evolving},
while~$\sigma_t$ records the accumulated sign; a potential~$V(A_t)$ supplies
the Feynman--Kac correction. The process and potential are constructed in
Section~\ref{sec:signed-dual}. Writing~$\E_A$ for expectation for this process
started from~$(A,+)$, we impose throughout the absolute-integrability condition
\begin{equation}
\tag{FK}
\label{eq:fk-integrability}
\E_A\Cb{\exp\bb{\int_0^t V(A_s)\,ds}}<\infty
\qquad
(A\Subset\Lambda,\ t\geq0).
\end{equation}
We refer to~\eqref{eq:fk-integrability} as \emph{Feynman--Kac
integrability}. Since~$\abs{\sigma_t\chi_{A_t}(\eta)}\leq1$, it is precisely
the absolute-integrability condition for the Feynman--Kac correction; see
\cite[p.~62]{JansenKurt2014}.

We will also use independent Bernoulli refreshes to perturb the dynamics. For
$p\in[0,1]$, write~$q=1-p$. The Bernoulli refresh operator at~$i$ is
\begin{align*}
E_{i,p}f(\eta)
&=
pf(\eta^{i,1})+qf(\eta^{i,0}),
\\
\bb{E_{i,p}-I}f(\eta)
&=
\bb{p\bb{1-\eta(i)}+q\eta(i)}
\bb{f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)}.
\end{align*}
Thus the refresh sets the spin to~$1$ with probability~$p$ and to~$0$ with
probability~$q$. For
$\mb s=(s_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda$, define
\begin{equation*}
\Ncal^{\mb s}f
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}\bb{E_{i,s_i}-I}f,
\qquad
\Lcal^{\varepsilon,\mb s}
=
\Lcal+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb s}.
\end{equation*}

The constant profile~$\mb 0$ gives independent transitions~$1\to0$. We say
that~$\Lcal$ contains a pure-death component of rate~$\varepsilon>0$ when
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pure-death-component}
\Lcal=\Lcal^\varepsilon+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mb 0}
\end{equation}
for another spin-system generator~$\Lcal^\varepsilon$; equivalently,
$c_i^1(\eta)\geq\varepsilon$ for every~$i$ and~$\eta$.

A probability measure~$\pi$ is invariant when~$\pi P_t=\pi$ for every~$t$.
We call the process ergodic when~$\mu P_t\Rightarrow\pi$ for every initial
law~$\mu$; the limit is then the unique invariant measure. Uniform exponential
ergodicity means that, for some~$\gamma>0$ and every local~$f$,
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{\eta\in\Omega}\abs{P_tf(\eta)-\pi(f)}
\leq C_fe^{-\gamma t}.
\end{equation*}
Since~$\Omega$ is compact in the product topology, convergence against local
functions determines weak convergence.
