\section{Spin systems and motivating examples}
\label{sec:spin-systems}

\subsection{Lattices and local observables}
\label{subsec:lattices-observables}

Let \(\Lambda\) be a countable set. For each \(i\in\Lambda\), fix a finite
neighbourhood
\[
N(i)\subseteq\Lambda\setminus\{i\}.
\]
The neighbourhoods may be directed: we do not assume that \(j\in N(i)\)
implies \(i\in N(j)\). Throughout the paper,
\[
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}|N(i)|<\infty.
\]
We write \(A\Subset\Lambda\) when \(A\) is finite.

The configuration space is
\[
\Omega=\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}.
\]
Following the convention for kinetically constrained spin models, the state
\(0\) is facilitating and the state \(1\) is calm. We use this terminology in
the general discussion and record the conventional names used for each model
below.

\begin{definition}[Local functions]
\label{def:local-function}
A function \(f:\Omega\to\R\) is \emph{local} if there is
\(A\Subset\Lambda\) such that \(f(\eta)=f(\xi)\) whenever
\(\eta|_A=\xi|_A\). Such an \(A\) is called a dependence set of \(f\).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Monomials]
\label{def:monomials}
For \(A\Subset\Lambda\), define
\[
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i),
\qquad
\chi_\varnothing=1.
\]
If a local function depends on \(A\), it has a unique expansion as a linear
combination of \(\{\chi_B:B\subseteq A\}\). Thus the monomials form a basis of
the local functions.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Spin-system rates and coefficient expansions}
\label{subsec:rates-coefficients}

For \(i\in\Lambda\) and \(a\in\{0,1\}\), let \(\eta^{i,a}\) be obtained from
\(\eta\) by setting the spin at \(i\) equal to \(a\). We write \(\eta^i\) for
the configuration obtained by flipping that spin.

\begin{definition}[Spin system]
\label{def:spin-system}
A spin system on \(\Omega\) is a continuous-time Markov process whose
generator acts on local functions by
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:spin-generator}
\Lcal f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}c_i(\eta)
\bigl(f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)\bigr),
\end{equation}
where \(c_i(\eta)\geq0\) is the flip rate at \(i\). We assume that \(c_i\)
depends only on \(\eta|_{N(i)\cup\{i\}}\) and that
\[
\sup_{i\in\Lambda, \eta\in\Omega}c_i(\eta)<\infty.
\]
\end{definition}

For \(x\in\{0,1\}\), define
\[
c_i^x(\eta)=c_i(\eta^{i,x}).
\]
Then \(c_i^x\) depends only on \(\eta|_{N(i)}\), and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:statewise-rates}
c_i(\eta)
=
(1-\eta(i))c_i^0(\eta)+\eta(i)c_i^1(\eta).
\end{equation}
Thus \(c_i^0\) is the rate of the flip \(0\to1\), while \(c_i^1\) is the
rate of the flip \(1\to0\).

The finite-neighbourhood function \(c_i^x\) has the unique multilinear
expansion
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:rate-expansion}
c_i^x(\eta)
=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}c_i^x(S)\chi_S(\eta).
\end{equation}
We use the same symbol for the rate and its coefficients; the argument
\(\eta\) or \(S\) distinguishes them. The coefficients are given by the usual
Möbius inversion formula on the subsets of \(N(i)\).

Under the assumptions above, the standard Harris construction gives a Feller
process; we take this construction for granted. See, for example, Liggett,
\emph{Interacting Particle Systems}, Chapter~I. Its semigroup is denoted by
\((P_t)_{t\geq0}\):
\[
P_tf(\eta)=\E_\eta[f(\eta_t)].
\]
For a probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\Omega\), write
\(\mu P_t(f)=\mu(P_tf)\).

\subsection{The calm--facilitating rate structure}
\label{subsec:calm-facilitating-rates}

The calm--facilitating terminology is an interpretation, not a definition of
a class of spin systems. One elementary coefficient condition already
reflects this interpretation. If
\[
c_i^x(S)\leq0
\qquad
\text{for every nonempty }S\subseteq N(i),
\]
then \(c_i^x\) is coordinatewise nonincreasing on \(\{0,1\}^{N(i)}\). In
other words, replacing facilitating spins by calm spins can only decrease the
flip rate. This coefficient condition is stronger than coordinatewise
monotonicity: a coordinatewise decreasing function on the Boolean cube need
not have all its nonconstant multilinear coefficients nonpositive.

The examples below have this sign pattern. The formal condition used in the
paper is patch positivity, introduced in Section~\ref{sec:patch-positivity};
it is a condition on the relation between the coefficients of \(c_i^0\) and
\(c_i^1\), rather than a definition based on this informal terminology.

\subsection{Three update mechanisms}
\label{subsec:motivating-models}

\begin{definition}[Bernoulli refresh]
\label{def:bernoulli-refresh}
For \(p\in[0,1]\), write \(q=1-p\) and set
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:bernoulli-refresh}
E_{i,p}f(\eta)
=
pf(\eta^{i,1})+qf(\eta^{i,0}).
\end{equation}
Here \(p\) is the density of calm spins and \(q\) is the density of
facilitating spins. In flip form,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:refresh-flip-form}
(E_{i,p}-I)f(\eta)
=
\bigl(p(1-\eta(i))+q\eta(i)\bigr)
\bigl(f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
In the KCSM and BABP literature, the prefactor multiplying
\(E_{i,p}-I\) is often denoted by \(c_i\) and called the constraint or refresh
rate. Throughout this paper, \(c_i\) always denotes the resulting flip rate;
the refresh prefactors in the examples are written explicitly.

\subsubsection{Contact process}
\label{subsubsec:contact-process}

The contact process is usually written with state \(1\) occupied or infected
and state \(0\) healthy. We reverse these labels, so that \(0\) is active and
\(1\) healthy, hence calm in our terminology. With recovery rate one and
infection parameter \(\lambda\geq0\), its generator is
\[
\Lcal_{\mathrm{CP}}f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\left(
1-\eta(i)
+
\lambda\eta(i)\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j))
\right)
\bigl(f(\eta^i)-f(\eta)\bigr).
\]
Thus
\[
c_i^0(\eta)=1,
\qquad
c_i^1(\eta)=\lambda\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j)).
\]
The only nonzero nonconstant coefficients are
\(c_i^1(\{j\})=-\lambda\), \(j\in N(i)\). The contact process is
attractive, so it already admits stochastic comparisons stronger than the
moment order developed below. We retain it as the basic birth-process
benchmark.

\subsubsection{FA-1f}
\label{subsubsec:fa-1f-model}

In FA-1f, state \(0\) is called vacant and state \(1\) occupied. A site may
refresh when at least one of its neighbours is vacant. On the present directed
neighbourhood system, the generator is
\[
\Lcal_{\mathrm{FA}}f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\bigl(1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta)\bigr)
(E_{i,p}-I)f(\eta).
\]
The corresponding flip rates are
\[
c_i^0(\eta)=p\bigl(1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta)\bigr),
\qquad
c_i^1(\eta)=q\bigl(1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta)\bigr).
\]
When \(N(i)\neq\varnothing\), their only nonzero coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_i^0(\varnothing)&=p,
&c_i^0(N(i))&=-p,\\
c_i^1(\varnothing)&=q,
&c_i^1(N(i))&=-q.
\end{aligned}
\]
The usual FA-1f model is defined on an undirected graph. The East model is the
oriented special case with a single prescribed facilitating neighbour.

\subsubsection{Biased annihilating branching process}
\label{subsubsec:babp-model}

BABP uses the same refresh law, but each vacant neighbour contributes one unit
to the refresh rate:
\[
\Lcal_{\mathrm{BABP}}f(\eta)
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}
\left(\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j))\right)
(E_{i,p}-I)f(\eta).
\]
Hence
\[
c_i^0(\eta)
=
p\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j)),
\qquad
c_i^1(\eta)
=
q\sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j)).
\]
Its nonzero coefficients are
\[
\begin{aligned}
c_i^0(\varnothing)&=p|N(i)|,
&c_i^0(\{j\})&=-p,\\
c_i^1(\varnothing)&=q|N(i)|,
&c_i^1(\{j\})&=-q,
\end{aligned}
\qquad j\in N(i).
\]
The name of the process uses the complementary particle variables
\(\xi(i)=1-\eta(i)\). If \(p>0\), rescaling time by \(p^{-1}\) and writing
\(\lambda=q/p\) gives a process in which an empty site becomes occupied at
rate \(\lambda\) for each neighbouring particle, while an occupied site loses
its particle at rate one for each neighbouring particle. BABP is not
attractive in the usual product order.

\subsection{Noise, invariant measures, and convergence}
\label{subsec:noise-ergodicity}

\begin{definition}[Independent Bernoulli noise]
\label{def:bernoulli-noise}
Let \(\mathbf p=(p_i)_{i\in\Lambda}\in[0,1]^\Lambda\). The independent
Bernoulli noise with profile \(\mathbf p\) has generator
\[
\Ncal^{\mathbf p}f
=
\sum_{i\in\Lambda}(E_{i,p_i}-I)f.
\]
A noise perturbation of \(\Lcal\) at level \(\varepsilon\geq0\) is the spin
system with generator
\[
\Lcal^\varepsilon
=
\Lcal+\varepsilon\Ncal^{\mathbf p}.
\]
\end{definition}

The noise adds the environment-independent rates \(\varepsilon p_i\) to the
flip \(0\to1\) and \(\varepsilon(1-p_i)\) to the flip \(1\to0\). We say that
a spin system has a \emph{uniform pure-death component} of level
\(\delta>0\) if
\[
c_i^1(\eta)\geq\delta
\qquad
\text{for every }i\in\Lambda\text{ and }\eta\in\Omega.
\]
Equivalently, a rate-\(\delta\) independent transition \(1\to0\) can be
removed while leaving nonnegative flip rates. In particular, the perturbation
above has a uniform pure-death component of level
\(\varepsilon\inf_i(1-p_i)\) whenever this quantity is positive.

\begin{definition}[Invariant measure and ergodicity]
\label{def:invariant-measure}
\label{def:ergodicity}
A probability measure \(\nu\) on \(\Omega\) is \emph{invariant} if
\[
\nu P_t=\nu
\qquad
\text{for every }t\geq0.
\]
The spin system is \emph{ergodic} if there is a probability measure \(\nu\)
such that
\[
\mu P_t\Rightarrow\nu
\qquad
\text{as }t\to\infty
\]
for every probability measure \(\mu\) on \(\Omega\). The limiting measure is
then the unique invariant measure.
\end{definition}

Since \(\Omega\) is compact in the product topology, weak convergence is
determined by local functions. We use the following local-observable form of
the quantitative property.

\begin{definition}[Uniform exponential ergodicity]
\label{def:uniform-exponential-ergodicity}
The spin system is \emph{uniformly exponentially ergodic} if it has an
invariant measure \(\nu\), and there is \(\gamma>0\) such that for every local
function \(f\) there is \(C_f<\infty\) satisfying
\[
\sup_{\eta\in\Omega}
\left|P_tf(\eta)-\nu(f)\right|
\leq
C_f e^{-\gamma t}
\qquad
\text{for every }t\geq0.
\]
\end{definition}

We will also prove convergence uniformly over specified classes of initial
laws. Such a restricted statement is not called ergodicity unless the estimate
holds for every point mass, in which case it also holds for every initial law
by integration.
