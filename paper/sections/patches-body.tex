% BLURB A. Why retain a successful-interaction skeleton
%
% Reader question:
%   What information from the full graphical construction matters for the patch
%   decomposition, and why do we discard the rest?
%
% Job:
%   Start from the graphical construction in the previous subsection. Explain
%   that nonempty-target marks which occur at active sources are the events that
%   transmit dependence between site lines. The patch construction retains the
%   source, time, and target of these cross-site interactions. Once these are
%   fixed, the remaining marks act locally on the intervals between them and can
%   later be averaged patch by patch.
%
%   Give the reader the picture before any formal definition:
%       full graphical construction
%       -> retained cross-site interaction skeleton
%       -> consecutive intervals on each site line = patches.
%
% Keep out:
%   The formal definition of I_T, the patch labels, and the finite-horizon
%   truncation. Avoid vague phrases such as "retain the information needed" with
%   no explanation of what that information is.
% ============================================================================

The full graphical construction contains many marks that do not connect
different site lines. Deaths affect only their source site, and marks at
inactive sources do not change the dual. By contrast, a nonempty-target mark at
an active source links its source to the sites in its target. The patch
construction retains the source, time, and target of exactly these cross-site
interactions. Once they are fixed, each site line is divided into intervals
between consecutive retained interactions, and the remaining randomness acts
within individual intervals. We call the retained record the
successful-interaction skeleton and these site-time intervals patches.
