\section{Examples}
\label{sec:examples}

\subsection{FA-1f}

Let \(q\in(0,1)\) be the vacancy density and \(p=1-q\) the one-density.
For a site \(i\) with finite nonempty neighbour set \(N(i)\), the
FA-1f constraint is
\[
  c_i(\eta)
  =
  \ind\{\text{some }j\in N(i)\text{ has }\eta(j)=0\}
  =
  1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta).
\]
At a legal update, the spin is refreshed from the Bernoulli law with
zero-density \(q\).  The only nonzero nonempty-neighbour flip-rate
coefficients are
\[
  c_i^0(N(i))=-p,
  \qquad
  c_i^1(N(i))=-q,
\]
while
\[
  c_i^0(\vn)=p,
  \qquad
  c_i^1(\vn)=q.
\]
Therefore
\[
  c_i^0(N(i))+c_i^1(N(i))=-1
\]
and the determinant in
\eqref{eq:patch-positive-nondegenerate} is zero.  FA-1f has patch
positivity, and
\[
  p_i^\star
  =
  \frac{-p}{-p-q}
  =
  p.
\]
The hard model does not satisfy \eqref{eq:uniform-death}, since the
all-one configuration is blocked.

For \(s\in(0,1)\), the soft FA-1f generator is
\begin{equation}
  \cL_{\mathrm{sFA}}f(\eta)
  =
  \sum_{i\in\Lambda}
  \bigl[s+(1-s)(1-\chi_{N(i)}(\eta))\bigr]
  \bigl(E_i^qf(\eta)-f(\eta)\bigr),
  \label{eq:soft-fa-generator}
\end{equation}
where \(E_i^q\) refreshes the spin at \(i\) from the same Bernoulli
law.  The nonempty coefficients are multiplied by \(1-s\), so patch
positivity and \(p^\star=p\) remain unchanged.  The \(1\)-to-\(0\)
rate is at least \(qs\), and the reversible product law \(\mu_q\) is
invariant.

\begin{corollary}[Soft FA-1f]
\label{cor:soft-fa}
Let \(\Lambda\) have polynomial growth exponent \(D\).  For every
local \(f\),
\[
  \sup_{\nu\in\Mminus}
  \abs{\nu(P_tf)-\mu_q(f)}
  \le
  K_f(1+t)^D e^{-qst/2}.
\]
Here \(\pstar=p\mathbf1\), so a product initial law with one-density
profile \(\mathbf p_0\) belongs to \(\Mminus\) precisely when
\[
  p_{0,i}\ge(2p-1)\vee0
  \qquad(i\in\Lambda).
\]
If \(p\le1/2\), the estimate is uniform over all initial
configurations.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{thm:common-limit} with uniform death rate \(qs\).  The
limit must be \(\mu_q\), since it is already invariant and belongs to
\(\Mminus\).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The biased annihilating branching process}

On a lattice with finite neighbour sets, the biased annihilating
branching process, abbreviated BABP, refreshes site \(i\) at rate
\[
  c_i(\eta)
  =
  \sum_{j\in N(i)}(1-\eta(j)).
\]
It has the same legal configurations as FA-1f but counts vacant
neighbours instead of applying an indicator.  In the complementary
particle variables, this is the classical process of
\citet{neuhauser-sudbury1993}.  The Bernoulli product law \(\mu_q\) is
reversible, while the all-one configuration is absorbing.

The only nonzero flip-rate coefficients are
\begin{align*}
  c_i^0(\vn)&=p\abs{N(i)},
  &
  c_i^1(\vn)&=q\abs{N(i)},
  \\
  c_i^0(\{j\})&=-p,
  &
  c_i^1(\{j\})&=-q,
  \qquad j\in N(i).
\end{align*}
Every nonempty target is therefore a singleton.  The
patch-positivity determinant again vanishes, and
\[
  p_i^\star=p.
\]
As for hard FA-1f, the common-limit theorem does not apply because the
rates vanish at the absorbing all-one configuration.

For BABP on \(\Z^d\), exponential convergence from every nontrivial
homogeneous Bernoulli law to \(\mu_q\) is known; see
\citet{martinelli-shapira-toninelli2025}.  Centered-moment order extends
this input as follows.

\begin{proposition}[High-density BABP convergence]
\label{prop:babp-high-density}
Suppose that the initial one-density profile satisfies
\[
  p\le p_{0,i}\le p_+<1
  \qquad(i\in\Z^d).
\]
Then \(\mu_{\mathbf p_0}P_t\) converges exponentially to \(\mu_q\) on
local functions.  More generally, the same conclusion holds for every
\(\nu\in\Mstar\) such that
\begin{equation}
  0
  \le
  \nu(\chi_B^\star)
  \le
  (p_+-p)^{\abs B}
  \qquad(B\Subset\Z^d).
  \label{eq:babp-centered-upper-bound}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let \(q_-=1-p_+>0\).  For every nonempty finite \(B\),
\[
  \mu_q(\chi_B^\star)=0,
  \qquad
  \mu_{\mathbf p_0}(\chi_B^\star)
  =
  \prod_{i\in B}(p_{0,i}-p),
  \qquad
  \mu_{q_-}(\chi_B^\star)
  =
  (p_+-p)^{\abs B}.
\]
\Cref{thm:centered-order} therefore gives
\[
  p^{\abs A}
  =
  \mu_q(P_t\chi_A)
  \le
  \mu_{\mathbf p_0}(P_t\chi_A)
  \le
  \mu_{q_-}(P_t\chi_A).
\]
The right-hand side converges exponentially to \(p^{\abs A}\) by the
homogeneous BABP theorem.  The same squeeze applies under
\eqref{eq:babp-centered-upper-bound}.  Expand a local function in the
finite monomial basis.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
For both FA-1f and BABP, adding Bernoulli noise with one-density
profile \(\mathbf p^+\) preserves patch positivity precisely when
\(p_i^+\ge p\) at every nontrivial site.  The determinant condition is
\(\varepsilon(p_i^+-p)\ge0\).
\end{remark}
