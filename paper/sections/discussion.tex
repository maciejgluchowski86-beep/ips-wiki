% BLURB A. What patch positivity is measuring
%
% Reader question:
%   After seeing the coefficient criterion and the examples, how should one
%   understand patch positivity as a condition on the local dynamics?
%
% Job:
%   Interpret the condition rather than restate Theorem~B. Ordinary
%   monotonicity of a constraint only compares two neighbourhood
%   configurations. Patch positivity imposes sign conditions on all mixed
%   effects of several neighbouring spins through the Moebius coefficients.
%   Explain that this is why FA-1f can satisfy the condition while higher-order
%   FA constraints can fail it despite having monotone constraints.
%
%   Make clear that the pointwise monotonicities of lambda_i and rho_i discussed
%   earlier are consequences of patch positivity, not equivalent definitions.
%   The reader should leave with a conceptual description of what extra
%   structure the coefficient criterion is imposing.
%
% Keep out:
%   The explicit criterion, the threshold formula, another calculation for the
%   examples, or another statement of centered-moment order preservation.
% ============================================================================

Patch positivity places a stronger condition on the dependence of the local
update rule on neighbouring spins than ordinary monotonicity. The pointwise
consequences described above, that changing neighbouring~$0$'s to~$1$'s
decreases~$\lambda_i$ and increases~$\rho_i$, compare the rule between two
neighbourhood configurations. The coefficient criterion also controls how the
effects of several neighbouring spins combine. In the multilinear expansion
of the local rule, the contributions associated with every nonempty set of
neighbours must have the prescribed sign. Thus different orders of dependence
cannot cancel to produce the correct pointwise monotonicity while violating the
patchwise sign condition. This distinction is already visible among KCSMs.
FA-1f is patch-positive, whereas higher-order FA constraints are still monotone
with respect to vacancies but have multilinear coefficients of both signs and
therefore fail patch positivity. Patch positivity therefore requires the
constraining direction of the local rule to be consistent across its
higher-order dependence on the neighbourhood, not only at the level of
pointwise monotonicity.
