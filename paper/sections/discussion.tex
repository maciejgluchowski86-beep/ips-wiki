\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The main role of patch positivity is to recover a useful order at the level of
probability laws even when the underlying dynamics has no monotone coupling.
The patch representation itself is more general: it remains valid without
patch positivity and captures some, but not all cancellations in the dual
representation. This suggests that the representation may still be useful
beyond the patch-positive class, provided the resulting cancellations can be
controlled by other means.

We expect the condition~$\mb p^\star\leq\mbs{\tfrac12}$ in the uniform
ergodicity result to be technical rather than sharp. Despite extensive search
we have found no counterexample above this threshold, and in the examples
considered here substantially stronger ergodicity results are available, either
for the original dynamics or after adding independent noise. More importantly,
the value~$1/2$ enters the proof only when passing from convergence on
$\mathcal M_-$ to convergence from every initial law: above~$1/2$, some
end-patch contributions have negative centered coefficients. Such negative
terms do not by themselves indicate slower convergence. One would instead
expect the additional cancellations to help, but we lack the tools to detect
this. The present argument avoids having to control these cancellations, which
is why the restriction appears.
