Interacting particle systems are stochastic models in which many local random
updates combine to produce nontrivial collective behaviour. In this paper, we
study spin systems: continuous-time Markov processes on~$\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}$,
where~$\Lambda$ is the set of sites representing space and updates flip one
site at a time. The flip rate at a site may depend on nearby spins, which is how
the sites interact. We are interested in how these local rules determine the
dependence of the long-time behaviour of the system on the initial conditions.

One of the main tools for such questions is monotonicity. The configuration
space has the usual coordinatewise order: $\eta\leq\xi$ if
$\eta(i)\leq\xi(i)$ at every site. A spin system is monotone if processes
started from~$\eta\leq\xi$ can be coupled so that the same order holds at all
later times. This gives direct comparisons between different initial
conditions. The same idea can often also be used to compare systems with
different transition rules. Such comparisons are basic tools for studying
dependence on the initial condition, invariant measures and convergence.

Kinetically constrained spin models (KCSMs) are spin systems constructed so
that their equilibrium measure is a Bernoulli product measure, with all spins
independent, while the dynamics is interacting through local constraints. At
an update attempt, the spin at a site is refreshed from the one-site Bernoulli
equilibrium law only if a constraint determined by neighbouring spins is
satisfied. We use the standard convention that vacancy, denoted~$0$, is a
facilitating state. The constraint is monotone with respect to vacancies:
adding vacancies can make an update legal but cannot make a legal update
illegal. These constraints can make relaxation very slow and lead to glassy
dynamics~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. The East and FA-1f
models are standard examples.

This monotonicity of the constraint does not make the spin system monotone in
the configuration order. Indeed, if~$\eta\leq\xi$, an update may be legal in
$\eta$ because it has more vacancies, while the same update is blocked in
$\xi$. If the legal refresh changes~$\eta(i)$ from~$0$ to~$1$ while~$\xi(i)$
remains~$0$, the coordinatewise order is lost. Thus the monotone structure of
the constraint does not give the usual order-preserving coupling.

The failure of configuration monotonicity makes it important to keep track of
cancellations between successive updates. Write~$P_t$ for the Markov semigroup
and, for a finite set of sites~$A$, let
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i).
\end{equation*}
The action of the generator on these monomials gives a signed Feynman--Kac dual
on finite sets: the set records the sites on which the observable depends,
while a sign records the signs of the coefficients arising from the generator.
Estimating this representation update by update can lose cancellations,
because dependence created by one interaction may later be overwritten or
cancelled by subsequent local updates.

Our main construction retains only the interactions that transmit this
dependence between sites. We condition on the times, sources and targets of
these interactions, but not on the remaining local graphical randomness. Once
this coarser record is fixed, the remaining randomness separates into one-site
spacetime regions between consecutive recorded interactions. We call these
regions \emph{patches}. Averaging the signed Feynman--Kac factor over the
remaining randomness inside each patch gives an exact factorization of
$P_t\chi_A$. In this way the local cancellations are performed before the
patch contributions are combined.

This representation leads to the class studied in this paper. We call a spin
system \emph{patch-positive} when the averaged contribution of every patch
lying entirely before the observation time is nonnegative. We show that patch
positivity is equivalent to explicit local inequalities for the multilinear
coefficients of the flip-rate function. The condition is motivated by KCSMs but
allows more general dependence on the neighbourhood: rather than requiring a
kinetic constraint followed by a fixed Bernoulli refresh, we allow the
neighbouring spins to affect the flip rate directly. Patch positivity implies
that adding neighbouring~$1$'s cannot increase either the local update
intensity or the~$1\to0$ rate, although this coordinatewise monotonicity is
strictly weaker than patch positivity.

Patches reaching the observation time leave affine factors depending on the
terminal spins. Their nonnegativity determines a \emph{patch threshold
profile}~$\mb p^\star$, which is fixed by the local rates and is not an
ergodicity or phase-transition threshold. For~$A\Subset\Lambda$, define
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A^*(\eta)
=
\prod_{i\in A}\bb{\eta(i)-p_i^\star}.
\end{equation*}
For probability laws~$\mu$ and~$\nu$, write~$\mu\preceq_*\nu$ when
$\mu(\chi_A^*)\leq\nu(\chi_A^*)$ for every finite~$A$. The same coefficient
inequalities that characterize patch positivity imply
\begin{equation*}
\mu\preceq_*\nu
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu P_t\preceq_*\nu P_t
\qquad (t\geq0).
\end{equation*}
Thus the configuration order need not be preserved, but a monotone direction
reappears at the level of centered moments of probability laws.

The representation also gives comparisons between different non-monotone
dynamics. In particular, a patch-positive system can be compared with the
system obtained by removing environment-independent~$1\to0$ transitions, which
we call \emph{pure deaths}. More importantly, these transitions provide a
long-time convergence mechanism. On interaction graphs of polynomial growth,
if a patch-positive system contains a pure-death component of uniform positive
rate~$\varepsilon$, then a broad explicit class of initial laws defined through
centered moments converges to a common invariant law. For each fixed class in
the theorem, local observables converge at rate
$O\bb{(1+t)^D e^{-\varepsilon t/2}}$. The invariant law need not be identified
in advance, and no reversibility assumption is used. If
$\mb p^\star\leq\mbs{\tfrac12}$, the initial-law restriction disappears: the
invariant measure is unique and convergence is uniform over all initial
configurations.

The examples show both the scope and the limitations of the condition. East
and FA-1f are patch-positive, but patch positivity does not contain all KCSMs:
cooperative FA-$j$f models with~$j\geq2$ are not patch-positive. The biased
annihilating branching process is patch-positive despite not having the KCSM
form. The contact process is also patch-positive, but in that case ordinary
configuration monotonicity is available and gives stronger comparisons.

\paragraph{Relation to previous work.}
Set-valued graphical constructions and additive dual processes go back to
Harris~\cite{Harris1974,Harris1978}; see also
Griffeath~\cite{Griffeath1979}, Holley--Stroock~\cite{HolleyStroock1979}, and
Gray~\cite{Gray1986}. We use the signed monomial Feynman--Kac duality; Jansen
and Kurt~\cite{JansenKurt2014} give a general account of Markov-process
duality. Conditioning on the coarse interaction record described above gives a
one-site factorization which is not present in the standard duality.
Information percolation also retains updates which carry information
\cite{LubetzkySly2016}, but uses them for a different decomposition. Our
construction is also different from the time-scaling observation
in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2025}, where each local update transition matrix is mixed
with the identity. It was motivated by the wall-type one-sided spin systems
studied in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2026}; many of their rate functions satisfy the
coefficient criterion below, although not all.

Classical correlation inequalities for monotone spin systems, traditionally
called attractive systems in the IPS literature, are tied to order-preserving
couplings and association~\cite{Harris1977,Liggett2006}; duality can also yield
moment inequalities in particular models~\cite{GiardinaRedigVafayi2010}. The
order considered here uses products centered at~$\mb p^\star$ and applies to
systems which are not monotone in configuration order. To our knowledge,
preservation of this centered-moment order has not previously been obtained
from a local coefficient condition of the present form. The convergence
theorem is also distinct from classical positive-rates results such as
Gray~\cite{Gray1982}: under patch positivity and a uniform pure-death component
it proves convergence from a broad class of initial laws without first
identifying the invariant law.

FA-1f originates in~\cite{FredricksonAndersen1984} and is placed in the KCSM
framework in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. Its
out-of-equilibrium theory, and that of related KCSMs such as East, is developed
in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2010,BlondelEtAl2013,MountfordValle2019,Mareche2019,
HartarskyToninelli2024}. BABP was introduced by Neuhauser and
Sudbury~\cite{NeuhauserSudbury1993}; its qualitative convergence and
quasi-duality theory are due in part to Sudbury and
Lloyd--Sudbury~\cite{Sudbury1997,LloydSudbury1997}. The product-law estimates
in~\cite{MartinelliShapiraToninelli2025} provide the endpoints for the
centered-moment corollary proved here.
