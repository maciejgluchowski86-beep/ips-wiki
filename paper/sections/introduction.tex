\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Averaging consecutive updates}

The patch construction detects cancellations among consecutive dual updates at
one site before the next dual interaction between sites. Such cancellations can
occur in the facilitating systems considered here and are lost if absolute
values are taken after each dual update. We condition on the nonempty-target
interactions that act on the dual process and average the other marked
interactions on the intervening site--time intervals.

We use the two states~$0$ and~$1$, with~$0$ as the facilitating state, following
the convention for kinetically constrained spin models (KCSM)
\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. The interpretation depends on
the model. A~$0$ may infect a neighbour, permit a constrained refresh, or cause
a neighbouring spin to flip. In the unperturbed facilitating models, a region
containing only~$1$'s has no internal source of constrained updates, while an
update enabled by a~$0$ may create further~$0$'s. Independent noise may also
create~$0$'s. This gives a direction to the comparison considered below, but
not monotonicity of the dynamics: a refresh or flip may create or remove a~$0$,
and ordered configurations need not remain ordered.

The question considered here is whether part of this comparison survives for
selected observables and whether independent updates which create facilitating
states erase the initial condition. Attractiveness and monotone coupling do not
apply to the main examples. Reversibility need not survive an independent
perturbation. The argument uses the graphical construction and does not assume
that the limiting measure is known.

For a monomial~$\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i)$, the standard signed
Feynman--Kac dual is a finite-set process which follows the dependence of
$P_t\chi_A$ backward through the graphical construction. Its signs come from
the multilinear coefficients of the flip rates. Taking absolute values after
each dual update discards cancellations between consecutive updates at the same
site. The representation below first groups the marked interactions by the
site--time intervals between successive interactions involving another site.

\subsection{The representation and its consequences}

We record only the times, sources, and target sets of dual interactions which
occur at active sources and have nonempty target. These records form the
successful-interaction skeleton. The skeleton does not record whether such an
interaction is a split or a birth, any death, or any ring at an inactive
source. The successful-interaction times partition each site line into patches.
Conditional on the skeleton, the marked interactions in distinct patches are
independent. Averaging the signed Feynman--Kac factor over each patch gives the
formula in Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-representation}. Unlike a termwise estimate of
the dual, this formula averages consecutive local updates before their signs
are discarded or their contributions are compared.

Patch positivity means that every completed patch has nonnegative averaged
contribution. The condition is stated in spacetime terms but is checked from
the generator: Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-positivity-order} gives necessary and
sufficient inequalities for the multilinear coefficients of the two
rates~$0\to1$ and~$1\to0$. The same coefficients determine the smallest terminal
one-density profile~$\mb p^\star$ for which all end-patch contributions are
nonnegative. This profile is a threshold for the sign argument, not a phase
transition or an ergodicity threshold.

Centering monomials at~$\mb p^\star$ defines an order~$\preceq_*$ on measures.
Patch positivity implies that the semigroup preserves this order even when it
does not preserve the pointwise order of configurations. It also yields a
comparison with the dynamics obtained by removing an
environment-independent~$1\to0$ component. These statements form
Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-positivity-order}. The centered-basis proof of order
preservation is algebraic: in finite volume, the generator has nonnegative
off-diagonal entries in that basis.

Theorem~\ref{thm:common-invariant-limit} gives the convergence result. Suppose
the interaction graph has polynomial growth of exponent~$D$ and every~$1$-spin
has an environment-independent transition to~$0$ at rate~$\varepsilon>0$.
For an affine extension~$\mathcal M_-$ of the
nonnegative centered-moment cone, all initial laws in~$\mathcal M_-$ converge
to the same invariant measure, and local observables converge at rate

\begin{equation*}
O\bb{(1+t)^D e^{-\varepsilon t/2}}.
\end{equation*}

If~$\mb p^\star\leq\mbs{\tfrac12}$, the class contains every initial law, so
the invariant measure is unique and convergence is uniform over initial
configurations. The proof separates three events: the pre-cut skeleton leaves
a linearly growing directed ball; a successful interaction occurs after the
cut; or no such interaction occurs and the end patches relax. Finite
propagation controls the first event. The other two estimates use the uniform
$1\to0$ component in different ways.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

Set-valued graphical constructions and additive dual processes go back to
Harris~\cite{Harris1974,Harris1978}; see also
Griffeath~\cite{Griffeath1979}, Holley--Stroock~\cite{HolleyStroock1979}, and
Gray~\cite{Gray1986}. We use the standard monomial Feynman--Kac duality under
the usual finite-speed assumptions for spin systems; Jansen and
Kurt~\cite{JansenKurt2014} give a general account of Markov-process duality.
The new step is to condition on the successful skeleton and average the
remaining Poisson marks on disjoint site--time intervals. Information
percolation also retains only updates which carry information
\cite{LubetzkySly2016}, but its clusters and oblivious updates serve a different
purpose. Here the conditional one-site factorization gives a semigroup
representation and retains cancellations inside each patch. It also differs
from the time-scaling observation in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2025}, where each
local update transition matrix is mixed with the identity. The construction
was motivated by the wall-type one-sided spin systems studied in
\cite{GluchowskiMenz2026}; many of their rate functions satisfy the coefficient
criterion below. We do not treat these systems as a separate application here.

Classical correlation inequalities for spin systems are tied to attractiveness
and association~\cite{Harris1977,Liggett2006}; duality can also yield moment
inequalities in specific models~\cite{GiardinaRedigVafayi2010}. The order here
uses products centered at~$\mb p^\star$ and applies to non-attractive systems.
To our knowledge, preservation of this centered-moment order has not previously
been proved under a local coefficient condition of the present form. The
uniform-noise convergence theorem is likewise distinct from classical
positive-rates results such as Gray~\cite{Gray1982}: its hypotheses are patch
positivity and a specified independent component, and its conclusion is first
obtained on the class~$\mathcal M_-$.

The examples belong to two established lines of work. FA-1f originates in
\cite{FredricksonAndersen1984} and is placed in the modern KCSM framework in
\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. Its out-of-equilibrium theory,
and that of related KCSMs such as East, is developed in
\cite{CancriniEtAl2010,BlondelEtAl2013,MountfordValle2019,Mareche2019,
HartarskyToninelli2024}. BABP was introduced
by Neuhauser and Sudbury~\cite{NeuhauserSudbury1993}; its qualitative
convergence and quasi-duality theory are due in part to Sudbury and
Lloyd--Sudbury~\cite{Sudbury1997,LloydSudbury1997}. The recent product-law
estimates in~\cite{MartinelliShapiraToninelli2025} provide the endpoints for
the centered-moment corollary proved here. The examples are included to show
how the same coefficient criterion treats two different facilitation rules.

\subsection{Organization}

Section~\ref{sec:setup} fixes the notation and
Section~\ref{sec:main-results} states the three principal theorems.
Section~\ref{sec:signed-dual} constructs the successful skeleton and proves the
patch representation, Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-representation}.
Section~\ref{sec:patch-positivity} proves the coefficient criterion and the
centered-moment comparisons in Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-positivity-order}.
Section~\ref{sec:convergence} proves the common invariant-limit theorem.
The FA-1f and BABP calculations are collected in
Section~\ref{sec:applications}. Generator calculations, one-site
formulas, and propagation estimates are deferred to the appendices.
