\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Kinetic constraints}

% Reader question: What kind of mathematical problem is this?
\paragraph{Spin systems.}
Spin systems describe stochastic dynamics in which the state at a site changes
at rates determined by the configuration in a local neighbourhood. A basic
problem is to understand how these local rules determine collective behaviour,
in particular how the law at later times depends on the initial state and
whether the system approaches an invariant distribution.

% Reader question: What structural property normally helps answer these questions?
\paragraph{Monotonicity.}
One of the most useful structures in the study of spin systems is monotonicity.
When ordered initial configurations can be coupled so that their order is
preserved by the dynamics, comparisons at the microscopic level propagate to
comparisons between probability laws at later times. This can provide
substantial information about invariant measures and long-time behaviour.

% Reader question: Where does this paper sit within spin systems?
\paragraph{Kinetically constrained spin models.}
Kinetically constrained spin models (KCSMs) form an important class in which
the local environment determines whether a site is allowed to update. In their
usual binary form, a vacancy acts as a facilitating state: suitable nearby
vacancies permit an update, whereas configurations without the required
facilitation suppress it. Once an update is allowed, the site is refreshed from
a fixed Bernoulli distribution. Canonical examples include the East model and
FA-1f. Although the equilibrium measures of the basic KCSMs are simple, their
constraints can make relaxation and out-of-equilibrium behaviour difficult to
analyse; see~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}.

% Reader question: If facilitation has a monotone direction, why is the dynamics not monotone?
\paragraph{Monotone constraints and non-monotone dynamics.}
Many standard KCSM constraints have an evident monotone direction: replacing
facilitating states by calm states can only inhibit updates. The resulting
stochastic dynamics, however, need not preserve the configuration order. An
update may be allowed in the configuration containing more facilitating states
and forbidden in an ordered configuration containing fewer; if the allowed
refresh removes a facilitating state, the original order can be destroyed.
Thus the facilitating mechanism can have a monotone direction even when the
spin system itself is not monotone.

% Reader question: Is the monotone structure of facilitation completely lost?
\paragraph{The question.}
This raises the first question of the paper: can the constraining asymmetry of a
KCSM still give rise to a useful dynamical comparison when configuration order
is not preserved? We ask more generally whether this mechanism persists for
spin systems in which the neighbourhood may affect not only whether a site
updates, but also the relative rates of its possible transitions.

\subsection{Centered-moment monotonicity}

% Reader question: What exactly is being generalized from KCSMs?
\paragraph{Beyond constrained refresh dynamics.}
In a standard KCSM, the neighbourhood controls the rate at which an update is
performed, while the state produced by an allowed update is sampled from a
fixed distribution. We consider a broader situation in which the neighbourhood
may also change the bias of the update. The restriction retained from the KCSM
picture is that calm surroundings act in the constraining direction rather
than creating additional activity.

% Reader question: What does the constraining direction mean for these broader dynamics?
\paragraph{Constraining local rates.}
For the binary systems considered here, our coefficient condition forces two
basic monotonicity properties: adding calm spins to the neighbourhood cannot
increase either the total update intensity or the rate at which a facilitating
state is created. The defining condition is slightly stronger than these two
coordinatewise statements. It also relates how strongly the facilitating-state
creation channel is suppressed to how strongly the total activity is
suppressed. This additional relation is what makes the comparison argument
possible.

% Reader question: Can the class be recognized directly from the generator?
\paragraph{Coefficient criterion.}
The class has an explicit local characterization. Expanding the two flip rates
as multilinear functions of the neighbouring calm-state indicators, the
condition becomes a pair of inequalities between their coefficients. In
particular, the nonconstant coefficients of the total update rate and of the
facilitating-state creation rate are nonpositive, so all-calm patterns enter
these rates as suppressive corrections; the second inequality controls their
relative strength. The criterion involves only the local rates and is therefore
directly checkable from the generator. For an ordinary KCSM, where the
neighbourhood merely rescales a fixed Bernoulli refresh, one of the two
inequalities is saturated and the other reduces to a sign condition on the
multilinear coefficients of the kinetic constraint. The East model and FA-1f
satisfy this condition, although arbitrary KCSM constraints need not.

% Reader question: What does this class gain dynamically?
\paragraph{Centered-moment order.}
The main structural consequence is a monotonicity property at the level of
probability laws. The coefficient criterion singles out a reference calm-state
density profile~$\mb p^\star$. For a finite set~$A$, consider the centered
product
\begin{equation*}
\prod_{i\in A}\bb{\eta(i)-p_i^\star}.
\end{equation*}
We write~$\mu\preceq_*\nu$ when every such centered moment under~$\mu$ is no
larger than the corresponding centered moment under~$\nu$. Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-positivity-order}
shows that the dynamics preserves this order:
\begin{equation*}
\mu\preceq_*\nu
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu P_t\preceq_*\nu P_t,
\qquad t\geq0.
\end{equation*}
This is an order on laws rather than configurations and does not produce a
monotone pathwise coupling. Nevertheless, it recovers a dynamical comparison
principle from the same directional asymmetry that underlies kinetic
constraints. The monotonicity visible in the facilitating mechanism, but lost
at the level of configurations, reappears at the level of centered
correlations.

% Reader question: Is this weaker monotonicity useful?
\paragraph{Comparison and convergence.}
The centered-moment order yields comparisons between non-monotone evolutions.
In particular, Corollary~\ref{cor:pure-death-comparison} compares the original
dynamics with the system obtained by removing an environment-independent
mechanism which creates facilitating states. The same structure also gives a
route to ergodicity which does not begin by identifying the invariant measure.
When facilitating states are created independently at a uniformly positive
rate, Theorem~\ref{thm:common-invariant-limit} gives convergence, under a mild
growth assumption, to a common invariant law from a class of initial measures
defined by centered moments. In a favourable range of the reference profile,
the conclusion is uniform over all initial configurations.

% Reader question: Why should a non-monotone process preserve this order?
\paragraph{Signed duality and cancellations.}
The preservation of the centered-moment order is not visible from individual
updates of the spin system. When monomial observables are traced backward
through the graphical construction, the resulting Feynman--Kac dual
representation contains terms of both signs. These signs encode cancellations
between successive local updates, and comparing the terms one update at a time
can destroy the cancellation responsible for positivity.

% Reader question: How are these cancellations retained?
\paragraph{Patches.}
We therefore average consecutive local evolution before making a sign
comparison. Tracing dependence backward, we retain the interactions which
transmit dependence between sites and group the intervening one-site evolution
into spacetime regions which we call patches. Conditional on the retained
interactions, the internal randomness of distinct patches is independent, and
the dual representation factorizes exactly into averaged patch contributions.
The local coefficient inequalities above are precisely the conditions under
which every bulk patch has nonnegative contribution. We call this property
\emph{patch positivity}. Thus
\begin{equation*}
\text{coefficient criterion}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\text{patch positivity}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{centered-moment monotonicity}.
\end{equation*}
The patch representation is therefore the mechanism which turns the local
constraining structure into a comparison principle for the full dynamics.

% Reader question: Which familiar models does the framework cover?
\paragraph{Examples and scope.}
The criterion applies to several familiar facilitated systems. It includes
canonical KCSM examples such as FA-1f and the East model and also applies to the
biased annihilating branching process, whose local rule is not simply a
kinetically constrained Bernoulli refresh. The contact process provides a
benchmark where the criterion holds but ordinary configuration monotonicity
already gives stronger comparison tools. The framework is therefore intended
to extend the constrained mechanism present in important KCSMs rather than to
characterize every possible kinetically constrained spin model.

\subsection{Relation to previous work}

Set-valued graphical constructions and additive dual processes go back to
Harris~\cite{Harris1974,Harris1978}; see also
Griffeath~\cite{Griffeath1979}, Holley--Stroock~\cite{HolleyStroock1979}, and
Gray~\cite{Gray1986}. We use the signed monomial Feynman--Kac duality; Jansen
and Kurt~\cite{JansenKurt2014} give a general account of Markov-process
duality.
Conditioning on the successful skeleton gives the one-site factorization over
patches which is not present in the standard duality. Information percolation
also retains updates which carry information~\cite{LubetzkySly2016}, but its
clusters and oblivious updates are used for a different decomposition. The
patch factorization retains cancellations among local updates. It is also
different from the time-scaling observation
in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2025}, where each local update transition matrix is mixed
with the identity. The construction was motivated by the wall-type one-sided
spin systems studied in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2026}; many of their rate functions
satisfy the coefficient criterion below.

Classical correlation inequalities for monotone spin systems, traditionally
called attractive systems in the IPS literature, are tied to order-preserving
couplings and association~\cite{Harris1977,Liggett2006}; duality can also yield
moment inequalities in particular models~\cite{GiardinaRedigVafayi2010}. The
order considered here uses products centered at~$\mb p^\star$ and applies to
systems which are not monotone in configuration order. To our knowledge,
preservation of this centered-moment order has not previously been obtained
from a local coefficient condition of the present form. The convergence
theorem is also distinct from classical positive-rates results such as
Gray~\cite{Gray1982}: it assumes patch positivity and a specified independent
component and proves convergence from the class~$\mathcal M_-$.

The examples come from two lines of work. FA-1f originates
in~\cite{FredricksonAndersen1984} and is placed in the KCSM framework
in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. Its out-of-equilibrium
theory, and that of related KCSMs such as East, is developed
in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2010,BlondelEtAl2013,MountfordValle2019,Mareche2019,
HartarskyToninelli2024}. BABP was introduced by Neuhauser and
Sudbury~\cite{NeuhauserSudbury1993}; its qualitative convergence and
quasi-duality theory are due in part to Sudbury and
Lloyd--Sudbury~\cite{Sudbury1997,LloydSudbury1997}. The product-law estimates
in~\cite{MartinelliShapiraToninelli2025} provide the endpoints for the
centered-moment corollary proved here. These examples show how the same
coefficient criterion applies to different facilitation rules.
