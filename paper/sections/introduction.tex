% Reader question:

% Job:

Interacting particle systems are stochastic models in which many local random
updates combine to produce nontrivial collective behaviour. In this paper, we
study spin systems: continuous-time Markov processes on~$\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}$,
where~$\Lambda$ is the set of sites representing space and only one spin is
updated at a time. The update at a site may depend on nearby spins, which
is how the sites interact. We are interested in how these local rules determine
the dependence of the long-time behaviour of the system on the initial
conditions.

%
% Terminology:

One of the main tools for such questions is monotonicity. The configuration
space has the usual coordinatewise order: $\eta\leq\xi$ if
$\eta(i)\leq\xi(i)$ at every site. A spin system is monotone if processes
started from~$\eta\leq\xi$ can be coupled so that the same order holds at all
later times. This gives direct comparisons between different initial
conditions. The same coupling idea can also be used to compare systems with
different transition rules. Such comparisons are basic tools for studying
dependence on the initial condition, invariant measures, and convergence.

% Citation anchors:
%   CancriniEtAl2008; HartarskyToninelli2025.

% Important framing:

Kinetically constrained spin models (KCSMs) are spin systems constructed so
that their equilibrium measure is a Bernoulli product measure, with all spins independent, while the
dynamics is interacting through local constraints. At an update
attempt, the spin at a site is refreshed from the one-site Bernoulli equilibrium
law only if a constraint determined by neighbouring spins is satisfied. We use
the standard convention that vacancy, denoted~$0$, is a facilitating state. The
constraint is monotone with respect to vacancies: adding vacancies can make an
update legal but cannot make a legal update illegal. These constraints can make
relaxation very slow and lead to glassy dynamics~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. The East and FA-1f models
are standard examples.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB D. Monotone constraints do not imply monotone dynamics
%
% Reader question:
%   If more facilitating states make updates easier, why is the dynamics not
%   already monotone?
%
% Job:
%   Make the distinction concrete:
%     - the kinetic constraint has a clear monotone/constraining direction;
%     - the stochastic dynamics generally does not preserve configuration
%       order, because an update may occur in one configuration but not the
%       ordered one, and the refresh/flip can reverse the local order.
%   Ideally include one very small verbal example.
%
% Reader takeaway:
%   The local mechanism has a monotone direction that ordinary pathwise
%   monotonicity fails to capture.
% ============================================================================

This monotonicity of the constraint does not make the spin system monotone in
the configuration order. Indeed, if~$\eta\leq\xi$, an update may be legal in
$\eta$ because it has more vacancies, while the same update is blocked in
$\xi$. If the legal refresh changes~$\eta(i)$ from~$0$ to~$1$ while~$\xi(i)$
remains~$0$, the coordinatewise order is lost. Thus the monotone structure of
the constraint does not give the usual order-preserving coupling.

% Keep out for now:

% ============================================================================

This leaves a natural question: does the monotone direction of the constraint
still lead to a useful comparison principle, even though the process is not
monotone in configuration order?

% Reader question:

We use an equivalent refresh description of the local dynamics. Given the
neighbouring spins, site~$i$ updates at rate~$\lambda_i$, and when it updates
the new spin is~$1$ with probability~$\rho_i$. For a standard KCSM,
$\lambda_i$ is determined by the kinetic constraint while~$\rho_i$ is the
fixed Bernoulli equilibrium density. We allow both~$\lambda_i$ and~$\rho_i$ to
depend on the neighbouring configuration. Within this broader class, the
systems singled out by our representation are called \emph{patch-positive}.

The examples place patch positivity relative to familiar spin systems. East
and FA-1f are patch-positive, so the class contains standard KCSMs. It is not,
however, a class of all KCSMs: cooperative models, like FA-jf, for~$j\geq 2$, in which a site can update only when at
least~$j$ neighbours are vacant, are not patch-positive. The framework also
extends beyond KCSMs. BABP and the contact process are also patch-positive, although the latter is also monotone in the
usual configuration order, where the standard monotone coupling gives stronger
comparisons. Thus patch positivity picks out a rate structure shared by some
KCSMs and by spin systems outside the KCSM class. {\color{red} Move this

The comparison principle takes the form of a new order on probability laws,
which we call the \emph{centered-moment order}. Our main structural result is
that the semigroup of every patch-positive spin system preserves this order for
arbitrary initial laws. Thus a form of monotonicity lost at the configuration

% BLURB J. What is the centered-moment order?
%
% Reader question:

%
% Job:

% Interpretation to include:

Each patch-positive system has an associated patch threshold~$p^\star$, or a
site-dependent patch threshold profile~$\mb p^\star$ in the general setting,
determined by its local rates. For each finite set of sites~$A$, consider
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A^*(\eta)
=
\prod_{i\in A}\bb{\eta(i)-p_i^\star}.
\end{equation*}
For probability laws~$\mu$ and~$\nu$, we write~$\mu\preceq_*\nu$ when
$\mu(\chi_A^*)\leq\nu(\chi_A^*)$ for every finite~$A$. In other words, the
order compares all finite products of spins centered at this rate-determined
reference profile. {\color{red} Add the limitation of the monotonicity analogy
here or immediately after the sandwiching statement: unlike configuration
order,~$\preceq_*$ generally has no universal smallest and largest probability
laws unless~$\mb p^\star=\mb 0$. This explains why the applications use chosen
comparison laws, typically product measures.}

These results have two main consequences. First, the centered-moment order gives
comparisons between non-monotone dynamics; in particular, we can compare a
system with the dynamics obtained by removing pure deaths, meaning
environment-independent transitions from~$1$ to~$0$. Second, when pure deaths
occur at a uniform positive rate, the patch representation yields convergence
to a common invariant law for a broad class of initial laws. If the additional
condition~$\mb p^\star\leq \mbs{\frac{1}{2}}$ holds, the invariant law is unique
and convergence is exponential, uniformly over all initial configurations. The
convergence argument does not assume reversibility and does not require the
invariant law to be identified in advance. {\color{red} Reorganize this
paragraph into three distinct consequences. First, centered-moment order
compares different initial laws under the same dynamics and transfers
convergence by sandwiching. Second, comparison after removing pure deaths is a
separate comparison between two dynamics obtained from the patch
representation. Third, uniform pure deaths give convergence to a common
invariant law. The current first sentence conflates the first two mechanisms.}

% BLURB L. Signed duality and patches
%
% Reader question:
%   What is the new idea in the representation, and where do the cancellations
%   occur?
%
% Job:
%   Define P_t and chi_A at first use. Explain the standard signed
%   Feynman--Kac dual, then the coarser graphical construction: retain only the

%   remaining randomness separates between patches and can be averaged locally.

The method is as follows: write~$P_t$ for the Markov semigroup and, for a finite set~$A$, let
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i).
\end{equation*}
Thus~$P_t\chi_A(\eta)$ is the expected value of~$\chi_A$ at time~$t$ when the
process starts from~$\eta$. Standard Feynman--Kac duality represents
$P_t\chi_A$ using a stochastic process on finite sets of sites and carrying a sign; the set records the
sites on which the observable depends. Our graphical construction
keeps only the events that pass this dependence between sites, recording when
they occur and which sites they connect. These events divide spacetime into single-site regions which we call \emph{patches}. Once this coarser graphical structure
is fixed, the remaining randomness separates between patches and can be
averaged within each one. The resulting patch factorization captures cancellations in the dual representation of~$P_t\chi_A(\eta)$. {\color{red}
Language cleanup here. ``Pass this dependence between sites'' is not natural;
say that the recorded interactions are those at which dependence is transferred
between sites. ``This coarser graphical structure'' can simply be ``this
record''.}

Patch positivity means that the averaged contribution of every patch lying
entirely before the observation time is nonnegative. Thus the cancellations in
the signed dual are performed locally, before the patch contributions are
combined to recover~$P_t\chi_A$. Patches that reach the observation time leave
factors depending on the terminal spins; the patch threshold
profile~$\mb p^\star$ introduced above is chosen so that these factors are
compatible with the centered-moment order. We show that patch positivity is
equivalent to an explicit local condition on the flip rates. The same condition
implies preservation of the centered-moment order. {\color{red} ``Compatible
with'' is vague. State instead that~$p_i^\star$ is the smallest reference value
for which every end-patch contribution based at~$i$ is nonnegative. Also

\paragraph{Relation to previous work.}
Set-valued graphical constructions and additive dual processes go back to
Harris~\cite{Harris1974,Harris1978}; see also
Griffeath~\cite{Griffeath1979}, Holley--Stroock~\cite{HolleyStroock1979}, and
Gray~\cite{Gray1986}. We use the signed monomial Feynman--Kac duality; Jansen
and Kurt~\cite{JansenKurt2014} give a general account of Markov-process
duality. Conditioning on the coarse interaction record described above gives a
one-site factorization which is not present in the standard duality.
Information percolation also retains updates which carry information
\cite{LubetzkySly2016}, but uses them for a different decomposition. Our
construction is also different from the time-scaling observation
in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2025}, where each local update transition matrix is mixed
with the identity. It was motivated by the wall-type one-sided spin systems
studied in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2026}; many of their rate functions satisfy the
coefficient criterion below, although not all.

Classical correlation inequalities for monotone spin systems, traditionally
called attractive systems in the IPS literature, are tied to order-preserving
couplings and association~\cite{Harris1977,Liggett2006}; duality can also yield
moment inequalities in particular models~\cite{GiardinaRedigVafayi2010}. The
order considered here uses products centered at~$\mb p^\star$ and applies to
systems which are not monotone in configuration order. To our knowledge,
preservation of this centered-moment order has not previously been obtained
from a local coefficient condition of the present form. The convergence
theorem is also distinct from classical positive-rates results such as
Gray~\cite{Gray1982}: under patch positivity and a uniform pure-death component
it proves convergence from a broad class of initial laws without first
identifying the invariant law.

FA-1f originates in~\cite{FredricksonAndersen1984} and is placed in the KCSM
framework in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. Its
out-of-equilibrium theory, and that of related KCSMs such as East, is developed
in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2010,BlondelEtAl2013,MountfordValle2019,Mareche2019,
HartarskyToninelli2024}. BABP was introduced by Neuhauser and
Sudbury~\cite{NeuhauserSudbury1993}; its qualitative convergence and
quasi-duality theory are due in part to Sudbury and
Lloyd--Sudbury~\cite{Sudbury1997,LloydSudbury1997}. The product-law estimates
in~\cite{MartinelliShapiraToninelli2025} provide the endpoints for the
centered-moment corollary proved here.
