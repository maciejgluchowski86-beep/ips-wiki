\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

% ============================================================================
% BLURB A. Broad entry: spin systems
%
% Reader question:
%   What kind of mathematical object/problem is this paper about?
%
% Job:
%   Give a reader who is not an IPS specialist enough context to enter the
%   paper. Motivate IPS broadly, then fix our narrower use of "spin system" as
%   a binary IPS with single-site updates. Foreshadow dependence on the initial

Interacting particle systems are stochastic models in which many local random
updates combine to produce nontrivial collective behaviour. In this paper, we
study spin systems: continuous-time Markov processes on~$\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}$,
where~$\Lambda$ is the set of sites representing space and only one spin is
updated at a time. The update at a site may depend on nearby spins, which
is how the sites interact. We are interested in how these local rules determine
the dependence of the long-time behaviour of the system on the initial
conditions.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB B. Why monotonicity matters
%
% Reader question:
%   What general structure often makes spin systems tractable/comparable?
%
% Job:
%   Explain why monotonicity is desirable before discussing its failure.
%   The basic message should be that an order-preserving coupling propagates
%   microscopic order into comparisons of laws, and that this is useful for
%   invariant measures and long-time behaviour.
%
% Terminology:
%   Use "monotone" in the exposition. The legacy term "attractive" can be
%   mentioned later in the literature discussion.
%
% Keep out for now:
%   Formal stochastic-order notation; correlation inequalities; detailed
%   literature history.
% ============================================================================

One of the main tools for such questions is monotonicity. The configuration
space has the usual coordinatewise order: $\eta\leq\xi$ if
$\eta(i)\leq\xi(i)$ at every site. A spin system is monotone if processes
started from~$\eta\leq\xi$ can be coupled so that the same order holds at all
later times. This gives direct comparisons between different initial
conditions. The same coupling idea can also be used to compare systems with
different transition rules. Such comparisons are basic tools for studying
dependence on the initial condition, invariant measures, and convergence.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB C. KCSMs as the motivating research class
%
% Reader question:
%   Which established class of spin systems motivates the paper, and why is it
%   mathematically interesting?
%
% Job:
%   Introduce KCSMs in community-standard language. Explain the basic kinetic
%   constraint/facilitating-state mechanism and give East/FA-1f as familiar
%   anchors. Also say why these models are difficult/interesting dynamically:
%   simple equilibrium structure in canonical examples versus constrained
%   relaxation and difficult out-of-equilibrium behaviour.
%
% Citation anchors:
%   CancriniEtAl2008; HartarskyToninelli2025.
%
% Important framing:
%   This should sound like the paper is entering an established KCSM
%   literature, not defining its own isolated universe.
% ============================================================================

Kinetically constrained spin models (KCSMs) are spin systems constructed so
that their equilibrium measure is a Bernoulli product measure, with all spins independent, while the
dynamics is interacting through local constraints. At an update
attempt, the spin at a site is refreshed from the one-site Bernoulli equilibrium
law only if a constraint determined by neighbouring spins is satisfied. We use
the standard convention that vacancy, denoted~$0$, is a facilitating state. The
constraint is monotone with respect to vacancies: adding vacancies can make an
update legal but cannot make a legal update illegal. These constraints can make
relaxation very slow and lead to glassy dynamics~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. The East and FA-1f models
are standard examples.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB D. Monotone constraints do not imply monotone dynamics
%
% Reader question:
%   If more facilitating states make updates easier, why is the dynamics not
%   already monotone?
%
% Job:
%   Make the distinction concrete:
%     - the kinetic constraint has a clear monotone/constraining direction;
%     - the stochastic dynamics generally does not preserve configuration
%       order, because an update may occur in one configuration but not the
%       ordered one, and the refresh/flip can reverse the local order.
%   Ideally include one very small verbal example.
%
% Reader takeaway:
%   The local mechanism has a monotone direction that ordinary pathwise
%   monotonicity fails to capture.
% ============================================================================

This monotonicity of the constraint does not make the spin system monotone in
the configuration order. Indeed, if~$\eta\leq\xi$, an update may be legal in
$\eta$ because it has more vacancies, while the same update is blocked in
$\xi$. If the legal refresh changes~$\eta(i)$ from~$0$ to~$1$ while~$\xi(i)$
remains~$0$, the coordinatewise order is lost. Thus the monotone structure of
the constraint does not give the usual order-preserving coupling.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB E. Motivating question
%
% Reader question:
%   Is the monotone structure of facilitation completely lost when
%   configuration monotonicity fails?
%
% Job:
%   State the conceptual question of the paper: whether the monotone direction
%   of KCSM-type constraints can still produce a useful comparison principle
%   when configuration monotonicity fails.
%
% Keep out for now:
%   Coefficient inequalities; p^star; centered moments; patches; duality.
% ============================================================================

This leaves a natural question: can the monotone structure of the constraint
still yield a useful comparison principle, even though the process is not
monotone in configuration order?

% ============================================================================
% BLURB F. Broader local dynamics
%
% Reader question:
%   What is being generalized beyond a standard KCSM?
%
% Job:
%   Introduce the refresh description used in the paper. Given the neighbouring
%   spins, a site updates at rate~$\lambda_i$, and the refreshed spin is~$1$
%   with probability~$\rho_i$. In a standard KCSM only the update rate depends
%   on the neighbourhood, while the refresh bias is fixed. Here both may depend
%   on the neighbourhood. Name the subclass as patch-positive and immediately
%   explain that the name comes from a graphical representation introduced
%   later; give the dynamical interpretation before introducing patches.
% ============================================================================

Motivated by the KCSM update rule, we describe the spin systems
in the same refresh form. Given the neighbouring spins, site~$i$ updates at
rate~$\lambda_i$, and when it updates the spin is refreshed by a Bernoulli coin
toss with parameter~$\rho_i$. For a standard KCSM,~$\lambda_i$ is
the kinetic constraint while~$\rho_i$ is the fixed Bernoulli equilibrium
density. We allow both~$\lambda_i$ and~$\rho_i$ to depend on the neighbouring
spins. This parametrization is general enough to
describe all spin systems. Among them, we identify a class that we call
\emph{patch-positive}. The name comes from a graphical representation
introduced later. Before describing that construction, we first give a
dynamical interpretation of patch-positivity and state the comparison principle it
yields.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB G. Dynamic interpretation of the new class
%
% Reader question:
%   What does "constraining direction" mean for the broader spin systems?
%
% Job:
%   Give the intuitive rate-level content before any coefficient formulas.
%   Explain that vacancies both facilitate updates and bias refreshes toward
%   vacancy. Present this monotone dependence explicitly as a consequence and
%   interpretation of patch positivity, not as its definition.
% ============================================================================

One consequence of patch positivity is that the facilitating direction of KCSMs
survives in this broader setting. Replacing neighbouring~$1$'s by~$0$'s 
increases the refresh rate and decreases the probability that an update refreshes the spin to~$1$. Thus the refresh rule is monotone in configuration order, but there is an asymmetry:~$0$'s facilitate updates and~$1$'s suppress them.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB H. Headline result: recovered monotonicity
%
% Reader question:
%   What is special about this class dynamically?
%
% Job:
%   State the main conceptual result before defining the order formally. The
%   comparison takes place at the level of probability laws rather than
%   configurations, and preservation of the centered-moment order recovers the
%   usual sandwiching strategy of monotone systems.
%
% This is the headline of the introduction. Give it rhetorical weight.
% ============================================================================

For this class, the comparison principle survives at the level of measures rather than configurations. We introduce an order on laws, the
\emph{centered-moment order}, and prove that the dynamics preserves it for
arbitrary initial laws. This recovers the usual sandwiching argument of
monotone systems: if one initial law lies between two comparison laws, the same
ordering holds at all later times, and convergence of the two bounding
under spin system evolution to the same limit forces convergence of the middle one.

% ============================================================================
% BLURB J. What is the centered-moment order?
%
% Reader question:
%   What is being centered, and how are two laws compared?
%
% Job:
%   Introduce the rate-determined reference profile~$\mb p^\star$ and the
%   centered monomials, define~$\mu\preceq_*\nu$, and state the preservation
%   implication. Calibrate the analogy with configuration monotonicity by
%   noting that this order generally has no universal lower and upper laws.
%
% Keep out for now:
%   The patch interpretation of~$\mb p^\star$ and the coefficient criterion.
% ============================================================================

For systems in this class, the local rates determine a reference
profile~$\mb p^\star=(p_i^\star)_{i\in\Lambda}$. For each finite set of
sites~$A$, let
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A^*(\eta)
=
\prod_{i\in A}\bb{\eta(i)-p_i^\star}.
\end{equation*}
For probability laws~$\mu$ and~$\nu$, we write~$\mu\preceq_*\nu$ when
$\mu(\chi_A^*)\leq\nu(\chi_A^*)$ for every finite~$A$. Writing~$P_t$ for the
Markov semigroup, our comparison theorem states that
\begin{equation*}
\mu\preceq_*\nu
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mu P_t\preceq_*\nu P_t
\qquad\text{for every }t\geq0.
\end{equation*}
This is an order on probability laws, not a pathwise ordering of configurations.
Unlike configuration order, it generally has no universal smallest and largest
probability laws; when~$\mb p^\star=\mb 0$, the all-$0$ and all-$1$ point masses
play those roles. In applications we therefore use chosen comparison laws,
typically product measures.

% BLURB O. Examples and scope
%
% Reader question:
%   Which familiar systems does this cover, and what is the intended scope?
%
% Job:
%   Anchor the framework after the main comparison principle has been stated:
%     - East / FA-1f as canonical KCSM examples;
%     - cooperative FA-jf, j>=2, as KCSMs outside the patch-positive class;
%     - BABP as an example beyond literal constrained Bernoulli refresh;
%     - contact process as a benchmark where ordinary configuration
%       monotonicity is already stronger.

This class contains familiar KCSMs: the East and FA-1f models are
patch-positive. It does not, however, contain all KCSMs. Cooperative models
such as FA-jf, for~$j\geq2$, in which a site can update only when at least~$j$
neighbours are vacant, are not patch-positive. The class also extends beyond
KCSMs: BABP and the contact process are patch-positive. The contact process is
already monotone in the usual configuration order, so its standard monotone
coupling gives stronger comparisons. Thus patch positivity overlaps with, but
is distinct from, both the KCSM class and the class of monotone spin systems.

These results have two main consequences. First, the centered-moment order gives
comparisons between non-monotone dynamics; in particular, we can compare a
system with the dynamics obtained by removing pure deaths, meaning
environment-independent transitions from~$1$ to~$0$. Second, when pure deaths
occur at a uniform positive rate, the patch representation yields convergence
to a common invariant law for a broad class of initial laws. If the additional
condition~$\mb p^\star\leq \mbs{\frac{1}{2}}$ holds, the invariant law is unique
and convergence is exponential, uniformly over all initial configurations. The
convergence argument does not assume reversibility and does not require the
invariant law to be identified in advance. {\color{red} Reorganize this
paragraph into three distinct consequences. First, centered-moment order
compares different initial laws under the same dynamics and transfers
convergence by sandwiching. Second, comparison after removing pure deaths is a
separate comparison between two dynamics. Third, uniform pure deaths give
convergence to a common invariant law. Do not say here that ``the patch
representation yields'' a result before the representation itself has been
explained; state the theorem-level consequence first and leave the method to L.
The current first sentence also conflates the first two mechanisms.}

% BLURB L. Signed duality and patches
%
% Reader question:
%   What is the new idea in the representation, and where do the cancellations
%   occur?
%
% Job:
%   Define P_t and chi_A at first use. Explain the standard signed
%   Feynman--Kac dual, then the coarser graphical construction: retain only the
%   events that pass dependence between sites. Once this record is fixed, the
%   remaining randomness separates between patches and can be averaged locally.
% ============================================================================

The method is as follows: write~$P_t$ for the Markov semigroup and, for a finite set~$A$, let
\begin{equation*}
\chi_A(\eta)=\prod_{i\in A}\eta(i).
\end{equation*}
Thus~$P_t\chi_A(\eta)$ is the expected value of~$\chi_A$ at time~$t$ when the
process starts from~$\eta$. Standard Feynman--Kac duality represents
$P_t\chi_A$ using a stochastic process on finite sets of sites and carrying a sign; the set records the
sites on which the observable depends. Our graphical construction
keeps only the events that pass this dependence between sites, recording when
they occur and which sites they connect. These events divide spacetime into single-site regions which we call \emph{patches}. Once this coarser graphical structure
is fixed, the remaining randomness separates between patches and can be
averaged within each one. The resulting patch factorization captures cancellations in the dual representation of~$P_t\chi_A(\eta)$. {\color{red}
This is the point where the deferred terminology from F is finally paid off, so
make that transition clear rather than letting patches appear incidentally.
Also clean up the language: ``pass this dependence between sites'' is not
natural; say that the recorded interactions are those at which dependence is
transferred between sites. ``This coarser graphical structure'' can simply be
``this record''.}

% Reader question:
%   What does patch positivity mean in this representation, and how does it
%   connect back to the centered-moment order?
%
% Job:
%   Use only the language introduced in L. State the sign condition on patches

Patch positivity means that the averaged contribution of every patch lying
entirely before the observation time is nonnegative. Thus the cancellations in
the signed dual are performed locally, before the patch contributions are
combined to recover~$P_t\chi_A$. Patches that reach the observation time leave
factors depending on the terminal spins; the patch threshold
profile~$\mb p^\star$ introduced above is chosen so that these factors are
compatible with the centered-moment order. We show that patch positivity is
equivalent to an explicit local condition on the flip rates. The same condition
implies preservation of the centered-moment order. {\color{red} Begin by
explicitly closing the loop from F, for example: ``This construction explains
the term patch positivity.'' Then state the patch sign condition. ``Compatible
with'' is vague: say instead that~$p_i^\star$ is the smallest reference value
for which every end-patch contribution based at~$i$ is nonnegative, and identify
here that this is why it is called the patch threshold. Also separate the two
proof mechanisms: the patch representation gives the coefficient criterion and
the threshold, while a separate generator calculation in the
centered-monomial basis shows that the same coefficient criterion preserves the
centered-moment order.}

\paragraph{Relation to previous work.}
Set-valued graphical constructions and additive dual processes go back to
Harris~\cite{Harris1974,Harris1978}; see also
Griffeath~\cite{Griffeath1979}, Holley--Stroock~\cite{HolleyStroock1979}, and
Gray~\cite{Gray1986}. We use the signed monomial Feynman--Kac duality; Jansen
and Kurt~\cite{JansenKurt2014} give a general account of Markov-process
duality. Conditioning on the coarse interaction record described above gives a
one-site factorization which is not present in the standard duality.
Information percolation also retains updates which carry information
\cite{LubetzkySly2016}, but uses them for a different decomposition. Our
construction is also different from the time-scaling observation
in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2025}, where each local update transition matrix is mixed
with the identity. It was motivated by the wall-type one-sided spin systems
studied in~\cite{GluchowskiMenz2026}; many of their rate functions satisfy the
coefficient criterion below, although not all.

Classical correlation inequalities for monotone spin systems, traditionally
called attractive systems in the IPS literature, are tied to order-preserving
couplings and association~\cite{Harris1977,Liggett2006}; duality can also yield
moment inequalities in particular models~\cite{GiardinaRedigVafayi2010}. The
order considered here uses products centered at~$\mb p^\star$ and applies to
systems which are not monotone in configuration order. To our knowledge,
preservation of this centered-moment order has not previously been obtained
from a local coefficient condition of the present form. The convergence
theorem is also distinct from classical positive-rates results such as
Gray~\cite{Gray1982}: under patch positivity and a uniform pure-death component
it proves convergence from a broad class of initial laws without first
identifying the invariant law.

FA-1f originates in~\cite{FredricksonAndersen1984} and is placed in the KCSM
framework in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2008,HartarskyToninelli2025}. Its
out-of-equilibrium theory, and that of related KCSMs such as East, is developed
in~\cite{CancriniEtAl2010,BlondelEtAl2013,MountfordValle2019,Mareche2019,
HartarskyToninelli2024}. BABP was introduced by Neuhauser and
Sudbury~\cite{NeuhauserSudbury1993}; its qualitative convergence and
quasi-duality theory are due in part to Sudbury and
Lloyd--Sudbury~\cite{Sudbury1997,LloydSudbury1997}. The product-law estimates
in~\cite{MartinelliShapiraToninelli2025} provide the endpoints for the
centered-moment corollary proved here.
