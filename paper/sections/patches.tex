\section{Successful interactions and patches}
\label{sec:patches}

The graphical construction contains more information than is needed to record
how dependence moves between sites. We retain the nonempty-target interactions
which act on the dual process and divide the remaining one-site histories at
their endpoints.

\subsection{The successful-interaction skeleton}
\label{subsec:successful-skeleton}

\begin{definition}[Successful interaction]
\label{def:successful-interaction}
The deterministic initial interaction~$\iota_0$ from
\eqref{eq:initial-interaction} is declared successful, with skeleton
\begin{equation*}
(\infty,0,A_0).
\end{equation*}
An ordinary interaction~$(i,t,\alpha,S)\in I^{\mathrm P}$ is
\emph{successful} when
\begin{equation*}
S\neq\varnothing
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
i\in A_{t-}.
\end{equation*}
Its successful-interaction skeleton is the triple~$(i,t,S)$.
\end{definition}

The skeleton records the source, time, and target of every nonempty-target
interaction which acts on the process, but not whether the interaction is a
split or a birth. It also omits all death clocks and all rings at inactive
sources. These hidden local data are the randomness averaged inside patches.
For~$T<\infty$, let~$\mathcal I_T$ be the initial skeleton together with all
ordinary successful-interaction skeletons up to time~$T$, and set
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal I=\bigcup_{T<\infty}\mathcal I_T,
\qquad
\mathcal G_T=\sigma(Y_0,\mathcal I_T).
\end{equation*}
The local-finiteness statement in
Section~\ref{subsec:dual-graphical-construction} implies that~$\mathcal I_T$ is
finite almost surely.

\subsection{Patch geometry and labels}
\label{subsec:patch-geometry}

A skeleton~$(j,t,S)\in\mathcal I$ gives an incoming touch at each site in~$S$.
If~$j\in\Lambda$, it also gives an outgoing touch at its source~$j$. Thus the
initial skeleton gives only incoming touches, whereas every ordinary successful
interaction gives one outgoing touch and one incoming touch at each target
site. For~$i\in\Lambda$, write
\begin{align*}
\mathcal T^{\mathsf I}(i)
&=
\{t\geq0:(j,t,S)\in\mathcal I
  \text{ for some~$j,S$ with~$i\in S$}\},
\\
\mathcal T^{\mathsf O}(i)
&=
\{t>0:(i,t,S)\in\mathcal I\text{ for some~$S$}\},
\end{align*}
and let~$\mathcal T(i)=\mathcal T^{\mathsf I}(i)\cup
\mathcal T^{\mathsf O}(i)$. For~$s\in\mathcal T(i)$, define the next touch at
$i$ by
\begin{equation*}
e_i(s)=\inf\{u>s:u\in\mathcal T(i)\},
\qquad
\inf\varnothing=\infty.
\end{equation*}

\begin{definition}[Patch]
\label{def:patch}
Let~$(j,s,S)\in\mathcal I$ touch~$i$. The patch beginning at~$(i,s)$ is the
labeled tuple
\begin{equation*}
P=
\bigl(i(P),[s(P),e(P)),\mathsf X(P)\mathsf Y(P),S(P)\bigr),
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
i(P)=i,
\qquad
s(P)=s,
\qquad
e(P)=e_i(s),
\qquad
S(P)=S.
\end{equation*}
Its initial and terminal labels are
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf X(P)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf I,&i(P)\in S(P),\\
\mathsf O,&i(P)\notin S(P),
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf Y(P)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf I,&e(P)<\infty\text{ and }e(P)\in\mathcal T^{\mathsf I}(i(P)),\\
\mathsf O,&e(P)<\infty\text{ and }e(P)\in\mathcal T^{\mathsf O}(i(P)),\\
\mathsf E,&e(P)=\infty.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
\end{definition}

The labels have the following formal and graphical meanings.
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{c c p{0.57\textwidth}}
\hline
boundary & label & meaning \\
\hline
initial & $\mathsf I$ & $i(P)\in S(P)$; the patch begins at a target of its initial interaction \\
initial & $\mathsf O$ & $i(P)\notin S(P)$; the patch begins at the source of its initial interaction \\
terminal & $\mathsf I$ & the next successful touch at~$i(P)$ is incoming \\
terminal & $\mathsf O$ & the next successful touch at~$i(P)$ is outgoing \\
terminal & $\mathsf E$ & there is no later successful touch at~$i(P)$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
For an ordinary interaction,~$S(P)\subseteq N(i(P))$ on the outgoing side, so
the initial label is determined by~$i(P)$ and~$S(P)$. We retain the label
because the four finite boundary types enter the contribution formulas
symmetrically. Let~$\mathcal P$ denote the family of all patches. Every patch
with terminal label~$\mathsf E$ is infinite, and every other patch is finite.

\subsection{Full patches and finite-horizon truncation}
\label{subsec:patch-truncation}

Fix~$t\geq0$. For a patch~$P\in\mathcal P$ with~$s(P)\leq t$, let
$P^{\downarrow t}$ be the patch obtained by replacing its endpoint by
$\min\{e(P),t\}$ and, when~$t<e(P)$, replacing its terminal label by
$\mathsf E$. Thus
\begin{equation*}
P^{\downarrow t}
=
\bigl(
 i(P),
 [s(P),\min\{e(P),t\}),
 \mathsf X(P)\mathsf Y^{\downarrow t}(P),
 S(P)
\bigr),
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf Y^{\downarrow t}(P)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf Y(P),&e(P)\leq t,\\
\mathsf E,&t<e(P).
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
The bulk and end patch families at time~$t$ are
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal B_t=\{P\in\mathcal P:e(P)\leq t\},
\qquad
\mathcal E_t=
\{P^{\downarrow t}:P\in\mathcal P,\ s(P)\leq t<e(P)\}.
\end{equation*}
The finite-horizon patch family is
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal P_t=\mathcal B_t\cup\mathcal E_t.
\end{equation*}
It is finite almost surely. Distinct end patches have distinct sites, since the
touch times partition each site line into consecutive intervals. Only the
completed full patches and the truncated end patches enter the finite-horizon
representation below, so we do not introduce a separate name for the
untruncated full patches which cross the observation time.
