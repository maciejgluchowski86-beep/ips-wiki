\section{Successful interactions and patches}
\label{sec:patches}

The graphical construction contains more information than is needed to record how dependence moves between sites. We retain the nonempty-target interactions which act on the dual process and use their endpoints to divide the remaining one-site histories into patches.

\subsection{The successful-interaction skeleton}
\label{subsec:successful-skeleton}

\begin{definition}[Successful interaction]
\label{def:successful-interaction}
The deterministic initial interaction~$\iota_0$ from~\eqref{eq:initial-interaction} is declared successful, with skeleton
\begin{equation*}
(\infty,0,A_0).
\end{equation*}
An ordinary interaction~$(i,t,\alpha,S)\in I^{\mathrm P}$ is \emph{successful} when
\begin{equation*}
S\neq\varnothing
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
i\in A_{t-}.
\end{equation*}
Its successful-interaction skeleton is the triple~$(i,t,S)$.
\end{definition}

The skeleton records the source, time, and target of every nonempty-target interaction which acts on the dual process. It does not record whether the interaction is a split or a birth, and it omits deaths and rings at sources outside the dual-active set. These are precisely the local data averaged inside patches. For~$T<\infty$, let~$\mathcal I_T$ consist of the initial skeleton and all ordinary successful-interaction skeletons up to time~$T$, and set
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal I=\bigcup_{T<\infty}\mathcal I_T,
\qquad
\mathcal G_T=\sigma(Y_0,\mathcal I_T).
\end{equation*}
The local-finiteness statement in Section~\ref{subsec:dual-graphical-construction} implies that~$\mathcal I_T$ is finite almost surely.

\subsection{Patch geometry and labels}
\label{subsec:patch-geometry}

A skeleton~$(j,t,S)\in\mathcal I$ gives an incoming touch at each site in~$S$. If~$j\in\Lambda$, it also gives an outgoing touch at its source~$j$. Thus the initial skeleton gives only incoming touches, whereas every ordinary successful interaction gives one outgoing touch and an incoming touch at each target. For~$i\in\Lambda$, write
\begin{align*}
\mathcal T^{\mathsf I}(i)
&=
\{t\geq0:(j,t,S)\in\mathcal I\text{ for some }j,S\text{ with }i\in S\},
\\
\mathcal T^{\mathsf O}(i)
&=
\{t>0:(i,t,S)\in\mathcal I\text{ for some }S\},
\end{align*}
and let~$\mathcal T(i)=\mathcal T^{\mathsf I}(i)\cup\mathcal T^{\mathsf O}(i)$. For~$s\in\mathcal T(i)$, define
\begin{equation*}
e_i(s)=\inf\{u>s:u\in\mathcal T(i)\},
\qquad
\inf\varnothing=\infty.
\end{equation*}

\begin{definition}[Patch]
\label{def:patch}
Let~$(j,s,S)\in\mathcal I$ touch~$i$. The patch beginning at~$(i,s)$ is the labeled tuple
\begin{equation*}
P=
\bigl(i(P),[s(P),e(P)),\mathsf X(P)\mathsf Y(P),S(P)\bigr),
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
i(P)=i,
\qquad
s(P)=s,
\qquad
e(P)=e_i(s),
\qquad
S(P)=S.
\end{equation*}
Its initial label is
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf X(P)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf I,&i(P)\in S(P),\\
\mathsf O,&i(P)\notin S(P),
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
and its terminal label is
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf Y(P)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf I,&e(P)<\infty\text{ and }e(P)\in\mathcal T^{\mathsf I}(i(P)),\\
\mathsf O,&e(P)<\infty\text{ and }e(P)\in\mathcal T^{\mathsf O}(i(P)),\\
\mathsf E,&e(P)=\infty.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
\end{definition}

The labels have the following formal and graphical meanings.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{c c p{0.57\textwidth}}
\hline
boundary & label & meaning \\
\hline
initial & $\mathsf I$ & $i(P)\in S(P)$; the patch begins at a target of its initial interaction \\
initial & $\mathsf O$ & $i(P)\notin S(P)$; the patch begins at the source of its initial interaction \\
terminal & $\mathsf I$ & the next successful touch at~$i(P)$ is incoming \\
terminal & $\mathsf O$ & the next successful touch at~$i(P)$ is outgoing \\
terminal & $\mathsf E$ & there is no later successful touch at~$i(P)$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

For an ordinary interaction, the source does not belong to its target, so~$S(P)$ together with~$i(P)$ determines whether the initial boundary is incoming or outgoing. We retain~$\mathsf X(P)$ because the contribution formulas are naturally indexed by the two boundary labels. Let~$\mathcal P$ denote the family of all patches. A patch with terminal label~$\mathsf E$ is infinite, while every other patch is finite.

\subsection{Full patches and finite-horizon truncation}
\label{subsec:patch-truncation}

Fix~$t\geq0$. For a patch~$P\in\mathcal P$ with~$s(P)\leq t$, let~$P^{\downarrow t}$ be obtained by truncating its interval at~$t$ and replacing its terminal label by~$\mathsf E$ when~$t<e(P)$. Thus
\begin{equation*}
P^{\downarrow t}
=
\bigl(
 i(P),
 [s(P),\min\{e(P),t\}),
 \mathsf X(P)\mathsf Y^{\downarrow t}(P),
 S(P)
\bigr),
\end{equation*}
where
\begin{equation*}
\mathsf Y^{\downarrow t}(P)=
\begin{cases}
\mathsf Y(P),&e(P)\leq t,\\
\mathsf E,&t<e(P).
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
The bulk and end patch families at time~$t$ are
\begin{equation*}
\mathcal B_t=\{P\in\mathcal P:e(P)\leq t\},
\qquad
\mathcal E_t=
\{P^{\downarrow t}:P\in\mathcal P,\ s(P)\leq t<e(P)\},
\end{equation*}
and the finite-horizon patch family is~$\mathcal P_t=\mathcal B_t\cup\mathcal E_t$. It is finite almost surely. Distinct end patches have distinct sites because the touch times partition each site line into consecutive intervals. Only completed full patches and truncated end patches enter the finite-horizon representation, so we do not introduce separate notation for the untruncated full patches crossing~$t$.
