\subsection{Convergence by centered-moment comparison}
\label{subsec:centered-moment-convergence}

A standard use of monotonicity is the sandwiching argument: if an initial law
lies between two comparison laws and both converge  to the
same equilibrium under action of the semigroup, then the middle one converges as well. The
centered-moment order gives the same principle for patch-positive systems, even
when the dynamics is not monotone in configuration order. There is one
important difference. The order~$\preceq_*$ does not in general come with
universal lower and upper laws. In the special case~$\mb p^\star=\mb 0$, we
have~$\chi_A^*=\chi_A$ and
\begin{equation*}
\delta_{\mb 0}\preceq_*\mu\preceq_*\delta_{\mb 1}
\qquad\text{for every probability measure }\mu.
\end{equation*}
For a nonzero threshold profile, suitable comparison laws must instead be
found. In the applications below these will typically be product measures.

\begin{corollary}[Convergence by centered-moment comparison]
\label{cor:centered-moment-convergence}
Suppose that the spin system is patch-positive and that
\begin{equation*}
\underline\mu\preceq_*\mu\preceq_*\overline\mu.
\end{equation*}
If~$\underline\mu P_t$ and~$\overline\mu P_t$ converge weakly to the same
probability measure~$\pi$, then~$\mu P_t\Rightarrow\pi$. 
More generally, suppose that~$\rho(t)\to0$ and that, for every
$A\Subset\Lambda$,
\begin{equation*}
\max_{\lambda\in\cb{\underline\mu,\overline\mu}}
\abs{(\lambda P_t)(\chi_A^*)-\pi(\chi_A^*)}
\leq
C_A\rho(t).
\end{equation*}
Then every local function~$f$ satisfies
\begin{equation*}
\abs{(\mu P_t)(f)-\pi(f)}
\leq
C_f\rho(t)
\end{equation*}
for some~$C_f<\infty$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-positivity-order},
\begin{equation*}
\underline\mu P_t\preceq_*\mu P_t\preceq_*\overline\mu P_t
\qquad\text{for every }t\geq0.
\end{equation*}
Hence, for every~$A\Subset\Lambda$,
\begin{equation*}
(\underline\mu P_t)(\chi_A^*)
\leq
(\mu P_t)(\chi_A^*)
\leq
(\overline\mu P_t)(\chi_A^*).
\end{equation*}
If the two comparison laws converge weakly to~$\pi$, the outer terms converge
to~$\pi(\chi_A^*)$, so the middle term does as well. On every finite set of
sites, the centered monomials form a basis of all functions of those spins.
Thus expectations of every local function converge to their~$\pi$-expectation,
which gives~$\mu P_t\Rightarrow\pi$.

Under the quantitative assumption, the same squeeze gives
\begin{equation*}
\abs{(\mu P_t)(\chi_A^*)-\pi(\chi_A^*)}
\leq
C_A\rho(t).
\end{equation*}
If~$f$ is supported on a finite set~$D$, write
\begin{equation*}
f=\sum_{A\subseteq D}b_A\chi_A^*.
\end{equation*}
Summing the preceding bounds with weights~$\abs{b_A}$ gives the claim with
$C_f=\sum_{A\subseteq D}\abs{b_A}C_A$.
\end{proof}
