Conditioning on~$\mathcal G_T$ fixes the successful interactions and hence the
patch decomposition up to time~$T$. It leaves unresolved the local information
that was omitted from the skeleton. In particular, deaths inside patches are
not recorded, and for a recorded outgoing interaction the skeleton does not say
whether it was a split or a birth. The fixed patch boundaries restrict this
remaining randomness: depending on the boundary types, they may force the
interaction kind or exclude deaths. These restrictions involve only one patch
at a time. Consequently, conditional on~$\mathcal G_T$, the unresolved
randomness factorizes over the patches.

Fix a patch~$P$, and write~$i=i(P)$,~$s=s(P)$,~$e=e(P)$, and~$S=S(P)$.
We first define a reference law for the information in the patch~$P$, that is not
revealed by the skeleton. Inside the patch, the outgoing marks from~$i$ are
\begin{equation*}
\Sigma_P^\circ
=
\cb{(i,u,\gamma,R)\in I:s<u<e}.
\end{equation*}
For each~$R$, the~$(\delta,R)$ and~$(\beta,R)$ marks in~$(s,e)$ are independent
Poisson processes with rates~$\delta_i(R)$ and~$\beta_i(R)$, respectively.

It remains to specify the information at the initial boundary. If
$\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf I$, the boundary is incoming, so there is no outgoing
mark from~$i$ to add and we set~$\Sigma_P=\Sigma_P^\circ$. If
$\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf O$, the boundary record specifies the target~$S$ but not
whether the interaction is a split or a birth. Let
$\alpha(P)\in\cb{\delta,\beta}$ denote its kind, independent of
$\Sigma_P^\circ$, with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-initial-kind-law}
\pP_P\bb{\alpha(P)=\delta}
=
\frac{\delta_i(S)}{\delta_i(S)+\beta_i(S)},
\qquad
\pP_P\bb{\alpha(P)=\beta}
=
\frac{\beta_i(S)}{\delta_i(S)+\beta_i(S)}.
\end{equation}
In this case,
\begin{equation*}
\Sigma_P
=
\Sigma_P^\circ\cup\cb{(i,s,\alpha(P),S)}.
\end{equation*}
Together, the interior Poisson marks and, when present, the random initial kind
define the reference patch law~$\pP_P$. Write~$\E_P$ for expectation under
$\pP_P$.

The marks in~$\Sigma_P$ determine a one-site process~$X^P\in\cb{0,1}$, where
$X_u^P=1$ means that the patch site is active at time~$u$. Its initial value is
\begin{equation*}
X_s^P
=
\pw{1}{\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf I}
{\ind\cb{\alpha(P)=\beta}}{\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf O}.
\end{equation*}
The interior marks are then read in chronological order. When~$X_{u-}^P=1$, a
$\delta$-mark sets~$X_u^P=0$ and a~$\beta$-mark leaves~$X_u^P=1$; when
$X_{u-}^P=0$, either mark leaves~$X_u^P=0$. Between marks,~$X^P$ is constant.
For a finite patch with terminal label~$\mathsf E$, set~$X_e^P=X_{e-}^P$.

Not every realization under~$\pP_P$ is compatible with the patch boundaries
fixed by the skeleton. A non-death mark inside patch would be a
successful interaction whenever the patch site is active immediately before
it, and would therefore create additional patches. Hence such
marks can only occur with~$X_{u-}^P=0$. If the terminal boundary is outgoing, the
recorded interaction must itself be successful, so~$X_{e-}^P=1$. There is no
additional endpoint condition for an incoming or end boundary. We therefore
define the consistency event
\begin{equation*}
\operatorname{Con}(P)
=
\cb{
\begin{aligned}
X_{u-}^P=0
&\quad\text{for every }(i,u,\gamma,R)\in\Sigma_P^\circ\\[-0.2em]
&\quad\text{with }R\neq\varnothing
\end{aligned}
}
\cap
\pw{\cb{X_{e-}^P=1}}{\mathsf Y(P)=\mathsf O}
{\Omega_P}{\mathsf Y(P)\in\cb{\mathsf I,\mathsf E}},
\end{equation*}
where~$\Omega_P$ is the sample space of the patch law. Depending on
the boundary types, this condition can determine the hidden initial interaction
kind or exclude deaths inside the patch.

For almost every patch arising from the graphical construction,
$\pP_P\bb{\operatorname{Con}(P)}>0$. Its consistent patch law is
\begin{equation*}
\pP_P^{\mathrm{con}}(\cdot)
=
\pP_P\bb{\,\cdot\,\middle|\,\operatorname{Con}(P)},
\end{equation*}
and~$\E_P^{\mathrm{con}}$ denotes expectation under this law.

Fix~$T<\infty$. Once~$\mathcal G_T$ is fixed, the patch family~$\mathcal P_T$
and all its boundaries are fixed. The marks assigned to distinct patches come
from disjoint parts of the Poisson graphical construction and, before the
consistency conditions are imposed, have the independent reference laws
$\pP_P$. Moreover, these marks give exactly the prescribed
successful-interaction skeleton if and only if~$\operatorname{Con}(P)$ holds for
every~$P\in\mathcal P_T$. Since each consistency event depends only on the marks
of its own patch, conditioning on the skeleton conditions the patches
separately.

\begin{theorem}[Patch factorization]
\label{thm:patch-factorization}
Conditional on~$\mathcal G_T$, the variables~$\Sigma_P$ are independent, and
the conditional law of~$\Sigma_P$ is~$\pP_P^{\mathrm{con}}$ for each
$P\in\mathcal P_T$. Equivalently, for bounded measurable functions~$f_P$ such
that either all~$f_P$ are nonnegative or the product on the left below is
integrable,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-factorization}
\CE{
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P\bb{\Sigma_P}
}{\mathcal G_T}
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}
\E_P^{\mathrm{con}}\Cb{f_P\bb{\Sigma_P}}.
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Write~$G_T=(Y_0,\mathcal I_T)$, so~$\mathcal G_T=\sigma(G_T)$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:dual-local-finiteness}, its positive-time records under~$\pP_A$
almost surely form a finite chronological list
\begin{equation*}
g=\bb{(i_k,t_k,S_k)}_{k=1}^n,
\qquad
0<t_1<\cdots<t_n\leq T,
\end{equation*}
where~$\varnothing\neq S_k\subseteq N(i_k)$ and
$i_k\in A\cup\bigcup_{\ell<k}S_\ell$. The list space and the finite
marked-configuration spaces are standard Borel.

On the component {\color{red} Component of what? Confusing proof prose} with fixed discrete records~$((i_k,S_k))_{k=1}^n$, set
\begin{equation*}
m_T(dg)
=
\prod_{k=1}^n
\bb{\delta_{i_k}(S_k)+\beta_{i_k}(S_k)}
\,dt_1\cdots dt_n,
\end{equation*}
and sum these measures over~$n\geq0$ and the discrete records, omitting
zero-rate components {\color{red} Describing steps instead of showing them makes this very unclear to the reader}. For nonnegative measurable~$h$ and~$f_P$, the
multivariate Mecke formula~\cite[Theorem~4.4]{LastPenrose2017}, applied to
ordered tuples of nonempty-target marks, gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-factorization-mecke}
\E_A\Cb{
h(G_T)
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P\bb{\Sigma_P}
}
=
\int h(g)
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\E_P\Cb{
f_P\bb{\Sigma_P}\ind_{\operatorname{Con}(P)}
}
\,m_T(dg).
\end{equation}
A mark with record~$(i,t,S)$ has combined intensity
$\bb{\delta_i(S)+\beta_i(S)}\,dt$ and, conditional on this record, its kind has
the law in~\eqref{eq:patch-initial-kind-law}. The remaining marks in the
disjoint patch intervals are independent with laws~$\pP_P$.

{\color{red} By now the reader is completely lost as to what is happening in the proof, its completely unclear what is needed to do.} It remains to identify the consistency indicators in
\eqref{eq:patch-factorization-mecke}. Suppose first that every patch is
consistent and read the records chronologically. The initial incoming patches
give the active set~$A$. Between record times, empty-target marks change the
local and global active indicators in the same way, while consistency makes
every unrecorded nonempty-target mark unsuccessful. At a recorded interaction,
consistency makes its source active; its kind determines the new state of the
source, and its targets start the incoming patches. Hence the prescribed
records are exactly the successful-interaction skeleton. Conversely, if the
prescribed records are the complete successful-interaction skeleton, the
one-site processes~$X^P$ are the restrictions of the global active process and
every patch is consistent.

Taking~$f_P=1$ in~\eqref{eq:patch-factorization-mecke} gives
\begin{equation*}
\pP_A\bb{G_T\in dg}
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\pP_P\bb{\operatorname{Con}(P)}
\,m_T(dg).
\end{equation*}
Taking the Radon--Nikodym derivative with respect to this marginal yields, for
$\pP_A\circ G_T^{-1}$-almost every~$g$,
\begin{align*}
\E_A\Cb{
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P\bb{\Sigma_P}
\,\middle|\,G_T=g
}
&=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\frac{
\E_P\Cb{f_P\bb{\Sigma_P}\ind_{\operatorname{Con}(P)}}
}{
\pP_P\bb{\operatorname{Con}(P)}
}
\\
&=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\E_P^{\mathrm{con}}\Cb{f_P\bb{\Sigma_P}}.
\end{align*}
This proves~\eqref{eq:patch-factorization} for nonnegative factors and
identifies the conditional law. The integrable signed case follows from this
conditional-law statement.
\end{proof}
