\section{Patch laws and representation}
\label{sec:representation}

\subsection{Reference and consistent patch laws}
\label{subsec:patch-laws}

Fix a labeled patch~$P$, either a full patch in~$\mathcal P$ or a patch
in~$\mathcal P_T$ for some finite~$T$. For brevity, write
\begin{equation*}
i=i(P),
\qquad
s=s(P),
\qquad
e=e(P),
\qquad
S=S(P).
\end{equation*}
The interior interaction data of~$P$ are the marked interactions outgoing
from~$i$ strictly between its endpoints:
\begin{equation*}
\Sigma_P^\circ
=
\{(i,u,\gamma,R)\in I:s<u<e\}.
\end{equation*}
Under the reference law, the type-$(\delta,R)$ and type-$(\beta,R)$ points in
this interval are independent Poisson processes with the rates from
Section~\ref{subsec:signed-set-process}.

If~$\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf I$, the initial interaction of~$P$ is incoming from
another source, and we set~$\Sigma_P=\Sigma_P^\circ$. Let~$\mathbb P_P$ be the
corresponding product Poisson law. If~$\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf O$, the initial
interaction is outgoing from~$i$, with target~$S$, but its kind is not revealed
by the skeleton. Let~$\alpha(P)\in\{\delta,\beta\}$ be this hidden kind,
independent of~$\Sigma_P^\circ$, with
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-initial-kind-law}
\mathbb P_P(\alpha(P)=\delta)
=
\frac{\delta_i(S)}{\delta_i(S)+\beta_i(S)},
\qquad
\mathbb P_P(\alpha(P)=\beta)
=
\frac{\beta_i(S)}{\delta_i(S)+\beta_i(S)}.
\end{equation}
In this case,
\begin{equation*}
\Sigma_P
=
\Sigma_P^\circ\cup\{(i,s,\alpha(P),S)\},
\end{equation*}
and~$\mathbb P_P$ is the product of the law in
\eqref{eq:patch-initial-kind-law} and the interior Poisson law.
Write~$\mathbb E_P$ for expectation under~$\mathbb P_P$.

The local active indicator~$X^P$ is determined from~$\Sigma_P$. Its initial
value is
\begin{equation*}
X_s^P
=
\begin{cases}
1,&\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf I,\\
\ind\{\alpha(P)=\beta\},&\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf O.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
Reading~$\Sigma_P^\circ$ chronologically, a~$\delta$-mark sends an active
source to~$0$, a~$\beta$-mark leaves an active source at~$1$, and every mark at
an inactive source is ignored. Between marks,~$X^P$ is constant. For a finite
patch with terminal label~$\mathsf E$, we also set~$X_e^P=X_{e-}^P$.
{\color{red} Consistency with what? This comes out of nowhere to the reader.}
The consistency event has two parts. No interior nonempty-target interaction
may have an active source, since it would be an additional successful
interaction. If the terminal boundary is outgoing, its source must be active
immediately before the boundary. Formally,
{\color{red} This could use a text in set representation, "no successful interaction inside patch and if patch ends with outgoing interaction then site must be active}
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Con}(P)
={}&
\left\{
X_{u-}^P=0
\text{ for every }(i,u,\gamma,R)\in\Sigma_P^\circ
\text{ with }R\neq\varnothing
\right\}
\\
&\cap
\begin{cases}
\{X_{e-}^P=1\},&\mathsf Y(P)=\mathsf O,\\
\Omega_P,&\mathsf Y(P)\in\{\mathsf I,\mathsf E\},
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
where~$\Omega_P$ is the reference sample space. For every patch in a realized
patch family,~$\mathbb P_P(\operatorname{Con}(P))>0$ almost surely. The
consistent patch law is
\begin{equation*}
\mathbb P_P^{\mathrm{con}}(\cdot)
=
\mathbb P_P(\cdot\mid\operatorname{Con}(P)),
\end{equation*}
and~$\mathbb E_P^{\mathrm{con}}$ denotes its expectation.

\subsection{Conditional factorization}
\label{subsec:factorization}

The successful skeleton fixes the patch boundaries but leaves the local data
between them hidden. Conditioning on the complete skeleton imposes one local
consistency event on each disjoint source-time strip.

\begin{theorem}[Patch factorization]
\label{thm:patch-factorization}
Fix~$T<\infty$. Conditional on~$\mathcal G_T$, the interaction
data~$(\Sigma_P)_{P\in\mathcal P_T}$ are independent, and the conditional law
of~$\Sigma_P$ is~$\mathbb P_P^{\mathrm{con}}$. Equivalently, for bounded
measurable functions~$f_P$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-factorization}
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P(\Sigma_P)
\middle|\mathcal G_T
\right]
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}
\mathbb E_P^{\mathrm{con}}[f_P(\Sigma_P)].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}

The argument should be that conditioning on $\mathcal{G}_T$ is the same as conditioning on the random family $\mathcal{P}_T$ and the intersection of Cons(P). Then argument is that data inside each patch region was independent before conditioning, each consistency event is independent from the rest and each $\Sigma_P$ is independent from $Con(Q)$ for $Q\neq P$. The correct chain of equalities must be explicitly stated, maybe even in abstract form.}
Condition on a realization of~$(Y_0,\mathcal I_T)$. It fixes a finite
family~$\mathcal R$ of labeled patches and all their boundary locations. Before
requiring that these boundaries form the complete successful skeleton, the raw
data in distinct patches have law
\begin{equation*}
\bigotimes_{P\in\mathcal R}\mathbb P_P.
\end{equation*}
Their source-time interiors are disjoint, and the hidden kind at an outgoing
initial boundary has the rate-proportional law
\eqref{eq:patch-initial-kind-law}.

Relative to the fixed boundary data, the revealed skeleton is complete if and
only if
\begin{equation*}
\bigcap_{P\in\mathcal R}\operatorname{Con}(P)
\end{equation*}
occurs. The forward implication is immediate. For the reverse implication,
read the boundary interactions chronologically. At time zero, the incoming
patches reproduce the prescribed active set. Between consecutive boundary
times, the local indicators agree with the global active state, and interior
consistency excludes every unrecorded successful interaction. At an outgoing
boundary, terminal consistency makes the source active, so the boundary
interaction is successful; its hidden kind gives the prescribed post-boundary
source state, while its targets start the prescribed incoming patches.
Induction reconstructs the active process and the skeleton through time~$T$.

Both the reference law and the consistency event factor over~$\mathcal R$.
Conditioning therefore gives
\begin{equation*}
\bigotimes_{P\in\mathcal R}\mathbb P_P^{\mathrm{con}},
\end{equation*}
which proves~\eqref{eq:patch-factorization}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Patch contributions}
\label{subsec:patch-contributions}
{\color{red} Needs a sentence that this is for factorisation of the FK duality random variable to remind the reader since that was introduced way back.}
The sign assigned to the initial boundary of a patch is
\begin{equation*}
\sigma(P)
=
\begin{cases}
+,&\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf I,\\
\sigma_{i(P)}^{\alpha(P)}(S(P)),&\mathsf X(P)=\mathsf O.
\end{cases}
\end{equation*}
For a full patch~$P\in\mathcal P$, define
\begin{equation*}
F(P)
=
\sigma(P)
\exp\left(
V_{i(P)}\int_{s(P)}^{e(P)}X_u^P\,du
\right),
\qquad
C(P)=\mathbb E_P^{\mathrm{con}}[F(P)].
\end{equation*}
When~$e(P)=\infty$, the exponential is interpreted by its limit over finite
upper endpoints; the limit always exists. For an end patch~$Q\in\mathcal E_t$
and~$z\in[0,1]$, define
\begin{equation*}
F(z,Q)
=
\sigma(Q)
\exp\left(
V_{i(Q)}\int_{s(Q)}^{e(Q)}X_u^Q\,du
\right)
z^{X_{e(Q)}^Q},
\qquad
C(z,Q)=\mathbb E_Q^{\mathrm{con}}[F(z,Q)].
\end{equation*}
The function~$z\mapsto C(z,Q)$ is affine. The full and end contributions are
elementary one-site quantities; Appendix~\ref{app:contribution-formulas}
derives explicit formulas for every boundary type.

\subsection{The patch representation theorem}
\label{subsec:representation-theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Patch representation]
\label{thm:patch-representation}
For every~$A\Subset\Lambda$,~$t\geq0$, and~$\eta\in\Omega$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-representation}
P_t\chi_A(\eta)
=
\E_A\left[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal B_t}C(P)
\prod_{Q\in\mathcal E_t}C(\eta(i(Q)),Q)
\right].
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
On the global graphical space, let~$X^P$,~$\alpha(P)$, and~$\sigma(P)$ denote
the local data induced on each patch. Every nonempty-target successful
interaction contributes its sign to the unique patch which begins at its
source. Pure deaths have positive sign
because~$a_i^\delta(\varnothing)=c_i^0(\varnothing) = c_i(\mb{1}^{i})\geq0$. Hence
\begin{equation*}
\sigma_t
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal B_t}\sigma(P)
\prod_{Q\in\mathcal E_t}\sigma(Q).
\end{equation*}
{\color{red} There is no need to call bulk patches with P and end patches with Q, just call all of them P}
Since~$V(A_u)=\sum_{i\in A_u}V_i$, the patch intervals also split the
Feynman--Kac integral:
\begin{equation*}
\int_0^tV(A_u)\,du
=
\sum_{P\in\mathcal B_t}
V_{i(P)}\int_{s(P)}^{e(P)}X_u^P\,du
+
\sum_{Q\in\mathcal E_t}
V_{i(Q)}\int_{s(Q)}^{t}X_u^Q\,du.
\end{equation*}
The end patches record the state at time~$t$, so
\begin{equation*}
\chi_{A_t}(\eta)
=
\prod_{Q\in\mathcal E_t}
\eta(i(Q))^{X_{e(Q)}^Q}.
\end{equation*}
Combining these identities gives the pathwise factorization
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pathwise-patch-factorization}
\sigma_t
\exp\left(\int_0^tV(A_u)\,du\right)
\chi_{A_t}(\eta)
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal B_t}F(P)
\prod_{Q\in\mathcal E_t}F(\eta(i(Q)),Q).
\end{equation}
Conditioning~\eqref{eq:pathwise-patch-factorization} on~$\mathcal G_t$ and
applying Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-factorization} replaces each local factor by
its consistent patch expectation. Taking expectation and using
Theorem~\ref{thm:monomial-duality} proves~\eqref{eq:patch-representation}.
\end{proof}

\subsection{General initial laws}
\label{subsec:general-initial-laws}

Let~$\mu$ be any probability measure on~$\Omega$. Integrating
\eqref{eq:patch-representation} gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-representation-general-law}
\mu(P_t\chi_A)
=
\E_A\left[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal B_t}C(P)\,
\mu\left(
\prod_{Q\in\mathcal E_t}C(\eta(i(Q)),Q)
\right)
\right].
\end{equation}
In particular, for the Bernoulli product measure~$\mu_{\mathbf p}$ with
one-density profile~$\mathbf p=(p_i)_{i\in\Lambda}$, the end-patch sites are
distinct and the end contributions are affine, so
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:patch-representation-product-law}
\mu_{\mathbf p}(P_t\chi_A)
=
\E_A\left[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal B_t}C(P)
\prod_{Q\in\mathcal E_t}C(p_{i(Q)},Q)
\right].
\end{equation}
The skeleton retains the interactions which transmit dependence between sites.
Everything local between consecutive successful touches is integrated
inside~$C(P)$ or~$C(z,Q)$ before its sign is estimated. Thus
\eqref{eq:patch-representation} is exact, but groups the signed dual history in
a way that preserves the cancellations hidden by termwise absolute values.
