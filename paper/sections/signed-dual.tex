\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-representation}}
\label{sec:signed-dual}

To prove Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-representation}, we start from the usual
set-valued dual construction for spin systems. Its graphical construction records the interactions
through which dependence passes between sites. These interactions define the
patches. The process measure, conditional on their, record factorizes by patches and yields the representation.

\subsection{Signed monomial dual}
\label{subsec:signed-set-process}

Let~$\cb{\eta_t}_{t\geq 0}$ be the stochastic process given by the spin system generator~$\mathcal{L}$. For~$A\Subset\Lambda$,
\begin{equation*}
P_t\chi_A(\eta)
=
\E_\eta\Cb{\prod_{i\in A}\eta_t(i)}.
\end{equation*}
We represent the dependence of~$\eta_t$ on the initial configuration by a signed
set-valued process. The representation constructed below is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:monomial-feynman-kac-duality-preview}
P_t\chi_A(\eta)
=
\E_A\Cb{
\sigma_t
\exp\bb{\int_0^t V(A_s)\,ds}
\chi_{A_t}(\eta)
}.
\end{equation}
Here~$A_s$ traces the dependence backward from the sites in~$A$ at the
observation time,~$\sigma_s\in\cb{+,-}$ records the accumulated sign, and~$V$
is a Feynman--Kac potential. Set-valued duals of this kind are standard for spin
systems~\cite{Harris1978,Griffeath1979,Gray1986}. We now define the signed
process and the potential in~\eqref{eq:monomial-feynman-kac-duality-preview}.

% BLURB B. General signed set-valued process
%
% Reader question:

% Job:

%   the D and B updates and explain them plainly as death, split, and birth.
%   State that this is a convenient parametrization of the standard additive
%   set-valued dynamics used in spin-system duality, with appropriate references
%   to Harris, Griffeath, and Gray. Then give the generator Dcal.
%
% Important framing:
%   The rates in this blurb are generic parameters. They are not yet the rates
%   of the dual associated with the spin system Lcal. The exact death/split/birth
%   notation is chosen for this paper and should not be presented as standard
%   notation from the literature.
% ============================================================================

We use the following parametrization of a signed additive set-valued process. A
state is
\begin{equation*}
Y=(A,\sigma)
\in
\cb{A:A\Subset\Lambda}\times\cb{+,-},
\end{equation*}
where~$A$ is the active set and~$\sigma$ is the accumulated sign. We identify
$+$ and~$-$ with~$+1$ and~$-1$. For now, fix arbitrary rates and sign labels
\begin{equation*}
\delta_i(S)\geq0,
\qquad
\sigma_i^\delta(S)\in\cb{+,-},
\qquad
S\subseteq N(i),
\end{equation*}
and
\begin{equation*}
\beta_i(S)\geq0,
\qquad
\sigma_i^\beta(S)\in\cb{+,-},
\qquad
\varnothing\neq S\subseteq N(i).
\end{equation*}
We put~$\beta_i(\varnothing)=0$. For~$i\in A$, define
\begin{align*}
D_{i,S}(A,\sigma)
&=
\bb{\bb{A\setminus\cb{i}}\cup S,
\sigma\sigma_i^\delta(S)},
\\
B_{i,S}(A,\sigma)
&=
\bb{A\cup S,
\sigma\sigma_i^\beta(S)}.
\end{align*}
On the active set, these updates are
\begin{align*}
D_{i,\varnothing}:&\quad A\longmapsto A\setminus\cb{i}
&&\text{death},
\\
D_{i,S}:&\quad A\longmapsto \bb{A\setminus\cb{i}}\cup S
&&\text{split},\qquad S\neq\varnothing,
\\
B_{i,S}:&\quad A\longmapsto A\cup S
&&\text{birth},\qquad S\neq\varnothing.
\end{align*}
Ignoring the sign coordinate, the corresponding graphical update maps
preserve unions. This is the standard additivity property for set-valued
particle systems~\cite{Harris1978,Griffeath1979,Gray1986}. The death, split,
and birth notation is the parametrization we use for the patch construction.
The generator is
\begin{align*}
\Dcal g(A,\sigma)
={}&
\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}
\delta_i(S)
\bb{g\bb{D_{i,S}(A,\sigma)}-g(A,\sigma)}
\\
&+
\sum_{i\in A}
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq N(i)\\S\neq\varnothing}}
\beta_i(S)
\bb{g\bb{B_{i,S}(A,\sigma)}-g(A,\sigma)}.
\end{align*}
At this stage the rates and sign labels are arbitrary. We next choose them from
$\Lcal$.

% BLURB C. Generator action and the dual coefficients
%
% Reader question:
%   Why do the jumps above have the right form for the spin system, and which
%   coefficients determine them?
%
% Job:
%   State the expansion of Lcal chi_A, define the two coefficient families, and
%   identify the corresponding active-set changes. The main text should show the
%   reader the structure of the dual, not repeat the coefficient calculation.
%   Refer to the appendix for the derivation.
% ============================================================================
\label{subsec:rates-to-dual}
Expanding~$\Lcal\chi_A$ in the monomial basis gives
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:signed-dual-generator-expansion}
\Lcal\chi_A
=
\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}
a_i^\delta(S)\chi_{\bb{A\setminus\cb{i}}\cup S}
+
\sum_{i\in A}\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}
a_i^\beta(S)\chi_{A\cup S},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:signed-dual-coefficients}
a_i^\delta(S)=c_i^0(S),
\qquad
a_i^\beta(S)=-c_i^0(S)-c_i^1(S).
\end{equation}
Thus the first family of terms has the active-set change
\begin{equation*}
A\longmapsto \bb{A\setminus\cb{i}}\cup S,
\end{equation*}
and the second has
\begin{equation*}
A\longmapsto A\cup S.
\end{equation*}
These are precisely the~$D_{i,S}$ and~$B_{i,S}$ updates from above. For the
second family,~$S=\varnothing$ leaves~$A$ unchanged and is therefore a diagonal
term. The derivation of~\eqref{eq:signed-dual-generator-expansion} is given in
Appendix~\ref{app:generator-action}.

% BLURB D. Rates, signs, and the Feynman--Kac potential
%
% Reader question:

%   and where does the potential come from?
%
% Job:
%   Take absolute values of the nonzero-transition coefficients to obtain jump
%   rates and record their signs in the sign labels. Explain separately the
%   beta term with S=empty: it leaves A unchanged, so it is a diagonal term and
%   is not represented by a jump. Define V(A) and explain that it also restores
%   the diagonal rate terms introduced when the coefficient expansion is written

%   follow from the standing assumptions.

For~$r\in\R$, let~$\operatorname{sgn}_{\pm}(r)$ be~$+$ for~$r\geq0$ and~$-$
for~$r<0$. We realize each non-diagonal coefficient in
\eqref{eq:signed-dual-generator-expansion} as a jump rate together with a sign:
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:death-split-data}
\delta_i(S)=\abs{a_i^\delta(S)},
\qquad
\sigma_i^\delta(S)=\operatorname{sgn}_{\pm}\bb{a_i^\delta(S)},
\qquad S\subseteq N(i),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:birth-data}
\beta_i(S)=\abs{a_i^\beta(S)},
\qquad
\sigma_i^\beta(S)=\operatorname{sgn}_{\pm}\bb{a_i^\beta(S)},
\qquad \varnothing\neq S\subseteq N(i).
\end{equation}
Thus rate times sign recovers the corresponding coefficient. The remaining
coefficient~$a_i^\beta(\varnothing)$ is diagonal and does not correspond to any
jump. The Feynman--Kac potential is
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:dual-potential}
V(A)=\sum_{i\in A}V_i,
\qquad
V_i=
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}\delta_i(S)
+
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq N(i)\\S\neq\varnothing}}\beta_i(S)
+
a_i^\beta(\varnothing).
\end{equation}
The standing bounds on the neighbourhood sizes and flip rates imply
\begin{equation*}
\sup_{i\in\Lambda}
\bb{
\sum_{S\subseteq N(i)}\delta_i(S)
+
\sum_{\substack{S\subseteq N(i)\\S\neq\varnothing}}\beta_i(S)
+
\abs{V_i}
}
<\infty.
\end{equation*}
In particular, the dual has uniformly bounded jump rate per active site, and
each jump activates at most~$\sup_i\abs{N(i)}$ new sites.

\subsection{Graphical construction}
\label{subsec:dual-graphical-construction}

For every~$i\in\Lambda$ and~$S\subseteq N(i)$, let
$I_{i,S}^\delta\subseteq(0,\infty)$ be a Poisson point process of
rate~$\delta_i(S)$. For every nonempty~$S\subseteq N(i)$, let
$I_{i,S}^\beta\subseteq(0,\infty)$ be a Poisson point process of
rate~$\beta_i(S)$. All these processes are independent, and for notational
convenience we set~$I_{i,\varnothing}^\beta=\varnothing$. The marked Poisson
interaction set is
\begin{equation*}
I^{\mathrm P}
=
\cb{(i,t,\alpha,S):i\in\Lambda,\ \alpha\in\cb{\delta,\beta},\
S\subseteq N(i),\ t\in I_{i,S}^\alpha}.
\end{equation*}
For~$(i,t,\alpha,S)\in I^{\mathrm P}$, the site~$i$ is the \emph{source},
$S$ is the \emph{target}, and~$\alpha\in\cb{\delta,\beta}$ is the interaction
kind. Deaths have empty target; splits and births have nonempty target.


To simplify the later patch construction, set the initial
we start the process by the deterministic initial interaction
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:initial-interaction}
\iota_0
=
(\infty,0,\mathsf{init},A_0;\sigma_0),
\end{equation}
where~$\infty\notin\Lambda$ is a formal source. Its target is~$A_0$, its sign
is~$\sigma_0$, and it has no rate. The full interaction set is
\begin{equation*}
I=\cb{\iota_0}\cup I^{\mathrm P}.
\end{equation*}
Start from~$Y_{0-}=(\varnothing,+)$ and apply~$\iota_0$,
giving~$Y_0=(A_0,\sigma_0)$. Suppose the process has been constructed up to
time~$s$ and has active set~$A_s$. Among the Poisson clocks whose sources lie
in~$A_s$, take the first ring after time~$s$. At a
mark~$(i,t,\delta,S)$, apply~$D_{i,S}$; at a mark~$(i,t,\beta,S)$,
apply~$B_{i,S}$. Repeat from the new state, and keep the process constant between
these relevant interactions. Rings at inactive sources are ignored.

The total rate of relevant clocks is bounded by a constant times~$\abs{A_s}$,
and one interaction creates at most~$\sup_i\abs{N(i)}$ new active sites.
Thus~$\abs{\bigcup_{s\leq t} A_s}$ is dominated by a nonexplosive continuous-time branching
process. In particular, only finitely many relevant interactions occur on every
bounded time interval. We use the full interaction set~$I$ throughout the
patch construction.
