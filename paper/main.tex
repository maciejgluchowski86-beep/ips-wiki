\ABSTRACT{%
Kinetically constrained spin models have local constraints with a natural
monotone direction, but the resulting dynamics need not be monotone in the
usual configuration order. Motivated by this structure, we introduce
patch-positive spin systems and show that the semigroup of every such system
preserves a centered-moment order on probability laws for arbitrary initial
distributions. Our main tool is a patch representation obtained from the signed
monomial Feynman--Kac dual: after conditioning on a coarse record of the
interactions that transmit dependence between sites, the remaining graphical
randomness separates into one-site spacetime patches and is averaged locally.
We show that nonnegativity of these patch contributions is equivalent to
explicit inequalities for the multilinear coefficients of the flip-rate
function; the same inequalities imply the centered-moment monotonicity above.
On interaction graphs of polynomial growth, uniform
independent creation of facilitating states yields convergence, at rate
$O((1+t)^D e^{-\varepsilon t/2})$, from a class~$\mathcal M_-$ defined by
centered moments to a common invariant law. When the threshold profile is at
most~$1/2$, convergence is uniform over all initial configurations. FA-1f and
the biased annihilating branching process illustrate the criterion.}
