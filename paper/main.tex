\begin{abstract}
We introduce a patch representation for finite-range spin systems on
\(\{0,1\}^{\Lambda}\).  The ordinary monomial dual is a signed
set-valued process with a Feynman--Kac weight.  After revealing only its
successful nonempty-target interactions, the remaining one-site
histories decompose into conditionally independent spacetime patches.
Averaging over these histories gives an exact random-product
representation for evolved monomials.

A local coefficient condition, called patch positivity, makes every
bulk factor nonnegative and determines a critical one-density profile
\(\mathbf p^\star\) above which all end factors are nonnegative.  The
same condition induces a centered-moment order, preserves the
high-density cone \(\mathcal M_\star\), and yields comparison when an
environment-independent \(1\)-to-\(0\) component is removed.

On a polynomial-growth lattice, a patch-positive spin system with a
uniform \(1\)-to-\(0\) component converges, uniformly over a larger
centered-moment class \(\mathcal M_-\), to a common invariant measure.
For every local observable \(f\), the error is bounded by
\(K_f(1+t)^D e^{-\varepsilon t/2}\), where \(\varepsilon\) is the
uniform death rate.  We illustrate the theory with the FA-1f model and
the biased annihilating branching process.
\end{abstract}
