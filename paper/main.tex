\section*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}

Facilitated spin systems have a calm state and a state that can cause, permit,
or accelerate neighbouring updates. Such effects need not persist: later local
events may overwrite or cancel them before they are transmitted. We capture
this failure to accumulate through the natural signed monomial Feynman--Kac
dual. Interactions that transmit dependence partition spacetime into one-site
intervals, called patches; conditional on their boundaries, the hidden events
and signs can be averaged independently inside each patch. This yields an exact
representation in which consecutive interactions are integrated out before any
comparison is made. We characterize patch positivity, the nonnegativity of these
averaged contributions, by local inequalities between the multilinear
coefficients of the directional flip rates. The same coefficients determine a
critical calm-state density profile, and patch positivity implies that the
semigroup preserves an order defined by monomials centered at this profile. On
polynomial-growth lattices, an environment-independent~$1\to0$ component of
rate~$\varepsilon>0$ yields convergence, at rate
$(1+t)^D e^{-\varepsilon t/2}$, from an explicit class containing sufficiently
high-density product laws and configurations with finitely many facilitating
states to a common invariant law. Its moments admit a clean patch
representation. If the critical profile is at most~$1/2$, convergence is uniform
over all initial configurations. For FA-1f and the biased annihilating branching
process, the critical profile equals the equilibrium one-density, and we
characterize the independent refresh perturbations preserving patch positivity.
For the latter, centered-moment comparison extends known product-law convergence
to dependent and non-translation-invariant initial laws.

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

\subsection{Facilitation}
\label{subsec:intro-calm-facilitating}
\label{subsec:intro-loss-of-memory}

Some \hyperref[def:spin-system]{spin systems} distinguish calm states from
states which facilitate updates at neighbouring sites. A calm state does not
by itself make another site update, whereas a facilitating state can cause,
permit, or accelerate an update. This distinction appears under different
model-specific names. In the contact process~\cite{Harris1974}, an infected site
infects neighbouring sites and may recover. In
\hyperref[subsubsec:fa-1f-model]{FA-1f}~\cite{BlondelEtAl2013}, a vacancy
permits a neighbouring site to refresh according to an independent Bernoulli
draw, but does not determine the outcome of the refresh. In the
\hyperref[subsubsec:babp-model]{biased annihilating branching process}
(BABP)~\cite{NeuhauserSudbury1993}, a particle causes a neighbouring site to
flip, resulting in branching or annihilation.

A region containing only calm states has no internal source of activity and
tends to remain calm until a facilitating state reaches it. Facilitating states
cause updates, some of which leave further facilitating states behind. This
does not make the dynamics monotone: a refresh or flip can create or remove a
facilitating state, so ordered configurations need not remain ordered. It
nevertheless suggests that some part of the calm--facilitating order may
survive for selected observables.

Suppose now that independent noise creates facilitating states throughout the
lattice. Since these events do not depend on the initial configuration, they
may interrupt the routes through which old information reaches a later local
observation. We ask whether the process, after adding such noise, loses memory
of its initial state and whether the calm--facilitating tendency yields a
useful comparison when attractiveness is absent. Neither monotone coupling nor
reversibility answers these questions in the class considered here. The
contact process is attractive, but FA-1f and BABP need not preserve the order
of two coupled configurations. We also allow nonreversible dynamics, since the
added noise need not preserve a reversible measure of the unperturbed system.
The argument must use the graphical history without ordering trajectories or
identifying the limiting law in advance.

\subsection{Persistence}
\label{subsec:intro-temporal-cancellation}

A facilitating interaction can change a flip rate from zero to a quantity of
order one, and may therefore decide whether a site is allowed to flip. Its
effect can nevertheless disappear before it is transmitted. If no information
leaves a site between two local updates, a second birth is redundant, a later
refresh overwrites the earlier refresh, and two flips cancel. The relevant
quantity is therefore not the instantaneous size of an interaction, but
whether its effect is transmitted before another local update removes it.

The natural \wiki{duality}{Feynman--Kac duality} records the transmission of
influence through a signed set-valued process. Its signs retain cancellations
among successive local events. Taking absolute values before those events are
grouped discards the cancellations and gives only large bounds controlled by
the full interaction rates, even when little influence persists. The dual
history must therefore be decomposed according to the interactions which
transmit information between sites.

\subsection{Patches}
\label{subsec:intro-patches}

Starting from the Feynman--Kac dual of a local observation, we reveal only the
times, sources, and target sets of those dual interactions which transmit
dependence between sites. Their records form the
\wiki{successful-interaction}{successful-interaction skeleton}. The split or
birth kind at a successful interaction, all deaths, and all rings at inactive
sources remain hidden. The one-site intervals between consecutive incoming or
outgoing successful interactions are the \wiki{patch}{patches}. Conditional on
the skeleton, the randomness hidden in distinct patches becomes independent.
The signed Feynman--Kac factors can therefore be averaged patch by patch, and
the resulting contributions give an exact
\wiki{patch-representation-of-spin-systems}{representation} of the original
spin system. This averaging retains sign cancellations between successive
interactions.

We say that a spin system has \emph{patch positivity} when every bulk patch
contribution is nonnegative. This condition is characterized by explicit
inequalities between the multilinear coefficients of the flip rates. It does
not imply stochastic order, but it yields comparisons for centered moments,
and the semigroup preserves the resulting moment order. The same
representation compares a system with the system obtained by removing an
independent pure-death component. When such deaths occur at a uniformly
positive rate, local observables converge exponentially to a common invariant
limit, which can be described by a patch formula.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation}
\label{subsec:meaning-of-patch-positivity}

Patch positivity is a condition on the accumulated effect of a one-site period
of influence, rather than on the sign or size of an instantaneous interaction.
The raw Feynman--Kac dual may attach alternating signs to successive local
events. Averaging all hidden events between two successful interactions first
produces the patch contribution~$C(P)$, and patch positivity asks that this
effective contribution be nonnegative. Although its definition refers to a
spacetime decomposition, Theorem~\ref{thm:patch-positivity-criterion} reduces it
to local inequalities between the multilinear coefficients of the flip rates.

Patch positivity is neither attractiveness nor positivity of all coefficients
in the signed dual. When~$\mb p^\star=\mb 0$, the centered monomials are the
ordinary monomials and~$\preceq_*$ becomes the upper-orthant, or
monomial-moment, order. Stochastic order implies this order, but the converse
fails in general. Thus, for the contact process, attractiveness gives the
stronger comparison for all increasing observables, while
Theorem~\ref{thm:centered-moment-order} extends its monomial consequences to
pairs of initial laws which need not be stochastically ordered. The main use of
patch positivity is for systems such as FA-1f and BABP, where the configuration
order is not preserved.

\subsection{Density threshold}
\label{subsec:density-threshold}

The patch critical profile~$\mb p^\star$ is the smallest terminal calm-state
profile for which every end-patch contribution is nonnegative. It is determined
by the signs of the end factors and is not an ergodicity threshold or a phase
transition parameter. The lower profile
$\mb p^-=(\max\cb{2p_i^\star-1,0})_{i\in\Lambda}$ enters only when the positive
estimate on~$\mathcal M_*$ is extended by the affine decomposition defining
$\mathcal M_-$. In particular, these profiles describe where the present sign
argument applies, not where the underlying dynamics is expected to change
qualitatively.

The restriction to~$\mathcal M_-$ comes entirely from the centered expansion of
the end factors. Outside this class some centered terms are negative, so the
proof can no longer compare each successful-interaction skeleton with a
positive envelope. This is not a dynamical obstruction to convergence. The
terms discarded by the positivity argument carry additional cancellations and
may provide further decay. Proving convergence from arbitrary laws requires a
way to retain those cancellations instead of taking termwise absolute values.
When~$\mb p^\star\leq\mbs{\tfrac12}$, every probability measure belongs
to~$\mathcal M_{-,1}$ and this issue disappears. For the refresh perturbations
of FA-1f and BABP,~$p^\star=p$, so once a uniform pure-death component is
present, the theorem covers all initial laws when the facilitating-state
density~$q=1-p$ is at least~$1/2$.

\subsection{Uniform deaths}
\label{subsec:removing-noise}

The uniform pure-death component is used in two separate estimates. Along a
backward outgoing trail it contributes a survival factor which suppresses
histories with a successful interaction after the cut time. On the
complementary event it contracts the centered slopes of the surviving end
patches, erasing their dependence on the terminal configuration. The rest of
the proof uses positivity, spatial confinement, and finite propagation. Thus
the same argument would apply whenever model-specific estimates replace
Lemmas~\ref{lem:late-interactions} and~\ref{lem:no-late-relaxation} by errors
which vanish as the cut time and continuation time increase. Removing the
uniform component is the main step needed to apply the method directly to the
hard models.

The full-patch formula can sometimes identify the invariant law exactly.
Suppose that every infinite patch of type~$\mathsf{OE}$ has zero contribution.
On~$\cb{\abs{\mathcal P}<\infty}$, any ordinary successful interaction has a
last occurrence, and the source patch beginning at that interaction is an
infinite~$\mathsf{OE}$ patch. Hence every history containing an ordinary
successful interaction has zero weight. The remaining no-interaction term is
the dual representation of the independent spin system with directional rates
$c_i^0(\vn)$ and~$c_i^1(\vn)$. The invariant law is therefore the Bernoulli
product measure with one-density
$c_i^0(\vn)/(c_i^0(\vn)+c_i^1(\vn))$ at site~$i$. This recovers~$\mu_p$ for the
equilibrium-refresh perturbations of FA-1f and BABP. Without this vanishing
condition, the formula still carries patchwise comparisons to the monomial
moments of the invariant laws, as in
Corollary~\ref{cor:pure-death-invariant-comparison}.

\subsection{Open problems}
\label{subsec:future-directions}

The first problem is convergence from arbitrary initial laws when
$\mb p^\star\nleq\mbs{\tfrac12}$. The proof fails only because the signs of the
end-patch expansion are no longer controlled. A satisfactory extension should
estimate the resulting cancellations uniformly over the
successful-interaction skeleton rather than replace them by absolute values.

The second problem is convergence from finitely many facilitating states in
the models without uniform deaths. This includes FA-1f started from finitely many vacancies and
BABP started from a finite nonempty particle set. Such point masses are not
placed between useful product laws by the centered-moment order, and the hard
dynamics has no uniform pure-death component. These problems therefore require
model-specific control of both late dual interactions and the terminal
end-patch dependence.
