# Literature record

## Precise target

State the exact theorem, problem, or gap relevant to the programme.

## Open-problem evidence

Record good published sources that explicitly describe the target or a sufficiently close gap as open, when such sources exist. Include exact page, theorem, remark, problem, or section locations when important.

Absence of such a source is not disqualifying. In that case explain the evidence that the target is still new and worthwhile.

## Closest known results

For each important predecessor, record the theorem, its hypotheses, and the precise difference from the intended project result.

## Successor and priority check

Record later papers, preprints, citation chains, and work by the same authors checked for a solution or stronger result.

## Alternate terminology

Record search terms and neighboring formulations that could hide prior art.

## Overlap risks

List unresolved novelty or attribution questions that could materially change the programme.

## Load-bearing external results

Record exact sources and hypotheses for external theorems used in the proof.
