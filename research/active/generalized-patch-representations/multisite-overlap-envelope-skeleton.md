# Overlapping multi-site flips: fatal or expensive?

Date: 2026-08-19

This note answers one question left by `multisite-overlapping-kawasaki-first-obstruction.md`: whether the geometry/sign conflict is fatal to the patch mechanism, or whether a richer successful record can restore it without reducing to the raw absolute bound.

## Decision

The obstruction is **expensive, not fatal**, provided one distinguishes two notions of geometry.

- If the skeleton reveals the **actual post-event active support**, then for the canonical monomial dual it also reveals the sign-bearing hidden branch. There is no nontrivial patch cancellation left at that selected event.
- If the skeleton reveals only a deterministic **boundary envelope** containing every site whose local dual state may change at the event, then the patch boundaries are fixed while the signed branch remains hidden. The paper's information split survives, but the local patch is now a multi-site hyperpatch.

Thus the correct extension does not reveal which endpoint actually carries the dual line after a Kawasaki exchange. It places a boundary on both endpoints and hides the routing/state update inside the hyperpatch.

## 1. Why actual post-support revelation kills the hidden sign

For the general multi-site flip algebra, fix a current dual support `B`, physical update block `A`, and

\[
D=A\cap B.
\]

For a rate mode `S` with `S cap D=emptyset`, the signed branches are

\[
B\longmapsto B'_J=(B\setminus D)\cup J\cup S,
\qquad J\subseteq D,
\]

with coefficient

\[
c_A(S)\theta_D(J),
\]

where

\[
\theta_D(J)=(-1)^{|J|}\quad(J\ne D),
\qquad
\theta_D(D)=(-1)^{|D|}-1.
\]

Suppose the successful record already contains `A,D,S` and is enlarged enough to reveal the actual post-event support `B'_J` on the source block. Then

\[
J=B'_J\cap D.
\]

Hence the supposedly hidden branch is measurable from the enlarged record. Its sign is therefore also measurable.

More generally, after equal resulting monomials have been combined canonically, two algebraic branches with the same actual post-support contribute to the same monomial coefficient. Any further decomposition of that coefficient into positive and negative submarks is artificial: averaging those submarks merely reconstructs the already-combined coefficient and yields no additional patch cancellation.

So an **actual-state geometry record** collapses the selected-event cancellation to the canonical signed-dual absolute bound.

For the Kawasaki test mode

\[
+\lambda\chi_{(B-x+y)\cup\{k\}}
-\lambda\chi_{B\cup\{k\}},
\]

revealing whether the line continues at `x` or moves to `y` identifies the `+/-` branch exactly. There is no second sign-carrying mark left to hide.

## 2. The paper needs boundary geometry, not post-event activity

The preceding conclusion is not fatal because the current paper's patches do not require the skeleton to determine the active state immediately after every boundary.

At a single-site successful record, the skeleton puts an outgoing boundary at the source and incoming boundaries at the target sites while hiding split versus birth. The hidden kind determines whether the source is active immediately after the record. Thus even in the original paper the boundary geometry is exposed while part of the post-boundary local state is hidden.

The same principle extends to a multi-site update.

For a coarse record `(A,t,D,S)`, define its **boundary envelope** to be

\[
E(A,S)=A\cup S.
\]

At time `t`, put a boundary on every site in this envelope, regardless of the hidden `J`. The record therefore fixes the spacetime cuts but does not assert which source sites are active after the event.

The hidden mark is still

\[
J\subseteq D,
\]

with its signed law proportional to

\[
|c_A(S)\theta_D(J)|.
\]

Thus geometry and sign are separated exactly as desired:

\[
\boxed{\text{record the envelope, hide the post-source state}.}
\]

For the Kawasaki edge mode, the envelope contains both endpoints `x,y` (and the environmental target `k`). Both endpoint lines are cut at the selected record. The hidden routing bit says whether the dual activity stays at `x` or moves to `y`, and it still carries the `+/-` sign.

## 3. Why this is not the trivial absolute bound

Let the hidden outcomes at one selected envelope record be indexed by `u`, with signed coefficients `a_u`, and let `F_u` be the downstream hyperpatch factor generated by outcome `u` before the next exposed envelope boundaries.

Raw absolute FK gives the local estimate

\[
\sum_u |a_u|\,|F_u|.
\]

The envelope patch instead averages the hidden outcome first and produces

\[
\left|\sum_u a_u F_u\right|.
\]

These differ whenever different signed outcomes are not distinguished by the exposed envelope before further local evolution. For the Kawasaki two-branch mode this is

\[
\lambda|F_{x\to y}-F_{x\to x}|
\le
\lambda\bigl(|F_{x\to y}|+|F_{x\to x}|\bigr),
\]

with strict inequality whenever the two downstream factors have the same nonzero sign and are not maximally separated.

Therefore the hidden mark retains genuine cancellation. The repair is not a disguised absolute-value construction.

## 4. What changes downstream: hyperpatches are unavoidable

The price is that one selected event can initialize several site strips with a **joint** hidden state. In Kawasaki, the hidden routing bit simultaneously determines the post-boundary activity at both edge endpoints. Those strips therefore cannot be averaged independently.

Moreover, with overlapping update blocks, an omitted clock on an edge/block `A` tests the active pattern on all sites of `A`. Hence the event that the coarse skeleton is complete is not a product of one-site consistency conditions.

The natural object is therefore the hyperpatch already identified in `multisite-flip-extension.md`:

1. cut every site line at every exposed envelope boundary;
2. connect atomic strips whenever a selected hidden mark jointly initializes them;
3. also connect strips whenever an omitted physical clock family can test/update their states jointly during an overlapping time interval;
4. take connected components.

Crucially, this component geometry is defined from the coarse records, physical update supports and time intervals, not from the hidden post-source outcome. Hence it is measurable with respect to the coarse skeleton.

Inside one component, reconstruct the full subset-valued candidate dual state, all hidden branch marks and all omitted clocks. The consistency event says that selected records have the prescribed pre-source patterns and that there are no additional successful nonempty-target records.

Distinct components use disjoint Poisson restrictions and disjoint selected hidden marks. Therefore the same Mecke/Radon--Nikodym architecture as the paper should apply **componentwise**: conditional on the envelope skeleton, hyperpatch variables factor under their consistent laws. The pathwise sign and FK potential also factor over the same components because every physical clock/potential term belongs to one update block and every block has been assigned to the component containing all of its contemporaneous strips.

This is a proof architecture, not yet a line-by-line theorem proof. The important point is that no new conceptual obstruction appears after replacing actual-state geometry by envelope geometry; what remains is bookkeeping and component-local finite-state dynamics.

## 5. The class for which this works

The envelope construction applies to bounded finite-range multi-site flip systems satisfying the usual local-finiteness hypotheses:

- uniformly bounded update-block size `|A|`;
- uniformly bounded dependence range / target size;
- uniformly bounded number or total rate of update blocks incident to one site;
- local rates admitting the finite multilinear expansion used by the monomial dual.

The update blocks may overlap arbitrarily. Overlap enlarges hyperpatches but does not invalidate the information split.

Important standard architectures in this class include:

- finite-range Kawasaki / speed-change exclusion dynamics, where nearest-neighbour or finite-range exchange blocks overlap at their endpoints and the exchange rate depends on a local environment;
- finite-range pair-flip spin systems;
- plaquette or other bounded-cluster flip dynamics with overlapping plaquettes/clusters;
- mixtures of single-site and bounded multi-site flips.

Pure SSEP also belongs formally, but its monomial dual is already positive and therefore does not use the signed cancellation mechanism.

The construction is most useful when a selected physical mode has at least two hidden post-source outcomes of opposite sign which share the same deterministic envelope. The canonical multi-site flip expansion has exactly this structure whenever `|D|>=1` and the relevant coefficient does not collapse to the `S cap D != emptyset` single-branch case.

## 6. Boundary of the conclusion

What survives at this stage is the **representation architecture**:

\[
\text{signed monomial dual}
\to
\text{envelope successful skeleton}
\to
\text{multi-site hyperpatches}
\to
\text{conditional hidden-mark averaging}.
\]

This does not restore the paper's simple one-site positivity criterion, scalar threshold profile, pure-death comparison or convergence theorem. Those remain separate questions.

The precise ruling on the previous Kawasaki obstruction is therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{not fatal, but the minimal one-site patch geometry is lost}.}
\]

Revealing actual post-event routing is the wrong repair because it reveals the sign. Exposing a deterministic interaction envelope is the right repair because it fixes the boundaries while retaining the sign-bearing hidden state.
