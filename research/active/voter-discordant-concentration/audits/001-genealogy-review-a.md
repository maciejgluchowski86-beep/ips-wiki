# Independent audit 001: genealogical variance theorem, review A

Date: 2026-08-16

I reviewed the claim as a fresh correctness audit. I did not use the Professor's conclusion as a premise. I reconstructed the deterministic inequality from the Harris construction, then checked the interface with Avena--Baldasso--Hazra--den Hollander--Quattropani (2024), *Discordant edges for the voter model on regular random graphs*, ALEA 21, 431--464, especially (1.9), (2.1), and (5.5)--(5.8).

## 1. Claim reviewed

Let `G=(V,E)` be a finite simple `d`-regular graph, `|V|=n`, `m=dn/2`, with `d>=1`. Start the rate-one continuous-time voter model from i.i.d. Bernoulli(`u`) opinions, `u in (0,1)`, and write

$$
\mathcal D_t=\frac{D_t}{m}
$$

for the discordant-edge density. If `pi` is uniform on `V` and `tau_meet` is the meeting time of two independent rate-one continuous-time simple random walks, the claimed deterministic inequality is

$$
\operatorname{Var}_u^G(\mathcal D_t)
\le 2q_t,
\qquad
q_t:=\mathbf P_{\pi\otimes\pi}^G(\tau_{\rm meet}\le t).
\tag{1.1}
$$

The random-regular consequence is the variance bound

$$
\operatorname{Var}_u^G(\mathcal D_{t_n}^n)
=O_{\mathbb P}\left(\frac{1+t_n}{n}\right)
\tag{1.2}
$$

for deterministic `t_n=o(n)`, with the sharper `O_P(t_n/n)` when `t_n>=1`.

I find (1.1)--(1.2) correct, subject only to the harmless convention `d>=1` so that the random walk is defined. Connectedness is not needed for (1.1).

## 2. Conditioning on the Harris genealogy

Let `H_t` be the sigma-field generated by all Harris arrows in `[0,t]`, excluding the initial opinions. On a finite graph there are almost surely finitely many arrows in this slab, so the ancestor map

$$
a_t:V\to V
$$

obtained by following arrows backward from `(x,t)` to time zero is an `H_t`-measurable finite-valued map. The initial labels `(xi_v)_{v\in V}` are independent of `H_t` and i.i.d. Bernoulli(`u`). Hence, exactly,

$$
\eta_t(x)=\xi_{a_t(x)}.
\tag{2.1}
$$

For `v in V`, let

$$
C_v=\{x:a_t(x)=v\}.
$$

The nonempty `C_v` form a partition of `V`, and distinct nonempty clusters carry distinct initial labels. Conditional on `H_t`, those labels are therefore independent Bernoulli(`u`). Product Bernoulli initial law is used essentially here; the conditional-label independence would be false for a general correlated initial measure.

For distinct ancestors `v,w`, let `N_vw` be the number of original graph edges with one endpoint in `C_v` and the other in `C_w`, and let

$$
J_t=\#\{\{x,y\}\in E:a_t(x)=a_t(y)\}.
$$

Then

$$
D_t=\sum_{v<w}N_{vw}\mathbf 1_{\{\xi_v\ne\xi_w\}},
\qquad
\sum_{v<w}N_{vw}=m-J_t.
\tag{2.2}
$$

With

$$
p=2u(1-u)\le\frac12,
$$

this gives the exact conditional mean

$$
\mathbf E[\mathcal D_t\mid H_t]
=p\left(1-\frac{J_t}{m}\right).
\tag{2.3}
$$

I find no measurability or independence gap in this step.

## 3. Conditional cut variance

For an unordered pair `r={v,w}` write

$$
I_r=\mathbf1_{\{\xi_v\ne\xi_w\}},
\qquad N_r=N_{vw}.
$$

If `r` and `s` are disjoint, then `I_r` and `I_s` depend on disjoint initial labels and are independent. If they intersect, then trivially

$$
|\operatorname{Cov}(I_r,I_s)|\le\frac14.
$$

For completeness, if `r={v,w}` and `s={v,z}` with `w\ne z`, writing `a=u(1-u)`, one has

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(I_{vw},I_{vz})
=a(1-4a)=a(2u-1)^2\ge0,
$$

while `Var(I_vw)=2a(1-2a)<=1/4`.

Therefore

$$
\operatorname{Var}(D_t\mid H_t)
\le\frac14
\sum_{r,s:r\cap s\ne\varnothing}N_rN_s.
\tag{3.1}
$$

For each ancestor `v`, put

$$
S_v=\sum_{w\ne v}N_{vw}.
$$

For distinct quotient edges sharing a vertex, the ordered pair is counted exactly once in `sum_v S_v^2`; a diagonal pair `(r,r)` is counted twice. Hence

$$
\sum_{r,s:r\cap s\ne\varnothing}N_rN_s
\le \sum_v S_v^2.
\tag{3.2}
$$

`S_v` is the number of original edges leaving `C_v`. Since `G` is `d`-regular,

$$
S_v\le d|C_v|.
\tag{3.3}
$$

Using `m=dn/2`, (3.1)--(3.3) give

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal D_t\mid H_t)
\le \frac1{n^2}\sum_v|C_v|^2.
\tag{3.4}
$$

There is no omitted covariance term: disjoint quotient edges are conditionally independent, and every non-disjoint ordered pair is included in (3.1).

Now

$$
\sum_v|C_v|^2
=\sum_{x,y\in V}\mathbf1_{\{a_t(x)=a_t(y)\}}.
\tag{3.5}
$$

For fixed `x,y`, the two backward Harris lineages are independent rate-one simple random walks until their first meeting, after which they coincide. Thus

$$
\mathbf P(a_t(x)=a_t(y))
=\mathbf P_{x,y}(\tau_{\rm meet}\le t).
\tag{3.6}
$$

This includes `x=y`: the source defines `tau_meet=inf{s>=0:X_s=Y_s}`, so two walks started at the same vertex meet at time zero. Averaging `(x,y)` under `pi tensor pi` yields the exact identity

$$
\frac1{n^2}\mathbf E\sum_v|C_v|^2=q_t.
\tag{3.7}
$$

Consequently

$$
\mathbf E\big[\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal D_t\mid H_t)\big]
\le q_t.
\tag{3.8}
$$

This part of the proof is correct with the claimed constant.

## 4. Conditional-mean variance and the four-walk join

By (2.3),

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf E[\mathcal D_t\mid H_t])
=\frac{p^2}{m^2}\operatorname{Var}(J_t).
\tag{4.1}
$$

Write

$$
Y_e=\mathbf1_{\{a_t(e^-)=a_t(e^+)\}},
\qquad
J_t=\sum_{e\in E}Y_e.
$$

The load-bearing issue is whether `Cov(Y_e,Y_f)` can be bounded by the cross-meeting event of four *independent* walks used in source (5.5), despite within-family coalescence. It can.

Fix oriented versions `e=(x_1,x_2)` and `f=(x_3,x_4)`. Take four independent rate-one simple-random-walk paths `X^1,...,X^4` from these starting points. From these raw paths construct a coalescing system by the usual identification rule: whenever two active paths meet, retain one of the two raw paths as the common future and retire the other. This has the standard coalescing-random-walk law. Construct on the same raw paths two isolated coalescing pairs, one using only `1,2` and the other only `3,4`.

Let

$$
H_{e,f}=\left\{\min_{i\in\{1,2\},\,j\in\{3,4\}}
\tau_{\rm meet}^{i,j}\le t\right\},
\tag{4.2}
$$

where the meeting times are those of the four raw independent paths. This is the source event in (5.5). On `H_{e,f}^c`, no raw path from the first family ever meets a raw path from the second family before time `t`; therefore the full four-lineage coalescing construction restricted to each family is exactly the corresponding isolated pair construction through time `t`.

Let `A_e,A_f` be the isolated-pair meeting indicators. Then `A_e,A_f` are independent. By consistency of coalescing random walks, `Y_e` and `A_e` have the same marginal law, and similarly for `f`. Moreover,

$$
Y_eY_f=A_eA_f
\qquad\text{on }H_{e,f}^c.
$$

Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\operatorname{Cov}(Y_e,Y_f)|
&=|\mathbf E[Y_eY_f]-\mathbf E[A_eA_f]|\\
&\le \mathbf P(H_{e,f}).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4.3}
$$

This also handles `e=f` or edges sharing an endpoint: under the source convention the cross-family interaction time is then zero, so the right-hand side of (4.3) is one and the bound is trivial.

This proves specifically that within-family coalescence introduces no additional error term. The source event may be larger than the first *active* cross-family collision because it also tracks retired raw paths, but that only helps the upper bound.

## 5. Edge averaging and the constant `4`

A uniform unoriented edge followed by an independent fair orientation has law

$$
\nu(x,y)=\pi(x)\frac1d\mathbf1_{\{x\sim y\}},
$$

because every oriented edge has probability `1/(2m)=1/(dn)`. The event `H_{e,f}` is invariant under reversing either orientation. Therefore averaging (4.3) over ordered pairs of uniform unoriented edges gives

$$
\frac1{m^2}\operatorname{Var}(J_t)
\le \mathbf P_{\nu\otimes\nu}(H_{e,f}).
\tag{5.1}
$$

This step uses `Var(J_t)=sum_{e,f}Cov(Y_e,Y_f)` and then `Cov<=|Cov|`; negative covariances, if any, are harmless.

The source's (5.6) is also immediate to rederive. Under `nu tensor nu`, each endpoint of `e` and each endpoint of `f` has marginal `pi`, and the two sampled edges are independent. For each of the four cross pairs, the two starting vertices are therefore independent with law `pi tensor pi`. By a union bound,

$$
\mathbf P_{\nu\otimes\nu}(H_{e,f})
\le4q_t.
\tag{5.2}
$$

Combining (4.1), (5.1), and (5.2),

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\mathbf E[\mathcal D_t\mid H_t])
\le4p^2q_t\le q_t,
\tag{5.3}
$$

because `p=2u(1-u)<=1/2`.

The constant in this part is therefore correct.

## 6. Deterministic inequality

The law of total variance and (3.8), (5.3) give

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{Var}_u^G(\mathcal D_t)
\le2q_t.
}
\tag{6.1}
$$

I find no missing cross term: total variance is an exact orthogonal decomposition into the two terms already bounded.

The proof does not require `G` to be connected. For a disconnected regular graph, uniform `pi` is still stationary, lineages in different components never meet, and (3.6)--(6.1) remain valid with `tau_meet=+infinity` on those trajectories. The only trivial boundary correction is that `d=0` must be excluded if one wants to speak of simple random walk.

At `t=0`, `q_0=1/n`. The proof remains valid without a limiting argument, because `a_0(x)=x` and the diagonal contribution in (3.7) is exactly `n/n^2`.

## 7. Published meeting estimate: convention, normalization, hypotheses

I obtained the published ALEA source. Its voter model is exactly the convention used here: every vertex updates at rate one and copies a uniformly selected neighbour. Its (2.1) defines `tau_meet` for two independent rate-one continuous-time simple random walks, with meeting at time zero when the initial vertices coincide.

In Section 5, the source samples oriented edges with exactly the law `nu` above. Equation (5.5) is the minimum of the four cross meeting times of four independent endpoint walks, and (5.6) is exactly the union bound (5.2).

The source next writes, for `t=o(n)`,

$$
q_t=O(t/n)
\tag{7.1}
$$

with high probability in (5.7). Literally, (7.1) cannot hold uniformly into `t=0`, because `q_0=1/n`. This is a wording defect in the source, not in the audited theorem. The correct estimate follows directly from the source's (5.8): on a good random-regular environment,

$$
\left|q_t-\frac{t}{\mathbf E\tau_{\rm meet}}\right|
\le
\left(\frac{t}{\mathbf E\tau_{\rm meet}}\right)^2
+\frac1{\lambda_*\mathbf E\tau_{\rm meet}},
\qquad
0\le t\le\mathbf E\tau_{\rm meet}.
\tag{7.2}
$$

For fixed `d>=3`, the same paper proves/uses with high environment probability

$$
\mathbf E\tau_{\rm meet}=\Theta(n)
$$

(in fact its stationary mean meeting time divided by `n` converges to a positive constant) and that the relevant product-chain spectral gap `lambda_*` is bounded away from zero. The graph is connected with high probability; on this event the product chain is irreducible and reversible with stationary law `pi tensor pi`, so the hypotheses of the Aldous--Brown estimate quoted as (5.8) apply.

Thus for every deterministic `t_n=o(n)`, eventually `t_n<=E tau_meet` on the same high-probability graph event, and (7.2) gives

$$
q_{t_n}=O_{\mathbb P}\left(\frac{1+t_n}{n}\right).
\tag{7.3}
$$

If `t_n>=1`, this becomes

$$
q_{t_n}=O_{\mathbb P}(t_n/n).
\tag{7.4}
$$

Equivalently, for `0<=t<=1`, monotonicity gives `q_t<=q_1=O_P(1/n)`. There is no rate normalization mismatch between the deterministic theorem and the published input.

## 8. Chebyshev and probability mode

Let

$$
a_n=\frac{1+t_n}{n},
\qquad
V_n=\operatorname{Var}_u^G(\mathcal D_{t_n}^n).
$$

By (6.1) and (7.3), `V_n/a_n=O_P(1)` with respect to the random graph. For the quenched voter probability

$$
R_n(G)=\mathbf P_u^G\left(
|\mathcal D_{t_n}^n-\mathbf E_u^G\mathcal D_{t_n}^n|
>C_n\sqrt{a_n}
\right),
$$

Chebyshev gives pointwise in the environment

$$
R_n(G)\le\frac{V_n}{C_n^2a_n}.
$$

Since an `O_P(1)` random variable divided by `C_n^2->infinity` converges to zero in probability,

$$
R_n\xrightarrow{\mathbb P}0.
\tag{8.1}
$$

This is exactly the claimed sequence-wise quenched-in-environment-probability conclusion. No uniform-in-time assertion is needed.

For `t_n>=1`, use (7.4) and the same argument with `a_n=t_n/n` to recover the source scale `C_n sqrt(t_n/n)`.

## 9. Small-time correction of source (1.9)

The published (1.9) quantifies over every sequence with `t_n/n->0` and every `C_n->infinity`; it has no lower bound on `t_n`. The positive-time counterexample recorded by Student D is correct.

At time zero, let `I_e` be the discordance indicator of edge `e`, put `a=u(1-u)`, and write `D_0=sum_e I_e`. Disjoint edge indicators are independent, while for two distinct edges sharing one endpoint

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(I_e,I_f)=a(1-4a).
$$

Counting edges and adjacent edge pairs gives, on every simple `d`-regular graph,

$$
\operatorname{Var}(D_0)
=nd\,a\,[d-(4d-2)a],
\tag{9.1}
$$

and hence

$$
\operatorname{Var}(\mathcal D_0)
=\frac{4a[d-(4d-2)a]}{dn}
=\Theta_{d,u}(1/n).
\tag{9.2}
$$

To turn this variance into a lower-tail statement, center the bounded edge variables. Their dependency graph is the line graph of `G`, of degree at most `2(d-1)`. In the fourth-moment expansion, any term having a dependency component consisting of a single centered factor vanishes. Bounded dependency degree therefore leaves only `O_d(n^2)` nonzero ordered four-tuples, so

$$
\mathbf E[(D_0-\mathbf ED_0)^4]=O_{d,u}(n^2).
$$

Paley--Zygmund applied to the square gives constants `c_1,c_2>0` such that

$$
\mathbf P_u^G\left(
|\mathcal D_0-\mathbf E\mathcal D_0|\ge c_1n^{-1/2}
\right)\ge c_2
\tag{9.3}
$$

uniformly over the graph.

Now take

$$
t_n=n^{-3},
\qquad C_n=\log n.
$$

The total voter-clock rate is `n`, so the probability of no ring before `t_n` is `exp(-n t_n)=exp(-n^{-2})->1`. A single update changes `mathcal D` by at most `2/n`, hence `|L mathcal D|<=2` and

$$
|\mathbf E\mathcal D_{t_n}-\mathbf E\mathcal D_0|\le2t_n.
$$

On the no-ring event, `mathcal D_{t_n}=mathcal D_0`, while

$$
C_n\sqrt{t_n/n}=\frac{\log n}{n^2}=o(n^{-1/2}).
$$

Together with (9.3), this leaves a non-vanishing deviation probability. Thus literal source (1.9) is false.

What is proved by the new theorem is precise: the corrected `sqrt((1+t_n)/n)` scale holds for every deterministic `t_n=o(n)`, and the source `sqrt(t_n/n)` scale holds for every deterministic `1<=t_n=o(n)` (indeed the same estimate works under any fixed positive lower time cutoff). I would read the phrase "the only defect in (1.9) is its unrestricted very-small-time quantifier" in this theorem-level sense, not as a claimed complete classification of every possible subunit sequence.

## 10. Final assessment

I tried to break the proof at the conditioning step, the ordered-pair count, the cluster-square identity, the four-walk coupling, and the source normalization. None fails.

The most delicate point is (4.3). The correct coupling uses four raw independent paths and obtains coalescing families by identification after meetings. On the complement of the source cross-interaction event (5.5), the joint four-lineage genealogy factors exactly into the two isolated within-family systems. This proves the covariance estimate with no extra term from within-family coalescence.

The source's printed `O(t/n)` wording should not be used literally for `t<1`; source (5.8) gives the required `O((1+t)/n)` estimate directly, and monotonicity gives the same correction. The project already makes this repair, so it does not narrow the claimed theorem.

I therefore find the deterministic constant `2`, the deterministic regular-graph generality (for `d>=1`, connected or not), the random-regular consequence, Chebyshev step, and positive-time counterexample correct.
