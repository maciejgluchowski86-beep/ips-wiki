## 1. Graphical setup and no spontaneous rightward damage

At each site `i` use a rate-one Poisson clock with independent uniform mark `U`. A configuration `eta` is updated by

\[
\eta_i\longmapsto
1_{\{U<r_{\eta_i,\eta_{i+1}}\}},
\tag{1.1}
\]

with

\[
r_{00}=a,
\qquad
r_{01}=b,
\qquad
r_{10}=c,
\qquad
r_{11}=0.
\tag{1.2}
\]

Two copies `X,Y` use the same clocks and uniforms. Put

\[
D_i=1_{\{X_i\ne Y_i\}},
\qquad
D_t=\{i:D_i(t)=1\}.
\]

The elementary one-sided fact needed below is exact.

### Lemma 1.1 (no spontaneous disagreement)

If immediately before an update of site `i`,

\[
D_i=D_{i+1}=0,
\]

then immediately after the update,

\[
D_i=0.
\]

Consequently if a half-line `[j,infinity)` is coupled at some time, it remains coupled forever.

### Proof

When `D_i=D_{i+1}=0`, both copies present the same local input pair to the same uniform mark in (1.1), so they produce the same output. Updates at other sites do not change `D_i`. Iterate in time. `square`

For finite `D_0`, let

\[
R_0=\max D_0.
\]

Lemma 1.1 implies that no disagreement can ever be created strictly right of `R_0`.

## 2. Rightmost disagreements are erased permanently

Meeting 004 proves the following actual common-uniform fact, not a trail-reset statement:

> whenever `D_i=1`, its predictable coalescence intensity at its own clock is at least
> \[
> q=1-c+a.
> \]

For a rightmost disagreement this coalescence is permanent.

Let

\[
\tau=\inf\{t:D_t=\varnothing\}
\]

and, on `{t<tau}`, let

\[
R_t=\max D_t.
\]

### Proposition 2.1 (sequential permanent-coupling front)

`R_t` is nonincreasing. While the process is nonempty, the waiting time until `R_t` strictly decreases has conditional tail bounded by `Exp(q)`:

\[
P(R_{s+u}=R_s\text{ for all }0\le u\le t\mid\mathcal F_s)
\le e^{-qt}
\tag{2.1}
\]

whenever `s<tau`.

If `S_m` denotes the time of the `m`-th strict decrease of the rightmost disagreement, with extinction counted as an earlier success, then `S_m` is stochastically dominated by a Gamma random variable with `m` rate-`q` stages. In particular,

\[
\boxed{
P(\tau>t,\ R_t>R_0-m)
\le
P(\operatorname{Pois}(qt)<m).
}
\tag{2.2}
\]

### Proof

At time `s<tau`, every site strictly right of `R_s` is coupled. By Lemma 1.1 this half-line remains coupled. The current rightmost disagreement has coalescence hazard at least `q`; hence its survival for another time `t` has conditional probability at most `e^{-qt}`. Once it coalesces, its right neighbour is coupled, so Lemma 1.1 makes the coalescence permanent and the rightmost index strictly decreases or the process becomes empty.

Apply the same argument after each such stopping time. Conditional stochastic domination of every stage by `Exp(q)` gives domination of the sum by `Gamma(m,q)`. The standard Gamma--Poisson identity yields (2.2). `square`

### Corollary 2.2 (permanent local coupling)

For every finite initial seed and every fixed site `j`, there is an almost surely finite random time after which `D_j=0` forever.

More quantitatively, if `j<=R_0` and

\[
m=R_0-j+1,
\]

the time by which the entire half-line `[j,infinity)` has become permanently coupled is stochastically dominated by `Gamma(m,q)`.

Thus common-uniform finite-seed disagreement cannot survive in a stationary spatial region.

## 3. Survival is exactly convective escape to the left

Let

\[
L_0=\min D_0
\]

and define the first discovery time of the `m`-th new site to the left by

\[
\sigma_m
=\inf\{t:D_{L_0-m}(t)=1\},
\qquad m\ge1.
\tag{3.1}
\]

### Lemma 3.1 (finite-speed discovery)

For every `m>=1`,

\[
\boxed{
P(\sigma_m\le t)
\le
P(\operatorname{Pois}(t)\ge m).
}
\tag{3.2}
\]

Equivalently `sigma_m` stochastically dominates `Gamma(m,1)`.

### Proof

Before site `L_0-m` can disagree, site `L_0-(m-1)` must previously have disagreed, by Lemma 1.1. At time `sigma_{m-1}`, the newly targeted site `L_0-m` has never disagreed. Its first possible creation after that stopping time requires a ring of its own independent rate-one clock. By the strong Markov property of the graphical construction, the next such ring is an independent `Exp(1)` wait. If the right disagreement disappears first, creation is delayed further.

Iterating gives the Gamma lower bound, hence (3.2). `square`

In particular infinitely many new sites cannot be discovered in finite time.

### Proposition 3.2 (convective-survival equivalence)

For a finite nonempty seed,

\[
\boxed{
\{D_t\ne\varnothing\text{ for every }t\ge0\}
=
\{\sigma_m<\infty\text{ for every }m\ge1\}
\quad\text{a.s.}
}
\tag{3.3}
\]

Hence any finite-seed survival is necessarily an unbounded leftward escape.

### Proof

On survival, Proposition 2.1 forces infinitely many permanent rightmost coalescences, so `R_t` tends to `-infinity`. Nearest-neighbour propagation then forces every intermediate site to have been discovered, giving `sigma_m<infinity` for all `m`.

Conversely, if every `sigma_m` is finite, Lemma 3.1 implies `sigma_m->infinity`. Thus disagreements exist at arbitrarily large times. Since the empty state of the disagreement process is absorbing, extinction at a finite time is impossible. `square`

This is the first structural answer to Assignment 006: local coalescence is not the issue. The unresolved question is whether a disagreement cloud can keep moving left indefinitely.

## 4. An additive convective Lyapunov function

The exact local common-uniform transition table yields a simple inequality stronger than needed for local erasure.

### Lemma 4.1 (local additive damage drift)

At every site,

\[
\boxed{
\mathcal L^{\rm coup}D_i
\le
-qD_i+cD_{i+1}.
}
\tag{4.1}
\]

### Proof

There are three cases.

1. If `D_i=D_{i+1}=0`, Lemma 1.1 gives zero drift.
2. If `D_i=0,D_{i+1}=1`, the two copies have a common spin at `i`. If that spin is zero, the creation probability at an update is
   \[
   |b-a|=d<c.
   \]
   If it is one, the creation probability is
   \[
   |c-0|=c.
   \]
   Thus the creation intensity is at most `c`.
3. If `D_i=1`, the coalescence intensity is at least `q`, so the contribution is at most `-qD_i`; adding the nonnegative term `cD_{i+1}` preserves the upper bound.

This proves (4.1). `square`

For `z>1`, define

\[
V_z(D)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb Z}z^iD_i.
\tag{4.2}
\]

Starting from a finite seed this is finite at every finite time almost surely; one may also localize to finite intervals and pass to the limit using Lemma 3.1.

### Proposition 4.2 (exponential convective contraction)

For every

\[
z>\frac cq,
\]
put

\[
\kappa_z=q-\frac cz>0.
\]

Then

\[
\boxed{
E V_z(D_t)
\le e^{-\kappa_z t}V_z(D_0).
}
\tag{4.3}
\]

### Proof

Multiply (4.1) by `z^i` and sum:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal LV_z
&\le
-q\sum_i z^iD_i
+c\sum_i z^iD_{i+1}\\
&=
\left(-q+\frac cz\right)V_z
=-\kappa_zV_z.
\end{aligned}
\]

Dynkin's formula and Gronwall give (4.3). `square`

For every fixed `j`,

\[
\boxed{
P(D_j(t)=1)
\le
z^{-j}e^{-\kappa_z t}V_z(D_0).
}
\tag{4.4}
\]

Thus the actual common-uniform coupling is exponentially stable in every fixed spatial window from every finite seed.

At the rational point (0.1),

\[
q=\frac1{5000},
\qquad
c=\frac{9999}{10000}.
\]

For example `z=10000` gives

\[
\boxed{
\kappa_z
=q-\frac cz
=\frac{10001}{100000000}>0.
}
\tag{4.5}
\]

This is an additive moving-frame estimate. It is not a resurrection of either refuted multiplicative Foster architecture: it deliberately discounts damage which has moved far to the left and therefore does **not** imply global extinction.

## 5. The finite-time random-map damage coefficient

Let `Phi_t^omega` be the random map on configurations defined by one graphical slab of length `t`. For a configuration `eta`, let `eta^i` denote the configuration obtained by flipping only site `i`. Define

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(t)
=\sup_{\eta,i}
E\,d_H(\Phi_t\eta,\Phi_t\eta^i),
}
\tag{5.1}
\]

where `d_H` is Hamming distance.

Translation invariance allows one to fix `i=0`, but retaining `i` makes the path-coupling statement transparent.

Lemma 3.1 gives finiteness. From a single initial discrepancy, the disagreement support at time `t` is contained between the initial site and the leftmost site ever discovered. Hence

\[
P(|D_t|\ge m+1)
\le P(\operatorname{Pois}(t)\ge m),
\]

and therefore

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(t)\le1+t.
}
\tag{5.2}
\]

### Lemma 5.1 (path extension)

If two initial configurations `eta,xi` differ at finitely many sites, then

\[
\boxed{
E\,d_H(\Phi_t\eta,\Phi_t\xi)
\le
\alpha(t)d_H(\eta,\xi).
}
\tag{5.3}
\]

### Proof

Enumerate the differing sites and interpolate from `eta` to `xi` by flipping one site at a time. For every fixed graphical realization, Hamming distance obeys the triangle inequality after applying the same random map `Phi_t`. Take expectations and use the definition of `alpha(t)` on each single-flip pair. `square`

### Proposition 5.2 (submultiplicativity)

For all `s,t>=0`,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(t+s)\le\alpha(t)\alpha(s).
}
\tag{5.4}
\]

### Proof

Use independent graphical slabs. Conditional on the first slab, the intermediate coupled pair differs at finitely many sites almost surely. Lemma 5.1 applied to the independent second slab gives

\[
E[d_H(D_{t+s})\mid X_t,Y_t]
\le
\alpha(s)d_H(X_t,Y_t).
\]

Take expectations, bound the first-slab single-flip expectation by `alpha(t)`, and then take the supremum over the initial single-flip state. `square`

Thus

\[
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac1{nT}\log\alpha(nT)
\]

has the usual subadditive upper exponent for every fixed block scale `T`. No local product over ancestry levels appears.

## 6. A single finite-time inequality proves quantitative extinction

### Theorem 6.1 (nonlocal block path-coupling criterion)

Suppose that for the common-uniform coupling at a fixed strict residual parameter point there is a finite `T` such that

\[
\boxed{\alpha(T)=:\rho<1.}
\tag{6.1}
\]

Then, for every finite initial disagreement seed,

\[
\boxed{
E|D_{nT}|
\le
\rho^n|D_0|,
}
\tag{6.2}
\]

and

\[
\boxed{
P(\tau>nT)
\le
\rho^n|D_0|.
}
\tag{6.3}
\]

In particular the finite disagreement process dies out almost surely, with an exponential tail on block times.

### Proof

Apply Lemma 5.1 at each independent graphical block of length `T`, or equivalently combine (5.3) with (5.4), to obtain (6.2). Since the empty disagreement state is absorbing,

\[
\{\tau>nT\}=\{D_{nT}\ne\varnothing\}.
\]

For an integer-valued nonnegative variable,

\[
1_{\{|D_{nT}|>0\}}\le |D_{nT}|.
\]

Taking expectations gives (6.3). `square`

This is strong enough for the type of nonlocal quantitative regeneration requested in Assignment 006. It is also a genuine test of the common coupling: if some finite seed survives with positive probability, then necessarily

\[
\alpha(T)\ge1
\quad\text{for every }T>0.
\tag{6.4}
\]

The converse of (6.4) is not asserted: almost-sure extinction could in principle be too nonuniform to force a one-block Hamming contraction.

## 7. Exact finite controlled-CTMC certificate for `alpha(T)<1`

The coefficient `alpha(T)` still contains a supremum over an infinite common background. One-sidedness reduces this to a rigorous finite certification hierarchy.

Fix integers `L,R>=0`. Consider the sites

\[
[-L,R].
\]

Sites `[-L,0]` carry their full coupled pair state. Sites `[1,R]` remain agreed forever and need only their common spin. At updates of site `R`, replace the common spin at `R+1` by a controller action

\[
z\in\{0,1\}.
\]

Allow the controller to choose `z` predictably from the entire finite state and time. This is a stronger environment than the true common right half-line, so it gives an upper bound.

Start from arbitrary common spins in the window with a single flip at site zero. Let

\[
A_{L,R}(T)
\]

be the maximal expected number of disagreements in `[-L,0]` at time `T`, optimized over those finite initial states and all such boundary controls.

This is an exact finite-state continuous-time control problem. The state-space size is at most

\[
4^{L+1}2^R.
\]

Its finite-horizon value function satisfies the HJB system with terminal payoff

\[
F_0(\sigma)=\sum_{i=-L}^0D_i(\sigma),
\]

and generator

\[
\partial_tF_t
=
\sum_{i=-L}^{R-1}\mathcal L_iF_t
+
\max_{z\in\{0,1\}}\mathcal L_R^{(z)}F_t.
\tag{7.1}
\]

At the rational point (0.1) all generator coefficients are rational.

Define the explicit left-discovery tail

\[
\ell_L(T)
:=
\sum_{m=L+1}^{\infty}
P(\operatorname{Pois}(T)\ge m)
=E[(\operatorname{Pois}(T)-L)_+].
\tag{7.2}
\]

### Proposition 7.1 (finite certificate hierarchy)

For every `L,R,T`,

\[
\boxed{
\alpha(T)
\le
A_{L,R}(T)+\ell_L(T).
}
\tag{7.3}
\]

Consequently the finite, checkable inequality

\[
\boxed{
A_{L,R}(T)+\ell_L(T)<1
}
\tag{7.4}
\]

for one triple `(L,R,T)` proves the extinction theorem of Section 6.

### Proof

Take any infinite initial single-flip pair at site zero. Because disagreement cannot move right, sites `1,2,...` remain common. Restrict the actual process to `[-L,R]`. Its actual spin at `R+1`, sampled whenever site `R` rings, is one admissible common boundary history for the enlarged controller. Therefore

\[
E\sum_{i=-L}^0D_i(T)
\le A_{L,R}(T).
\]

For `m>L`, Lemma 3.1 gives

\[
P(D_{-m}(T)=1)
\le
P(\operatorname{Pois}(T)\ge m).
\]

Summing over all sites left of `-L` gives at most `ell_L(T)`. Add the two estimates, then take the supremum over infinite backgrounds. `square`

This finite control problem is not being offered as another scalar finite-range Foster ansatz. It does not assign local credits or iterate a spatial product. It computes the complete finite-time random map, while the Poisson term controls the omitted damage cone.

I have not certified (7.4) at the hard rational point. Naive control which exposes the boundary too close to the seed is too pessimistic, and brute-force state size grows rapidly when `L` must be large enough to make `ell_L(T)` small. The value of Proposition 7.1 is that it identifies exactly what a computer-assisted extinction certificate would have to establish, with no uncontrolled infinite-volume remainder.

## 8. Why a short-time/local argument cannot decide the sign

At the rational point (0.1), choose a single disagreement at site `0` with orientation `01`, common right spin zero, and common left spin one.
