## A. What the East distinguished-zero induction actually uses

At the structural level, the East argument needs four separate facts.

### A1. Marker measurability from the unscreened side

The distinguished-zero path must be determined without revealing the protected-side graphical marks. Conditioning on that path is then harmless for the region already screened.

This is not merely a convenient stopping-time property. It is what allows one to condition on the moving boundary without biasing the law that is claimed to remain at equilibrium.

### A2. Fixed-zero-boundary invariance between moves

While the marker stays in place, the protected interval evolves with a fixed zero immediately to its right. Its equilibrium restriction is invariant for that finite dynamics.

For the positive-rates chain, this part does survive formally: `pi_N` is, by definition, invariant for the `N`-site chain with fixed zero boundary.

### A3. Correct release at a move

When the East marker advances, the old marker site is released using fresh update randomness with exactly the equilibrium one-site distribution.

For a general positive-rates chain, one may also have fresh right-side/reset randomness. Freshness by itself is not enough: it only specifies a kernel for newly released coordinates.

### A4. Equilibrium consistency under interval growth

The decisive East property is that the old equilibrium block plus the freshly released site has exactly the equilibrium law on the enlarged block. For Bernoulli equilibrium this is automatic product consistency.

This is where the zero-boundary family fails. One-sidedness gives the accepted **right-suffix** projectivity

$$
R_{N,M}\pi_N=\pi_M,
$$

but an East marker growing the protected interval to the right needs **left-prefix** compatibility

$$
\bar\pi_{N+1}=\pi_N.
$$

Those are different directions of consistency.

## B. Exact one-move compatibility gate

### Proposition B.1

Let `X=(X_1,...,X_N)` be the old screened block. Let `G` be the sigma-field generated by the marker/right-side data used to decide the next move and generate release randomness. Suppose

$$
\mathcal L(X\mid G)=\pi_N
$$

and the move does not inspect or modify `X`. Let `Y` denote all newly released coordinates. If the enlarged block is required to satisfy

$$
\mathcal L((X,Y)\mid G)=\pi_{N+1},
$$

then necessarily

$$
\boxed{\bar\pi_{N+1}=\pi_N.}
$$

### Proof

Marginalize the second conditional law over `Y`. Its `X`-marginal is `bar pi_{N+1}`. The first conditional law says that the same marginal is `pi_N`. Hence they are equal.

The argument is unchanged if the release law depends arbitrarily on right-side randomness or is randomized conditionally on `G`. A kernel acting only on new/released coordinates cannot change the marginal of coordinates it leaves untouched. `square`

This proves the elementary gate requested in Assignment 011 and shows why resampling only the newly released marker site cannot repair a prefix mismatch.

## C. Exact finite calculation

Use the programme's normalized random-map convention: each site rings at rate one and, if its current spin is `x` and its right neighbour is `y`, it is reset to `1` with probability `r_xy`. On the face `r_11=0`,

$$
r_{00}=a,\qquad r_{01}=b,\qquad r_{10}=c,\qquad r_{11}=0.
$$

### C1. One site

With fixed zero boundary,

$$
0\to1\text{ at rate }a,
\qquad
1\to0\text{ at rate }1-c.
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\pi_1(1)=\frac{a}{a+1-c}.
}
$$

### C2. Two sites

Write the state as `(eta_1,eta_2)` from left to right and put

$$
H=2ab-ac+3a-bc+b+c^2-3c+2,
\qquad
D=(a+1-c)H.
$$

Direct solution of the four-state stationarity equations gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi_2(00)&=\frac{(1-c)(a-bc+b+c^2-3c+2)}{D},\\
\pi_2(01)&=\frac{a(2a+c^2-3c+2)}{D},\\
\pi_2(10)&=\frac{a(1-c)(2b-c+2)}{D},\\
\pi_2(11)&=\frac{a(2ab-ac+a-bc+b)}{D}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\bar\pi_2(1)-\pi_1(1)
=
-\frac{2a\,[a-b(1-c)]}
{(a+1-c)\,[2ab-ac+3a-bc+b+c^2-3c+2]}.
}
$$

In the positive-rate residual chamber the denominator is nonzero and `a>0`. Thus the depth-one compatibility gate is exact precisely when

$$
\boxed{a=b(1-c).}
$$

### C3. Hard point

At

$$
P_h=\left(\frac1{10000},\frac1{100},\frac{9999}{10000}\right),
$$

one has

$$
\pi_1(1)=\frac12,
$$

and

$$
(\pi_2(00),\pi_2(01),\pi_2(10),\pi_2(11))
=\frac1{60604}(20101,30001,10201,301).
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
\bar\pi_2(1)=\frac{5251}{30302},
\qquad
\bar\pi_2(1)-\pi_1(1)=-\frac{4950}{15151}.
}
$$

The literal `pi_N` analogue of East's exact conditional-equilibrium lemma is therefore false at the hard point.

## D. Why the compatibility locus is exactly the product surface

Set

$$
\rho=\frac{b}{1+b}.
$$

For a Bernoulli-`rho` product law, conditional detailed balance for a site with right neighbour `0` is

$$
(1-\rho)a=\rho(1-c),
$$

while with right neighbour `1` it is

$$
(1-\rho)b=\rho.
$$

The second identity holds by the definition of `rho`; the first is equivalent to `a=b(1-c)`. Therefore on that locus

$$
\pi_N=\operatorname{Ber}(\rho)^{\otimes N}
$$

for every zero-boundary box, by invariance plus finite-state irreducibility. Prefix compatibility then holds at every depth.

This includes the corrected reversible reference point used in Assignment 010. Thus the symbolic locus has a structural meaning: the exact East induction survives exactly where the supposedly surrogate nonproduct equilibrium family collapses back to a product-consistent family.

## E. Buffered moving boundary

Suppose after a move the old block remains untouched but its last `m` sites are declared contaminated. Keep only the protected prefix

$$
U_{N,m}=\{1,\ldots,N-m\}.
$$

Define

$$
S_m
=
\sup_{N\ge m+1}
\sup_{\substack{|f|\le1\\
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subseteq U_{N,m}}}
|\bar\pi_{N+1}(f)-\pi_N(f)|.
$$

By the definition of the old zero-frequency/tail-shift defect, setting `K=N+1` and `M=m+1` gives the exact identity

$$
\boxed{S_m=\Delta_{m+1}.}
$$

If the newly released coordinate is counted as the first site of the boundary layer, the same statement is written with `Delta_m`; this is only an indexing convention.

Meeting 014 already proves

$$
\Delta_M
=
\|\theta\pi_\infty^0-\pi_\infty^0\|_{\mathcal F_{M-1}},
$$

and

$$
\Delta_M\to0
\iff
\theta\pi_\infty^0\text{ and }\pi_\infty^0
\text{ agree on the remote-left tail sigma-field}.
$$

Hence the most direct approximate East repair is exactly the stopped tail-shift theorem.

### Does conditioning on the marker path improve the quantity?

Not at this compatibility gate. If the marker path is right-measurable in the East sense, conditioning on it does not inspect or modify the protected old prefix. Thus its conditional law remains the old `pi_N` prefix. The target enlarged invariant law still has the `bar pi_{N+1}` prefix. Their discrepancy on protected observables is unchanged by such conditioning.

One can ask only about a single fixed local observable after a finite future time. That is numerically weaker than `Delta_M`, but it does not close a moving-screen induction without a separate all-depth dynamical theorem. The causal support of the evolved observable grows with the future time. Meeting 014 already supplies the existing reduction of such a one-segment boundary response to `Delta_{M-d}` plus finite propagation. A different proof would need a new contraction/cancellation mechanism for the dynamically injected boundary defect. No such mechanism is produced by the distinguished-zero bookkeeping itself; the already-stopped dynamical candidates are the common-uniform all-depth route and, on the connected-renewal architecture, Meeting 030's signed boundary-transmission operator `(V)`.

Thus C1 produces no new screening object upstream of the pre-registered blockers.

## F. Finite regenerative release kernels

Now allow the marker move to destroy/resample a fixed-width old boundary layer.

Write the old block as

$$
(U,V)\in\{0,1\}^{N-m}\times\{0,1\}^{m},
$$

where `U` is protected and `V` may be inspected/replaced. Let `K(v,dw)` be an arbitrary Markov kernel using `V` and fresh right-side randomness to generate the new boundary layer `W`. Starting from `pi_N`, the output law is

$$
\nu(du,dw)=\sum_v\pi_N(du,dv)K(v,dw).
$$

Marginalizing `W` gives

$$
\nu_U=(\pi_N)_U.
$$

Therefore exact output `nu=pi_{N+1}` requires

$$
\boxed{(\pi_N)_U=(\pi_{N+1})_U.}
$$

If one width-`m` release architecture is to work uniformly for every `N>m`, then necessarily

$$
\boxed{\Delta_{m+1}=0.}
$$

This condition is stronger than the asymptotic theorem `Delta_M->0`.

It is not sufficient. Exact release also requires the conditional factorization

$$
\pi_{N+1}(dw\mid U=u)
=
\sum_v\pi_N(dv\mid U=u)K(v,dw)
$$

through one `U`-blind finite kernel.

For approximate release, restriction to `sigma(U)` gives the lower bound

$$
\|\nu-\pi_{N+1}\|_{\sigma(U)}
=
\|\pi_N-\pi_{N+1}\|_{\sigma(U)},
$$

so a growing-width release with vanishing uniform error again requires `Delta_M->0`.

## G. Stop-condition application

The assignment says to stop if every credible buffered/release formulation requires an already-stopped object upstream.

That condition is met.

- **Literal exact screen:** false off the product surface already at `N=1 -> 2`.
- **Buffered approximate screen:** exact error `S_m=Delta_{m+1}`.
- **Fixed-width exact release:** requires `Delta_{m+1}=0` before the conditional release-kernel problem even begins.
- **Growing-width approximate release:** error is bounded below by the same tail-shift discrepancy, so vanishing requires `Delta_M->0`.
- **Finite-time local relaxation after release:** can be weaker for one chosen observable, but turning it into an all-depth screen requires a new dynamical boundary-error contraction. The distinguished-zero construction has not supplied such an object; the existing concrete versions are precisely among the stopped common-coupling/boundary-transmission routes.

Accordingly there is no `CONTINUE-NEW-BRIDGE` object satisfying Assignment 011's four continuation requirements. In particular there is no need to analyze marker creation, persistence, advance, or success sigma-fields in Part D: compatibility fails before marker existence becomes load-bearing.

## Final interpretation

The principal's proposal was worth testing because suffix projectivity and fixed-zero-boundary invariance superficially resemble the East ingredients. The test shows exactly why the resemblance stops short of a new architecture.

East does not use invariance alone. It uses an equilibrium family that is consistent in the **same direction in which the marker releases sites**. For the residual positive-rates chain, the accepted invariant family is projective in the opposite, right-suffix direction. Its failure of left-prefix consistency is not an auxiliary nuisance; after buffering it is exactly the previously isolated spatial tail-shift defect.

Thus distinguished-zero geometry does not weaken the stopped stationary theorem. It repackages it.
