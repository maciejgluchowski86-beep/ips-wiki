## Result

The literal East conditional-equilibrium induction does not survive replacement of Bernoulli equilibrium by the zero-boundary invariant family `pi_N`, except on a codimension-one parameter locus already visible at `N=1,2`.

Let the old screened block be `X=(X_1,...,X_N)` and suppose that immediately before a marker move

`Law(X)=pi_N`.

Assume the marker move time and all release randomness are measurable from the marker/right-side graphical history and do not inspect or modify `X`. If after the move the enlarged screened block `(X,Y)` is required to have law `pi_{N+1}`, then necessarily

`bar pi_{N+1}=pi_N`,

where `bar pi_{N+1}` is the marginal of `pi_{N+1}` on its leftmost `N` sites. No choice of the released-site law `Y`, including a law correlated with right-side randomness, can repair failure of this prefix condition.

At the hard point

`P_h=(a,b,c)=(1/10000,1/100,9999/10000)`,

the condition already fails from one site to two sites:

`pi_1(1)=1/2`,

while

`bar pi_2(1)=5251/30302`,

so

`bar pi_2(1)-pi_1(1)=-4950/15151`.

Thus the literal analogue of East Lemma 8.2 with `pi_N` as the screened equilibrium family is false at the programme's hard point.

The exact symbolic difference is

$$
\bar\pi_2(\eta_1=1)-\pi_1(\eta_1=1)
=
-\frac{2a\,[a-b(1-c)]}
{(a+1-c)\,[2ab-ac+3a-bc+b+c^2-3c+2]}.
$$

In the positive-rate residual chamber the denominator is nonzero and `a>0`, so exact depth-one prefix compatibility holds precisely on

$$
a=b(1-c).
$$

## A. Structural reconstruction of the East induction

The East distinguished-zero argument uses four separate properties.

1. **Marker/path measurability from the unscreened side.** The future distinguished-zero path is determined without revealing the graphical marks in the already screened region. Conditioning on the marker path therefore does not bias the protected-side evolution.
2. **Fixed-zero-boundary invariance between marker moves.** While the distinguished vacancy stays at the boundary of the screened interval, the screened region evolves as the corresponding finite East chain with a fixed zero boundary, and the Bernoulli equilibrium restriction is invariant for that chain.
3. **Correct release law at a marker move.** When the distinguished zero advances, the site it leaves behind is refreshed by the legal update with the Bernoulli equilibrium coin. The newly released coordinate therefore has exactly the equilibrium one-site law and uses fresh randomness.
4. **Prefix consistency when the interval grows.** Bernoulli equilibrium is a product family, so adjoining that fresh coordinate to the old equilibrium block gives exactly the equilibrium law on the enlarged interval. This is the measure-consistency step that makes the induction exact.

For the generic one-sided positive-rates chain, items 1 and 2 have formal analogues: one-sided graphical measurability can in principle protect left marks, and `pi_N` is invariant for the `N`-site zero-boundary chain. What fails is the combination of items 3 and 4. A fresh law on the newly released site cannot alter the old block's marginal, while `pi_{N+1}` generically has a different left-prefix marginal from `pi_N`.

Suffix projectivity

`R_{N,M} pi_N = pi_M`

survives exactly, but it is the wrong projective direction for an East marker that enlarges the screened interval to the right.

## B. Measure-theoretic one-move theorem

Let `G` be the sigma-field generated by all marker/right-side data used to decide the move and release. Assume the old screened block `X` is not inspected or modified by the move and that, conditional on `G`,

`Law(X | G)=pi_N`.

Let `Y` denote every newly released coordinate generated at the move. If

`Law((X,Y) | G)=pi_{N+1}`

for almost every successful move realization, then marginalizing over `Y` gives

`Law(X | G)=bar pi_{N+1}`.

Comparing with the first display yields `bar pi_{N+1}=pi_N`. The same argument holds without conditioning after integrating over `G`.

The proof uses only the fact that a kernel acting on new coordinates cannot change the marginal of coordinates it leaves untouched. Therefore randomizing the released marker site, changing its coin law, or correlating it with right-side marks cannot repair a prefix mismatch.

## C. Exact finite calculation

Under the programme's normalized random-map convention, a rate-one ring at a site currently in state `x` with right neighbour `y` resets that site to `1` with probability `r_xy`, with

`r_00=a`, `r_01=b`, `r_10=c`, `r_11=0`.

For one site with fixed zero boundary,

$$
\pi_1(1)=\frac{a}{a+1-c},
\qquad
\pi_1(0)=\frac{1-c}{a+1-c}.
$$

For two sites, write states in left-to-right order `(eta_1,eta_2)` and put

$$
H=2ab-ac+3a-bc+b+c^2-3c+2,
\qquad
D=(a+1-c)H.
$$

Direct stationarity gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\pi_2(00)&=\frac{(1-c)(a-bc+b+c^2-3c+2)}{D},\\
\pi_2(01)&=\frac{a(2a+c^2-3c+2)}{D},\\
\pi_2(10)&=\frac{a(1-c)(2b-c+2)}{D},\\
\pi_2(11)&=\frac{a(2ab-ac+a-bc+b)}{D}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Hence

$$
\bar\pi_2(1)
=\frac{a(2ab-ac+a-3bc+3b+c^2-3c+2)}{(a+1-c)H},
$$

and subtraction from `pi_1(1)` factors as stated above.

At `P_h`,

$$
(\pi_2(00),\pi_2(01),\pi_2(10),\pi_2(11))
=\frac1{60604}(20101,30001,10201,301),
$$

so the prefix failure is exact and large, not a numerical-rounding effect.
