## 6. No finite-cylinder cycle for invertible factorized filters

There is a separate obstruction to the simplest factorized-sign certificate.

Let `V_m` be functions on `m` sites and let `S_m \subset V_m` be the functions independent of the first coordinate. One-sidedness gives `Q_m S_m \subseteq S_m`. Suppose `K_m:V_m->V_m` is invertible, suffix-compatible, and preserves these subspaces. Define

\[
T_mf=Y_{m+1}K_mf.
\]

### Proposition 6.1

There are no nonzero finite-cylinder `phi`, block length `p>=1`, and `lambda!=0` satisfying

\[
T_{m+p-1}\cdots T_m\phi
=\lambda\,\phi\circ\theta^p.
\tag{6.1}
\]

### Proof

The right side is independent of the first coordinate. The final factor `Y_{m+p}` is nowhere zero (`-c` or `g`), so after dividing by it the output of the final `K` is independent of the first coordinate. Since an invertible operator preserving a finite-dimensional invariant subspace maps that subspace onto itself, `K^{-1}` preserves it too. Peeling the block therefore forces the preceding profile, and eventually the original `phi`, to be independent of its first coordinate.

Suffix compatibility reduces (6.1) to the same equation with memory `m-1`. Iterate until `phi` is constant. But the first transfer of a nonzero constant acquires the nonconstant nowhere-zero factor `Y`, so it cannot reproduce a shifted constant. Contradiction. `square`

For the legal Laplace filters `K_m=R_m(d)` from (1.6),

\[
R_m(d)=F_d(Q_m),
\qquad
F_d(z)
=
\frac{\omega+d-z+1+B+a}
{(\omega+d-z+a)(\omega+d-z+1+B)-a}.
\]

For every Markov eigenvalue `Re z<=0` and `d>=0`, the argument `omega+d-z` lies in the open right half-plane. Neither the numerator nor the stable killed-chain resolvent determinant vanishes there. Hence `R_m(d)` is invertible, and Proposition 6.1 applies.

This does not refute `(J+)`; it rules out only the generic nonsingular factorized-resolvent plus finite-cylinder implementation.
