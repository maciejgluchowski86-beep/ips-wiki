The gap-coordinate dynamics makes this possibility concrete. Away from the moving front, it is a reversible coagulation/fragmentation process with iid geometric equilibrium gaps. > **FRONT-UNIQUENESS LEMMA.** For every fixed `lambda>0`, the infinite right-front process has a unique invariant probability law.

## 1. The infinite front process

Fix `lambda>0`. Let

$$
E=\{0,1\}^{\mathbb N}
$$

with the product topology. A state

$$
u=(u_1,u_2,\ldots)
$$

records the sites behind the rightmost particle; the implicit rightmost particle is `u_0=1`.

For cylinder functions define

$$
S_+u=(1,u_1,u_2,\ldots),
\qquad
S_-u=(u_2,u_3,\ldots),
$$

and let `u^(j)` denote the configuration with bit `j` flipped. The front generator is

$$
\begin{aligned}
Q_\infty f(u)
={}&\lambda\bigl[f(S_+u)-f(u)\bigr]\\
&+u_1\bigl[f(S_-u)-f(u)\bigr]\\
&+\sum_{j\ge1}(u_{j-1}+u_{j+1})
\bigl[\lambda(1-u_j)+u_j\bigr]
\bigl[f(u^{(j)})-f(u)\bigr].
\end{aligned}
\tag{1.1}
$$

Only finitely many terms in the last sum affect a fixed cylinder function.

### Proposition 1.1: `Q_infinity` generates a Feller process and has invariant laws

The process generated by (1.1) is a conservative Feller process on `E`. Cylinder functions are a core for its generator. In particular its set of invariant probability laws `I_lambda` is nonempty.

### Proof

Split

$$
Q_\infty=A+B,
$$

where `A` is the ordinary half-line nearest-neighbour spin generator with fixed boundary bit `u_0=1`, and

$$
Bf(u)
=\lambda[f(S_+u)-f(u)]
+u_1[f(S_-u)-f(u)].
$$

The local flip rates in `A` are uniformly bounded and finite-range. The standard graphical construction therefore gives a Feller process on the compact product space, and cylinder functions are a core for `A`.

Both `S_+` and `S_-` are continuous maps on `E`. Hence `B` maps `C(E)` into itself and

$$
\|Bf\|_\infty\le 2(\lambda+1)\|f\|_\infty.
$$

It is itself a bounded-rate jump generator. The bounded perturbation theorem for strongly continuous semigroups therefore gives a conservative Feller semigroup with generator `Q_infinity=A+B` and domain `D(A)`. Since `B` is bounded, the graph norms of `A` and `A+B` are equivalent, so every core for `A` is a core for `Q_infinity`. In particular cylinders are a core.

Finally `E` is compact. Krylov--Bogoliubov averaging of the Feller semigroup gives at least one invariant probability law. `square`

This core statement is the functional-analytic point omitted in the previous note.

## 2. Exact finite LP dual

For `k>=1`, write

$$
E_k=\{0,1\}^k.
$$

For `u in E_k` and an exterior bit `z in {0,1}`, define `Q_k^z` by separating the raw edge displacement from the exact finite-window drift:

$$
D_{k,\lambda}(u,z;\phi)
=\lambda-u_1+Q_k^z\phi(u).
\tag{2.1}
$$

Thus

$$
v_k(\lambda)
=\sup_\phi\min_{u,z}
\left[\lambda-u_1+Q_k^z\phi(u)\right].
\tag{2.2}
$$

Let `M_k` be the set of arrays `m(u,z)>=0` satisfying

$$
\sum_{u,z}m(u,z)=1
\tag{2.3}
$$

and

$$
\sum_{u,z}m(u,z)Q_k^z f(u)=0
\qquad\text{for every }f:E_k\to\mathbb R.
\tag{2.4}
$$

The set is nonempty: fix either exterior policy `z=0` or `z=1`, take a stationary law of the resulting finite continuous-time chain on `E_k`, and put all occupation mass on that value of `z`.

### Proposition 2.1: finite-dimensional duality

For every `k` and fixed `lambda>0`,

$$
v_k(\lambda)
=\min_{m\in M_k}
\sum_{u,z}m(u,z)(\lambda-u_1)
=\lambda-\max_{m\in M_k}\sum_{u,z}m(u,z)u_1.
\tag{2.5}
$$

### Proof

Write the primal constraints as

$$
-Q_k^z\phi(u)+v\le \lambda-u_1,
\qquad (u,z)\in E_k\times\{0,1\}.
$$

A nonnegative dual multiplier for each constraint gives normalization (2.3) from the coefficient of `v`, and the stationarity equations (2.4) from the coefficients of `phi`. The objective is the first expression in (2.5). The primal is feasible, for example with `phi=0` and sufficiently negative `v`; the dual is feasible as observed above and has bounded objective. Finite-dimensional strong LP duality therefore applies and the extrema are attained. `square`

Thus the exterior bit in the finite LP is exactly a stationary boundary control in the dual problem.

## 3. Finite windows converge to the worst invariant infinite front

For a probability law `mu` on `E`, write

$$
b(u)=\lambda-u_1.
$$

### Theorem 3.1

For every fixed `lambda>0`,

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{k\to\infty}v_k(\lambda)
=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal I_\lambda}\mu(b).
}
\tag{3.1}
$$

### Proof: projection gives the upper bound

Fix `mu in I_lambda` and define

$$
m_k(u,z)
=\mu(U_1,\ldots,U_k=u,\ U_{k+1}=z).
$$

If `f:E_k->R` is viewed as a cylinder function on `E`, then pointwise

$$
Q_\infty f
=Q_k^{U_{k+1}}f.
\tag{3.2}
$$

Indeed every event changing the first `k` bits is exactly one of the finite-window events, and all events farther left leave `f` unchanged. Invariance of `mu` therefore implies (2.4), so `m_k in M_k`. Proposition 2.1 yields

$$
v_k(\lambda)\le\mu(b).
$$

Taking the infimum over invariant `mu`,

$$
v_k(\lambda)\le s_*(\lambda)
:=\inf_{\mu\in\mathcal I_\lambda}\mu(b).
\tag{3.3}
$$

### Proof: compactness gives the reverse bound

Let `m_k` be a dual minimizer and let `rho_k` be its marginal on `E_k`. Extend `rho_k` arbitrarily to a probability law `tilde rho_k` on `E`, preserving its first `k` coordinates. Compactness of `E` gives a weakly convergent subsequence

$$
\widetilde\rho_{k_n}\Longrightarrow\mu.
$$

Fix a cylinder function `f` depending on the first `ell` coordinates. If `k_n>=ell+1`, then `Q_infinity f` depends only on the first `ell+1` bits. Equivalently, when `f` is regarded as a function on `E_{k_n}`,

$$
Q_{k_n}^z f
$$

is independent of the adversarial exterior bit `z`. Hence the arbitrary coordinates appended after `k_n` do not enter, and dual stationarity gives

$$
\widetilde\rho_{k_n}(Q_\infty f)
=\sum_{u,z}m_{k_n}(u,z)Q_{k_n}^z f(u)
=0.
\tag{3.4}
$$

Since `Q_infinity f` is again a cylinder function, passing to the weak limit gives

$$
\mu(Q_\infty f)=0
\qquad\text{for every cylinder }f.
\tag{3.5}
$$

By Proposition 1.1, cylinders are a core. If `f in D(Q_infinity)`, approximate it in graph norm by cylinders; (3.5) extends to

$$
\mu(Q_\infty f)=0
\qquad(f\in D(Q_\infty)).
$$

For the Feller semigroup `P_t`, `P_t D(Q_infinity) subset D(Q_infinity)` and

$$
Q_\infty P_t f=P_tQ_\infty f.
$$

Therefore, for `f in D(Q_infinity)`,

$$
\frac d{dt}\mu(P_t f)
=\mu(Q_\infty P_t f)=0.
$$

Density of `D(Q_infinity)` in `C(E)` gives `mu P_t=mu`. Thus `mu in I_lambda`.

The dual objective depends only on the first bit, so along this subsequence

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{k_n}(\lambda)
=\mu(b)
\ge s_*(\lambda).
\tag{3.6}
$$

For fixed `lambda`, the already proved window nesting gives

$$
v_{k+1}(\lambda)\ge v_k(\lambda).
$$

The sequence is bounded above by (3.3), so it has a limit. Equation (3.6) therefore supplies the reverse inequality in (3.3), proving (3.1). `square`

This also checks that no hidden boundary-control closure condition survives in the infinite-window limit: every compactness limit of optimal finite controls is a genuine invariant law of the autonomous infinite front process.

### Corollary 3.2

For fixed `lambda>0`, the following are equivalent:

1. `v_k(lambda)>0` for some finite `k`;
2. `lim_k v_k(lambda)>0`;
3. every invariant front law has mean edge drift bounded below by one common positive constant:

   $$
   \inf_{\mu\in\mathcal I_\lambda}
   \mu(\lambda-u_1)>0.
   $$

This statement is pointwise in `lambda`. It makes no assertion about monotonicity in the parameter.

## 4. Exact first-bit balance and the front-gap criterion

Fix `mu in I_lambda` and set

$$
r=\mu(u_1=1),
\qquad
a=\mu(u_1=0,u_2=1),
\qquad
q=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda}.
$$

When `u_1=0`, it becomes one through either the outward edge birth, at rate `lambda`, or a local birth at site one, at rate `lambda(1+u_2)`. Hence its total `0->1` rate is

$$
\lambda(2+u_2).
$$

When `u_1=1`, a local death changes it to zero at rate `1+u_2`; the edge-death/left-shift changes the new first bit to zero exactly when `u_2=0`, at rate `1-u_2`. The total `1->0` rate is therefore exactly two.

Stationarity gives

$$
\lambda\mu\bigl((1-u_1)(2+u_2)\bigr)
=2\mu(u_1),
$$

that is,

$$
\lambda[2(1-r)+a]=2r.
$$

Solving,

$$
\boxed{
r=q\left(1+\frac a2\right).
}
\tag{4.1}
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
\mu(\lambda-u_1)
=q\left(\lambda-\frac12\mu(01)\right).
}
\tag{4.2}
$$

Combining with Theorem 3.1,

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{k\to\infty}v_k(\lambda)
=q\left(
\lambda-rac12
\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal I_\lambda}\mu(01)
\right).
}
\tag{4.3}
$$

Thus the proposed FRONT-GAP LEMMA is indeed the exact finite-window target:

$$
\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal I_\lambda}\mu(01)<2\lambda.
\tag{FG}
$$

For `lambda>1/2` this is trivial from `mu(01)<=1`; the hard regime is small `lambda`.

## 5. Invariant front laws have infinitely many tail particles

The gap representation below requires infinitely many particles behind the front. This is automatic under every invariant front law when `lambda>0`.

Let

$$
\mathcal F
=\left\{u:\sum_{j\ge1}u_j<\infty\right\}.
$$

The set `F` and its complement are invariant under the front dynamics: finite-range flips and one-site shifts cannot turn a finite tail into an infinite one or vice versa in finite time.

Suppose `mu in I_lambda` has `mu(F)>0`. Conditioning on `F` gives an invariant probability law `mu_F` supported on finite tails. The corresponding absolute BABP configuration is finite and nonempty. Let

$$
N(u)=1+\sum_{j\ge1}u_j
$$

be its particle number.

Martinelli--Shapira--Toninelli (2025), Application 1, proves all-parameter linear growth of the particle number from every finite nonempty initial configuration. In particular, for each fixed finite starting state,

$$
N_t\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad\text{a.s.}
$$

Under `mu_F`, dominated convergence therefore gives, for every fixed `M`,

$$
\mathbf P_{\mu_F}(N_t\le M)
\longrightarrow0.
$$

But stationarity makes this probability equal to `mu_F(N<=M)` for every `t`. Since `N<infinity` almost surely under `mu_F`, some `M` has `mu_F(N<=M)>0`, a contradiction.

Hence

$$
\mu(\mathcal F)=0
\qquad\text{for every }\mu\in\mathcal I_\lambda.
\tag{5.1}
$$

## 6. Gap coordinates: a boundary-driven reversible coagulation/fragmentation process

By (5.1), under an invariant front law enumerate the particles as

$$
x_0=0>x_1>x_2>\cdots
$$

and define the inter-particle gaps

$$
g_i=x_{i-1}-x_i-1\in\mathbb N_0.
$$

The front dynamics has the following exact gap-coordinate description.

### Boundary moves

An outward birth at the right edge occurs at rate `lambda` and prepends a zero gap:

$$
(g_1,g_2,\ldots)
\longmapsto
(0,g_1,g_2,\ldots).
\tag{6.1}
$$

If `g_1=0`, the rightmost particle dies at rate one, deleting the leading zero:

$$
(0,g_2,g_3,\ldots)
\longmapsto
(g_2,g_3,\ldots).
\tag{6.2}
$$

The mean current of these two particle-index moves is exactly

$$
J(\mu)=\lambda-\mu(g_1=0)
=\mu(\lambda-u_1).
\tag{6.3}
$$

### Bulk fragmentation

A positive gap `n>0` can receive a birth from either endpoint. For `n>1` these are the two transitions

$$
n\longmapsto(0,n-1),
\qquad
n\longmapsto(n-1,0),
$$

each at rate `lambda`. For `n=1`, both channels lead to `(0,0)`, with total rate `2lambda`.

### Bulk coagulation

The particle between adjacent gaps `(a,b)` dies at rate

$$
\mathbf1_{\{a=0\}}+\mathbf1_{\{b=0\}},
$$

and its deletion merges the two gaps:

$$
(a,b)\longmapsto a+b+1.
\tag{6.4}
$$

These rules expose a useful equilibrium structure. Put

$$
p=\frac1{1+\lambda},
\qquad
q=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},
$$

and let

$$
\gamma(n)=q p^n,
\qquad n\ge0.
\tag{6.5}
$$

The iid product law of geometric gaps is exactly the gap law induced by Bernoulli particle equilibrium of density `q`. The bulk fragmentation/coagulation moves satisfy detailed balance with respect to `gamma^otimes` because

$$
\frac{\gamma(0)\gamma(n-1)}{\gamma(n)}
=\frac qp
=\lambda.
\tag{6.6}
$$

For `n=1`, both forward channels and both reverse death clocks carry the same factor two, so the same identity applies.

The moving-front boundary is the only nonequilibrium forcing. For the paired boundary transition (6.1)--(6.2), the forward/reverse activity relative to the geometric reference is

$$
\frac{\lambda}{\gamma(0)}
=\frac\lambda q
=1+\lambda>1.
\tag{6.7}
$$

This is a useful interpretation of the sign problem: the bulk is reversible and the front injects particles at an activity strictly above the bulk equilibrium activity. A finite-state one-reservoir entropy-production argument would therefore force nonnegative current. On the present half-line, however, a semi-infinite tail can in principle carry entropy/current from particle-index infinity, so (6.7) by itself is **not** a proof of `J>0`.

This is the most promising structural route I found beyond local balance identities: prove that no invariant front law can sustain an incoming compensating flux from infinity, then use the positive boundary affinity. I have not made that last step rigorous.

## 7. First-gap balance hierarchy does not close locally

The target pattern `01` is exactly the event

$$
g_1=1.
$$

Write

$$
p_n=\mu(g_1=n).
$$

The first-gap process is not autonomous because deaths can expose or merge with `g_2`.

If `g_1=n>0`, then:

- two rate-`lambda` events reset `g_1` to zero (outward edge birth and birth from the front into the gap);
- one rate-`lambda` event changes `g_1` to `n-1` (birth from the left endpoint of the gap);
- if `g_2=0`, death of the next particle changes `g_1` to `n+1` at rate one.

If `g_1=0`, edge death changes `g_1` to `g_2` at rate one, while death of the next particle changes it to `g_2+1` at rate `1+1_{g_2=0}`.

For example stationarity of the event `{g_1=1}` gives the exact identity

$$
3\lambda p_1
+\mu(g_1=1,g_2=0)
=
\lambda p_2
+\mu(g_1=0,g_2=1)
+2\mu(g_1=0,g_2=0).
\tag{7.1}
$$

The first-bit identity (4.1) is the corresponding closed equation for `{g_1=0}`. Equation (7.1) shows where the next obstruction enters: bounding `p_1` requires genuine information about the next gap, and iterating generates a hierarchy rather than a closed one-gap birth--death chain.

## 8. A rigorous no-go: the nearest-gap corrector cannot beat `1/3`

The need for deeper front information is not an artifact of the finite bit encoding.

Consider **any** corrector of the form

$$
H=R+h(g_1),
$$

where `h:N_0->R`; boundedness is irrelevant for the following necessary conditions. Fix the gauge `h(0)=0` and write `h_1=h(1)`.

Suppose its statewise drift were strictly positive for every semi-infinite gap configuration.

### Dense state `(g_1,g_2)=(0,0)`

An outward edge birth contributes `+1` at rate `lambda`; edge death contributes `-1` at rate one; death of the next particle changes `g_1` from zero to one at total rate two. Therefore

$$
\mathcal L H
=\lambda-1+2h_1.
$$

Strict positivity forces

$$
h_1>\frac{1-\lambda}{2}.
\tag{8.1}
$$

### Isolated one-vacancy gap: `g_1=1`, `g_2>0`

There is no death of the next particle. Three distinct rate-`lambda` births change the observable:

- outward edge birth: increment `1-h_1`;
- birth from the front into the gap: increment `-h_1`;
- birth from the other endpoint: increment `-h_1`.

Hence

$$
\mathcal L H
=\lambda(1-3h_1).
$$

Strict positivity forces

$$
h_1<\frac13.
\tag{8.2}
$$

Combining (8.1)--(8.2) gives

$$
\lambda>\frac13.
\tag{8.3}
$$

Thus no corrector which sees only the distance to the next particle can enter the small-parameter regime. The improvements below `1/3`, including verified `BABP-EDGE-001`, necessarily exploit deeper gap correlations. This is the gap-coordinate form of the one-site historical obstruction.

## 9. The finite-seed front already has positive invariant current for every parameter

This separates the physical finite-seed front from the worst-invariant-front criterion.

Start BABP from the singleton `{0}` and let `U_t` be its right-edge environment, with right and left edges `R_t,L_t`. The right-edge compensator is exact:

$$
\mathbf E R_t
=\int_0^t
\mathbf E[\lambda-U_1(s)]\,ds.
\tag{9.1}
$$

Martinelli--Shapira--Toninelli (2025), Application 1, proves that for every `lambda>0` there is `c_lambda>0` such that the particle number grows linearly from the singleton; their proof gives an exponential lower-tail estimate. In particular,

$$
\liminf_{t\to\infty}
\frac{\mathbf E|B_t|}{t}
\ge c_\lambda>0.
\tag{9.2}
$$

For every finite configuration,

$$
R_t-L_t\ge |B_t|-1.
$$

The singleton law is reflection symmetric, so

$$
\mathbf E R_t=-\mathbf E L_t
$$

and hence

$$
\liminf_{t\to\infty}
\frac{\mathbf E R_t}{t}
\ge\frac{c_\lambda}{2}>0.
\tag{9.3}
$$

Define the Cesaro front laws

$$
\overline\mu_t
=\frac1t\int_0^t\operatorname{Law}(U_s)\,ds.
$$

Compactness gives weak limit points. For each cylinder `f`,

$$
\overline\mu_t(Q_\infty f)
=\frac{\mathbf E[f(U_t)-f(U_0)]}{t}
\longrightarrow0,
$$

and the core argument of Section 3 makes every such limit invariant. Moreover, by (9.1), for every subsequential Cesaro limit `mu_phys`,

$$
\mu_{\rm phys}(\lambda-u_1)
=\lim\frac{\mathbf E R_t}{t}
\ge\frac{c_\lambda}{2}>0
\tag{9.4}
$$

along the chosen subsequence (using the `liminf` lower bound).

Therefore:

> **Every invariant front law selected as a Cesaro limit from the singleton has strictly positive mean edge drift for every `lambda>0`.**

This is an all-parameter positive result, but it is not enough for Theorem 3.1, which sees the **worst** invariant semi-infinite front law.

Consequently, if the FRONT-GAP LEMMA is false, any counterexample must be a genuinely different invariant semi-infinite-tail phase which is not selected by the singleton front.

## 10. Why a hostile semi-infinite phase cannot simply be dismissed

At the endpoint `lambda=0`, the front process is highly non-unique. For example

$$
(0,1,0,1,0,1,\ldots)
$$

is absorbing: there are no births, no adjacent tail particles can die, and `u_1=0` prevents edge death. More generally there are many hard-core absorbing tails with `u_1=0`.

This does not produce a counterexample at any positive `lambda`, but it shows that the limit `lambda downarrow 0` is singular and that uniqueness of the front law cannot be justified by continuity from zero.

It also explains the logical strength of the finite LP. The adversarial boundary bit can approximate longer and longer hostile tails. The compactness argument in Theorem 3.1 proves that any obstruction surviving all window sizes is not merely a finite-LP artifact: it converges to a genuine invariant semi-infinite front law.

Conversely, the all-parameter growth theorem only proves positivity for the finite-tail basin selected from a finite seed. It does not by itself exclude another invariant phase at infinity.

## 11. Precise next lemma

The cleanest non-circular sufficient statement I can isolate is:

### FRONT-UNIQUENESS LEMMA

For every fixed `lambda>0`, `Q_infinity` has a unique invariant probability law `mu_lambda` on `{0,1}^N`.

If this lemma holds, Section 9 implies

$$
\mu_\lambda(\lambda-u_1)>0.
$$

Theorem 3.1 then gives

$$
\lim_k v_k(\lambda)>0.
$$

Because `v_k(lambda)` is nondecreasing in `k`, some finite `k` has

$$
v_k(\lambda)>0.
$$

Verified `BABP-CONV-001` then proves finite-seed convergence for that fixed `lambda`. Repeating this pointwise for every positive `lambda` gives the all-parameter theorem.

Front uniqueness is stronger than necessary. An exact weaker replacement is any theorem excluding invariant laws outside the closed convex hull of the singleton Cesaro limit set, since every law in that limit set has the common positive drift lower bound (9.4). I do not currently know how to prove either statement.

A tempting coupling argument is to wait for many consecutive outward edge births, which overwrite a long initial segment of the front. This does not prove uniqueness: later edge deaths shift the frame back into the untouched tail. Controlling the probability of such returns is essentially a positive-current estimate and would be circular.

The gap representation suggests a different route. The bulk is reversible with geometric gap law and the front boundary has positive affinity `log(1+lambda)`. If one can prove that an invariant half-line front cannot receive compensating entropy/free-energy flux from particle-index infinity, stationarity should force nonnegative boundary current, with strictness then requiring a separate equality analysis. I have not established the required entropy-flux bound and do not promote this as a lemma.

## 12. Status and handoff

The front route is **not refuted**, but the all-parameter front-gap lemma is not proved. The work narrows the issue from a local probability inequality to a phase-selection question for a one-boundary-driven reversible bulk system.
