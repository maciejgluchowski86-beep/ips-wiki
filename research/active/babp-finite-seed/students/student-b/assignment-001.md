The active target is:

> For every `lambda>0`, prove that one-dimensional BABP started from a finite nonempty particle set converges locally to the Bernoulli product equilibrium of particle density `lambda/(1+lambda)`.

Classical work proves this for `lambda>0.0347`. The 2025 paper proves, for every `lambda>0`, exponential ergodicity of the double-flipping process (DFP), all-parameter linear growth of BABP from finite seeds, and exponential BABP convergence from Bernoulli product initial laws. The point of this assignment is to decide whether these newer inputs actually move the old finite-seed threshold.

Do not begin with patch-weight algebra. The first job is to understand the exact historical obstruction.

## 1. Reconstruct the best finite-seed proof and the number `0.0347`

Give a mathematically complete dependency map of Sudbury's finite-seed convergence theorem.

Identify the earliest load-bearing statement that fails or is not proved below `lambda=0.0347`. Do not merely quote the numerical threshold. Derive the inequality, polynomial, submartingale drift condition, edge-speed estimate, coupling probability, or other calculation that produces it.

Translate all parameters into one consistent convention. Use

$$
q=\frac{\lambda}{1+\lambda},
\qquad
p=\frac1{1+\lambda}
$$

when comparing with the canonical patch paper.

Separate the proof into:

- statements now known for every `lambda>0` because of the 2025 paper;
- statements still genuinely threshold-dependent;
- statements that are only technical artifacts of the old proof and plausibly replaceable.

If later literature improved, superseded, or solved the threshold, report that immediately with exact theorem references.

## 2. Re-derive the self-duality convergence criterion

For finite initial `B` and finite test set `B'`, BABP self-duality uses

$$
F_{B'}(C)
=
\left(-\frac1\lambda\right)^{|C\cap B'|}.
$$

Write carefully why

$$
\mathbf E_B F_{B'}(B(t))\longrightarrow0
$$

for every nonempty finite `B'` is enough for local convergence to equilibrium. Check the equilibrium value directly and make clear which finite family of such functions spans local observables.

The small-`lambda` difficulty is that `|1/lambda|>1`; do not take absolute values unless the resulting quantity is genuinely controllable.

## 3. Test the 2025 DFP quasi-duality on the actual finite-test observable

Let

$$
y=\sqrt{1+\lambda}.
$$

The BABP--DFP quasi-duality is

$$
\mathbf E\left[
\left(\frac1{y+1}\right)^{|D\cap B(t)|}
\left(\frac{-1}{y-1}\right)^{|D^c\cap B(t)|}
\right]
=
\mathbf E\left[
\left(\frac1{y+1}\right)^{|B\cap D(t)|}
\left(\frac{-1}{y-1}\right)^{|B\cap D^c(t)|}
\right].
$$

Determine whether the finite-test self-duality function `F_{B'}` can be represented exactly as:

- one such quasi-duality observable with a deterministic or random initial `D`;
- a finite signed mixture of them;
- a product/tensor combination on `B'`;
- or a controlled approximation whose coefficients remain bounded well enough as `lambda -> 0`.

Do this explicitly first for

$$
B'=\{0\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
B'=\{0,1\}.
$$

If there is a representation, combine it with Theorem 5.2 and state exactly what remains to prove. If there is no stable representation, prove the obstruction as an algebraic statement rather than saying that the formulas look incompatible.

A useful way to organize the finite-set algebra is to view all multiplicative kernels on `{0,1}^{B'}` as vectors and ask what cone or linear span is generated by the DFP quasi-duality kernels when the law of `D cap B'` varies. Track coefficient norms as `lambda -> 0`; an exact representation with exponentially large coefficients may be useless.

## 4. Check whether all-parameter linear growth replaces any old ingredient

Martinelli--Shapira--Toninelli derive linear growth of `|B(t)|` from finite nonempty seeds for every `lambda>0`. Determine precisely where Sudbury's argument previously needed a growth/edge-speed estimate and whether this new theorem removes that part of the threshold.

Do not confuse global cardinality growth with local equilibration. The question is whether the old proof plus the new growth theorem leaves one sharply isolated local statement.
