## Source qualifications and exact pinpoints

### Asymptotic strong Feller support uniqueness

Primary: Martin Hairer and Jonathan C. Mattingly, *Ergodicity of the 2D Navier-Stokes Equations with Degenerate Stochastic Forcing*, Annals of Mathematics 164 (2006), 993-1032, DOI `10.4007/annals.2006.164.993`.

- Proposition 3.12: practical gradient estimate implying asymptotic strong Feller.
- Theorem 3.16: if two ergodic invariant measures are distinct, an ASF point cannot lie in both supports.
- Corollary 3.17: if the semigroup is ASF and a point belongs to the support of every invariant measure, there is at most one invariant measure.
- Theorem 4.1: ASF for the degenerate stochastic Navier-Stokes semigroup on the invariant subspace generated by the forced modes.
- Corollary 4.2: uniqueness of the invariant measure after the support/accessibility argument.

The page is deliberately a **uniqueness criterion**. It does not claim existence or quantitative convergence from ASF alone.

### Hörmander-Malliavin verification of ASF

Primary: Martin Hairer and Jonathan C. Mattingly, *A Theory of Hypoellipticity and Unique Ergodicity for Semilinear Stochastic PDEs*, Electronic Journal of Probability 16 (2011), 658-738, DOI `10.1214/EJP.v16-875`.

- Sections 5.2-5.6: transfer of an initial-condition variation to a perturbation of the driving Wiener path and control of the residual derivative.
- Section 6.2, especially Theorems 6.7 and 6.12: quantitative lower bounds preventing the Malliavin covariance from being nearly singular on fixed regular finite-dimensional projections under the bracket hypotheses.
- Section 7: Wiener-polynomial estimates with nonadapted coefficients, replacing the finite-dimensional Norris-lemma step needed for the bracket iteration.
- Sections 8.3-8.4, including Theorem 8.21 and Remark 8.22: reaction-diffusion/Ginzburg-Landau applications and the resulting unique-ergodicity conclusions once the abstract smoothing and accessibility inputs are assembled.

This remains separate from the 2006 ASF page. The 2006 page says **what asymptotic smoothing plus support overlap proves**; the 2011 page says **how degenerate SPDE noise can be shown to generate that smoothing** through brackets, Malliavin covariance and parabolic dissipation. These pages should be cross-linked during integration rather than merged.

### Swendsen-Wang / heat-bath comparison

Primary: Mario Ullrich, *Comparison of Swendsen-Wang and Heat-Bath Dynamics*, Random Structures & Algorithms 42 (2013), 520-535, DOI `10.1002/rsa.20431`.

- Section 2.3, equations (2)-(5): Edwards-Sokal joint representation and the conditional kernels relating Potts spins to FK bonds; Swendsen-Wang is represented as a composition of these conditional operators.
- Theorem 1: explicit bounded-degree spectral-gap comparison from single-site heat-bath to Swendsen-Wang.
- Section 3.1, Lemma 5: the sandwich `Q = P_HB P_SW P_HB` has spectral gap at least that of heat bath.
- Lemmas 6-7: pointwise control of Swendsen-Wang transition probabilities under graph-edge removal and one-spin endpoint perturbations; these estimates compare `Q` back to `P_SW`.
- Corollaries 2-3: concrete Potts/Ising rapid-relaxation applications on the square lattice and in bounded-degree high-temperature regimes.

The interface is exact joint-representation/operator comparison. It does not route a heat-bath move through a path of Swendsen-Wang transitions, so it is distinct from the live canonical-path/Dirichlet comparison page.

### Entropic Ricci curvature perturbation

Primary: Matthias Erbar, Christopher Henderson, Georg Menz and Prasad Tetali, *Ricci Curvature Bounds for Weakly Interacting Markov Chains*, Electronic Journal of Probability 22 (2017), Paper No. 40, 23 pp., DOI `10.1214/17-EJP49`.

- Definition 2.1: entropic Ricci curvature is convexity of relative entropy along geodesics of the discrete transport metric.
- Proposition 2.2: equivalent Bochner-type Hessian inequality.
- Section 2.3: positive curvature implies MLSI, Poincare, a modified Talagrand inequality, exponential entropy/variance relaxation and contraction in the discrete transport metric.
- Theorem 1.1, restated as Theorem 3.9: for a commuting mapping representation, the explicit jump-rate defect quantity in equation (1.1) gives `Ric >= 2 lambda` when `lambda >= 0`.
- Corollary 4.3: high-temperature d-dimensional nearest-neighbour Ising application.
- Corollary 4.4: Curie-Weiss application with a size-uniform positive curvature regime.

Pinpoint discipline note: the spin applications are **Corollaries 4.3 and 4.4**. I checked these numbers directly before staging because the previous wave exposed how easy a correct theorem statement can acquire an incorrect well-formed theorem number.

This entry is distinct from the live discrete Bochner-Bakry-Emery entropy page, but the overlap is intentional and should be cross-linked. The source itself translates Ricci curvature to a Bochner Hessian inequality and notes that Ricci implies convex entropy decay. The new object is the entropy-geodesic curvature in the discrete transport geometry and the perturbative rate-defect criterion for proving it; the resulting package also includes transport-entropy and transport-contraction consequences. It is likewise distinct from pathwise Wasserstein coupling, since no coupling of two sample trajectories is constructed.

## Taxonomy decisions

- **Targets 1 and 2 stay separate.** ASF support separation is an abstract invariant-law uniqueness interface; Hörmander-Malliavin propagation is a source-supported verification architecture for producing ASF in degenerate semilinear SPDEs. Integration should cross-link them in both directions.
- **Target 3 stays separate from canonical paths.** Ullrich uses the Edwards-Sokal joint law, conditional Markov operators, an operator sandwich, and pointwise kernel domination. There is no multistep path simulation or congestion bound.
- **Target 4 stays separate from the existing Bochner page, with an explicit bridge.** Entropic Ricci curvature is geodesic entropy convexity in a discrete transport geometry. Proposition 2.2 makes Bochner calculus an equivalent verification tool, so the two interfaces overlap mathematically but are organized around different reusable objects and have different downstream consequences.
