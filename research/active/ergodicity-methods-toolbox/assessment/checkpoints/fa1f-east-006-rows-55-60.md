| 55 | Finite-volume coercivity plus semigroup exhaustion | C | This is the correct *limiting decomposition* to test any finite-volume route: finite-speed error + finite-volume relaxation + limiting equilibrium term, with box size coupled to time. For the Bernoulli quench, however, the natural density of `mu_{q0}` relative to `mu_q` on a box of length `n` has an `L2` cost exponential in `n`; finite speed requires `n` proportional to `t`. A black-box use of the known FA spectral gap therefore needs an exponential relaxation rate large enough to beat an exponential-in-`t` initial-density cost, which is not available uniformly in the low-vacancy regime. A local-start/screening estimate avoiding the global density ratio would be the new theorem. Pointer: `finite-volume-coercivity-exhaustion-uniqueness.md`; rows 23, 30, 43. |
| 56 | Finite-speed finite-volume transfer | C | Quantitative graphical localization is indispensable infrastructure for converting any finite-window estimate to the infinite system, but the checked theorem controls equilibrium variance. Applied naively to the quench it again forces a box of order `t`; existing finite-volume FA relaxation estimates do not by themselves control the singular product start on that box. The method becomes useful after a regeneration/screen theorem reduces the effective memory region. Pointer: `finite-speed-finite-volume-transfer.md`. |
| 57 | Tightness/compactness construction of infinite-particle dynamics | N | FA infinite-volume dynamics and its product stationary law are already well defined. Compactness of stationary finite-volume path laws would only recover existence/subsequential limits, not select the quench limit or remove the trap component. Pointer: `tightness-compactness-infinite-particle-dynamics.md`, limitations. |
| 58 | Projective consistency of Gibbs marginals | N | FA equilibrium is already the explicit Bernoulli product law, and exact projective extension constructs an invariant law rather than proving dynamical attraction to it. | 59 | Number rigidity / tail Dirichlet ergodicity | N | The method is built for locally finite point fields with number rigidity and symmetric configuration-space diffusions. Bernoulli FA has no number-rigidity structure, and `L2(mu_q)` ergodicity is already weaker than the open quench target. Pointer: `number-rigidity-tail-dirichlet-ergodicity.md`. |
| 60 | Relative-entropy-loss Gibbs attractor | X | This page looks superficially ideal because the stationary Gibbs specification for FA is the unique Bernoulli product specification and the initial Bernoulli quench is translation invariant. But the load-bearing dynamical Gibbs variational principle (Theorem 2.6) assumes rate irreducibility R6: from every configuration, every finite local replacement must be reachable by a finite positive-rate path. Hard FA violates R6 at the all-ones absorbing configuration, which cannot create a first vacancy. Thus the theorem cannot be applied to hard FA; dropping R6 would require a new zero-entropy-loss characterization strong enough to distinguish the Bernoulli Gibbs law from blocked classes. Primary pointer: Jahnel--Koppl (2023), assumptions R1--R6, Theorem 2.6, Remark 2.7; live page `relative-entropy-loss-gibbs-attractor.md`. |

## Batch conclusion

No new A/B method. Rows 55--56 clarify the quantitative role of the eventual screen: once a local regeneration theorem has reduced exterior memory, finite-speed and finite-volume coercivity are ready-made downstream tools. Without such a screen, global density-ratio growth defeats the naive exhaustion route in precisely the low-vacancy regime of interest.

The entropy-attractor theorem is a useful **hard negative**: its irreducibility hypothesis fails exactly because hard FA retains blocked communicating classes. It must not be rediscovered later as an apparent one-line solution.
