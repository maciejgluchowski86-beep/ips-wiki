## 1. Gray one-dimensional edge coalescence

**RULING: PASS**

### Attack

Gray's checked theorem uses attractiveness (or the repulsive transform) at the structural level, not merely as a convenient coupling choice. It supplies the hybrid identities that define left/right splice edges, keeps those edges ordered and noncrossing, and protects agreement between them. The residual positive-rates chamber contains genuinely non-attractive/non-repulsive systems, so none of those properties can be imported from Gray.

Student G's Bridge G therefore asks for substantial new mathematics: a nonmonotone grand coupling with scalar splice edges, noncrossing/coalescence, a protected region, local positive-rate repair, and a stationary edge-density argument. This is much more than applying Gray's theorem.

The main possible circularity is that "protected hybrid regions" could simply repackage attractiveness. The bridge avoids that only if the proposed coupled generator is explicitly constructed and the edge identities are verified directly without introducing a hidden spin order. The finite rational local feasibility test proposed by G is the correct first attack: infeasibility of no-crossing/protection for one unavoidable local interface pattern would kill the direct extension.

### Load-bearing reason

No established positive-rates obstruction proves that every nonmonotone grand coupling with ordered **interface variables** is impossible. The common-uniform `alpha(t)>1` result is coupling-specific, and the balanced-circulation theorem concerns scalar Foster/coboundary certificates, not arbitrary coupled transition rates. Gray's architecture also does not require the signed PR1 operator or a positive coefficient norm.

### If PASS

The bridge remains genuinely open because its missing object is a new coupled edge geometry, not an already-refuted norm or a theorem consequence known to fail.

### Cheapest next check

Do the joint local-rate LP together with Candidate 2. Add Gray constraints only after exact marginal feasibility is imposed: scalar interface preservation, no crossing, permanent coalescence after meeting, and protection of the region between adjacent splice edges. A local infeasibility certificate kills the direct Gray extension. A feasible small window is evidence only.

### Synthesis warning

PASS does **not** preserve the primary audit's practical separation between Gray and refined coupling. They should be treated as one research family: refined non-diagonal coupling is the concrete candidate mechanism for constructing the nonmonotone edge process. Nor does PASS mean the five-part Bridge G is already an A-level short lemma; final prioritization should rank the concrete local coupled-rate test ahead of the full Gray architecture.

## 2. Refined non-diagonal discrepancy coupling

**RULING: PASS**

### Attack

Gobron--Saada's source theorem is exclusion-specific and uses conservative jump structure plus monotonicity inequalities. Those theorem hypotheses do not hold for the present spin-flip model. The only transferable ingredient is the design principle: coupled transition rates are variables, and a move in one marginal may be paired with a different move in the other.

Bridge R is therefore not justified by the source theorem. It must be proved directly for this generator. Its required inequality

`bar L |D| <= -kappa |D|`

for every finite-disagreement pair is strong: it is a uniform positive coarse-Ricci/Hamming-curvature statement for a genuinely new local Markovian coupling. It is much stronger than fixed-site coalescence and stronger than what the conjecture logically requires.

Nevertheless the implication chain is correct if R holds. Finite propagation creates only an `O(t)` initial base, while the exponential disagreement contraction beats that growth. The existing `alpha(t)>1` certificate does not apply because it is for the common-uniform random map. The balanced-circulation Foster obstruction likewise does not apply unless the proposed coupling reduces to a scalar correction of that old process.

### Load-bearing reason

The candidate changes the coupled generator itself. None of the programme's exact no-go theorems covers all finite-range Markovian couplings that pair different microscopic sites. The bridge is also sharply falsifiable by a finite exact marginal-rate LP, so it is not an unbounded "better coupling" search.

### If PASS

The bridge remains genuinely open because the known coupling obstructions are specific to the common/basic coupling or scalar correctors, whereas R asks for a different admissible joint generator with an exact local drift property.

### Cheapest next check

Run the exact rational local coupled-rate LP at `P_h` before any infinite-volume work. Maximize `kappa` subject to exact marginal generators over every unavoidable local pair pattern. One certified pattern with optimum `kappa <= 0` kills R in its stated form. This is the highest-value first positive-rates experiment because the same variables can test the direct Gray constraints.

## 3. Information percolation / backward histories

**RULING: PASS**

### Attack

Lubetzky--Sly do not provide a theorem for arbitrary positive-rate IPS. Their Ising argument uses a specially chosen random-map representation, many oblivious updates, quantitative control of update supports, and model-specific estimates for red clusters. Near the hard residual point a naive branching-number estimate is expected to be poor, so importing high-temperature subcritical genealogy would be invalid.

There is nevertheless a legitimate positive-rates random-map starting point. Strict positivity permits state-independent reset-to-zero and reset-to-one marks at the minima of the corresponding flip rates; the remaining marks are local residual maps. Thus true oblivious events exist. The difficult question is whether the **minimal** backward supports that survive these resets are sparse enough in pair-intersection, not whether every ancestor dies.

This candidate does not collapse automatically to the trajectory-kernel Dobrushin coefficient one: the latter concerns total variation between full one-site path laws under two deterministic neighbour trajectories, whereas information percolation asks how much of a finite top set still carries time-zero information after averaging over the random maps. Nor is it ancestor-clan extinction; red clusters may survive.

The largest circularity risk is using an a priori mixing/magnetization estimate to prove red sparsity. Any positive-rates implementation must obtain the red-intersection estimate from the graphical history geometry itself (or from an independently proved local estimate), not from the desired ergodicity conclusion.

### Load-bearing reason

This is the cleanest shortlist item that genuinely bypasses PR1--PR4. It tolerates surviving histories and therefore avoids both common-coupling extinction and CFTP/clan finiteness. No existing no-go theorem controls the pair-intersection law of minimal backward supports for an optimized random-map decomposition.

### If PASS

The bridge remains genuinely open because sparse red-history intersection is strictly weaker than every ancestor dying and is not covered by the existing Dobrushin, Hamming-contraction, coefficient-norm, or signed-renewal obstructions.

### Cheapest next check

At `P_h`, enumerate admissible local random-map decompositions and compute finite-depth pair-support transfer for the intersection weight. Do **not** use a supercritical first-moment branching number as a kill criterion. A meaningful negative certificate would need to show, uniformly over admissible decompositions, a nondecaying lower bound for the red-pair intersection quantity or another obstruction directly tied to the Miller--Peres `L^2` interface.

## 4. Block coupling / joint-block stationary control

**RULING: DEMOTE**

### Attack

The live block-coupling theorem is an ordered block-resampling theorem. It requires tractable conditional block laws, a monotone coupling of those laws, and simulation/comparison back to the local chain. None of that structure is presently available in the stationary boundary-control hierarchy.

More importantly, G's displayed Bridge BC

`inf_{K_{N+ell}} m(s_N^+) + inf_{K_{N+ell}} m(s_N^-) >= delta D_N(h)`

is not yet an independent block-coupling estimate. By the exact scale-extension identity from Meeting 024, it is precisely equivalent to

`D_{N+ell}(h) <= (1-delta) D_N(h)`.

Meeting 024 already identified the missing theorem in essentially this form and stopped the current Bellman-corrector route because no independent mechanism controlled the **weighted adaptive** mismatch. It also proved that additive independently constructed block correctors cannot improve the endpoints, and that fixed/exogenous East relaxation does not control the adaptive feedback.

Calling the desired inequality "joint block coupling" does not itself supply the cross-block mechanism that Meeting 024 requires. Small-N LP ratios can diagnose the hierarchy but cannot validate a B architecture without a new structural inequality connecting a genuine block coupling to the weighted Bellman slacks.

### Load-bearing reason

As stated, BC is too close to the target itself: the exact Bellman identity turns it directly into geometric diameter collapse. The source method's actual load-bearing input—an independently controlled joint block conditional coupling—is missing. This is the precise repackaging risk the assessment protocol forbids.

### If DEMOTE

Strongest remaining auxiliary use: retain the exact `K_N` hierarchy and small-width LPs as a diagnostic for whether weighted cross-block slack is ever forced away from zero. A future upgrade would need a new theorem stated in variables *upstream* of `D_{N+ell} <= (1-delta)D_N`, for example a model-specific coupling/transport inequality that quantitatively lower-bounds the weighted Bellman mismatch.

### Cheapest next check

No proof block should be opened from the current BC statement. If another route independently produces a concrete two- or three-site boundary coupling inequality, test that inequality against the exact `K_N` LPs. Do not run larger-N searches for `delta` merely to rediscover the target numerically.

## 5. Physical-front regeneration adapted to the disagreement front

**RULING: DEMOTE**

### Attack

The Jara--Moreno--Ramirez source has a physical front advancing into genuinely fresh randomness. Its regeneration event prevents old particles from catching the front; after success, the translated post-front future has a fresh conditional law. The positive-rates common-uniform disagreement front does not currently have that renewal property.

Meetings 015--018 already developed the actual disagreement-front interface substantially: fixed-site permanent coalescence, convective-survival equivalence, a convergent finite-time random-map approximation, an exact first-exposure state retaining pre-exposure common spins, and quantitative first-discovery tails. Meeting 019 then hit the zero-frequency obstruction: the retained local state forgets right-ancestry capacity after coalescence, and the robust projected Bellman closure loses every strict factor. Under the explicit stopping rule, the **common-uniform global-coalescence/occupation mechanism was abandoned as a load-bearing proof interface**; enlarging the exposure state or adding ancestry counters was specifically not authorized.

Bridge FR returns to that same common-uniform finite-disagreement process and asks for a fixed finite/compact regeneration class, a fresh future, and a uniform terminal extinction hazard. Those are exactly the properties for which the prior front work found no closed state. A rightmost disagreement does have a permanently coupled half-line to its right, but the common spins near and behind the moving front still encode the previously exposed history; the source's "fresh region ahead" is therefore not supplied for free.

A genuinely new regeneration theorem is not logically impossible. The problem is assessment status: the present bridge does not identify the new state variable or screening event that defeats the Meeting-019 ancestry obstruction. It therefore reopens an explicitly stopped global-coalescence interface by naming the missing object "regeneration".

### Load-bearing reason

The candidate is not killed as a mathematical possibility: common-coupling extinction and regeneration were never disproved. It is demoted because the current bridge lacks a mechanism that is materially different from the stopped all-depth front/occupation problem, while the source theorem's crucial fresh-region hypothesis is absent.

### If DEMOTE

Strongest remaining auxiliary use: front variables remain valuable diagnostics. The accepted first-discovery and moving-frame estimates can falsify proposed renewal states or quantify approximate screening. A future A/B proposal would need to exhibit a specific finite regeneration sigma-field or reset event and prove that it erases the hidden ancestry identified in Meeting 019 before asking for terminal extinction.

### Cheapest next check

Do not begin with wider moving-frame truncations. First propose an explicit candidate regeneration sigma-field and test it on the two actual histories used in Meeting 019 that share the same visible projection but have different return probabilities. If the candidate cannot distinguish or erase those histories, the regeneration proposal is immediately circular. Only after passing that test should one compute cycle tails or extinction probabilities.
