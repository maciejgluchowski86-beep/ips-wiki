# Entropic Ricci curvature from weak-interaction perturbation

## Criterion

For a finite irreducible reversible Markov chain, Erbar, Henderson, Menz and Tetali use the entropic Ricci curvature associated with the discrete transport distance W of Maas and Erbar-Maas. Definition 2.1 says `Ric >= κ` when relative entropy is κ-convex along constant-speed W-geodesics between probability densities.

Theorem 1.1, restated as Theorem 3.9, gives a directly checkable weak-interaction criterion. Write the generator using commuting bijective moves δ in a mapping representation,

`L f(x) = sum_delta c(x,delta) [f(delta x)-f(x)]`,

and let π be the reversible law. Set

`q(x,delta,eta) = c(x,delta) c(x,eta) π(x)`

and let `q_star(x,delta,eta)` be the minimum of the four values of `q` obtained around the commuting square generated by δ and η, with inverse moves used on the opposite edges. Equation (1.1) defines λ as the smallest jump rate after subtracting the explicit reverse-move term and the sum of the defects `q-q_star` caused by inhomogeneity of the interacting rates. If `λ >= 0`, then

`Ric >= 2 λ`.

The criterion reduces positive curvature to a quantitative statement that weak interactions do not perturb the commuting product-chain geometry too strongly.

## Mechanism

The proof interface is geometric. The discrete transport metric W is chosen so that the heat equation generated by the chain is the gradient flow of relative entropy. Positive entropic Ricci curvature means that this entropy has a uniformly positive Hessian along W-geodesics.

Proposition 2.2 converts the geodesic-convexity statement into an equivalent Bochner-type Hessian inequality `B(rho,psi) >= κ A(rho,psi)`. Section 3 reorganizes the Hessian using the mapping representation. For a product chain the commuting-square contributions are nonnegative. Weak interactions make the rates depend on the surrounding configuration; the `q-q_star` defects measure exactly how much positivity can be lost. Theorem 1.1 bounds those defects and leaves a positive curvature remainder.

Section 2.3 then converts positive curvature into several relaxation statements at once. If `Ric >= κ > 0`, the chain satisfies MLSI with constant κ in the paper's normalization, a Poincare inequality with constant κ, a modified Talagrand transport-entropy inequality, and exponential contraction in W. Thus curvature is stronger structure than merely proving one coercive inequality.

This method is related to, but distinct from, the live discrete Bochner-Bakry-Emery entropy page. The source itself makes the bridge explicit: Proposition 2.2 is a Bochner representation of the Ricci Hessian, and Ricci curvature implies convex entropy decay. The additional load-bearing object here is entropy-geodesic curvature in the discrete transport geometry, together with a perturbative rate criterion that proves it. It is also distinct from Wasserstein coupling contraction: W acts on probability densities and no coupling of two sample paths is constructed.

## Representative IPS use

Section 4 applies the criterion to Glauber spin dynamics. For a finite Ising model, individual spin flips form the commuting moves and the interaction changes their heat-bath rates. Corollary 4.3 obtains a positive curvature bound for the d-dimensional nearest-neighbour Ising model in an explicit high-temperature regime, uniformly in the system size.

Corollary 4.4 treats the Curie-Weiss model and again obtains a positive, size-uniform curvature bound in an explicit weak-interaction regime. Through Section 2.3 these curvature estimates immediately provide MLSI and Poincare inequalities and hence quantitative entropy and variance relaxation. The paper also treats hard-core Glauber dynamics, where the perturbative condition recovers a known entropy-decay regime.

## Limitations

The criterion is perturbative and typically applies only at sufficiently high temperature or weak activity; it is not designed to reach criticality. The main theorem assumes a finite reversible chain and, in its cleanest form, commuting move maps. The paper develops a separate noncommutative version for certain Cayley-graph walks, but that does not make the criterion automatic for arbitrary IPS generators.

Positive curvature gives strong functional inequalities, but a failure of the explicit λ bound does not imply failure of MLSI, spectral gap, or ergodicity. The bound measures the success of this particular curvature perturbation argument.
