\section{Coarsened patch measures and the remaining problem}
\label{sec:coarsening}

The positive and negative theorems can be placed in one measure-theoretic family. Fix an observation point $(t,x)$ and a finite decorated skeleton $\tau$. Let $\mu_\tau$ be its intrinsic signed measure on the canonical raw mark space $\cM_\tau$, so that
\begin{equation*}
  \mu_\tau(\cM_\tau)=F_\tau(t,x).
\end{equation*}
Choose a measurable map
\begin{equation*}
  \cC_\tau:\cM_\tau\longrightarrow\overline{\cM}_\tau
\end{equation*}
and set
\begin{equation}
  \overline\mu_\tau
  =
  (\cC_\tau)_{\#}\mu_\tau.
  \label{eq:coarsening-main}
\end{equation}
We call $(\cC_\tau)_\tau$ a \emph{coarsening scheme}.

\subsection{The total-variation criterion}

Suppose $\pi$ is a full-support probability mass function on the finite decorated skeletons. Conditional on $S=\tau$, choose any positive proposal $Q_\tau$ dominating $\overline\mu_\tau$ and return the canonical coarsened importance-sampling weight
\begin{equation*}
  Y
  =
  \frac1{\pi(\tau)}
  \frac{d\overline\mu_\tau}{dQ_\tau}.
\end{equation*}
Then
\begin{equation}
  \E\abs Y
  =
  \sum_{\tau\in\cT}
  \norm{\overline\mu_\tau}_{\TV}.
  \label{eq:coarsened-L1-sum}
\end{equation}
The skeleton proposal and the conditional proposals cancel from the first moment. Thus the canonical coarsened estimator is in $L^1$ exactly when
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{\tau\in\cT}
  \norm{(\cC_\tau)_{\#}\mu_\tau}_{\TV}
  <\infty.
  \label{eq:coarsening-open-sum}
\end{equation}

For every coarsening,
\begin{equation}
  \abs{F_\tau(t,x)}
  \leq
  \norm{(\cC_\tau)_{\#}\mu_\tau}_{\TV}
  \leq
  \norm{\mu_\tau}_{\TV}.
  \label{eq:coarsening-sandwich}
\end{equation}
The left inequality follows from the total mass, and the right inequality from contraction of total variation under pushforward.

\subsection{Three locations in the coarsening order}

\paragraph{Identity.}
If $\cC_\tau=\Id$, then $\overline\mu_\tau=\mu_\tau$. For the fixed smooth datum of~\cref{thm:raw-faithful-obstruction}, the subseries over the long combs already has infinite total variation. Thus the identity coarsening is impossible in $L^1$.

\paragraph{Constant map.}
If $\cC_\tau$ is constant, then
\begin{equation*}
  (\cC_\tau)_{\#}\mu_\tau
  =
  F_\tau(t,x)\,\delta_*.
\end{equation*}
Hence
\begin{equation*}
  \norm{(\cC_\tau)_{\#}\mu_\tau}_{\TV}
  =
  \abs{F_\tau(t,x)}.
\end{equation*}
Under the C-prime smallness condition, the sum of these quantities is finite. Thus the constant coarsening is exactly the skeleton-averaged estimator.

Equation~\eqref{eq:coarsening-sandwich} shows more: the constant coarsening minimizes total variation skeleton by skeleton. In this precise sense, C-prime is total-variation optimal within the deterministic coarsening class. This does not imply that every nonconstant coarsening diverges. It only identifies the smallest possible first moment after a fixed skeleton has been chosen.

\paragraph{Gaussian-bridge coarsening.}
For a consecutive Hessian chain with durations $r_1,\ldots,r_m$ and Gaussian increments $Z_1,\ldots,Z_m$, let $\cC_\tau$ retain the total displacement
\begin{equation*}
  Y=\sum_{j=1}^m\sqrt{r_j}Z_j
\end{equation*}
and forget the $m-1$ bridge coordinates. The conditional score identity~\eqref{eq:bridge-score} shows that a bare chain then carries one $\He_{2m}$ endpoint weight rather than $m$ separate $\He_2$ weights. After the fixed-total-time simplex is integrated, the coefficient has geometric rather than factorial growth. Spatial multipliers prevent a literal one-score collapse for a complete patch, but deterministic commutator estimates show the same type of geometric cancellation after the corresponding interior variables are averaged. This makes Gaussian-bridge coarsening a natural intermediate point between identity and complete averaging.

\begin{problem}[Nonconstant integrable coarsening]
\label{prob:nonconstant-coarsening}
In the small-data regime of~\cref{thm:cprime}, does there exist a family of nonconstant coarsenings $(\cC_\tau)_\tau$ which retains nontrivial continuous interior information and satisfies
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{\tau\in\cT}
  \norm{(\cC_\tau)_{\#}\mu_\tau}_{\TV}
  <\infty?
\end{equation*}
A first candidate is a patchwise Gaussian-bridge coarsening, possibly combined with signed couplings across side-patch variables.
\end{problem}

A positive answer would give an $L^1$ representation with genuine continuous randomness but without raw-faithfulness. A negative answer would require an obstruction stable under all nonconstant coarsenings of the allowed type, which is strictly stronger than the raw-faithful theorem. The present paper establishes the two endpoints and the proposal-invariant obstruction at the identity; it does not decide~\cref{prob:nonconstant-coarsening}.
