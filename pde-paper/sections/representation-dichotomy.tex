\section{A representation-level dichotomy}
\label{sec:dichotomy}

The coding-tree obstruction by itself does not distinguish a difficult PDE from a difficult representation. The following benchmark shows that the latter distinction is real.

Fix $\eta\neq0$ and
\begin{equation*}
  0<a\leq
  \frac{\erfc(1/\sqrt2)}{\sqrt3},
  \qquad
  \erfc(r)=\frac2{\sqrt\pi}\int_r^\infty e^{-s^2}\,ds.
\end{equation*}
Consider
\begin{equation}
  \partial_tu+\frac12u_{xx}
  +\eta\pa{e^{(u_x)^4}-1}=0,
  \qquad
  u(T,x)=a\cos x.
  \label{eq:dichotomy-benchmark}
\end{equation}
Put
\begin{equation}
  T_*(\eta)
  =
  \frac1{2\pi e(e-1)^2\eta^2}.
  \label{eq:T-star}
\end{equation}

For the marked branching representation of Henry-Labord\`ere, Oudjane, Tan, Touzi, and Warin~\cite{HenryLabordereEtAl2019}, take monomial types and coefficients
\begin{equation*}
  L=\set{(0,4r):r\geq1},
  \qquad
  c_r=\frac\eta{r!},
  \qquad
  p_r=\frac1{(e-1)r!},
\end{equation*}
and lifetime density
\begin{equation}
  \rho_T(s)
  =
  \frac{s^{-1/2}e^{-s/(2T)}}{\sqrt{2\pi T}},
  \qquad s>0.
  \label{eq:HLOTW-lifetime}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Representation-level dichotomy]
\label{thm:representation-dichotomy}
For~\eqref{eq:dichotomy-benchmark} the following statements hold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(\roman*)}]
\item Let
\begin{equation*}
  f(z_0,z_1)=\eta(e^{z_1^4}-1).
\end{equation*}
For every $T>0$, $0\leq t<T$, and $x\in\R$, the Nguwi--Penent--Privault coding-tree functionals satisfy
\begin{equation*}
  \E\abs{H(\mathcal T_{t,x,f^*})}=\infty,
  \qquad
  \E\abs{H(\mathcal T_{t,x,\Id})}=\infty.
\end{equation*}
The conclusion holds for every strictly positive coding-tree lifetime density and the positive mechanism-selection probabilities of the construction.

\item If $0<T<T_*(\eta)$ and the HLOTW estimator uses the data above, then its marked branching functional $\psi_{t,x}$ satisfies
\begin{equation*}
  \E\abs{\psi_{t,x}}^2\leq1,
  \qquad
  0\leq t\leq T,
  \quad x\in\R.
\end{equation*}
Moreover
\begin{equation*}
  u(t,x)=\E[\psi_{t,x}]
\end{equation*}
is a continuous viscosity solution of~\eqref{eq:dichotomy-benchmark}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The lifetime density~\eqref{eq:HLOTW-lifetime} is singular at the origin. The theorem uses the positive-time extension of the HLOTW moment argument: internal lifetimes are almost surely positive, and the proof of the second-moment bound evaluates $\rho$ only at positive arguments. This is the only modification to the literal endpoint-continuity wording of the published assumptions.

\begin{proof}
We first prove the NPP statement. The terminal datum is $\phi(x)=a\cos x$, so
\begin{equation*}
  \phi''(x)=-a\cos x\not\equiv0.
\end{equation*}
Thus the direction $j=1$ is active in~\cref{thm:repeated-hessian}. Since
\begin{equation*}
  e^{z^4}
  =
  \sum_{q=0}^\infty\frac{z^{4q}}{q!},
\end{equation*}
termwise differentiation gives, for real $z$ and $r\geq1$,
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_z^{4r}e^{z^4}
  =
  \sum_{q=r}^\infty
  \frac{(4q)!}{q!(4q-4r)!}z^{4(q-r)}
  \geq
  \frac{(4r)!}{r!}.
\end{equation*}
Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \abs{\partial_{z_1}^{4r}f(z_0,z_1)}
  \geq
  \abs\eta\frac{(4r)!}{r!}
  \label{eq:dichotomy-jet-lower}
\end{equation}
uniformly on $\R^2$. For every bounded set $B$ of positive measure,
\begin{equation*}
  D_{2r}(B;f,1)
  \geq
  \abs B\,\abs\eta\frac{(4r)!}{r!}.
\end{equation*}
Stirling's formula then implies
\begin{equation*}
  \limsup_{m\to\infty}
  \pa{\frac{D_m(B;f,1)}{m!}}^{1/m}
  =\infty.
\end{equation*}
The repeated-Hessian obstruction gives
\begin{equation*}
  \E\abs{H(\mathcal T_{t,x,f^*})}
  =\infty
\end{equation*}
for every positive remaining horizon.

For the identity code, the NPP mechanism has a unique child $f^*$. Condition on the first identity branching occurring before $T$. Its child is an $f^*$-rooted tree with positive remaining horizon, whose conditional absolute expectation is infinite by the preceding paragraph for every possible first branch time and position. Tonelli's theorem gives
\begin{equation*}
  \E\abs{H(\mathcal T_{t,x,\Id})}
  =\infty.
\end{equation*}

We next verify the HLOTW assumptions used in the moment estimate. The monomial series is absolutely convergent and equals the driver in~\eqref{eq:dichotomy-benchmark}. Moreover,
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{r\geq1}4rp_r
  =
  \frac{4e}{e-1}<\infty,
  \qquad
  \frac{\abs{c_r}}{p_r}
  =
  \abs\eta(e-1).
  \label{eq:HLOTW-offspring-check}
\end{equation}
The diffusion coefficients are constant, and the terminal datum has both supremum norm and Lipschitz constant equal to $a$.

The survival function of~\eqref{eq:HLOTW-lifetime} satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \overline F_T(T)
  =
  \erfc(1/\sqrt2).
  \label{eq:HLOTW-survival-T}
\end{equation}
Take $q=2$ in HLOTW Assumption 3.10. For a Brownian edge of duration $s-t$,
\begin{equation*}
  X_s-x=\sqrt{s-t}\,Z,
  \qquad
  \mathcal W_{t,s}=\frac Z{\sqrt{s-t}},
\end{equation*}
with $Z\sim N(0,1)$. The terminal constant in their $q=2$ estimate is therefore
\begin{equation*}
  C_{1,2}=3a^2,
\end{equation*}
because $\E Z^4=3$. In view of~\eqref{eq:HLOTW-survival-T}, the terminal condition becomes
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{3a^2}{\erfc(1/\sqrt2)^2}
  \leq1,
\end{equation*}
which is exactly the assumed restriction on $a$.

For the internal gradient marks, $C_{2,2}=\E Z^2=1$. Furthermore
\begin{equation*}
  \sqrt s\,\rho_T(s)
  =
  \frac{e^{-s/(2T)}}{\sqrt{2\pi T}},
\end{equation*}
so for $0<s\leq T$,
\begin{equation*}
  \frac1{\sqrt s\rho_T(s)}
  =
  \sqrt{2\pi T}\,e^{s/(2T)}
  \leq
  \sqrt{2\pi eT}.
\end{equation*}
Using~\eqref{eq:HLOTW-offspring-check}, the internal factor in Assumption 3.10 is bounded by
\begin{equation*}
  \abs\eta(e-1)\sqrt{2\pi eT}.
\end{equation*}
Thus the $q=2$ internal majorant is at most one provided
\begin{equation}
  2\pi e(e-1)^2\eta^2T\leq1,
  \label{eq:HLOTW-time-condition}
\end{equation}
which is equivalent to $T\leq T_*(\eta)$.

It remains only to justify the singular lifetime density at zero. The density~\eqref{eq:HLOTW-lifetime} is a probability density on $(0,\infty)$, and every sampled internal lifetime is strictly positive almost surely. The age-dependent branching genealogy is nonexplosive because~\eqref{eq:HLOTW-offspring-check} gives finite mean offspring number. Every density factor in the HLOTW estimator is evaluated at a strictly positive lifetime increment, while terminal particles use only the survival function. Hence the value $\rho_T(0)$ is never evaluated.

In the proof of the HLOTW $L^q$ estimate, the multiplicative majorant is a product of terminal factors
\begin{equation*}
  C_{1,q}\pa{\frac1{\overline F(\Delta T)}}^q
\end{equation*}
and internal factors
\begin{equation*}
  C_{2,q}
  \pa{
    \frac{\norm{c_I}_\infty}
    {p_I\sqrt{\Delta T}\rho(\Delta T)}
  }^q,
\end{equation*}
with $\Delta T>0$ almost surely. The checks above bound every such factor by one for $q=2$. The published uniform-integrability and first-branch arguments therefore go through unchanged when endpoint continuity of $\rho$ is replaced by continuity and positivity on $(0,T]$. For $T<T_*(\eta)$ this gives
\begin{equation*}
  \E\abs{\psi_{t,x}}^2\leq1.
\end{equation*}
The first-branch identity recovers the absolutely convergent monomial series for the driver, and the HLOTW argument then identifies $\E[\psi_{t,x}]$ with a continuous viscosity solution of~\eqref{eq:dichotomy-benchmark}.
\end{proof}

The theorem is deliberately representation-level. The NPP mechanism repeatedly differentiates the jet nonlinearity and therefore encounters the factorial scale~\eqref{eq:dichotomy-jet-lower}. The HLOTW mechanism instead branches over the monomial coefficients $\eta/r!$ and transfers only first spatial derivatives through marked Gaussian weights. The same mild equation is sampled by two different architectures with different absolute-moment behavior. This does not contradict the NPP Feynman--Kac theorem, whose all-code $L^1$ hypothesis fails in the benchmark.
