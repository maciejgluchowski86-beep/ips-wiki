\section{Raw-faithful patches have infinite first moment}
\label{sec:raw-faithful}

Theorem C-prime proves that averaging complete interiors can restore $L^1$. We now show that, for one fixed smooth datum, no change of positive proposal can achieve the same result while the canonical raw marked contribution remains the conditional barycenter.

\subsection{Right combs and the raw-faithful class}

A length-$m$ \emph{right comb} is the planar binary tree in which the right child continues through exactly $m$ consecutive quadratic branchings, while the left child at each branching is terminal, and the distinguished right child is terminal after the $m$-th branching. The choice of the right child is only a planar convention: the quadratic product is symmetric. It will matter later because patches are maximal left-child chains.

Fix an evaluation horizon $h>0$. We restrict to combs whose distinguished edge durations satisfy
\begin{equation*}
  r_j>0,
  \qquad
  r_1+\cdots+r_m<\frac h2.
\end{equation*}
The side leaves and final distinguished leaf then have remaining heat time in $[h/2,h]$.

Let $\Gamma_m$ be the raw cylinder on which the distinguished lineage has exactly this length-$m$ right-comb genealogy. Let $\mu_m$ denote the intrinsic signed measure on $\Gamma_m$, including its durations, centered Gaussian/Hermite marks, and terminal Brownian marks, after the positive lifetime, genealogy, and survival proposal factors have been cancelled against their reciprocal compensators. The cylinders $\Gamma_m$ are pairwise disjoint.

Let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,Q)$ be a simulation probability space with exposed canonical raw coordinate $R$. Write $Q_m$ for the restriction of the law of $R$ to $\Gamma_m$ and assume $\mu_m\ll Q_m$. By~\cref{def:raw-faithful}, an assumed $L^1(Q)$ estimator is raw-faithful on $\Gamma_m$ if its conditional barycenter there is $d\mu_m/dQ_m$.

\subsection{The fixed smooth datum}

Choose an integer $K\geq3$ and set
\begin{equation}
  N_m=K^m,
  \qquad
  b_m=(m!)^{-1/2}.
  \label{eq:Nm-bm}
\end{equation}
For $m_0$ sufficiently large and $\varepsilon>0$, define
\begin{equation}
  g_\varepsilon(x)
  =
  \varepsilon\br{
    \cos x+
    \sum_{m\geq m_0}
    b_m\cos(N_mx)
  }.
  \label{eq:fixed-smooth-g}
\end{equation}
For every fixed integer $k\geq0$,
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_{m\geq m_0}b_mN_m^k
  =
  \sum_{m\geq m_0}
  \frac{K^{km}}{\sqrt{m!}}
  <\infty,
\end{equation*}
since the ratio of consecutive terms is $K^k/\sqrt{m+1}$. Hence $g_\varepsilon\in C^\infty(\Torus)$ by termwise differentiation. It has zero mean and therefore equals $\phi_\varepsilon''$, where
\begin{equation}
  \phi_\varepsilon(x)
  =
  -\varepsilon\cos x
  -\varepsilon\sum_{m\geq m_0}
  \frac{b_m}{N_m^2}\cos(N_mx).
  \label{eq:fixed-smooth-phi}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Raw-faithful $L^1$ obstruction]
\label{thm:raw-faithful-obstruction}
Fix $T>0$ and $\lambda\neq0$. Let $\phi_\varepsilon$ be the single smooth datum~\eqref{eq:fixed-smooth-phi}. For every evaluation horizon $h\in(0,T]$ and every $x\in\Torus$, there is no $L^1$ estimator which is raw-faithful on the right-comb cylinders.

More precisely, let $(\Omega,\mathcal F,Q)$ be any simulation probability space whose raw coordinate $R$ has restrictions $Q_m$ satisfying $\mu_m\ll Q_m$. If a scalar estimator $Y(h,x)$ were in $L^1(Q)$ and satisfied, for every $m$,
\begin{equation*}
  \E_Q[Y(h,x)\mid\sigma(R)]
  =
  \frac{d\mu_m}{dQ_m}(R)
  \qquad Q\text{-a.s. on }\Gamma_m,
\end{equation*}
then necessarily
\begin{equation*}
  \E_Q\abs{Y(h,x)}=\infty,
\end{equation*}
a contradiction. Hence no such $L^1$ estimator exists.
The conclusion is unchanged if one changes the lifetime law, genealogy proposal, Gaussian proposal, or dependence structure of these proposals, or adds auxiliary conditionally unbiased randomness.
\end{theorem}

The theorem is driven by total variation rather than by an operator norm. For the frequency $N_m$ already present in~\eqref{eq:fixed-smooth-g}, the length-$m$ right comb satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}
  \geq
  A_h\varepsilon
  (C_h\abs\lambda\varepsilon)^m
  \frac{m^m}{\sqrt{m!}}
  \label{eq:comb-TV-lower}
\end{equation}
for all sufficiently large $m$. Thus
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_m\norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}=\infty.
\end{equation*}
The construction uses one fixed datum, not a sequence of generation-dependent tests.

\begin{proof}
We first prove the quantitative comb bound. Let $g$ be any smooth terminal Hessian datum with Fourier coefficients
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat g(1)=a_0,
  \qquad
  \widehat g(N)=b_0,
\end{equation*}
where $N>m$. Let $p_s^\Torus(x,y)$ be the periodic heat kernel and put
\begin{equation*}
  \kappa_h
  =
  \min_{\substack{h/2\leq s\leq h\\x,y\in\Torus}}
  p_s^\Torus(x,y)
  >0.
\end{equation*}
For an integer $k$, a leaf born at $x$, and a remaining time $s\in[h/2,h]$, define
\begin{equation*}
  \Psi_{k,s,x}(y)
  =
  \frac{\kappa_h}{p_s^\Torus(x,y)}
  e^{-ik(y-x)}.
\end{equation*}
Its modulus is at most one. Total-variation duality therefore permits this test, and
\begin{equation}
  \int_\Torus
  p_s^\Torus(x,y)g(y)\Psi_{k,s,x}(y)\,dy
  =
  2\pi\kappa_h\widehat g(k)e^{ikx}.
  \label{eq:raw-Fourier-leaf-projection}
\end{equation}
A complex test may be replaced by a real or imaginary part at the cost of a universal constant.

Apply~\eqref{eq:raw-Fourier-leaf-projection} with frequency $N$ to the final distinguished leaf and frequency $1$ to each of the $m$ side leaves. Because the comb is right-oriented, these are exactly the terminal factors encountered successively along the distinguished lineage. Starting from $e^{iNx}$, multiplication by the innermost side mode produces $e^{i(N+1)x}$, then $e^{i(N+2)x}$, and so on. The $m$ centered Hessian edges therefore act on frequencies
\begin{equation*}
  K_j\in\set{N+1,\ldots,N+m}.
\end{equation*}
For a pure mode $e^{iKx}$, one raw edge contributes
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{\He_2(Z)}r
  \pa{e^{iK\sqrt rZ}-1}.
\end{equation*}
Put
\begin{equation*}
  G(q)
  =
  \E\br{
    \abs{\He_2(Z)}\abs{e^{iqZ}-1}
  }.
\end{equation*}
There is $c_G>0$ such that $G(q)\geq c_G$ for every $q\geq1$. Indeed $G$ is continuous and strictly positive on compact subsets of $(0,\infty)$, while for large $q$ the Riemann--Lebesgue lemma prevents the oscillatory term from cancelling the positive $L^1$ mass of $\abs{\He_2}$.

Restrict every distinguished duration to the rectangular region
\begin{equation}
  N^{-2}
  \leq r_j\leq\frac h{4m},
  \qquad 1\leq j\leq m.
  \label{eq:raw-duration-box}
\end{equation}
which lies inside $\sum_jr_j<h/2$. Since $K_j\sqrt{r_j}\geq1$,
\begin{equation*}
  \int_{N^{-2}}^{h/(4m)}
  \frac{G(K_j\sqrt r)}r\,dr
  \geq
  c_G\log\pa{\frac{hN^2}{4m}}.
\end{equation*}
The fresh Gaussian marks are independent in the intrinsic comb measure. Taking the phases of the pure-mode factors into the total-variation test, multiplying the $m$ leaf projections and the $m$ coefficients $\abs\lambda$, and restricting to~\eqref{eq:raw-duration-box} gives constants $A_h,B_h>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}
  \geq
  A_h
  \pa{B_h\abs\lambda\abs{a_0}}^m
  \abs{b_0}
  \br{\log\pa{\frac{hN^2}{4m}}}^m.
  \label{eq:general-comb-TV}
\end{equation}
There is no simplex factor $1/m!$: the whole rectangular box is retained, and each edge contributes its own logarithmic integral.

For the fixed datum~\eqref{eq:fixed-smooth-g},
\begin{equation*}
  \abs{\widehat g_\varepsilon(1)}
  =\frac\varepsilon2,
  \qquad
  \abs{\widehat g_\varepsilon(N_m)}
  =\frac{\varepsilon b_m}{2}.
\end{equation*}
Take $N=N_m=K^m$ in~\eqref{eq:general-comb-TV}. Then
\begin{equation*}
  \log\pa{\frac{hN_m^2}{4m}}
  =
  2m\log K-\log m+O_h(1)
  \geq
  c_Km
\end{equation*}
for all sufficiently large $m$. Hence~\eqref{eq:comb-TV-lower} follows. Stirling's formula gives
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{m^m}{\sqrt{m!}}
  =
  \exp\pa{\frac12m\log m+O(m)},
\end{equation*}
which beats every fixed exponential factor. Therefore
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{m\geq m_0}\norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}
  =\infty.
  \label{eq:raw-comb-TV-divergence}
\end{equation}
This is the point at which one fixed smooth datum defeats the earlier generation-dependent-frequency caveat.

Now suppose that a raw-faithful $Y(h,x)$ were in $L^1(Q)$. On $\Gamma_m$, conditional Jensen and raw-faithfulness give
\begin{align*}
  \E_Q\br{\abs{Y(h,x)}\ind_{\Gamma_m}}
  &\geq
  \E_Q\br{
    \abs{\E_Q[Y(h,x)\mid\sigma(R)]}
    \ind_{\Gamma_m}
  } \\
  &=
  \int_{\Gamma_m}
  \abs{\frac{d\mu_m}{dQ_m}}\,dQ_m
  =
  \norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}.
\end{align*}
The cylinders are pairwise disjoint, so Tonelli and~\eqref{eq:raw-comb-TV-divergence} imply
\begin{equation*}
  \E_Q\abs{Y(h,x)}
  \geq
  \sum_{m\geq m_0}\norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}
  =\infty,
\end{equation*}
a contradiction.

The same Radon--Nikodym identity proves proposal invariance. Changing the lifetime, genealogy, or Gaussian proposal merely replaces $Q_m$ by another positive dominating measure; its density cancels inside
\begin{equation*}
  \int\abs{d\mu_m/dQ_m}\,dQ_m
  =
  \norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}.
\end{equation*}
Jointly dependent proposal variables are covered by the same joint density. Additional auxiliary randomness cannot improve the first moment because of conditional Jensen.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The same small-data regime has opposite outcomes}

The fixed datum may be made arbitrarily small without affecting the divergence mechanism. For every fixed $0<\alpha<1$ and $T>0$, choose $\varepsilon$ small enough that
\begin{equation}
  4\abs\lambda C_{\cD}(\alpha,T)
  \norm{P_\cdot\phi_\varepsilon''}_{X_{\alpha,T}}<1.
  \label{eq:raw-overlap-Cprime}
\end{equation}
Then~\cref{thm:cprime} gives an unbiased $L^1$ skeleton-averaged estimator for the solution associated with $\phi_\varepsilon$, while~\cref{thm:raw-faithful-obstruction} rules out every raw-faithful estimator for the same datum at every positive evaluation horizon.

\begin{corollary}[Averaged versus raw-faithful]
\label{cor:clean-contrast}
There are arbitrarily small $C^\infty$ terminal data for which complete interior averaging gives an unbiased $L^1$ representation, while every raw-faithful representation has infinite first moment.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For every fixed $\alpha$ and $T$, the bracketed function in~\eqref{eq:fixed-smooth-phi} has finite $C^{2+\alpha}$ norm. Multiplication by $\varepsilon$ scales the norm linearly. Hence $\varepsilon$ may be chosen so that~\eqref{eq:raw-overlap-Cprime} holds. The two conclusions then follow from~\cref{thm:cprime,thm:raw-faithful-obstruction} for the same $\phi_\varepsilon$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Randomness itself is not obstructed}

The conclusion concerns raw-faithfulness, not randomness as such. Two examples should be kept next to the theorem.

\paragraph{Decorative randomness.}
Sample the C-prime skeleton $S$ and, in addition, sample any family of independent continuous marks $G$. Return
\begin{equation*}
  \frac{F_S(t,x)}{\pi(S)}
\end{equation*}
without using $G$. The estimator remains the C-prime estimator. The simulation contains continuous random variables, but the Gaussian variables are decorative. This example rules out any interpretation of the theorem which treats the mere presence of random marks as mathematically significant.

\paragraph{Antithetic randomness.}
The estimator~\eqref{eq:antithetic-prelim} uses its Gaussian mark nontrivially and has the correct signed expectation, but it changes the conditional barycenter relative to the one-sided raw state. It therefore lies outside the theorem. More complicated bridge averages, control variates, and coupled multi-sample constructions can escape for the same reason.

The correct negative statement is consequently sharp at the level of conditional barycenters: pure importance sampling and auxiliary unbiased randomization cannot repair the canonical raw signed measure. Any surviving random-mark representation must first redistribute signed mass between raw states.
