\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Probabilistic representations for nonlinear parabolic equations begin from a simple algebraic observation. Duhamel's formula converts the nonlinearity into a time integral, and a branching process converts products inside that integral into products over descendants. If the equation contains spatial derivatives of the solution, Gaussian integration by parts or a Malliavin weight transfers those derivatives to the sampled motion. At finite depth this procedure is often exact. The difficulty addressed in this paper is that exactness is a signed statement, whereas a probabilistic representation requires an absolute-moment statement.

This distinction becomes acute for derivative weights. A second derivative of the heat semigroup may be written as
\begin{equation}
  \partial_x^2P_rF(x)
  =
  \E\br{
    \frac{\He_2(Z)}{r}
    \pa{F(x+\sqrt r Z)-F(x)}
  },
  \qquad
  \He_2(z)=z^2-1,
  \label{eq:intro-centered-hessian}
\end{equation}
where $Z$ is standard Gaussian. The centering in~\eqref{eq:intro-centered-hessian} is the source of the regularizing factor. It is also destroyed if the absolute value is taken too early. Thus the main question is not only whether the branching identity is correct, but where cancellation is allowed to occur before the first absolute value.

The results below support the following principle.

\begin{quote}
For branching representations with singular derivative weights, cancellation before absolute values is a structural part of integrability, not a variance-reduction refinement added after the representation has been constructed.
\end{quote}

The final theorem makes this principle exact. If the raw signed measure of a finite skeleton is
\begin{equation*}
  \mu_\tau=R_\tau\nu_\tau
\end{equation*}
and a coarsening retains the sigma-field $\mathcal G_\tau$, then the first-moment cost which survives that coarsening is exactly
\begin{equation}
  \int
  \abs{
    \E_{\nu_\tau}[R_\tau\mid\mathcal G_\tau]
  }
  d\nu_\tau.
  \label{eq:intro-residual-variation}
\end{equation}
For a fixed observation target, summability of~\eqref{eq:intro-residual-variation} over the skeletons is both necessary and sufficient in the skeleton-preserving coarsened conditional-barycenter class. Thus ``cancellation before absolute values'' is precisely the removal of signed variation by conditional averaging. The type of retained coordinate---Gaussian, branch time, or otherwise---is not the invariant. In the quadratic-Hessian application this characterization is pointwise in the target $(t,x)$; constructing one coarsening architecture which works simultaneously for all targets is a separate, stronger problem.

The paper reaches this point in three stages. The first concerns existing branching representations and shows that $L^1$ can depend on the architecture even when the PDE and terminal datum are fixed. The second constructs and obstructs representations for a quadratic Hessian equation, including a fully averaged $L^1$ representation and a fixed-datum raw-faithful non-$L^1$ theorem. The third identifies the exact fixed-target coarsening invariant, places the structured raw, time-spine, and fully averaged constructions on the same scale, and shows that even the entire Gaussian configuration may survive on sufficiently small pieces without destroying $L^1$. The time-spine construction is additionally target-uniform, but requires a stronger small-data condition.

\subsection{Formal exactness and the coding-tree obstruction}

Nguwi, Penent, and Privault construct a coding-tree Feynman--Kac formula for fully nonlinear equations in which the nonlinearity may depend on derivatives of arbitrary order~\cite{NguwiPenentPrivault2023}. Their code set is closed under the differentiations generated by the PDE. In particular, a composite code contains a Hessian mechanism which may be repeated indefinitely along one distinguished lineage.

For the terminal problem
\begin{equation}
  \partial_tu+\frac12u_{xx}+f(J_nu)=0,
  \qquad
  u(T)=\phi,
  \label{eq:intro-general-pde}
\end{equation}
we isolate this repeated Hessian genealogy. If $g^*$ is an allowed composite code, $j$ is an active jet direction, and
\begin{equation*}
  D_m(B;g,j)
  =
  \int_B
  \abs{\partial_{z_j}^{2m}g(J_n\phi(y))}\,dy,
\end{equation*}
then the contribution of the length-$m$ restricted genealogy has lower-bound scale
\begin{equation*}
  c^m\frac{D_m(B;g,j)}{m!}.
\end{equation*}
The lifetime density, mechanism-selection probability, and survival probabilities cancel their reciprocal compensators. The only combinatorial loss is the ordered-time simplex. This gives the repeated-Hessian obstruction~\cref{thm:repeated-hessian}. In particular, if $D_m/m!$ has superexponential growth, the raw code-rooted functional is not in $L^1$ at any positive remaining horizon.

The same argument has a converse interpretation. If the relevant code-rooted functionals are integrable, then the even terminal derivatives satisfy a factorial bound. Applying the estimate to both $f^*$ and $(\partial_{z_j}f)^*$ gives
\begin{equation*}
  \abs{\partial_{z_j}^r f(J_n\phi(y))}
  \leq
  C_yA_y^r\Gamma\pa{\frac r2+1}
\end{equation*}
for almost every $y$. Thus the formal directional Taylor series is entire with at most Gaussian growth. This Gevrey-$1/2$ necessity is~\cref{thm:gevrey-necessity}.

These results do not say that the underlying PDE lacks a probabilistic representation. They say that one exact architecture can have an infinite absolute moment. The distinction is visible on the benchmark
\begin{equation}
  \partial_tu+\frac12u_{xx}
  +\eta\pa{e^{(u_x)^4}-1}=0,
  \qquad
  u(T,x)=a\cos x,
  \label{eq:intro-benchmark}
\end{equation}
The Nguwi--Penent--Privault functional is non-$L^1$ at every positive horizon, while the marked branching representation of Henry-Labord\`ere, Oudjane, Tan, Touzi, and Warin~\cite{HenryLabordereEtAl2019} has a finite second moment on an explicit positive interval. This is~\cref{thm:representation-dichotomy}. We use the example only to establish the first step of the paper's logic: formal exactness does not imply $L^1$, and the moment question cannot be read off from the PDE alone.

\subsection{The quadratic Hessian equation and patch regrouping}

The main part of the paper studies
\begin{equation}
  \partial_tv
  =
  \frac12v_{xx}+\lambda(v_{xx})^2
  \qquad\text{on }\Torus=\R/(2\pi\mathbb Z).
  \label{eq:intro-hessian-pde}
\end{equation}
Writing $z=v_{xx}$ gives
\begin{equation}
  z(t)
  =
  P_t\phi''
  +\lambda\int_0^t
  \partial_x^2P_{t-s}\br{z(s)^2}\,ds.
  \label{eq:intro-hessian-mild}
\end{equation}
Repeated substitution produces planar binary Duhamel trees. Each internal vertex carries a Hessian transfer. We group maximal chains obtained by repeatedly following the left child into \emph{patches}. Replacing each such chain by its complete iterated Duhamel integral is an exact reindexing of every finite Picard expansion. No estimate and no infinite sum enter this statement; see~\cref{thm:finite-patch-regrouping}.

The regrouping changes where one is allowed to take absolute values. Along a patch, several centered Hessian transfers can be composed first. In a bare Gaussian chain, conditioning on the total displacement replaces the product of second Hermite scores by one higher Hermite score. With spatial multipliers between derivative transfers, the same cancellation is expressed by commutator expansions. The finite patch theorem itself is only a signed identity; its role is to make those cancellations available before absolute moments are estimated.

A deterministic semi-implicit iteration gives a small uniformly parabolic solution of~\eqref{eq:intro-hessian-pde}. We include it because it supplies an overlapping deterministic solution theory and an implicit self-consistent diffusion formula. It is not a principal representation result. Under a uniform Schauder smallness condition, the iteration
\begin{equation*}
  \partial_tv_{n+1}
  =
  \pa{\frac12+\lambda z_n}v_{n+1,xx},
  \qquad
  z_{n+1}=v_{n+1,xx},
\end{equation*}
remains in a fixed ellipticity window and contracts in $H^{-1}$; see~\cref{thm:deterministic-iteration}.

\subsection{Complete interior averaging}

Let
\begin{equation*}
  X_{\alpha,T}=C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}([0,T]\times\Torus),
  \qquad
  (\cD f)(t)=\int_0^t\partial_x^2P_{t-s}f(s)\,ds.
\end{equation*}
The Hessian Duhamel operator satisfies
\begin{equation*}
  \norm{\cD(fg)}_X
  \leq
  C_{\cD}(\alpha,T)\norm f_X\norm g_X.
\end{equation*}
For a finite planar binary tree $\tau$, define its interior-averaged profile recursively by
\begin{equation*}
  F_\bullet=P_\cdot\phi'',
  \qquad
  F_{[\tau_1,\tau_2]}
  =
  \lambda\cD(F_{\tau_1}F_{\tau_2}).
\end{equation*}
All branch times, Brownian increments, and Hermite marks inside the decorated skeleton have been integrated out in $F_\tau$. If
\begin{equation}
  4\abs\lambda C_{\cD}(\alpha,T)
  \norm{P_\cdot\phi''}_{X_{\alpha,T}}<1,
  \label{eq:intro-cprime-smallness}
\end{equation}
then the Catalan count of binary trees gives
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_\tau\norm{F_\tau}_X<\infty.
\end{equation*}
Consequently the skeleton series solves~\eqref{eq:intro-hessian-mild} absolutely, and sampling one skeleton $S$ from any full-support law $\pi$ gives
\begin{equation*}
  \widehat z(t,x)=\frac{F_S(t,x)}{\pi(S)}\in L^1.
\end{equation*}
This is Theorem C-prime,~\cref{thm:cprime}.

The construction is intentionally ordered as follows: first average every continuous variable inside the skeleton, then take absolute values, then randomize the countable skeleton. One cannot instead begin from an unresolved infinite raw functional $H$ and write $\E[H\mid S]$, because ordinary conditional expectation already requires $H\in L^1$.

\subsection{Raw-faithfulness is impossible for one fixed smooth datum}

For a fixed finite raw genealogy, let $\mu$ denote its intrinsic signed measure after the positive proposal factors and their reciprocal compensators have been cancelled. Let $Q$ be any positive proposal dominating $\mu$. An estimator $Y$ is \emph{raw-faithful} if, after the canonical raw marks are exposed,
\begin{equation}
  \E_Q[Y\mid\text{raw marks}]
  =
  \frac{d\mu}{dQ}.
  \label{eq:intro-raw-faithful}
\end{equation}
The proposal may change the lifetime law, genealogy distribution, Gaussian law, and dependencies among these variables, and additional auxiliary randomness is allowed. Condition~\eqref{eq:intro-raw-faithful} says that these changes may not move signed mass between distinct canonical raw states.

For one fixed smooth datum, this entire class has no $L^1$ estimator. Choose
\begin{equation}
  g_\varepsilon(x)
  =
  \varepsilon\br{
    \cos x+
    \sum_{m\geq m_0}
    \frac{\cos(N_mx)}{\sqrt{m!}}
  },
  \qquad
  N_m=K^m,
  \label{eq:intro-lacunary-datum}
\end{equation}
with $K\geq3$. For each fixed derivative order $k$,
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_m\frac{N_m^k}{\sqrt{m!}}<\infty,
\end{equation*}
so~\eqref{eq:intro-lacunary-datum} is $C^\infty$. It is the second derivative of a smooth periodic datum $\phi_\varepsilon$.

The lower bound uses disjoint right-comb genealogies. Side leaves are projected onto frequency $1$ and the final distinguished leaf onto frequency $N_m$. Restrict every Hessian duration to
\begin{equation*}
  N_m^{-2}\leq r_j\leq\frac{h}{4m}.
\end{equation*}
This rectangle lies inside the chronological simplex. Each retained centered Hessian mark then contributes one logarithm,
\begin{equation*}
  \int_{N_m^{-2}}^{h/(4m)}\frac{dr}{r}
  \asymp m,
\end{equation*}
and the intrinsic total variation satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}
  \gtrsim
  \varepsilon(C\abs\lambda\varepsilon)^m
  \frac{m^m}{\sqrt{m!}}.
  \label{eq:intro-raw-tv}
\end{equation}
The right side is not summable. Disjointness and conditional Jensen applied to~\eqref{eq:intro-raw-faithful} imply
\begin{equation*}
  \E_Q\abs Y
  \geq
  \sum_m\norm{\mu_m}_{\TV}
  =\infty.
\end{equation*}
This is~\cref{thm:raw-faithful-obstruction}. The amplitude $\varepsilon$ may be arbitrarily small, so the same datum may lie inside~\eqref{eq:intro-cprime-smallness}. Hence
\begin{equation*}
  \boxed{
  \begin{minipage}{0.82\textwidth}
  For the same small smooth terminal datum, complete interior averaging gives an unbiased $L^1$ representation, while every raw-faithful representation has infinite first moment.
  \end{minipage}}
\end{equation*}

\subsection{Randomness itself is not obstructed}

The raw-faithful theorem is not an impossibility theorem for randomness. There is first a vacuous counterexample: append independent Gaussian variables to the C-prime simulation and do not use them. More substantively, the one-edge antithetic estimator
\begin{equation}
  \widetilde K_rF(x,Z)
  =
  \frac{\He_2(Z)}{2r}
  \br{F(x+\sqrt rZ)+F(x-\sqrt rZ)-2F(x)}
  \label{eq:intro-antithetic}
\end{equation}
still uses the sampled Gaussian mark and has expectation $\partial_x^2P_rF$, but conditional on $Z$ it is not the canonical one-sided raw integrand~\eqref{eq:intro-centered-hessian}. It therefore falls outside raw-faithfulness. Antithetic and ghost constructions of this kind are standard in branching methods; see~\cite{Warin2017}.

The final characterization goes further. Even the **entire** raw Gaussian configuration may remain visible on a nonnull subset of each mark space without destroying $L^1$, provided the total variation carried by those subsets is summable. Separately, an abstract signed-measure example shows that retaining only a one-dimensional time coordinate can leave nonsummable residual variation. This second example is not a quadratic-Hessian counterexample. Variable type is therefore not a proxy for integrability.

\subsection{Residual signed variation: the exact characterization}

For a finite decorated skeleton $\tau$, write
\begin{equation*}
  \mu_\tau=R_\tau\nu_\tau
\end{equation*}
with $\nu_\tau$ finite positive, and let
\begin{equation}
  \overline\mu_\tau
  =
  (\cC_\tau)_\#\mu_\tau.
  \label{eq:intro-coarsening}
\end{equation}
Theorem~\ref{thm:residual-characterization} proves the exact identity
\begin{equation}
  \norm{\overline\mu_\tau}_{\TV}
  =
  \int
  \abs{
    \E_{\nu_\tau}
    [R_\tau\mid\sigma(\cC_\tau)]
  }
  d\nu_\tau.
  \label{eq:intro-residual-TV}
\end{equation}
For a fixed target and skeleton-preserving coarsenings, an $L^1$ conditional-barycenter representation exists if and only if the sum of~\eqref{eq:intro-residual-TV} over $\tau$ is finite. The canonical coarsened importance sampler attains this first moment exactly; conditional Jensen proves necessity for every auxiliary estimator with the same coarsened barycenter. This criterion is pointwise in $(t,x)$ when applied to the quadratic-Hessian measures $\mu_\tau^{t,x}$.

The structured constructions sit exactly where this theorem predicts. Identity coarsening leaves the full raw total variation and is ruled out by~\cref{thm:raw-faithful-obstruction}. Constant coarsening leaves only $|F_\tau(t,x)|$ and is Theorem C-prime. Retaining the branch times on the root maximal-left patch gives one target-uniform nonconstant intermediate representation under the additional geometric condition
\begin{equation}
  4a<1,
  \qquad
  b\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4a}}{2a}<1,
  \label{eq:intro-time-spine-smallness}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation*}
  a=\abs\lambda C_{\cD}\norm{P_\cdot\phi''}_X,
  \qquad
  b=\abs\lambda K_{\mathrm{time}}\norm{P_\cdot\phi''}_X.
\end{equation*}
The retained time vector affects the estimator nontrivially. Naive patchwise Gaussian-bridge coarsening, on the other hand, fails on the right-comb obstruction because every maximal-left patch there has one edge and therefore no bridge coordinate to remove.

At each **fixed** target $(t,x)$, the characterization also closes the existential relaxation of the retained-randomness problem throughout the full C-prime regime. Since
\begin{equation*}
  \sum_\tau|F_\tau(t,x)|<\infty,
\end{equation*}
one may choose a nonnull set $A_\tau$ of arbitrarily small raw total-variation mass, retain the complete raw state on $A_\tau$, and collapse its complement. The residual variation is bounded by
\begin{equation*}
  |F_\tau(t,x)|+2|\mu_\tau|(A_\tau),
\end{equation*}
so the sets may be chosen to make the sum finite. On the retained pieces the Gaussian/Hermite, branch-time, and descendant coordinates remain explicitly observable in the retained state. This pointwise construction does not prove that every retained coordinate changes the estimator value, and it does not by itself give one target-uniform architecture. The remaining open problem is to construct natural, non-sparse, target-uniform coarsenings with useful quantitative information.

This gives the precise endpoint of the paper's thesis:
\begin{equation*}
  \boxed{
  \begin{minipage}{0.84\textwidth}
  The $L^1$ cost of a coarsened branching representation is its residual signed variation. Cancellation before absolute values is exactly the reduction of that variation by conditional averaging.
  \end{minipage}}
\end{equation*}
