Slide 13: How much of the dual should we reveal?
------------------------------------------------
11:00--11:50 (50 seconds)

First focus on the signed exponential weight. We want a sigma-algebra G-t with two properties: the conditional mean should be nonnegative, and it should be explicit enough to analyse.

If we reveal nothing, we average all the cancellations. The mean is nonnegative: it is P-t chi-A evaluated at the all-one configuration. But we have gained no way to compute it.

If we reveal the entire dual path, the weight is known, but it still carries its random sign. We need an intermediate amount of information.

Slide 14: The interactions define the patches
---------------------------------------------
11:50--12:50 (60 seconds)

Here is our choice. In the graphical construction, births and splits are interactions between sites; deaths are not. Let G-t be the sigma-algebra generated by the spacetime positions of the interactions and the sites involved, up to time t. It does not reveal whether an interaction is a birth or a split, or any deaths.

The left picture shows the dual realization. On each site line, the incoming and outgoing interactions divide time into intervals. These are the patches shown on the right.

There are four bulk types, according to whether the interaction at each end is incoming or outgoing. Patches reaching the observation time are cut there and become end patches. The thick red line separates bulk patches from end patches.

Slide 15: Conditional independence across patches
-------------------------------------------------
12:50--13:35 (45 seconds)

The sigma-algebra G-t has the properties we wanted. First, it provides enough information for explicit computation: conditional on G-t, the law of the dual factorizes over patches. The remaining randomness in different patches is independent.

Each patch has an explicit one-site law P_P^con, which is why G-t satisfies the second condition.

Slide 17: The patch representation
----------------------------------
13:45--15:00 (75 seconds)

The signed Feynman-Kac weight is itself a product over patches. Conditional independence with respect to G-t lets us average each factor separately. A bulk patch contributes a number C-P. An end patch contributes C-z-P, where z is the initial spin at that site in the original system.

We call the product of these contributions W-t-mu. The initial law mu acts on the whole product of end contributions, since the initial spins may be correlated.

We can give all these factors explicitly, and all dependence on the initial law remains in the end patches. This is the patch representation. Next we use patch positivity to control the signs of these contributions.
