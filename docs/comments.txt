1. Entry: spin-system.md (and interacting-particle-system.md)

The wiki restricts "spin system" to the two-state case, {0,1}^Lambda with single-site
flip updates. This is not a universal definition of the term but the specific technical
convention of Tom Liggett's books (Interacting Particle Systems, 1985, Chapter III
"Spin Systems"; Stochastic Interacting Systems), where "spin system" names one class of
IPS among several (spin systems, exclusion processes, nearest-particle systems, ...),
deliberately restricted to two states and flip dynamics. The current page's references
line already cites exactly these two Liggett books, so the definition is faithful to
that source -- but the page does not say so.

In the broader statistical-mechanics / Gibbs-measure literature (e.g. Georgii, Gibbs
Measures and Phase Transitions, and the physics literature generally), "spin system" is
used far more broadly: a "spin" is simply the local state, with no restriction to two
values or to a discrete space. Potts-model dynamics (q>2 states), clock models, and
continuous-spin models (XY, Heisenberg, O(n)) are all routinely called spin systems in
that tradition.

Ask: the page should say explicitly that its restriction to two states is Liggett's
specific usage/legacy, in contrast with the general statistical-mechanics usage of
"spin system", not the only meaning of the term. As currently written, a reader coming
from the statistical-mechanics side could easily mistake the definition for the
standard/universal one.


2. Entry: spin-system.md, section "Locality" -- order of exposition

The section states the locality condition using N_*(i) first, and only afterwards
explains what N_*(i) is:

    "there is a finite neighbourhood N_*(i), containing i, such that c_i(eta) depends
     only on eta|_{N_*(i)}. The neighbour set N(i) itself does not contain i, and
     N_*(i) = N(i) cup {i}."

So the reader meets the condition before the notation that carries its entire content.
The two symbols differ by a single subscript star, and the distinction is exactly the
mathematically decisive one: whether the flip rate at i may depend on the spin at i.

The distinction is not a triviality. If c_i(eta) depended only on eta|_{N(i)}, i.e. on
the neighbours excluding i, then c_i(eta) = c_i(eta^i), and reversibility
pi(eta) c_i(eta) = pi(eta^i) c_i(eta^i) together with positive rates forces
pi(eta) = pi(eta^i) for all i and eta. The only reversible measure would then be the
uniform product measure: no Gibbs measure with nontrivial coupling, no phase transition.
Concretely, for 1d Glauber-Ising, c_i(sigma) = (1/2)[1 - (gamma/2) sigma_i (sigma_{i-1}
+ sigma_{i+1})] with gamma = tanh(2 beta J), the dependence on sigma_i is what makes a
spin that disagrees with both neighbours flip at rate (1+gamma)/2 while one that agrees
flips at (1-gamma)/2, ratio e^{4 beta J}. A reader who carries away "the rate depends on
the neighbourhood of i" in the graph-theoretic sense has lost detailed balance entirely.

Ask: state the N versus N_* distinction before it is used, not after -- either a short
sentence opening the section ("write N(i) for the neighbours of i, which by convention
excludes i, and N_*(i) = N(i) cup {i}"), or an explicit prerequisite pointer to
lattice-and-graph.md, section "Neighbourhood description", where the distinction is
already set up correctly. The existing link on the word "neighbourhood" points to that
page but does not signal that the reader needs the convention in order to parse the
sentence it appears in.

Related, same section: the page writes "N_*(i) = i + N_* for a fixed finite
neighbourhood shape N_* subset Z^d containing 0", overloading N_* to denote both the
site-indexed set and the translation shape. lattice-and-graph.md, section
"Translation-invariant lattice convention", instead fixes the shape as N subset
Z^d \ {0} and writes N_*(i) = i + (N cup {0}). The two are consistent in content but
not in notation. Pick one.


3. General (site format, not a single entry): formulas should be copy-pasteable

Reading the wiki alongside a chat assistant (Claude app, ChatGPT) is a normal working
mode, and it requires getting a displayed formula out of the page and into the chat as
TeX. The current setup makes that awkward on two counts.

(a) Rendering. mkdocs.yml uses pymdownx.arithmatex in generic mode with MathJax 3
(tex-mml-chtml) from CDN. MathJax renders to CHTML, so selecting a formula in the
browser and copying it yields the accessibility MathML / reflowed unicode rather than
the TeX that produced it. MathJax's own right-click menu does have "Show Math As -> TeX
Commands", which recovers the source, but nothing on the site tells a reader this
exists. Note that content.code.copy is already enabled in the theme, so code blocks have
a copy button while mathematics -- the thing readers most need to move -- does not.

(b) Macros. Even once a reader has the TeX, docs/javascripts/mathjax.js defines a
private macro set that exists nowhere else: \R \Z \N \Q \C \pP \E \Var \Cov \ind \vn
\eps \cL \cD \cG \cF \cP \supp \Law \Flip. Entry source uses these freely; for instance
spin-system.md writes \cL f(\eta) = \sum_i c_i(\eta)(f(\eta^i) - f(\eta)). Pasted into
any other renderer, or into a chat, \cL is undefined -- the reader gets an error or an
assistant guessing at intent. This is the more damaging of the two problems, because it
degrades even the ideal copy path.

Possible fixes, roughly in increasing order of effort:

  - Document the MathJax "Show Math As -> TeX Commands" route once, on the notation page
    or the index, so at least a manual path exists.
  - Publish the macro preamble as a copy-pasteable block on meta/notation.md, so a
    reader can prime a chat session once and then paste formulas freely.
  - Switch the renderer to KaTeX and enable its copy-tex contrib extension, which makes
    an ordinary browser copy of rendered mathematics produce the underlying $...$ source
    automatically. This is the option that actually solves (a) with no reader effort.
  - Reduce or drop the private macros in entry source in favour of literal
    \mathcal{L}, \mathbb{Z}, and so on. Costs some source brevity, buys portability of
    every formula on the site.

Also worth noting that the theme's edit_uri already exposes raw Markdown per page on
GitHub, which is the reliable source-of-truth path today, but it is a page-level
detour rather than a formula-level copy.


4. Entry: pure-noise-spin-system.md -- undefined notation c_i^x, and a superscript
   collision with interacting-particle-system.md

The page asserts

    "Thus N^r has only empty-neighbour spin-rate coefficients:
     c_i^0(vn) = r_i,  c_i^1(vn) = 1 - r_i."

Neither c_i^x nor the set-argument form c_i^x(S) is defined on the page, and there is no
link to where they are defined. The definition lives in
monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md: the split

    c_i(eta) = (1 - eta(i)) c_i^0(eta) + eta(i) c_i^1(eta),   c_i^x(eta) = c_i(eta^{i,x}),

followed by the monomial expansion c_i^x(eta) = sum_{S subseteq N(i)} c_i^x(S) chi_S(eta),
whose S = vn term is the constant term because chi_vn = 1. Without that, the reader has
no way to know that "empty-neighbour" indexes the empty subset of N(i), rather than
asserting that the neighbourhood is empty or that the neighbours are vacant. Note also
that c_i^x(.) is overloaded within that definition: a configuration argument gives the
half-rate function, a subset argument gives an expansion coefficient.

The sharper problem is that the superscript already means something else in a core
entry. interacting-particle-system.md, section "Single-site update form", defines
c_i^a(eta) as the rate for REPLACING the state at site i by a -- the target value.
monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md defines c_i^x(eta) = c_i(eta^{i,x}) -- the rate
given that the site currently holds x, the source value. In the two-state case these are
exactly swapped: the birth rate 0 -> 1 is c_i^1 in the first convention and c_i^0 in the
second. So a reader who arrives with the core-entry convention reads

    c_i^0(vn) = r_i

as "the rate of setting site i to 0 is r_i", which is the reverse of the model actually
defined two lines above by c_i(eta) = (1-r_i) eta(i) + r_i (1 - eta(i)), where r_i is
the 0 -> 1 rate. The collision is not accidental: with more than two states the target
label is needed, so the IPS entry indexes by target; with two states the flip is
determined by the source, so the duality entry indexes by source. Both are reasonable in
isolation; sharing the symbol c_i^{.} is not.

meta/notation.md registers eta^i, eta^{i,a}, and c_i(eta), but neither c_i^x nor
c_i^x(S) nor the vn convention.

Ask: at minimum, link to monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md at first use of c_i^x in
pure-noise-spin-system.md; better, state the two-line split and the meaning of the
S = vn coefficient inline, since the page is otherwise self-contained and the reader
should not have to visit a duality entry to parse a definition entry. Separately,
register c_i^x and c_i^x(S) in meta/notation.md, and either reconcile the
target-versus-source superscript with interacting-particle-system.md or flag the
difference explicitly at both sites.

Addendum to 4: the notation itself is defined well, just elsewhere.
interacting-particle-system.md, section "Single-site update form", introduces
eta^{i,a} and c_i^a(eta) clearly. So the fix on pure-noise-spin-system.md is only a
pointer plus a warning that the superscript there is a source value, not a target value.

Related, same page: the superscript on E collides too. bernoulli-refresh-operator.md
defines E_i^q with q the VACANCY density, yielding c_i(eta) = q eta(i) + p (1 - eta(i)).
pure-noise-spin-system.md writes N^r f = sum_i (E_i^r f - f), but its stated flip rate
c_i(eta) = (1 - r_i) eta(i) + r_i (1 - eta(i)) forces r_i to be the ONE-density. Both
conventions are documented in the wiki -- bernoulli-product-measure.md, section "KCSM
convention", records the inversion -- but E_i^{.} carries opposite meanings in the two
entries with nothing said at the crossing point. Reading E_i^r under the convention of
the entry that defines E gives the time-reversed target measure.


5. General (site organization): the wiki is organized as a reading order, and that
   loses the structure

Current state. 95 entries, all flat in docs/entries/, with a single hand-maintained nav
in mkdocs.yml. That one tree is asked to be three things at once -- a taxonomy, a
reading order, and a table of contents -- and it cannot be all three.

What goes wrong:

  - Taxonomy is lost. interacting-particle-system and spin-system are siblings under
    "Core entries", ordered by prerequisite. That a spin system IS an interacting
    particle system is stated only in prose (spin-system.md, "Relation to general IPS";
    interacting-particle-system.md, "Relation to spin systems") and is not navigable.
  - Multi-membership is impossible. fa-1f-out-of-equilibrium belongs to both FA-1f and
    out-of-equilibrium. A tree forces one choice, and the repository has already made
    two different ones: mkdocs.yml splits KCSM / Models / Out of equilibrium, while
    docs/index.md lists all thirteen under "KCSM entries". Two hand-maintained copies of
    the same classification, already inconsistent.
  - It does not scale. The PDE block is 45 entries in one flat nav list.
  - Existing metadata is inert. Every entry carries tags: (about 100 distinct values)
    and status: (38 standard fact, 36 definition, 9 conditional, 6 observation,
    6 literature). mkdocs.yml has no plugins: key at all, so nothing consumes any of it.

How Wikipedia solves this. Storage is flat -- every article sits at /wiki/Title and the
path never encodes a parent. Hierarchy is then one of several overlapping access layers,
none of them canonical:

  - categories: many-to-many, forming a DAG rather than a tree, with auto-generated
    member listings;
  - navigation templates ("Part of a series on ..."): curated sibling neighbourhood,
    transcluded onto every member page;
  - the lead sentence: the is-a link stated in prose, "X is a Y such that ...";
  - Outline of / Index of / Portal pages: curated reading orders, kept separate from
    the categories;
  - What links here: automatic backlinks, the reverse index;
  - redirects: many names to one article, surviving renames;
  - See also and hatnotes: lateral and disambiguating links;
  - assessment class: quality metadata orthogonal to all of the above.

Note what Wikipedia does NOT have: a sidebar listing every article. The nav here is
trying to be a structure Wikipedia deliberately avoids.

Proposal, in order of cost:

  (1) Keep the files flat. Do not move entries into docs/entries/kcsm/ and so on; that
      would freeze one classification into the URLs, which is the mistake to avoid.
  (2) Turn on the tags plugin -- three lines in mkdocs.yml, plugins: search + tags with
      tags_file: meta/tags.md. It is built into Material 9, already pinned in
      requirements.txt, and it converts the existing frontmatter into category pages
      immediately. Needs a controlled vocabulary (meta/tag-vocabulary.md plus a check in
      scripts/check_wiki_quality.py), since the present tag set is free-form with many
      singletons.
  (3) Encode the relations the nav is faking, in frontmatter: generalizes: (is-a),
      prerequisites:, see_also:. Then a script over docs/entries/*.md generates what
      cannot be hand-maintained at 95 pages -- per-page series boxes, backlinks
      ("what links here", currently absent entirely), an A-Z index, and a dependency
      graph of the prerequisite DAG. Note that is-a edges are mathematical claims and
      should be sourced from statements already in the entries, not invented.
  (4) Shrink the nav to about ten items: Home, one portal per area, reading paths, A-Z,
      tags, meta. Entries stay reachable by link, search, tag, and portal. In MkDocs 1.6
      set validation: nav: omitted_files: info so the not-in-nav pages do not warn.
  (5) Put the hierarchy in portal pages as content -- for instance interacting particle
      system > spin system > {pure noise, oriented, KCSM > {FA-1f, East, BABP, soft}} --
      stated once, visibly, instead of implied by list order.
  (6) Remove the duplicate index. docs/index.md re-lists what nav lists and the two
      disagree; make it portal-only or generate both from frontmatter.
  (7) Surface status: as a badge and a filter. The 9 conditional entries are exactly the
      ones whose logical standing a reader must not mistake, and that is invisible today
      without opening the file. This is also the natural hook for the claim-registry
      discipline.
  (8) Add mkdocs-redirects so entries can be renamed without breaking links.

Steps (2), (4), (6) are configuration-only and fix multi-membership plus the duplicate
taxonomy immediately. Step (3) needs a script but delivers the backlinks and the is-a
ladder. Step (7) is a research-hygiene win independent of the rest.


6. Notation: c_i^{x}(.) has two different domains, and the wiki never says so

This is a genuine ambiguity, not merely a missing pointer, and it is load-bearing.

Reading A, configuration argument. interacting-particle-system.md, "Single-site update
form", defines c_i^a(eta) as a RATE, a nonnegative function of a configuration.
monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md keeps that domain when it writes
c_i^x(eta) = c_i(eta^{i,x}).

Reading B, subset argument. The same entry then writes

    c_i^x(eta) = sum_{S subseteq N(i)} c_i^x(S) chi_S(eta),

where c_i^x(S) is an expansion COEFFICIENT, a real number indexed by a subset of N(i).
Both readings appear in that single displayed line, with no remark.

Reading B is then used, without ever being restated, in at least six entries:
monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md, monomial-monotonicity-for-high-density-measures.md,
patch-contribution.md, patch-critical-density.md, patch-positivity-property.md, and
pure-noise-spin-system.md.

Why this is not harmless overloading:

  (a) The two domains are not distinguishable by argument type. On {0,1}^Lambda a
      configuration IS canonically a subset of Lambda, namely its occupied set, and this
      wiki performs that identification constantly -- monomials chi_A are indexed by
      sets, and the dual processes are set-valued. So "the argument is a set" does not
      tell the reader which reading is meant.

  (b) The two readings give different numbers. Writing v_i^x(S) for the value of the
      half-rate at the configuration whose occupied set inside N(i) is S, and c_i^x(S)
      for the coefficient, Moebius inversion gives

          v_i^x(S) = sum_{T subseteq S} c_i^x(T),
          c_i^x(S) = sum_{T subseteq S} (-1)^{|S \ T|} v_i^x(T).

      Concretely, take the contact-process birth rate c_i^0(eta) = lambda sum_{j in N(i)}
      eta(j) with N(i) = {j,k}. The coefficients are c_i^0({j}) = c_i^0({k}) = lambda and
      c_i^0({j,k}) = 0, while the rate when both neighbours are occupied is 2 lambda. A
      reader using reading A gets 2 lambda where the entry means 0.

  (c) The two readings agree exactly at S = vn, since chi_S vanishes at the all-empty
      configuration unless S = vn. This is why pure-noise-spin-system.md's display
      c_i^0(vn) = r_i, c_i^1(vn) = 1 - r_i is numerically correct under either reading --
      and why it is the worst possible place to meet the notation for the first time.
      The reader is introduced to the symbol at the unique argument where the
      distinction is invisible.

  (d) The distinction is decisive in the patch entries. patch-positivity-property.md
      states conditions c_i^0(S) + c_i^1(S) <= 0 and c_i^1(S) <= 0, and
      patch-critical-density.md uses c_i^0(S) + c_i^1(S) < 0 and ratios built from it.
      These are only meaningful under reading B: under reading A the quantities are
      rates, hence nonnegative, so c_i^1(S) <= 0 would force the rate to vanish. A reader
      carrying reading A forward from the core entry will conclude that the
      patch-positivity hypotheses are degenerate or contradictory.

Ask: state the coefficient reading explicitly where the monomial expansion is
introduced, and give it distinct notation -- for instance hat c_i^x(S) or c_i^x[S] for
the coefficient, reserving c_i^x(eta) for the rate. If the symbols are kept, then at a
minimum the expansion line in monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md needs an explicit
warning that the argument type switches, meta/notation.md needs both forms registered
(it currently has neither), and every downstream entry using c_i^x(S) needs a pointer
to the definition. See also item 4 for the separate, orthogonal collision in the
superscript: target value in interacting-particle-system.md, source value in
monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md.


7. Entries: lattice-and-graph.md, polynomial-growth-lattice.md, oriented-spin-system.md
   -- "lattice" is never defined, and "oriented" is used for what is standardly called
   "acyclic"

7A. "Lattice" is introduced ostensively, not defined.

lattice-and-graph.md opens with "A lattice is the default index set for configurations
in this wiki. It is denoted by Lambda, and the standard examples are Z and Z^d." That
gives a role and two examples, not a definition. No axioms, no closure conditions, no
statement of what must be checked to certify that a given object is a lattice.

The word already carries two established formal meanings in mathematics, neither of
which is the one intended here:

  - order theory: a partially ordered set in which every pair of elements has a meet and
    a join (Birkhoff, Lattice Theory; Davey and Priestley, Introduction to Lattices and
    Order, CUP);
  - geometry of numbers / group theory: a discrete subgroup of R^d of full rank, or a
    discrete cocompact subgroup of a Lie group (Cassels, An Introduction to the Geometry
    of Numbers; Conway and Sloane, Sphere Packings, Lattices and Groups).

The order-theoretic collision is not hypothetical. The section "Reachability and
orientation" constructs the relation i -> j on Lambda, which is a preorder, and the
orientation axiom (antisymmetry) is exactly the condition making it a PARTIAL ORDER. So
the entry builds order structure on an object called a lattice while not meaning the
order-theoretic lattice. Worse, in both standard examples the resulting poset happens to
be an order-theoretic lattice as well, for unrelated reasons: for the East model on Z
with N(i) = {i-1}, reachability is i -> j iff j <= i, a total order; for oriented
percolation on Z^d with shape N = {e_1, ..., e_d}, reachability is i -> j iff j >= i
coordinatewise, whose meet is the coordinatewise minimum. A reader arriving from order
theory receives confirmation of the wrong reading.

What is actually meant is a countable set of sites together with a neighbourhood system
-- that is, a locally finite countable directed graph. The entry effectively concedes
this in its own section "Graph description", where the same data is presented as a
digraph G = (Lambda, E_G) with N(i) = {j : (i,j) in E_G}. Note that Liggett's setup does
not use the word as a defined term either: the site space there is simply a countable
set, with the neighbourhood structure carried by the rates.

The one thing "lattice" supplies that "directed graph" does not is the TRANSLATION
structure. The convention N(i) = i + N with a shape N inside Z^d \ {0} uses the abelian
group structure of Z^d, and the same is true of translation-invariant rates, interaction
cones, and the patch constructions. So Lambda is doing two jobs under one word:
  (a) countable set plus neighbourhood system, for all of the general theory;
  (b) an abelian group, for everything translation-invariant.
That conflation is the reason the term resists definition.

7B. Why a definition is needed: the word carries hypotheses.

If "lattice" were informal shorthand for "Z^d, or something similar", leaving it
undefined would be harmless. It is not. Theorem statements quantify over it. From
fa-1f-out-of-equilibrium.md: "Let (eta_t) be FA-1f with vacancy density q > 1/2 on an
infinite connected polynomial-growth lattice with (k,D)-polynomial growth." A reader
must be able to check that hypothesis, and three of its words are undefined:

  - "lattice or graph". polynomial-growth-lattice.md literally writes "Let Lambda be a
    connected lattice or graph with finite neighbour sets N(i)". The disjunction
    concedes that the two are not being distinguished.
  - d(i,j). The graph distance is used, and used again in the FA-1f vacancy-distance
    R_i(eta) = inf{d(i,j) : eta(j) = 0}, but never defined. On a DIRECTED neighbourhood
    system there are at least three candidates -- shortest directed path from i to j,
    shortest directed path from j to i, and distance in the underlying undirected graph
    -- and they differ. For the East model on Z the undirected distance is |i - j|,
    while the directed distance is i - j for j <= i and infinite otherwise. Ball sizes,
    and hence the growth exponent D, depend on which one is meant.
  - "connected". For digraphs this is ambiguous between weak and strong connectivity,
    and strong connectivity is INCOMPATIBLE with orientedness: two distinct mutually
    reachable sites violate antisymmetry immediately. Under the strong reading no
    oriented lattice satisfies the hypothesis at all. This is currently latent rather
    than live -- FA-1f is unoriented, and east-out-of-equilibrium.md does not invoke
    polynomial growth -- but it is a hypothesis that would empty out silently the first
    time a growth bound is applied to East.

7C. How to formalize it. The following costs one paragraph and decides no mathematics:

    A lattice is a pair (Lambda, N) in which Lambda is a countable set and N assigns to
    each i in Lambda a finite set N(i) contained in Lambda \ {i}. Equivalently, a
    lattice is a locally finite countable directed graph. Write N_*(i) = N(i) cup {i}.
    The underlying undirected graph is obtained by symmetrizing, j ~ i whenever
    j in N(i) or i in N(j); graph distance d and connectedness refer to it unless
    stated otherwise. A translation-invariant lattice is Lambda = Z^d together with a
    fixed finite shape N inside Z^d \ {0} and N(i) = i + N.

Consequences: local finiteness stops being an ad-hoc side condition repeated per entry;
d is pinned down, so polynomial growth and R_i are well posed; "connected" means weakly
connected, which is the reading compatible with acyclic lattices; and the separation
between (a) and (b) above becomes explicit, so entries can state which of the two they
need.

One caveat: the choice of WHICH distance the FA-1f out-of-equilibrium estimates actually
require is a mathematical question belonging to whoever owns that theorem. The comment
here is only that the current text does not say, and that all three readings are
available to a reader.

7D. "Oriented" versus "acyclic", and where the terms come from.

Standard graph-theoretic terminology distinguishes three notions:

  - directed graph (digraph): edges are ordered pairs;
  - ORIENTED GRAPH: a digraph with no 2-cycles, that is, at most one of (u,v) and (v,u)
    is present; equivalently, an orientation of a simple undirected graph;
  - ACYCLIC DIGRAPH (DAG): no directed cycles at all.

Every DAG is an oriented graph, but not conversely: the directed triangle
1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 1 is an oriented graph in the standard sense and has a cycle.

References for that vocabulary: Bang-Jensen and Gutin, Digraphs: Theory, Algorithms and
Applications, 2nd ed., Springer (2009), which defines an oriented graph as a digraph
without 2-cycles and treats acyclic digraphs separately; Diestel, Graph Theory, Springer
GTM 173, for orientations of graphs; Harary, Norman and Cartwright, Structural Models:
An Introduction to the Theory of Directed Graphs, Wiley (1965), for the older usage;
Knuth, The Art of Computer Programming vol. 1, section 2.2.3, for topological ordering
of acyclic relations. For the identification of an antisymmetric reachability preorder
with a partial order, see Davey and Priestley as above.

The wiki's condition is acyclicity, and the entry itself says so: lattice-and-graph.md,
"Reachability and orientation", ends with "Equivalently, the directed graph determined
by the neighbour sets has no directed cycles." So "oriented lattice" here means acyclic
digraph, which is STRICTLY STRONGER than the standard meaning of "oriented graph". A
reader importing the graph-theoretic term would admit the directed triangle, which the
wiki excludes.

The usage is not invented, however. It follows the probabilistic tradition, where
"oriented" names models whose edges point along a preferred direction:

  - oriented percolation: Durrett, Oriented percolation in two dimensions, Annals of
    Probability 12 (1984); see also Durrett, Lecture Notes on Particle Systems and
    Percolation, Wadsworth (1988). The subject descends from Broadbent and Hammersley,
    Percolation processes, Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 53 (1957), where directed and
    undirected variants both appear.
  - the East model, whose constraint is one-sided: Jaeckle and Eisinger, A
    hierarchically constrained kinetic Ising model, Z. Phys. B 84 (1991). For the
    kinetically constrained literature that inherited the terminology, see Ritort and
    Sollich, Glassy dynamics of kinetically constrained models, Advances in Physics 52
    (2003), and Cancrini, Martinelli, Roberto and Toninelli, Kinetically constrained
    spin models, Probability Theory and Related Fields 140 (2008).

Note the difference in content. In the percolation tradition "oriented" usually means
that edges follow a fixed direction, typically given by a linear functional that
strictly increases along every edge, which implies acyclicity. The wiki instead states
acyclicity directly. These are not obviously interchangeable conditions in general, and
the entry asserts only the second.

Ask: rename the condition "acyclic", which is the standard name for exactly what is
defined, and keep "oriented lattice" at most as a stated synonym with a one-line note
that the probabilistic literature uses it in this sense while graph theory reserves
"oriented graph" for the weaker no-2-cycles condition. If the name "oriented" is kept as
primary, then lattice-and-graph.md and oriented-spin-system.md should both say
explicitly that it means acyclic, since oriented-spin-system.md currently repeats the
antisymmetry definition without the acyclicity remark that lattice-and-graph.md
supplies.


8. Entry: kinetically-constrained-spin-model.md, section "Reversibility and convergence
   questions" -- the reversibility argument is too short to be usable

The entire treatment is one sentence: "Because legal updates refresh from the one-site
marginal of mu_q, and because c_i does not depend on eta(i), the measure mu_q is
reversible for the KCSM dynamics." Two hypotheses are named and the conclusion is
asserted. What the hypotheses DO is never said, on this page or on any other.

What is missing:

  (a) A definition of reversibility. The word is used in six entries --
      kinetically-constrained-spin-model.md, soft-kcsm.md, babp-model.md,
      babp-out-of-equilibrium.md, bernoulli-refresh-operator.md, and
      kcsm-relaxation-and-mixing.md -- and defined in none of them. A reader arrives
      with the Markov-chain form pi(x) k(x,y) = pi(y) k(y,x), which does not parse on
      {0,1}^Lambda with Lambda infinite: there are no point masses. The operative
      definition is the L^2 symmetry
          integral g (L f) d mu = integral f (L g) d mu   for local f, g,
      and the wiki never states it. This belongs in invariant-measure.md or its own
      short entry, with the finite-volume detailed-balance form given as the special
      case.

  (b) Which Bernoulli measure, and why it is forced. "The one-site marginal of mu_q"
      encodes the answer but does not flag that it is rigid. The natural reader question
      -- is any Bernoulli product measure reversible? -- has a sharp answer that takes
      two lines. Let the refresh sample a 1 with probability p' = 1 - q' while the
      reference measure is mu_q. Take eta with eta(i) = 0, write m for the product of
      the one-site weights over j =/= i, and use c_i(eta) = c_i(eta^i) =: a. Detailed
      balance for the flip eta <-> eta^i reads
          q m a p' = p m a q',
      that is q p' = p q', which holds if and only if q' = q. So exactly one parameter
      works, and the two hypotheses are visibly what makes the cancellation go through:
      matching parameters cancels the measure weights, and eta(i)-independence of c_i
      cancels a.

  (c) The actual mechanism, which is one observation. Because mu_q is a PRODUCT measure,
      E_i^q is the conditional expectation given the spins off i -- this is already
      written at bernoulli-refresh-operator.md, second display of the section "Refresh
      operator", but that entry is not cited from the reversibility paragraph. Hence
      E_i^q is an orthogonal projection on L^2(mu_q): self-adjoint and idempotent. And
      because c_i does not depend on eta(i), it is measurable for the conditioning
      sigma-field, so it pulls out: E_i(c_i g) = c_i E_i(g), that is, c_i commutes with
      E_i as operators. The proof is then three steps:
          <c_i (E_i - I) f, g> = <(E_i - I) f, c_i g>      (multiplication is self-adjoint)
                              = <f, (E_i - I)(c_i g)>       (E_i - I is self-adjoint)
                              = <f, c_i (E_i - I) g>        (pull-out).
      Sum over i. No enumeration of configurations, and the two hypotheses of the
      current sentence map one-to-one onto the two properties used.

  (d) The Dirichlet form, which is the same computation reused and which the wiki does
      not contain at all. With P = E_i a projection commuting with c_i,
          <c_i (I - P) f, f> = <c_i (I - P)^2 f, f> = <c_i (I - P) f, (I - P) f>
                             = integral c_i (f - E_i f)^2 d mu_q,
      and pulling c_i out of E_i once more gives
          D(f) = sum_i mu_q( c_i Var_i(f) ),   Var_i(f) = E_i(f^2) - (E_i f)^2.
      This is the standard object of the KCSM literature and it is missing. Its absence
      is felt downstream: kcsm-relaxation-and-mixing.md defines the spectral gap as "the
      smallest nonzero eigenvalue of -L in L^2(mu_q)", which presupposes the
      self-adjointness asserted cryptically here, and then says "Equivalently, T_rel is
      the best constant in the corresponding variance decay estimate" without ever
      writing the variational formula gap = inf_f D(f) / Var(f). The two entries are
      separated by exactly the computation that is being skipped.

  (e) The general pattern. Reversibility here is not a KCSM accident: any generator of
      heat-bath form L = sum_i a_i (E_i - I), with E_i a pi-conditional expectation and
      a_i >= 0 measurable for the conditioning field, is reversible for pi. That covers
      heat-bath Glauber for Gibbs measures as well, and it explains why Metropolis-type
      rates, which are not of this shape, need a case-by-case verification. One sentence
      would save a reader from thinking the KCSM case is special.

The one part of the current paragraph that is well done is the caveat that reversibility
does not imply ergodicity, with blocked classes as the reason. That should stay.

Duplication: the same cryptic assertion is repeated nearly verbatim in babp-model.md
("The update rate c_i does not depend on eta(i), and the refresh law is the one-site
marginal of mu_q. Hence mu_q is reversible.") and in soft-kcsm.md ("If the hard
constraint c_i does not depend on eta(i), then neither does c_i^eps, and the Bernoulli
product measure mu_q remains reversible."). Three assertions of the same fact, no proof
in any of them, and no shared pointer.

Ask: expand the section into a short proof -- items (b) and (c) are about eight lines
together -- add the Dirichlet form as (d) with a link to kcsm-relaxation-and-mixing.md,
define reversibility once somewhere central per (a), and have babp-model.md and
soft-kcsm.md cite that argument instead of restating the conclusion.

10. Entries: lattice-and-graph.md, oriented-spin-system.md -- the neighbour sets are
    never given a semantic gloss, and the graph edges point opposite to influence

What N(i) MEANS is never stated. It is introduced as a set assigned to each site, and
everything else -- locality, orientation, reachability, the graph encoding -- is built on
top of it, but no sentence says what membership signifies. The operative meaning, read
off the generator, is:

    j in N(i) means that the spin value at site j can influence the flip rate at
    site i.

That is a statement about influence, and as an INFLUENCE GRAPH it is the directed edge
j -> i, pointing from the site that is read to the site whose rate depends on it.

The wiki encodes the opposite edge. lattice-and-graph.md, section "Graph description",
sets

    N(i) = { j in Lambda : (i,j) in E_G },

so j in N(i) is recorded as the edge (i,j), from i to j -- from the dependent site to
the site it depends on. Section "Reachability and orientation" inherits this: i -> j
means j is reachable by iterating N, that is, j is an ancestor of i in the dependence
order, not a site that i influences. The translation-invariant convention has the same
sense, with edges (i, i+u) for u in the shape N.

The wiki's choice is defensible and internally consistent. It is the DEPENDENCE or
ANCESTRY direction, and it is the direction the dual process actually travels: in
monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md the dual replaces i by a subset S of N(i), and
interaction-cone.md accordingly grows Cone_T by collecting {i} cup S over successful
interactions (i,t,S). So the dual set process propagates along the wiki's arrows, which
is exactly right, since duality reverses time and the dual traces influence backwards.
Nothing here is mathematically wrong, and orientedness is unaffected either way, because
a digraph is acyclic if and only if its reverse is.

The defect is that this is never said, and the single place where the arrows are given
an intuitive reading gives them the OPPOSITE one. oriented-spin-system.md states "Thus
information can follow neighbour sets, but it cannot return to where it started."
Information does not follow neighbour sets; it follows the reversed edges. Concretely,
for the East model on Z with N(i) = {i-1}, the wiki's reachability runs leftward,
i -> i-1 -> i-2 and so on, while the physical propagation of change runs rightward: a
vacancy at i-1 is what permits site i to move. A reader tracing how orientation enters
the dynamics will find the direction reversing under them between the two pages.

Ask (item 10): add one sentence to lattice-and-graph.md where N(i) is introduced, saying what
membership means -- the spin at j can influence the flip rate at i -- and one more where
the graph encoding is given, saying that the recorded edge (i,j) points from the
dependent site to the site it depends on, that this is the ancestry direction followed
by the dual set process, and that influence propagates along the reversed edges. Then
either reword the sentence in oriented-spin-system.md so that it refers to influence
travelling against the arrows, or restate it in terms of dependence so that the two
pages agree.


11. General (house style): every process and definition needs a plain mechanism sentence
    at the top

Neuhauser and Sudbury open the abstract of The biased annihilating branching process
with a single sentence that conveys the whole model: "In the biased annihilating
branching process, particles place offspring on empty neighboring sites at rate lambda
and destroy neighbors at rate 1." A reader knows what the process does before any
formalism appears.

The wiki does contain that description, but at the wrong place. babp-model.md opens
taxonomically -- "The biased annihilating branching process, abbreviated BABP, is a
Bernoulli-refresh spin system in which the update rate at a site equals the number of
vacant neighbours" -- which is accurate but says nothing about what happens, and does
not explain the name. The mechanism appears only in the fourth section, "Particle
convention", after two generator displays and the comparison with FA-1f: "A particle is
born at an empty site at rate lambda for each neighbouring particle, while a particle is
removed at rate 1 for each neighbouring particle." That is roughly a hundred lines below
where the source paper puts it.

Two further obstacles specific to this entry. The wiki works in the complementary
variables, particles being the vacancies, xi = 1 - eta, and its natural parameter is the
vacancy density q rather than the branching rate lambda; the translation lambda = q / p
with the generator rescaled by p^{-1} appears only at that same late section. So a
reader holding Neuhauser and Sudbury next to the entry has no way to see that these are
the same object until the end. One sentence in the lead -- this is the BABP of Neuhauser
and Sudbury, in complementary variables, with lambda = q/p and time scaled by p -- would
remove the whole difficulty.

The general point. Sampling the opening prose sentence of the definition entries shows
two patterns, and neither states a mechanism:

  Taxonomic openings, which locate the object in the wiki's hierarchy:
    interacting-particle-system: "is a continuous-time Markov process on a product
      configuration space over a countable lattice";
    spin-system: "is a continuous-time interacting particle system with two possible
      states at each site";
    kinetically-constrained-spin-model: "is a spin system whose single-site refreshes are
      allowed only when a local vacancy constraint is satisfied";
    babp-model: as quoted above.

  Construction openings, which drop the reader inside a setup with no statement of what
  the object is or what it is for:
    patch: "Fix a signed additive set process, its graphical construction, and the full
      successful-interaction ...";
    successful-interaction: "Fix a signed additive set process with full interaction set
      I, constructed as in the graphical construction ...";
    duality: "Let (eta_t) be a Markov process with generator L, and let (Y_t) be another
      Markov process with generator D."

The second pattern is the more damaging: an entry that begins "Fix" or "Let" is
unreadable to anyone who does not already know the answer, which defeats the purpose of
a wiki entry as opposed to a section of a paper.

The house style can already do this when it chooses. east-model.md's second sentence is
"A site may refresh only when at least one oriented neighbour is vacant", and
update-family.md opens with "An update family specifies which local vacancy patterns
allow a constrained refresh". Both are exactly right. The problem is that it is not
required.

Ask:

  - Make it a rule that every definition or process entry opens with one sentence in
    plain language stating the mechanism -- what happens, to what, at what rate -- before
    any taxonomy, any formalism, and before any "Fix" or "Let". Taxonomic placement is
    valuable and should stay, but as the second sentence.
  - For babp-model.md specifically, promote the sentence now at the end of "Particle
    convention" into the lead, and state the correspondence with the cited paper
    (complementary variables, lambda = q/p, time scaled by p) at the same place.
  - Record the rule in meta/entry-template.md and meta/style-decisions.md so that new
    entries inherit it.
  - A weak mechanical check is available for scripts/check_wiki_quality.py: flag any
    entry whose first prose sentence after the H1 begins with "Fix" or "Let". That does
    not judge prose quality, only that the entry does not open inside a construction.

This is the same discipline as Wikipedia's lead sentence, referred to in item 5: the
first sentence says what the thing is, in terms someone who does not already know it can
follow.


12. Attractiveness is used as a load-bearing term, is never defined, its consequences are
    never stated, and the one negative claim about BABP is unargued

babp-model.md, section "Reversibility and absorbing state", ends with "BABP is not
attractive in the usual product order." Three separate things are missing.

12A. The term is never defined. "Attractive" or "attractiveness" occurs in exactly five
places -- babp-model.md, front-growth-and-vacancy-density-for-fa-1f.md,
kcsm-out-of-equilibrium.md, kinetically-constrained-spin-model.md, update-family.md --
and there is no definition anywhere in the wiki. This is the same failure as item 8 for
"reversible": a word carrying real weight, used repeatedly, defined never.

The ingredients are already present, which makes the omission cheap to fix.
bernoulli-product-measure.md, section "Coordinatewise order", already defines the
product order and stochastic domination with an explicit coupling, and
monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md already splits the flip rate into the birth rate
c_i^0 and the death rate c_i^1. With those in hand the definition is two lines: a spin
system is attractive if, whenever eta <= zeta coordinatewise,

    c_i^0(eta) <= c_i^0(zeta)     (birth rate increasing)
    c_i^1(eta) >= c_i^1(zeta)     (death rate decreasing)

that is, more occupied sites make creation easier and destruction harder.

12B. The consequences are never stated, although the wiki depends on their absence. A
reader cannot tell why non-attractiveness is worth remarking on. What attractiveness
would supply:

  - a monotone coupling of two copies started from ordered configurations, preserved for
    all time;
  - order preservation of the semigroup, nu_0 <=_st nu_1 implies nu_0 P_t <=_st nu_1 P_t;
  - the limits from the all-zero and all-one configurations, hence extremal invariant
    measures and the criterion that the invariant measure is unique exactly when the two
    coincide;
  - comparison by deletion, which is precisely the move
    front-growth-and-vacancy-density-for-fa-1f.md reports as unavailable: "The lack of
    attractiveness prevents obtaining it by deleting all but one vacancy from a denser
    initial configuration."

This matters beyond bookkeeping. The project's patch-positivity line is an attempt to
recover a weaker substitute for exactly the second item -- see
monomial-monotonicity-for-high-density-measures.md, whose conditional conclusion is
order preservation for centered monomials rather than for configurations, and
high-density-measure.md, which notes that its order "is not, in general, stochastic
domination". A reader who does not know what attractiveness gives cannot see what the
patch programme is substituting for, which is the main motivating idea of the whole
direction.

12C. No argument is given for BABP. The check appears to be one line, using the rates
already on the page. In the wiki convention c_i(eta) = sum_{j in N(i)} (1 - eta(j)) is
the number of vacant neighbours, and the flip rates are c_i(eta) p for 0 -> 1 and
c_i(eta) q for 1 -> 0. Since c_i is DECREASING in eta, the birth rate p c_i is decreasing
as well, whereas attractiveness requires it to be increasing. So the condition fails
whenever p > 0 and N(i) is nonempty, and a single pair exhibits it: fix any j in N(i),
let eta have eta(i) = eta(j) = 0, and let zeta agree with eta except zeta(j) = 1. Then
eta <= zeta and eta(i) = zeta(i) = 0, yet the birth rate at i is strictly larger in the
smaller configuration eta. The death rate condition, by contrast, is satisfied.

In the particle variables of the same page, xi = 1 - eta, the statement is more telling:
a particle is born at an empty site at rate lambda per neighbouring particle, which is
increasing and harmless, while a particle is removed at rate 1 per neighbouring particle,
which is also increasing and is exactly what attractiveness forbids for a death rate. In
other words the ANNIHILATING half of the name is what destroys attractiveness; the pure
branching part alone would be attractive, as for the contact process. That observation
also serves item 11, since it makes the name do explanatory work.

The same computation shows FA-1f non-attractive, its constraint 1 - chi_{N(i)} being
decreasing in eta, which is the general statement already asserted without proof at
update-family.md.

Ask: add a short attractiveness entry giving the definition in 12A, the consequences in
12B, and the KCSM/BABP failure in 12C, and have the five existing mentions link to it
instead of each asserting non-attractiveness on its own authority. Since the failure is
generic to KCSM rather than special to BABP, the argument belongs in that shared entry or
in kinetically-constrained-spin-model.md, with babp-model.md citing it. The concrete
computation above should be confirmed by whoever owns the entry before it is written in;
it is offered as the missing argument, not as an audited one.


13. Naming: "attractive" versus "(stochastically) monotone"

Both names denote the same property -- the semigroup maps increasing functions to
increasing functions, equivalently nu_0 <=_st nu_1 implies nu_0 P_t <=_st nu_1 P_t,
equivalently ordered initial states admit an order-preserving coupling. The wiki should
fix one as primary, record the other as a synonym, and make both findable by search.

Case for "attractive" as primary:

  - it is the term of the interacting-particle-systems literature, originating with
    Liggett's books, which are this wiki's own standing references for the IPS layer;
  - the KCSM papers cited by the model entries state the fact in exactly this phrasing,
    so a reader arriving from Cancrini, Martinelli, Roberto and Toninelli, or from
    Hartarsky and Toninelli, is looking for "attractive";
  - it is unambiguous in this wiki, whereas "monotone" is not.

Case for "(stochastically) monotone" as primary:

  - it is the general Markov-process term, standard in the stochastic-ordering and
    perfect-simulation literature;
  - it is self-explanatory, whereas "attractive" is opaque to a reader who has not met
    it;
  - it names the property rather than an intuition about it.

The decisive local consideration is collision. "Monotone" and "monotonicity" already
carry at least four distinct meanings on this site:

  - update-family.md, section "Monotonicity": monotonicity of the CONSTRAINT in the
    product order -- and that section exists precisely to distinguish this from
    attractiveness of the dynamics, ending "This monotonicity is a property of the
    constraint, not of the full dynamics, and KCSM are generally non-attractive";
  - monomial-monotonicity-for-high-density-measures.md and high-density-measure.md: the
    centered-moment monotonicity theorem, an order on monomial functionals which
    high-density-measure.md explicitly notes "is not, in general, stochastic
    domination";
  - viscosity-solutions.md: operator monotonicity, that is, degenerate ellipticity;
  - residual-signed-variation-characterization-for-coarsened-patches.md, section
    "Monotonicity under further coarsening".

Making "monotone process" the primary name would add a fifth sense to the most
overloaded word on the site, and would collide most sharply with the one sentence whose
purpose is to separate constraint monotonicity from dynamical attractiveness.

DECISION (principal, 2026-08-15): "attractive" is the primary term for this wiki.
"(Stochastically) monotone" is to be recorded as a synonym at the point of definition and
in meta/notation.md. Item 14 specifies the page.

Recommendation: keep "attractive" as the primary term, state at the point of definition
that it is also called stochastically monotone or simply monotone in the general
Markov-process literature, and register both in meta/notation.md so that either search
term finds the page. If the principal prefers "monotone process" as primary instead,
then the definition entry must disambiguate against the four uses above explicitly, and
update-family.md needs rewording so that its two monotonicity notions do not share a
name.

Either way the substantive requirement is item 12: the property must be defined
somewhere, once, before it is used in five entries as a load-bearing term.


14. New entry needed: attractive-spin-system.md

Item 12 established that the property is undefined; item 13 fixed the name. This item
specifies the page. Suggested filename attractive-spin-system.md, title "Attractive spin
system", which matches the existing pattern of oriented-spin-system.md and

Proposed contents, in order.

  (1) Lead sentence, per item 11, stating the mechanism before any formalism: a spin

  (2) The order. Omega = {0,1}^Lambda with the coordinatewise partial order, eta <= zeta

        (D1) P_t maps increasing functions to increasing functions, for every t;
        (D2) eta <= zeta implies delta_eta P_t <=_st delta_zeta P_t, equivalently
             nu_0 <=_st nu_1 implies nu_0 P_t <=_st nu_1 P_t;

      already used by monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md: attractive iff for every i
      and every eta <= zeta,
            c_i^0(eta) <= c_i^0(zeta)      birth rate increasing,
            c_i^1(eta) >= c_i^1(zeta)      death rate decreasing,
      that is, a larger environment makes creation easier and destruction harder. Give
      the basic-coupling proof, which is three short cases at a clock ring at site i:
      both configurations 0 at i, where the order breaks only if the lower one flips up
      alone, excluded by the birth condition; both 1 at i, symmetric, excluded by the
      death condition; and 0 below with 1 above, where no single flip can break the
      order. This makes visible that the two inequalities are exactly what the coupling
      needs, and it delivers (D3).

  (6) Consequences, which is what item 12B found missing: order preservation of the
      semigroup; the monotone limits delta_0 P_t increasing and delta_1 P_t decreasing in
      <=_st, hence extremal invariant measures; every invariant measure lying between
      them, hence uniqueness exactly when the two coincide; and comparison by deletion,
      the move that front-growth-and-vacancy-density-for-fa-1f.md reports as unavailable
      for FA-1f.

  (7) Examples. Contact process: birth rate proportional to the number of occupied
      neighbours, constant death rate, attractive. Glauber dynamics for the Ising model:
      attractive exactly in the ferromagnetic case, since the birth and death rates carry
      opposite signs of tanh(2 beta J); the antiferromagnetic case fails both conditions.
      These give the reader a positive instance before the negative one.

  (8) Non-examples, and the general obstruction. For a Bernoulli-refresh spin system the
      two half-rates are c_i^0 = c_i p and c_i^1 = c_i q, the SAME refresh rate driving
      both directions, so for p and q both in (0,1) the criterion forces c_i to be
      simultaneously increasing and decreasing, hence constant. Consequently such a system
      is attractive if and only if its refresh rate does not depend on the configuration,
      which is the pure-noise case of pure-noise-spin-system.md. This covers FA-1f, East
      and BABP in one statement and supersedes the model-by-model assertions now sitting
      in five entries. Add the remark that a global relabelling 0 <-> 1 cannot repair
      this, because the flip reverses the order and exchanges the birth and death
      conditions with each other, leaving their conjunction invariant; the conclusion
      "c_i is constant" is itself relabelling-invariant.

  (9) Cautions. Attractiveness is a property of the dynamics, not of an invariant measure.
      Attractive is not the same as having a monotone constraint: update-family.md's
      constraints are monotone while the dynamics is not attractive, and that sentence
      should link here. Attractive and reversible are independent: KCSM are reversible and
      not attractive.

Once the page exists, the five current mentions -- babp-model.md,
front-growth-and-vacancy-density-for-fa-1f.md, kcsm-out-of-equilibrium.md,
kinetically-constrained-spin-model.md, update-family.md -- should link to it instead of
each asserting non-attractiveness on its own authority.

Status metadata (item 14): the definition, the criterion and the consequences are standard facts
and should carry the corresponding status with a Liggett citation. Two items are NOT
standard textbook material as stated here and should be verified before being written in
as proved: the Bernoulli-refresh characterization in (8), and the flip-invariance remark.
Both were worked out in conversation, not read from a source. They look routine, but the
page should not present them as audited until someone with the authority to do so checks
them.


15. Entry: monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md is not readable

This page is the algebraic hinge of the entire dual programme. Equation (1) feeds the
signed additive set process, the patch representation, patch positivity, the critical
density, and every comparison result downstream. It is also the least readable page on
the site.

Two things it does well and which should be preserved: it separates the unconditional
algebra, equation (1), from the conditional semigroup identity (3), and it lists the
analytic hypotheses for that passage explicitly rather than hiding them. The problem is
exposition, not honesty.

Partial correction, see item 16: several of the explanations asked for below are not
absent from the wiki, they are on signed-additive-set-process.md, which the suggested
reading order places AFTER this page. That applies to the explicit dual generator
(15E), the reading of delta and beta as death, split and birth (15A(v)), the meaning of
the sign labels (15A(iv)), and the reason for the beta_i(vn) = 0 convention (15D). The
remaining complaints stand wherever the reader starts.

15A. Implicit conventions.

  (i) The argument of c_i^x switches between a configuration and a subset within a single
      display. Item 6 covers this; not repeated here.

  (ii) "Monomial expansion" is never defined, although the operation is simple and one
       sentence would say it: the monomials form a BASIS of the relevant function space,
       and the expansion is just the function rewritten in that basis. That is the whole
       content. Once it is stated, the coefficients c_i^x(S) are visibly coordinates, and
       the notation stops looking like a private device.

       The wiki never says it. The phrase occurs exactly twice, both on this page, at
       lines 35 and 80. monomials.md, the natural home, gives chi_A, the empty-product
       convention, the product identity chi_A chi_B = chi_{A union B}, single-site
       replacement and the Bernoulli refresh identity, but never states that the
       monomials span or that they are linearly independent. local-functions.md states
       only that a local function depends on finitely many coordinates.

       Ask: a short section in monomials.md, "Monomials as a basis", saying that

         - the relevant space is the functions on {0,1}^V for a finite V, of dimension
           2^{|V|};
         - there are 2^{|V|} monomials chi_S with S subseteq V, and they are linearly
           independent, because chi_S evaluated at the configuration equal to 1 exactly
           on T is 1 when S subseteq T and 0 otherwise, so in point-evaluation
           coordinates they form a unitriangular matrix;
         - hence they are a basis, and every such f has a unique expansion
           f = sum_{S subseteq V} fhat(S) chi_S -- this IS the monomial expansion; and
         - the coordinates are recovered by Moebius inversion over the subset lattice,
           fhat(S) = sum_{T subseteq S} (-1)^{|S \ T|} f(configuration that is 1 exactly
           on T).

       Then this entry cites that section in a clause instead of assuming it.

       Uniqueness is what makes the downstream theory well posed:
       patch-positivity-property.md and patch-critical-density.md impose inequalities on
       the numbers c_i^x(S), which presupposes that the rate determines them.

       Notation gap, same ask: stating the evaluation rule needs a symbol for the
       configuration that is 1 exactly on T, and the wiki has none. eta_T is not
       available, since lattice-and-graph.md already uses eta_A for the RESTRICTION of
       eta to A, and bold 1 already carries the all-one configuration, the constant-one
       profile, and event indicators. monomials.md should introduce and register one
       symbol for it. This is the same silent identification of subsets with
       configurations that produces the ambiguity in item 6.

  (iii) The basis fact is never applied to c_i^x on the page. Two steps are skipped.

        First, that c_i^x is a function on {0,1}^{N(i)} at all. Line 35 poses this as a
        hypothesis -- "Assume c_i^0 and c_i^1 depend only on the finite neighbour set
        N(i)" -- when it follows from locality: c_i reads eta on N_*(i), and
        c_i^x(eta) = c_i(eta^{i,x}) pins the i-coordinate, leaving N(i). As written it
        looks like an extra restriction on the model class.

        Second, the application itself: the basis fact with V = N(i) gives the display at
        line 38. One clause.

        A worked instance would make both concrete, and the wiki supplies one. FA-1f has
        c_i(eta) = 1 - chi_{N(i)}(eta), independent of eta(i), so c_i^0 = c_i^1 =
        1 - chi_{N(i)}, already written in the basis: c_i^x(vn) = 1, c_i^x(N(i)) = -1,
        every other coefficient zero. It also exhibits a NEGATIVE coefficient in two
        lines, which is the obstruction motivating the absolute values, the signs and V
        later on the page, so it serves 15B and 15C too. Short, but worth checking by the
        entry owner before it is written in.

  (iv) sgn_pm is used at lines 55 and 63 and defined nowhere, including
        meta/notation.md. From the use it must take values in {+,-} rather than in
        {+1,0,-1}, which is why line 66 has to add "Signs at zero rates are arbitrary" --
        but the reader is left to reconstruct that from the remark rather than being told
        the convention.

  (v) The superscripts delta and beta are never glossed. They are evidently death and
      branching, matching the two transition types in (1): the first family sends A to
      (A \ {i}) union S, removing i, and the second sends A to A union S, adding without
      removing. One word each at the point of definition would make (1) legible.

  (vi) The page introduces roughly ten symbols -- c_i^x, c_i^x(S), a_i^delta, a_i^beta,
      delta_i, beta_i, sigma_i^delta, sigma_i^beta, V, V_i, and the dual generator D_Y --
      and not one of them appears in meta/notation.md, which does register the related
      pair (A_t, sigma_t).

15B. The repair is presented before the defect it repairs.

Lines 50 to 66 take absolute values and extract signs before the reader has seen equation
(1). At that point there is no way to know what problem is being solved, so the
definitions look arbitrary. The natural order is: derive (1); observe that its right-hand
side has the SHAPE of a jump generator in the set variable; observe that this reading is
licensed only if the coefficients are nonnegative rates; observe that a_i^delta and
a_i^beta can be negative; and only then introduce |a|, sigma, and V as the repair. This
is the same structural complaint as item 2.

15C. The decisive interpretive sentence is missing altogether.

After (1) the page says only "Equation (1) is the algebraic input for the signed additive
dual." Nowhere does it say HOW to read (1). The missing content is: freeze eta and regard
B -> chi_B(eta) as a function on finite subsets; then the right-hand side is a difference
operator in A, of the form sum over targets of coefficient times (value at target minus
value at A); the two families of terms are the two transition types; and set-valued
targets identify sites automatically because chi_S chi_B = chi_{S union B}, which is the
idempotency eta^2 = eta. That single paragraph is what turns the display from an
algebraic identity into a process. Compare the voter model, where the same reading is
immediate precisely because the coefficients there really are rates.

15D. V is a formula with no derivation.

Lines 106 to 113 give V_i = sum_S delta_i(S) + sum_{S =/= vn} beta_i(S) + a_i^beta(vn).
Two different things are happening and neither is stated. The first two sums are the
diagonal compensation incurred by declaring |a| to be jump rates, since a generator
subtracts the total jump rate on the diagonal. The last term is diagonal from the start:
the S = vn term of the beta sum in (1) is a_i^beta(vn) chi_{A union vn} = a_i^beta(vn)
chi_A, a self-transition rather than a jump. That is exactly why line 66 sets
beta_i(vn) = 0 while a_i^beta(vn) enters V_i directly, neither signed nor absolute-valued.
A reader cannot reconstruct this from what is written.

15E. The dual generator is never written down.

Equation (2) asserts L_eta H = D_Y H + V(A) H, with D_Y described only as "the signed
additive set process with the rates and signs above", that is, by pointer to another
entry. The page's central identity is therefore asserted rather than derived from (1),
which sits four lines above it. Since the passage from (1) to (2) is pure bookkeeping --
collect the |a| as rates, the sigma as sign flips, the diagonal as V -- it should be
carried out on the page.

15F. The sign-carrying duality function is unmotivated.

H(Y,eta) = sigma chi_A(eta) appears without the one sentence that explains it: a Markov
process cannot have negative jump rates, so the sign is promoted from the coefficients to
an extra coordinate of the dual state, and the duality function reads it off.

Ask: restructure as (1) the expansion, with existence, uniqueness and Moebius inversion;
(2) the generator calculation; (3) how to read the result as a set-valued jump operator,
including the chi_S chi_B = chi_{S union B} remark; (4) the obstruction, that the
coefficients are not rates; (5) the repair, with the diagonal accounting spelled out;
(6) D_Y written explicitly and (2) derived; (7) the conditional semigroup identity with
its hypothesis list, which is already good. Register the symbols in meta/notation.md. Add
one worked instance where no signs and no V are needed -- the voter model, or the
pure-noise system of pure-noise-spin-system.md, whose dual is a pure death process and
can be checked independently by hand -- so that the reader meets the general machinery
against a case they can verify.


16. Reading order: monomial duality is placed before the process it depends on

monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md needs signed-additive-set-process.md. Its section
"Conditional Feynman-Kac formula" opens with "Let Y = (A,sigma) and let (Y_t) =
(A_t,sigma_t) be the signed additive set process with the rates and signs above", and
neither equation (2) nor equation (3) can be read without the dual generator, which that
other page supplies. But both suggested orders put the dependency last: mkdocs.yml lists
a Duality block (duality, monomial duality, duality noise lemma) and only then a Signed
additive set processes block, and docs/index.md repeats the same sequence.

The dependency is one-directional, so the fix is a swap rather than a reshuffle.
signed-additive-set-process.md is self-contained with respect to the duality entry: it
defines its process from abstract nonnegative rates delta_i(S), beta_i(S) and sign labels
sigma_i^delta(S), sigma_i^beta(S), with no input from the duality calculation. Its only
mention of monomial duality is a motivating sentence in the lead, not a logical
prerequisite. Nothing becomes circular if the two blocks are exchanged.

The gain is larger than ordering hygiene, and it revises part of item 15. Several things
that item asks the duality entry to supply already exist on the later page:

  - the dual generator is written out explicitly in its "Generator" section, which is
    exactly what item 15E reports as missing;
  - delta and beta are glossed there as death, split and birth, with the update maps
    D_{i,S} and B_{i,S} given, which is item 15A(v);
  - the sign labels are declared to take values in {+,-}, which is most of item 15A(iv);
  - the convention beta_i(vn) = 0 is explained there as removing the empty birth, which
    is part of item 15D, whereas the duality page states the convention with no reason.

So a reader following the wiki's own order meets all of this AFTER the page that needs
it. Some of what looked like missing exposition is misordering. What still stands
regardless of order: the passage from (1) to (2) is asserted rather than derived, and the
monomial expansion is undefined wherever one starts.

Wider pattern. Comparing link targets against the nav order across the 96 nav pages gives
228 forward references. Most are benign and should not be "fixed": index.md alone
accounts for 51 and pde-reading-path.md for 23, since hub pages necessarily point
forward, and a definition page pointing ahead to its applications is a see-also, not a
prerequisite. The distinction that matters is exactly prerequisite versus see-also, and a
linear nav cannot express it -- which is item 5's point, that a single ordered list is
being asked to encode a dependency DAG.

Ask: swap the Duality and Signed additive set processes blocks in both mkdocs.yml and
docs/index.md. Better, adopt the frontmatter prerequisites proposed in item 5 and
generate the reading order from them, so that a prerequisite pointing forward becomes a
CI failure rather than something a reader discovers. Minimally, and independently of
either, monomial-duality-for-spin-systems.md should link to the dual generator at the
point where Y and D first appear, so that a reader who does follow the order is told
where to look.
