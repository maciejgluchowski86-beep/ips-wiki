# Coalescing-random-walk duality for voter clustering

## Criterion

For the voter model on a graph, use the standard graphical construction: when a site copies a neighbour, draw a backward arrow from the updated site to the copied site. Tracing finitely many sites backward produces random walks that evolve independently until they meet and then coalesce. If $A_t$ denotes this coalescing system started from a finite set $A$, Astoquillca Section 2.1 records the duality

$$
\mathbb P_\eta(\eta_t\equiv1\text{ on }A)
 =\mathbb P_A(\eta\equiv1\text{ on }A_t).
$$

For Bernoulli-$\alpha$ initial spins this becomes

$$
\mathbb P_{\mu_\alpha}(\eta_t\equiv1\text{ on }A)
 =\mathbb E_A\!\left[\alpha^{|A_t|}\right],
$$

and the limit defines a stationary law by replacing $|A_t|$ with the eventual number $|A_\infty|$ of surviving coalescence classes.

The collision behavior of the underlying walks is therefore the load-bearing criterion. Theorem 2.2 states that, under the continuous infinite-collision property, the only extremal stationary voter laws are the two consensus states. Under the continuous finite-collision property together with trivial random-walk tail sigma-field, the extremal stationary laws are the nontrivial one-parameter family $\{\mu_\alpha:0\le\alpha\le1\}$ generated by the dual formula.

## Mechanism

The duality converts a many-spin question into the geometry of ancestral lineages. For two sites $x,y$, their spins at time $t$ depend on the initial spins at the two backward ancestors. Once the ancestral walks have coalesced, those two spins are forced to agree under any common graphical realization. Thus

$$
\mathbb P(\eta_t(x)\ne\eta_t(y))
\le \mathbb P_{x,y}(\tau_{\rm coal}>t),
$$

up to the initial-spin disagreement probability. If two ancestral walks meet almost surely, the right side tends to zero and the voter model clusters locally.

For finite sets, every collision reduces the number of distinct ancestors. Formula (2.5) consequently transports the entire distribution of coalescence classes into spin correlations. This is stronger than a generic [finite-dual extinction](duality-extinction-finite-ancestor-process.md) argument: the voter dual does not have to die. Its information content decreases because lineages merge.

## Representative IPS use

For nearest-neighbour voter dynamics on $\mathbb Z^d$, the recurrence/transience dichotomy of random walk gives the classical phase split. In $d=1,2$, two independent ancestral walks meet almost surely, so local disagreements disappear and only the two consensus measures are extremal stationary laws. In $d\ge3$, walks can avoid one another with positive probability; nontrivial coexistence laws $\mu_\alpha$ survive, indexed by their density $\alpha$.

The same criterion extends beyond Euclidean lattices. Theorem 2.2 formulates the classification using graph-level collision properties and a tail-triviality hypothesis rather than dimension itself.

## Limitations

Coalescing-walk duality is highly model-specific. It relies on voter updates having a single parent, so that backward histories are genuine random walks and merging is exact. Branching, nonlinear copying rules, mutation, or other local reactions generally change the dual process.

Clustering is also not the same as uniqueness of the stationary distribution. Even in the recurrent regime both all-zero and all-one configurations are invariant; local agreement means interfaces disappear, not that the infinite system selects one deterministic phase independently of its initial law.

In the transient regime, pairwise noncollision permits coexistence but invariant-law classification needs more than transience alone. The finite-collision part of Theorem 2.2 also assumes triviality of the random-walk tail sigma-field; the source notes graphs where collision behavior alone is insufficient. Finally, the graphical argument concerns local finite-dimensional convergence and invariant laws; quantitative spectral-gap or finite-volume mixing estimates require additional input.
