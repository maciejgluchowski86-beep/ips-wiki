# Patch factorization

Fix a finite horizon $T<\infty$. Let $\mathcal G_T=\sigma(Y_0,\mathcal I_T)$ be the sigma algebra generated by the initial state and the [successful-interaction](successful-interaction.md) skeleton through time $T$, and let $\mathcal P_T$ be the corresponding finite-horizon [patch](patch.md) family.

For $P\in\mathcal P_T$, let $\Sigma_P$ and $\mathbb P_P^{\mathrm{con}}$ be the reference data and [consistent patch law](patch-consistency-event.md).

## Theorem

Conditional on $\mathcal G_T$, the variables $(\Sigma_P)_{P\in\mathcal P_T}$ are independent, and the conditional law of $\Sigma_P$ is $\mathbb P_P^{\mathrm{con}}$ for every patch $P$.

Equivalently, for measurable factors $f_P$ such that either all $f_P$ are nonnegative or the product below is integrable,

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P(\Sigma_P)
\middle|\mathcal G_T
\right]
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}
\mathbb E_P^{\mathrm{con}}\left[f_P(\Sigma_P)\right].
\tag{1}
$$

This is a theorem of the canonical paper, not a conditional project hypothesis.

## Why the factorization holds

Fix the finite chronological list of positive-time successful-interaction records

$$
g=((i_k,t_k,S_k))_{k=1}^n,
\qquad 0<t_1<\cdots<t_n\le T.
$$

Once these records are fixed, the remaining Poisson marks lie in disjoint source-time strips, one for each patch. Their reference laws therefore factor. The event that the selected records are exactly the complete successful-interaction skeleton is the intersection of the patch consistency events.

A multivariate Mecke calculation gives the joint density of the records together with patch factors as the record intensity measure multiplied by

$$
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\mathbb E_P\left[
 f_P(\Sigma_P)\mathbf 1_{\operatorname{Con}(P)}
\right].
$$

Taking every $f_P=1$ gives the marginal density of the skeleton,

$$
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\mathbb P_P(\operatorname{Con}(P)).
$$

Dividing by this marginal identifies the regular conditional law as the product of the consistent patch laws and proves (1).

The theorem is the step that allows cancellations from consecutive local updates to be averaged **inside each patch** before global products or comparisons are taken. Applying it to the patch-local Feynman-Kac factors yields the [patch representation of spin systems](patch-representation-of-spin-systems.md).
