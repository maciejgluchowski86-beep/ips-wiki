# Patch factorization

Fix a finite horizon $T<\infty$. Let

$$
\mathcal G_T=\sigma(Y_0,\mathcal I_T)
$$

be the sigma algebra generated by the initial signed active set and the [successful-interaction](successful-interaction.md) skeleton through time $T$, and let $\mathcal P_T$ be the corresponding finite-horizon [patch](patch.md) family. For each $P\in\mathcal P_T$, let $\Sigma_P$ be its reference patch data and let $\mathbb P_P^{\mathrm{con}}$ be the [consistent patch law](patch-consistency-event.md).

## Theorem

Conditional on $\mathcal G_T$, the variables $(\Sigma_P)_{P\in\mathcal P_T}$ are independent, and the conditional law of $\Sigma_P$ is $\mathbb P_P^{\mathrm{con}}$ for every patch $P$.

Equivalently, for bounded measurable functions $f_P$ such that either all $f_P$ are nonnegative or the product on the left is integrable,

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P(\Sigma_P)
\middle|\mathcal G_T
\right]
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}
\mathbb E_P^{\mathrm{con}}\left[f_P(\Sigma_P)\right].
\tag{1}
$$

This is the exact conditional factorization theorem proved in the canonical paper.

## Proof

Write

$$
G_T=(Y_0,\mathcal I_T),
$$

so that $\mathcal G_T=\sigma(G_T)$. Local finiteness of the graphical dual implies that, almost surely, the positive-time records in $G_T$ form a finite chronological list

$$
g=((i_k,t_k,S_k))_{k=1}^n,
\qquad
0<t_1<\cdots<t_n\le T,
\tag{2}
$$

with

$$
\varnothing\ne S_k\subseteq N(i_k),
\qquad
i_k\in A_0\cup\bigcup_{\ell<k}S_\ell.
$$

The space of such finite lists and the finite marked-configuration spaces used for the patch interiors are standard Borel spaces. After fixing an enumeration of $\Lambda$, the construction of $\mathcal P_T(g)$ from $g$ is Borel: it consists only of finite sorting and recursion along site lines. The same is true of the patch consistency tests.

Fix one component with discrete source-target data $((i_k,S_k))_{k=1}^n$. Define the record-intensity measure

$$
m_T(dg)
=
\prod_{k=1}^n
\bigl(\delta_{i_k}(S_k)+\beta_{i_k}(S_k)\bigr)
\,dt_1\cdots dt_n,
\tag{3}
$$

and sum these measures over $n\ge0$ and over all discrete record choices, omitting zero-rate components.

Let $h$ and all $f_P$ be nonnegative measurable. Apply the multivariate Mecke formula to the ordered tuple of selected nonempty-target Poisson marks. A selected record $(i,t,S)$ has combined intensity

$$
\bigl(\delta_i(S)+\beta_i(S)\bigr)dt.
$$

Conditional on that record, the interaction kind is $\delta$ or $\beta$ with the probabilities used in the reference patch law. All remaining marks lie in disjoint source-time strips, hence are independent with the reference laws $\mathbb P_P$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_A\left[
 h(G_T)
 \prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P(\Sigma_P)
\right]\\
&\qquad=
\int h(g)
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\mathbb E_P\left[
 f_P(\Sigma_P)\mathbf1_{\operatorname{Con}(P)}
\right]
\,m_T(dg).
\end{aligned}
\tag{4}
$$

The essential point in (4) is that the product of consistency indicators is exactly the event that the selected records form the complete successful-interaction skeleton. This equivalence is checked in both directions.

Assume first that every patch is consistent and read the selected records chronologically. The initial incoming patches produce the initial active set $A_0$. Between selected record times, empty-target marks change the local active indicator and the global dual active set in the same way, while consistency forces every unselected nonempty-target mark at an active candidate strip to be unsuccessful. At a selected record, consistency makes its source active immediately before the interaction; its hidden kind determines the post-interaction source state, and its target sites start the incoming patches. Induction over the selected record times shows that no additional successful record occurs and that every selected record does occur. Thus the selected list is the complete skeleton.

Conversely, suppose the selected records are the complete skeleton. Then, on every patch, the reconstructed one-site process is the restriction of the actual global active indicator. Any unselected nonempty-target interior mark must therefore occur while its source is inactive, and any outgoing terminal boundary has an active source immediately before the selected interaction. Hence every patch is consistent.

Taking $f_P=1$ in (4) gives the marginal law of the skeleton:

$$
\mathbb P_A(G_T\in dg)
=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\mathbb P_P(\operatorname{Con}(P))
\,m_T(dg).
\tag{5}
$$

Every realized patch has positive consistency probability, so the factors in (5) are positive on the support of the skeleton law. Taking the Radon-Nikodym derivative of (4) with respect to the marginal (5) gives, for $\mathbb P_A\circ G_T^{-1}$-almost every $g$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E_A\left[
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T}f_P(\Sigma_P)
\middle|G_T=g
\right]\\
&\qquad=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\frac{
\mathbb E_P\left[f_P(\Sigma_P)\mathbf1_{\operatorname{Con}(P)}\right]
}{
\mathbb P_P(\operatorname{Con}(P))
}\\
&\qquad=
\prod_{P\in\mathcal P_T(g)}
\mathbb E_P^{\mathrm{con}}\left[f_P(\Sigma_P)\right].
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

This proves (1) for nonnegative factors and simultaneously identifies the conditional law of the whole patch-data family as the product of the consistent patch laws. Once the conditional law is identified, the integrable signed case follows by applying it to positive and negative parts.

## Role in the representation

The theorem is stronger than a heuristic statement that disjoint Poisson strips are independent. Conditioning on the skeleton changes each strip law by its local consistency event; (5)-(6) show that these are the only changes and that no residual dependence remains between patches.

Applying (1) to the patch-local Feynman-Kac factors replaces each raw local factor by its averaged [patch contribution](patch-contribution.md). This is the conditional step in the [patch representation theorem](patch-representation-of-spin-systems.md).
