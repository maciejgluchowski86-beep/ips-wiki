# Mild formulation and branching-diffusion representation

A mild formulation separates the linear propagation generated by a diffusion from the nonlinear source term. Branching diffusions randomize the resulting time integrals; for polynomial nonlinearities, offspring reproduce products of solution or gradient factors.

**References.** Lawrence C. Evans, *Partial Differential Equations*, 2nd ed., American Mathematical Society, 2010. Pierre Henry-Labordère, Nadia Oudjane, Xiaolu Tan, Nizar Touzi, and Xavier Warin, *Branching diffusion representation of semilinear PDEs and Monte Carlo approximation*, *Annales de l'Institut Henri Poincaré, Probabilités et Statistiques* 55(1) (2019), 184--210. See [References](../meta/references.md).

## Semilinear terminal problem

Let $(P_{t,s})_{0\leq t\leq s\leq T}$ be the transition family of a diffusion with generator $L_t$. Consider

$$
\partial_tu+L_tu+f(t,x,u,Du)=0,
\qquad
u(T,\cdot)=g,
$$

where the expression $f(t,\cdot,u,Du)$ is defined in the function class under consideration. Formally applying variation of constants gives

$$
u(t,\cdot)
=
P_{t,T}g
+
\int_t^T
P_{t,s}\bigl[f(s,\cdot,u(s,\cdot),Du(s,\cdot))\bigr]\,ds.
\tag{1}
$$

A **mild solution** is a function satisfying an identity of the form (1) for every $t$ for which all terms are defined. The definition uses the linear transition family rather than pointwise second derivatives. If a mild solution has sufficient regularity, differentiating the identity recovers the classical PDE.

## Heat equation as the canonical example

For $L=\frac12\Delta$ on $\mathbb R^d$,

$$
(P_rh)(x)=\mathbb E[h(x+B_r)],
$$

where $B_r$ is a standard $d$-dimensional Brownian increment. Then (1) becomes

$$
u(t,x)
=
\mathbb E[g(x+B_{T-t})]
+
\int_t^T
\mathbb E\bigl[f(s,x+B_{s-t},u,Du)\bigr]\,ds,
$$

with $u$ and $Du$ evaluated at $(s,x+B_{s-t})$ inside the integrand.

For a smooth solution, the formula follows by differentiating $P_{t,s}u(s,\cdot)$ in $s$ and integrating from $t$ to $T$.

## Derivative transfer

For the heat semigroup and sufficiently integrable $h$, [Gaussian integration by parts](gaussian-integration-by-parts-and-automatic-differentiation.md) yields

$$
D P_rh(x)
=
\mathbb E\left[h(x+B_r)\frac{B_r}{r}\right].
\tag{2}
$$

The stochastic weight has moment size of order $r^{-1/2}$. More general nondegenerate diffusions may admit Bismut--Elworthy--Li or Malliavin automatic-differentiation formulas; see [Malliavin and Bismut automatic differentiation](malliavin-and-bismut-automatic-differentiation.md).

## From a mild equation to branching

Suppose a term in $f$ is a monomial $c\,u_1\cdots u_m$. Sampling an integration time with density $\rho$ replaces the time integral by an expectation with compensator $1/\rho$. At the sampled time, one may create $m$ conditionally independent descendants, one for each factor. If their conditional expectations are $u_1,\ldots,u_m$, then the product of descendants has conditional expectation $u_1\cdots u_m$.

Iterating this operation produces the [Duhamel tree](branching-diffusions-and-duhamel-trees.md). Gradient factors can be represented by marked descendants carrying automatic-differentiation weights, as in the [HLOTW construction](marked-branching-diffusion-for-gradient-nonlinearities.md).

The algebraic recursion and its integrability are separate. Reciprocal sampling probabilities make a tree term unbiased only when the required expectations exist; finite-horizon nonexplosion, $L^1$ integrability, and any passage to an infinite expansion must be checked independently.
