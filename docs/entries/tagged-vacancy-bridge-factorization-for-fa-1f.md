# Tagged-vacancy bridge factorization for one-dimensional FA-1f

This entry records a local two-sided factorization for the one-dimensional [FA-1f](fa-1f-model.md). It is designed for geometric constructions in which a vacancy worldline is used as a moving spacetime barrier.

The point is that the two-sided OR constraint has a negative overlap term at the level of a single update,

$$
L\vee R=L+R-LR,
$$

but the same overlap becomes a positive factor after the possible update times of the tagged vacancy are averaged over a time interval.

## Tagged vacancy

Use vacancy indicators. Suppose a tagged site \(x\) is vacant throughout a time interval \([0,t)\). Let

$$
L_s=\mathbf 1\{x-1\text{ is vacant at time }s\},
\qquad
R_s=\mathbf 1\{x+1\text{ is vacant at time }s\}.
$$

With \(x\) kept vacant, the graphical randomness strictly to the left and strictly to the right of \(x\) is independent. In particular, \((L_s)\) and \((R_s)\), together with the corresponding half-line histories, are independent before conditioning on the lifetime of the tagged vacancy.

The tagged vacancy becomes occupied at instantaneous rate

$$
p(L_s\vee R_s)=p(L_s+R_s-L_sR_s).
\tag{1}
$$

Hence, conditional on the two neighboring histories, the probability that the tagged vacancy survives throughout \([0,t]\) is

$$
\exp\left[-p\int_0^t(L_s\vee R_s)\,ds\right].
\tag{2}
$$

## Positive overlap factor

Using the OR identity in (1),

$$
\exp\left[-p\int_0^t(L_s\vee R_s)\,ds\right]
=
\exp\left[-p\int_0^tL_s\,ds\right]
\exp\left[-p\int_0^tR_s\,ds\right]
\exp\left[p\int_0^tL_sR_s\,ds\right].
\tag{3}
$$

The first two factors are one-sided. The synchronized overlap is now the positive last factor. Expanding it gives

$$
\exp\left[p\int_0^tL_sR_s\,ds\right]
=
\sum_{m\ge0}p^m
\int_{0<u_1<\cdots<u_m<t}
\prod_{j=1}^m L_{u_j}R_{u_j}\,du_1\cdots du_m.
\tag{4}
$$

Each integrand in (4) factors into a left part and a right part.

Consequently, for nonnegative functionals \(F_-\) and \(F_+\) of the graphical histories on the two sides of the tagged vacancy,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\mathbb E\left[
F_-F_+
\mathbf 1\{\text{the tagged vacancy survives to }t\}
\right]
\\
&\quad=
\sum_{m\ge0}p^m
\int_{0<u_1<\cdots<u_m<t}
\mathbb E_-\left[
F_-e^{-p\int_0^tL_sds}\prod_{j=1}^mL_{u_j}
\right]
\mathbb E_+\left[
F_+e^{-p\int_0^tR_sds}\prod_{j=1}^mR_{u_j}
\right]
\,d\boldsymbol u.
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

Thus a single common list of bridge times \(u_1,\ldots,u_m\) restores exact left-right factorization. The full update order on either side remains averaged inside the two expectations in (5).

Equivalently, apart from an overall deterministic normalization, the last factor in (3) can be generated by an auxiliary rate-\(p\) Poisson process whose points are required to occur at times when both neighboring sites are vacant.

## A jump of the tagged lineage

When the tagged vacancy is filled, at least one neighbor is vacant. Transfer the tag to a uniformly chosen vacant neighbor. This auxiliary choice does not alter the FA configuration. The instantaneous rates assigned to the two possible tag jumps are

$$
r_-=pL\left(1-\frac12R\right),
\qquad
r_+=pR\left(1-\frac12L\right).
\tag{6}
$$

Indeed,

$$
r_-+r_+
=p(L+R-LR)
=p(L\vee R).
$$

Both jump weights again factor into a nonnegative left function and a nonnegative right function. For example, a left jump contributes the terminal factors \(L\) and \(1-R/2\).

Therefore a complete tagged-vacancy segment, including its terminal jump direction, admits a positive expansion in which the graphical histories on the two sides are independent conditional on the common bridge times.

## Barrier interpretation

A tagged vacancy worldline is a nearest-neighbor spacetime path that always lies on a vacancy. Formula (5) shows how to use such a path as a two-sided barrier without revealing the microscopic order of the updates on either side. The only extra data required along a vertical segment are the auxiliary bridge times from (4); at a jump one also records the chosen direction from (6).

For several tagged vacancy paths, these data form a planar scaffold. Regions between neighboring paths retain their complete internal FA chronology. The proposed global use is to choose a sparse collection of such lineages from the Bernoulli initial vacancies, close it under the bridge and coalescence data needed to certify the barriers, and then integrate every complementary region as one finite-volume FA kernel. The local factorization above is exact; the construction and control of a useful global sparse scaffold remain open.
