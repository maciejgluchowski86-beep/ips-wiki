# Liggett--Nash inequalities for polynomial relaxation

## Criterion

A Nash-type inequality replaces the linear coercivity of a Poincare inequality by an interpolation between Dirichlet energy and a stronger, semigroup-controlled size functional. Abstractly, suppose a reversible semigroup has variance \(V(f)=\operatorname{Var}_\mu(f)\), Dirichlet form \(\mathcal E(f,f)\), and a seminorm \(\mathcal N\) such that \(\mathcal N(P_t f)\le \mathcal N(f)\) and

$$
V(f)\le C\,\mathcal E(f,f)^\theta\,\mathcal N(f)^{1-\theta},
\qquad 0<\theta<1.
\tag{N}
$$

Since

$$
\frac d{dt}V(P_t f)=-2\mathcal E(P_t f,P_t f),
$$

`(N)` closes a nonlinear differential inequality and yields algebraic variance decay of order \(t^{-\theta/(1-\theta)}\), rather than exponential decay.

For the infinite conservative system of Inglis--Neklyudov--Zegarlinski, Theorem 8.1 gives a Liggett--Nash form

$$
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{rG}}(f)
\le C_1(-Lf,f)_{L^2(\mu_{rG})}^{N/(N+4)}
\bigl(A_r(f)B_r(f)\bigr)^{4/(N+4)},
\tag{LN}
$$

for the gradient seminorms \(A_r,B_r\) defined in Section 7. The exponent corresponds to polynomial scale \(t^{-N/4}\).

## Mechanism

For diffusive systems with a conserved mode, low spatial frequencies make a volume-independent inequality \(V\le C\mathcal E\) false. Nash coercivity does not try to suppress those modes uniformly. Instead it says that a function can have large variance at small energy only if it is also large in a stronger localization/regularity norm.

Applying the inequality to \(P_t f\), while controlling that stronger norm under the semigroup, converts energy dissipation into a nonlinear ODE for variance. This is the same analytic pattern behind heat-kernel smoothing: the semigroup progressively spreads a localized perturbation, so decay is polynomial with an exponent dictated by the underlying spatial diffusion.

In the cited paper the authors first obtain the needed gradient heat-kernel estimate directly and prove polynomial ergodicity in Section 7; Section 8 then records the resulting coercivity as Liggett--Nash inequalities. Thus the source exhibits both sides of the architecture--polynomial semigroup smoothing and its Nash functional-inequality encoding--without falsely asserting a positive gap.

## Representative IPS use

The paper studies infinite-dimensional stochastic systems on \(\mathbb R^{\mathbb Z^N}\) generated by sums of squares of local vector fields preserving quantities locally. A basic heat-conduction example uses nearest-neighbor rotational fields, closely related to conservative oscillator/energy-exchange models.

Corollary 7.2 shows, for functions in the natural differentiability class with finite \(B_r(f)\),

$$
\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_{rG}}(P_t f)
\le C t^{-N/4}A_r(f)B_r(f),
$$

and analogous entropy/gradient estimates. Proposition 7.4 extends ergodicity to an Orlicz space. Remark 7.3 checks a quadratic observable whose evolution is exactly governed by the lattice heat equation and concludes that polynomial decay is intrinsic: the generator has no spectral gap.

## Limitations

A Nash inequality needs an auxiliary seminorm that is stable or sufficiently controlled by the semigroup. If that norm grows rapidly, the differential argument does not close. The decay it gives is typically observable-class dependent and weaker than the uniform \(L^2\) contraction furnished by a positive spectral gap.

The exact exponents and seminorms are model- and dimension-dependent. The cited generator is a continuous-coordinate, degenerate conservative IPS-like system, not a finite-state Glauber chain. Theorem 8.1 is derived from the paper's prior smoothing estimates rather than used there as the original route to Corollary 7.2; the toolbox value is the equivalence of proof interfaces, not a claim about the historical order of that proof.

This is distinct from spectral-profile or Cheeger methods. Nash coercivity is an analytic interpolation inequality between variance, energy and a stronger norm; isoperimetric methods instead control boundary flow of subsets of state space.
