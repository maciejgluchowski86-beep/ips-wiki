# Asymptotic strong Feller support separation and uniqueness

## Criterion

Let `(P_t)` be a Markov semigroup on a Polish space. Hairer and Mattingly introduce the **asymptotic strong Feller** (ASF) property as an asymptotic replacement for ordinary strong Feller smoothing. Their Theorem 3.16 states the decisive support criterion: if `mu` and `nu` are distinct ergodic invariant probability measures and the semigroup is ASF at a point `x`, then `x` cannot belong to both `supp(mu)` and `supp(nu)`.

Consequently, Corollary 3.17 gives a usable uniqueness test. If the semigroup is ASF and there exists a point `x` lying in the support of every invariant probability measure, then there is at most one invariant probability measure. Existence must be supplied separately.

Proposition 3.12 gives a practical route to ASF. In simplified form, it suffices to find times `t_n`, numbers `delta_n -> 0`, and locally bounded `C` such that differentiable test functions satisfy

`||D P_{t_n} phi(u)|| <= C(||u||) (||phi||_infinity + delta_n ||D phi||_infinity)`.

The term involving the derivative of `phi` is allowed to survive at finite time because its coefficient tends to zero.

## Mechanism

Ordinary strong Feller regularizes bounded measurable observables immediately and therefore prevents two ergodic invariant measures from sharing support. ASF preserves exactly the part of that argument needed for uniqueness while allowing smoothing to emerge only along large times and increasingly fine metrics.

The proof interface is therefore **support separation**, not contraction of two trajectories. First establish asymptotic smoothing. Then prove a weak irreducibility or accessibility statement strong enough to exhibit a common support point for all invariant measures. The two statements are incompatible with coexistence of distinct ergodic invariant laws.

This differs from Harris recurrence. There is no small-set minorization and no regeneration/contraction estimate. It also differs from asymptotic coupling: one does not construct two paths that approach each other. ASF is a semigroup regularization property used to rule out overlapping invariant supports.

## Representative IPS use

For the two-dimensional stochastic Navier-Stokes equation on the torus with forcing in only finitely many Fourier directions, ordinary strong Feller smoothing is generally unavailable. Hairer and Mattingly characterize the smallest closed invariant subspace generated by the forced modes. Theorem 4.1 proves that the transition semigroup restricted to this subspace is ASF. In the proof of Corollary 4.2 they show that zero belongs to the support of every invariant measure on the invariant subspace. Corollary 3.17 then yields uniqueness of the invariant probability measure there.

Thus degenerate noise need not directly smooth every direction at finite time: nonlinear transfer plus dissipation can produce enough asymptotic smoothing to separate invariant measures.

## Limitations

ASF alone proves neither existence of an invariant measure nor a quantitative convergence rate. A common accessible support point, or another condition forcing overlap of invariant supports, is still required for uniqueness. The derivative estimate in Proposition 3.12 can be difficult to verify for degenerate infinite-dimensional equations; in the Navier-Stokes application it requires nonadapted stochastic analysis and a Hörmander-type propagation argument.

The criterion is aimed at uniqueness of invariant laws. It should not be silently upgraded to total-variation mixing, a spectral gap, or finite-time strong Feller regularity.
