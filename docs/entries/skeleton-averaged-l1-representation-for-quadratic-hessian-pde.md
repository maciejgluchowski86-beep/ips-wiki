# Skeleton-averaged L1 representation for the quadratic Hessian PDE

For the quadratic Hessian equation, complete averaging of the continuous variables inside each finite decorated skeleton produces an absolutely summable discrete skeleton expansion for small data. Sampling only the skeleton then gives an unbiased \(L^1\) estimator.

This is **Theorem C-prime**. It is one endpoint of the later [coarsening characterization](residual-signed-variation-characterization-for-coarsened-patches.md): constant coarsening removes every continuous interior variable and leaves only the signed mass of each skeleton.

The unresolved fully random infinite functional is not used to define this theorem. Each finite skeleton profile is defined directly by its deterministic Duhamel integral. This avoids writing an ordinary conditional expectation of an object whose \(L^1\) integrability has not been established.

**Prerequisites.** The finite tree/patch correspondence is in [Finite-depth Duhamel patch regrouping](finite-depth-duhamel-patch-regrouping.md). The analytic input is [Parabolic Holder bound for the Hessian Duhamel operator](parabolic-holder-bound-for-hessian-duhamel-operator.md). The signed-measure viewpoint used later is summarized in [Finite signed measures, pushforwards, and conditional barycenters](finite-signed-measures-pushforwards-and-conditional-barycenters.md).

## Equation and function space

Fix

$$
0<\alpha<1,
\qquad
T>0,
\qquad
\lambda\in\mathbb R,
\qquad
\phi\in C^{2+\alpha}(\mathbb T),
$$

where

$$
\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z).
$$

Consider

$$
\partial_tv
=
\frac12v_{xx}
+\lambda(v_{xx})^2,
\qquad
v(0)=\phi.
\tag{1}
$$

Set

$$
z=v_{xx},
\qquad
L(t)=P_t\phi'',
$$

where \((P_t)_{t\ge0}\) is the periodic heat semigroup generated by \(\frac12\partial_x^2\). Then

$$
z
=
L+\lambda\mathcal D(z^2),
\tag{2}
$$

with

$$
(\mathcal Df)(t)
=
\int_0^t
\partial_x^2P_{t-s}f(s)\,ds.
\tag{3}
$$

Let

$$
X=X_{\alpha,T}
=C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}([0,T]\times\mathbb T)
$$

with norm

$$
\|f\|_X
=
\|f\|_\infty
+[f]_{t;\alpha/2}
+[f]_{x;\alpha}.
$$

By the Hessian Duhamel estimate, there is a finite constant \(C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)\) such that

$$
\|\mathcal D(fg)\|_X
\le
C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
\|f\|_X\|g\|_X.
\tag{4}
$$

Put

$$
M=\|L\|_X,
\qquad
 a=|\lambda|C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)M.
\tag{5}
$$

## Decorated skeletons and deterministic profiles

Let \(\mathfrak T\) be the set of finite rooted planar full binary trees. A leaf represents \(L\). If

$$
\tau=[\tau_1,\tau_2],
$$

define recursively

$$
F_\bullet=L,
\qquad
F_\tau
=
\lambda\mathcal D(F_{\tau_1}F_{\tau_2}).
\tag{6}
$$

These are the deterministic Duhamel contributions of the finite trees. Equivalently, one may first decompose each tree into maximal left-child patches and integrate every branch time, Brownian increment, and Gaussian/Hermite mark inside the resulting decorated skeleton. The resulting deterministic function is again \(F_\tau\).

Let \(\mathfrak T_n\) be the trees with \(n\) internal vertices. Then

$$
|\mathfrak T_n|
=C_n
=
\frac1{n+1}\binom{2n}{n}.
\tag{7}
$$

## Catalan majorant

For every \(\tau\in\mathfrak T_n\),

$$
\boxed{
\|F_\tau\|_X
\le
Ma^n.
}
\tag{8}
$$

### Proof

The statement is immediate for the leaf. If \(\tau=[\tau_1,\tau_2]\) and \(\tau_i\) has \(n_i\) internal vertices, then \(n=n_1+n_2+1\). Using (4) and the induction hypothesis,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\|F_\tau\|_X
&\le
|\lambda|C_{\mathcal D}
\|F_{\tau_1}\|_X
\|F_{\tau_2}\|_X\\
&\le
|\lambda|C_{\mathcal D}
M^2a^{n_1+n_2}
=Ma^n.
\end{aligned}
$$

Therefore

$$
\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak T}\|F_\tau\|_X
\le
M\sum_{n\ge0}C_na^n.
\tag{9}
$$

The Catalan generating function is

$$
\sum_{n\ge0}C_na^n
=
\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4a}}{2a},
\qquad 0\le a<\frac14,
\tag{10}
$$

with value \(1\) at \(a=0\).

## Theorem C-prime

Fix \(0<\alpha<1\), \(T>0\), \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), and \(\phi\in C^{2+\alpha}(\mathbb T)\), and define \(X,M,a\) by (5). Assume

$$
\boxed{4a<1.}
\tag{11}
$$

Then

$$
z_*
=
\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak T}F_\tau
\tag{12}
$$

converges absolutely in \(X\) and satisfies

$$
z_*
=
L+\lambda\mathcal D(z_*^2).
\tag{13}
$$

Define

$$
R_*(a)
=
M\frac{1-\sqrt{1-4a}}{2a},
\qquad
R_*(0)=M.
\tag{14}
$$

Then \(z_*\) is the unique fixed point of (13) in the closed \(X\)-ball of radius \(R_*(a)\).

If \(\pi\) is any probability mass function on \(\mathfrak T\) with

$$
\pi(\tau)>0
\qquad\text{for every }\tau,
$$

sample \(S\sim\pi\) and set

$$
\widehat z(t,x)
=
\frac{F_S(t,x)}{\pi(S)}.
\tag{15}
$$

Then, for every \((t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb T\),

$$
\widehat z(t,x)\in L^1,
\qquad
\mathbb E[\widehat z(t,x)]
=z_*(t,x).
\tag{16}
$$

Finally,

$$
v_*(t)
=
P_t\phi
+\lambda\int_0^t
P_{t-s}[z_*(s)^2]\,ds
\tag{17}
$$

belongs to

$$
C^{1+\alpha/2,2+\alpha}([0,T]\times\mathbb T),
$$

satisfies \(v_{*,xx}=z_*\), and is a classical solution of (1).

### Proof

Absolute convergence follows immediately from (9)--(11). Because the series is absolutely convergent in the Banach space \(X\) and \((f,g)\mapsto\lambda\mathcal D(fg)\) is continuous bilinear, the double series over ordered pairs of subtrees may be rearranged. Removing the root of every non-leaf tree gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
z_*-L
&=
\sum_{\tau_1,\tau_2}
\lambda\mathcal D(F_{\tau_1}F_{\tau_2})\\
&=
\lambda\mathcal D
\left[
\left(\sum_{\tau_1}F_{\tau_1}\right)
\left(\sum_{\tau_2}F_{\tau_2}\right)
\right]
=
\lambda\mathcal D(z_*^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

The Catalan identity implies

$$
R_*(a)
=
M+|\lambda|C_{\mathcal D}R_*(a)^2.
$$

Thus the fixed-point map

$$
\Phi(z)=L+\lambda\mathcal D(z^2)
$$

maps the closed ball of radius \(R_*(a)\) into itself. For \(\|z\|_X,\|w\|_X\le R_*(a)\),

$$
\|\Phi(z)-\Phi(w)\|_X
\le
2|\lambda|C_{\mathcal D}R_*(a)
\|z-w\|_X,
$$

and

$$
2|\lambda|C_{\mathcal D}R_*(a)
=
1-\sqrt{1-4a}<1.
$$

Hence the fixed point is unique in that ball.

For the estimator,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathbb E|\widehat z(t,x)|
&=
\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak T}|F_\tau(t,x)|\\
&\le
\sum_{\tau\in\mathfrak T}\|F_\tau\|_X
<\infty.
\end{aligned}
$$

The expectation is therefore absolutely convergent and equals the series (12), proving (16). Standard periodic parabolic Schauder regularity applied to (17) yields the final regularity and classical-solution statement.

## Data-only sufficient condition

If \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) and

$$
m_\alpha=\mathbb E|Z|^\alpha,
$$

then

$$
M
\le
(1+m_\alpha)\|\phi''\|_{C^\alpha}.
$$

Therefore a sufficient condition for (11) is

$$
4|\lambda|C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
(1+m_\alpha)
\|\phi''\|_{C^\alpha}
<1.
$$

## Relation to the deterministic iteration

The [self-consistent deterministic theorem](self-consistent-patch-iteration-for-quadratic-hessian-pde.md) has a different smallness hypothesis and a different uniqueness class. The two constructions can be identified only when all of the following hold:

1. the C-prime condition (11);
2. the deterministic smallness condition
   $$
   |\lambda|C_{\mathrm{Sch}}(\alpha,T)
   \|\phi\|_{C^{2+\alpha}}
   \le\frac18;
   $$
3. the C-prime profile itself satisfies
   $$
   |\lambda z_*|\le\frac18
   \quad\text{on }[0,T]\times\mathbb T.
   $$

Under these three assumptions, both constructions give classical solutions in the deterministic theorem's uniqueness class, so they coincide. Without the third assumption, the deterministic uniqueness theorem alone does not identify the C-prime solution.

## Position after the coarsening characterization

C-prime is the **constant-coarsening endpoint**. For a fixed skeleton \(\tau\), the intrinsic raw signed measure \(\mu_\tau^{t,x}\) has total mass \(F_\tau(t,x)\). Collapsing the entire raw mark space to one point gives a pushforward signed measure with total variation

$$
|F_\tau(t,x)|.
$$

The [residual signed variation characterization](residual-signed-variation-characterization-for-coarsened-patches.md) proves that this is the smallest possible total variation for any deterministic coarsening of that fixed skeleton measure. It also shows that complete averaging is **not necessary** for \(L^1\): the structured [time-spine coarsening](time-spine-coarsening-for-quadratic-hessian-patches.md) is integrable on a stronger small-data regime, and at each fixed target sparse nonconstant coarsenings exist throughout the full C-prime regime.
