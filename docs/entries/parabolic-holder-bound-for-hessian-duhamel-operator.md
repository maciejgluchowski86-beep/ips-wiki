# Parabolic Holder bound for the Hessian Duhamel operator

The second-derivative heat kernel has pointwise short-time size of order $r^{-1}$, which is not time-integrable. On a positive Hölder class its mean-zero Hermite structure gives enough cancellation for the time-integrated Hessian operator to be bounded on the natural parabolic Hölder space. This is a constant-coefficient Schauder/Duhamel estimate.

**Prerequisites and references.** The heat kernel and $P_r$ notation are fixed in [Heat equation and Gaussian heat kernel](heat-equation-and-gaussian-heat-kernel.md). This is a standard parabolic Schauder estimate in the constant-coefficient setting. The proof below is included because the explicit operator norm and its dependence on the Hermite constants are useful. See [Parabolic Hölder spaces](parabolic-holder-spaces.md), [Hölder cancellation for heat-semigroup derivatives](holder-cancellation-for-heat-semigroup-derivatives.md), and the references in [Parabolic maximum principle and Schauder estimates](parabolic-maximum-principle-and-schauder-estimates.md).

Fix $0<\alpha<1$, $T>0$, and $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)$. For a function $f=f(t,x)$, write

$$
[f]_{x;\alpha}
=
\sup_t\sup_{x\neq y}
\frac{|f(t,x)-f(t,y)|}{|x-y|^\alpha},
$$

and

$$
[f]_{t;\alpha/2}
=
\sup_x\sup_{s\neq t}
\frac{|f(t,x)-f(s,x)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha/2}}.
$$

The norm

$$
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
=
\lVert f\rVert_\infty
+[f]_{x;\alpha}
+[f]_{t;\alpha/2}
\tag{1}
$$

defines a norm equivalent to the usual $C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}([0,T]\times\mathbb T)$ norm.

Let $P_r$ be the heat semigroup generated by $\frac12\partial_x^2$. Assume that

$$
f\in L^\infty(0,T;C^\alpha(\mathbb T))
\tag{2}
$$

in the Bochner sense: $s\mapsto f(s)$ has a strongly measurable $C^\alpha$-valued representative and an essentially bounded $C^\alpha$ norm. For each $t\in[0,T]$, define

$$
(\mathcal D f)(t)
=
\int_0^t
\partial_x^2P_{t-s}f(s)\,ds,
\tag{3}
$$

where the integral is a Bochner integral in $C(\mathbb T)$. Indeed, the centered heat-kernel estimate below gives

$$
\lVert\partial_x^2P_{t-s}f(s)\rVert_\infty
\leq
c_{2,\alpha}(t-s)^{-1+\alpha/2}[f(s)]_{C^\alpha},
$$

and the power $-1+\alpha/2$ is integrable at $s=t$. Strong measurability of $f$ and boundedness of $\partial_x^2P_r:C^\alpha\to C$ for every $r>0$ give strong measurability of the integrand away from the harmless endpoint.

For $Z\sim N(0,1)$, set

$$
m_\alpha=\mathbb E|Z|^\alpha,
\qquad
c_{k,\alpha}
=\mathbb E\left[|He_k(Z)|\,|Z|^\alpha\right].
\tag{4}
$$

## Proposition

The operator $\mathcal D$ has a representative in $X_{\alpha,T}$. More explicitly,

$$
\lVert\mathcal D f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{0\leq s\leq T}[f(s,\cdot)]_{C^\alpha},
\tag{5}
$$

where one may take

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0(\alpha,T)
&=
\frac{2c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}T^{\alpha/2},\\
A_x(\alpha)
&=
\frac{4c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
+
\frac{2c_{3,\alpha}}{1-\alpha},\\
A_t(\alpha)
&=
\frac{2c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
+m_\alpha A_x(\alpha),\\
C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
&=
A_0(\alpha,T)+A_x(\alpha)+A_t(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
$$

Consequently the bilinear map

$$
\mathcal B(f,g)
=
\mathcal D(fg)
\tag{7}
$$

satisfies

$$
\lVert\mathcal B(f,g)\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\lVert g\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}.
\tag{8}
$$

## Sup-norm estimate

Change variables $r=t-s$ in (3). The [Hermite increment estimate](holder-cancellation-for-heat-semigroup-derivatives.md) gives

$$
\lVert\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r)\rVert_\infty
\leq
c_{2,\alpha}r^{-1+\alpha/2}
[f(t-r)]_{C^\alpha}.
$$

Hence

$$
\lVert\mathcal D f(t)\rVert_\infty
\leq
\frac{2c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
t^{\alpha/2}
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha},
$$

which gives the contribution $A_0$ in (6).

## Spatial Hölder estimate

Let $d=|x-y|$ be the periodic distance. Split the integral in (3) according to whether the heat time $r$ is smaller or larger than $d^2$.

For $0<r<d^2$, use the preceding sup-norm estimate at the two spatial points. This gives

$$
\int_0^{d^2}
\left|
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,x)
-
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,y)
\right|dr
\leq
\frac{4c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
d^\alpha
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{9}
$$

For $r\geq d^2$, the mean-zero Hermite formula for the third derivative gives

$$
\lVert\partial_x^3P_r h\rVert_\infty
\leq
c_{3,\alpha}
r^{-3/2+\alpha/2}[h]_{C^\alpha}.
$$

The mean-value theorem therefore yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{d^2}^t
\left|
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,x)
-
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,y)
\right|dr\\
&\qquad\leq
dc_{3,\alpha}
\int_{d^2}^\infty
r^{-3/2+\alpha/2}\,dr
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{2c_{3,\alpha}}{1-\alpha}
d^\alpha
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{10}
$$

Together (9)--(10) give

$$
[\mathcal D f]_{x;\alpha}
\leq
A_x(\alpha)
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{11}
$$

## Time Hölder estimate

Let $0\leq s<t\leq T$ and put $h=t-s$. The semigroup property gives

$$
\mathcal D f(t)-\mathcal D f(s)
=
(P_h-I)\mathcal D f(s)
+
\int_s^t
\partial_x^2P_{t-r}f(r)\,dr.
\tag{12}
$$

For $g\in C^\alpha(\mathbb T)$, synchronous Brownian coupling gives

$$
\lVert P_hg-g\rVert_\infty
\leq
m_\alpha h^{\alpha/2}[g]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{13}
$$

Applying (11) to the first term in (12) and the sup-norm calculation on the interval of length $h$ to the second yields

$$
[\mathcal D f]_{t;\alpha/2}
\leq
A_t(\alpha)
\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{14}
$$

Equations (5)--(6) follow.

## Product estimate

The space $X_{\alpha,T}$ is a Banach algebra. Indeed,

$$
[fg]_{x;\alpha}
\leq
\lVert f\rVert_\infty[g]_{x;\alpha}
+
\lVert g\rVert_\infty[f]_{x;\alpha},
$$

and the same calculation gives

$$
[fg]_{t;\alpha/2}
\leq
\lVert f\rVert_\infty[g]_{t;\alpha/2}
+
\lVert g\rVert_\infty[f]_{t;\alpha/2}.
$$

With the norm (1), these terms are bounded by

$$
\lVert fg\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\lVert g\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}.
\tag{15}
$$

Combining (5) and (15) proves (8).

## Heat lift of initial data

For $g\in C^\alpha(\mathbb T)$, let

$$
L_g(t)=P_tg.
$$

Spatial Hölder contraction and (13) give

$$
\lVert L_g\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
(1+m_\alpha)\lVert g\rVert_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{16}
$$

The estimate is therefore a generic reference for constant-coefficient parabolic Duhamel terms whose source is controlled in a positive spatial Hölder class. When the source is nonlinear, it can be combined with the Banach-algebra estimate above or with other problem-specific product estimates.
