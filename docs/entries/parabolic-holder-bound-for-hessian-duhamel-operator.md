# Parabolic Holder bound for the Hessian Duhamel operator

The second-derivative heat kernel has pointwise short-time size of order \(r^{-1}\), which is not time-integrable. On a positive Holder class its mean-zero Hermite structure gives enough cancellation for the *time-integrated* Hessian operator to be bounded on the natural parabolic Holder space. This is the deterministic estimate behind the skeleton-only expansion for the quadratic Hessian equation.

**References.** This is a standard parabolic Schauder estimate in the constant-coefficient setting. The proof below is included because the explicit operator norm and its dependence on the Hermite constants are useful here. See [Parabolic Holder spaces](parabolic-holder-spaces.md), [Holder cancellation for heat-semigroup derivatives](holder-cancellation-for-heat-semigroup-derivatives.md), and the references in [Parabolic maximum principle and Schauder estimates](parabolic-maximum-principle-and-schauder-estimates.md).

Fix \(0<\alpha<1\), \(T>0\), and \(\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\). For a function \(f=f(t,x)\), write

$$
[f]_{x;\alpha}
=
\sup_{t}\sup_{x\neq y}
\frac{|f(t,x)-f(t,y)|}{|x-y|^\alpha},
$$

and

$$
[f]_{t;\alpha/2}
=
\sup_x\sup_{s\neq t}
\frac{|f(t,x)-f(s,x)|}{|t-s|^{\alpha/2}}.
$$

The norm

$$
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
=
\lVert f\rVert_\infty
+[f]_{x;\alpha}
+[f]_{t;\alpha/2}
\tag{1}
$$

defines a norm equivalent to the usual \(C^{\alpha/2,\alpha}([0,T]\times\mathbb T)\) norm.

Let \(P_r\) be the heat semigroup generated by \(\frac12\partial_x^2\), and define the Hessian Duhamel operator

$$
(\mathcal D f)(t)
=
\int_0^t
\partial_x^2P_{t-s}f(s)\,ds.
\tag{2}
$$

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), set

$$
m_\alpha=\mathbb E|Z|^\alpha,
\qquad
c_{k,\alpha}
=
\mathbb E\left[|He_k(Z)|\,|Z|^\alpha\right].
\tag{3}
$$

## Proposition

The operator \(\mathcal D\) maps \(L^\infty([0,T];C^\alpha(\mathbb T))\) continuously into \(X_{\alpha,T}\). More explicitly,

$$
\lVert\mathcal D f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}
[f(s,\cdot)]_{C^\alpha},
\tag{4}
$$

where one may take

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_0(\alpha,T)
&=
\frac{2c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}T^{\alpha/2},\\
A_x(\alpha)
&=
\frac{4c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
+
\frac{2c_{3,\alpha}}{1-\alpha},\\
A_t(\alpha)
&=
\frac{2c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
+m_\alpha A_x(\alpha),\\
C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
&=
A_0(\alpha,T)+A_x(\alpha)+A_t(\alpha).
\end{aligned}
\tag{5}
$$

Consequently the bilinear map

$$
\mathcal B(f,g)
=
\mathcal D(fg)
\tag{6}
$$

satisfies

$$
\lVert\mathcal B(f,g)\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
C_{\mathcal D}(\alpha,T)
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\lVert g\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}.
\tag{7}
$$

## Sup-norm estimate

Change variables \(r=t-s\) in (2). The [Hermite increment estimate](holder-cancellation-for-heat-semigroup-derivatives.md) gives

$$
\lVert\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r)\rVert_\infty
\leq
c_{2,\alpha}r^{-1+\alpha/2}
[f(t-r)]_{C^\alpha}.
$$

Hence

$$
\lVert\mathcal D f(t)\rVert_\infty
\leq
\frac{2c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
t^{\alpha/2}
\sup_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha},
$$

which gives the contribution \(A_0\) in (5).

## Spatial Holder estimate

Let \(d=|x-y|\) be the periodic distance. Split the integral in (2) according to whether the heat time \(r\) is smaller or larger than \(d^2\).

For \(0<r<d^2\), use the preceding sup-norm estimate at the two spatial points. This gives

$$
\int_0^{d^2}
\left|
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,x)
-
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,y)
\right|dr
\leq
\frac{4c_{2,\alpha}}{\alpha}
d^\alpha
\sup_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{8}
$$

For \(r\geq d^2\), the mean-zero Hermite formula for the third derivative gives

$$
\lVert\partial_x^3P_r h\rVert_\infty
\leq
c_{3,\alpha}
r^{-3/2+\alpha/2}[h]_{C^\alpha}.
$$

The mean-value theorem therefore yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\int_{d^2}^t
\left|
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,x)
-
\partial_x^2P_r f(t-r,y)
\right|dr\\
&\qquad\leq
dc_{3,\alpha}
\int_{d^2}^\infty
r^{-3/2+\alpha/2}\,dr
\sup_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}\\
&\qquad=
\frac{2c_{3,\alpha}}{1-\alpha}
d^\alpha
\sup_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{9}
$$

Together (8)--(9) give

$$
[\mathcal D f]_{x;\alpha}
\leq
A_x(\alpha)
\sup_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{10}
$$

## Time Holder estimate

Let \(0\leq s<t\leq T\) and put \(h=t-s\). The semigroup property gives

$$
\mathcal D f(t)-\mathcal D f(s)
=
(P_h-I)\mathcal D f(s)
+
\int_s^t
\partial_x^2P_{t-r}f(r)\,dr.
\tag{11}
$$

For \(g\in C^\alpha(\mathbb T)\), synchronous Brownian coupling gives

$$
\lVert P_hg-g\rVert_\infty
\leq
m_\alpha h^{\alpha/2}[g]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{12}
$$

Applying (10) to the first term in (11) and the sup-norm calculation on the interval of length \(h\) to the second yields

$$
[\mathcal D f]_{t;\alpha/2}
\leq
A_t(\alpha)
\sup_s[f(s)]_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{13}
$$

Equations (4)--(5) follow.

## Product estimate

The space \(X_{\alpha,T}\) is a Banach algebra. Indeed,

$$
[fg]_{x;\alpha}
\leq
\lVert f\rVert_\infty[g]_{x;\alpha}
+
\lVert g\rVert_\infty[f]_{x;\alpha},
$$

and the same calculation gives

$$
[fg]_{t;\alpha/2}
\leq
\lVert f\rVert_\infty[g]_{t;\alpha/2}
+
\lVert g\rVert_\infty[f]_{t;\alpha/2}.
$$

With the norm (1), these terms are bounded by

$$
\lVert fg\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
\lVert f\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\lVert g\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}.
\tag{14}
$$

Combining (4) and (14) proves (7).

## Heat lift of the initial datum

For \(g\in C^\alpha(\mathbb T)\), let

$$
L_g(t)=P_tg.
$$

Spatial Holder contraction and (12) give

$$
\lVert L_g\rVert_{X_{\alpha,T}}
\leq
(1+m_\alpha)\lVert g\rVert_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{15}
$$

Thus smooth initial data enter the same parabolic Holder space with an explicit norm bound.

## Why this matters here

For the Hessian mild equation

$$
z
=
P_\cdot\phi''
+
\lambda\mathcal D(z^2),
$$

the singular operator \(\mathcal D\) is a bounded bilinear perturbation on \(X_{\alpha,T}\). A binary-tree expansion can therefore be controlled by an ordinary Catalan majorant after the continuous heat/Hermite variables have been averaged. No pathwise Holder norm of a raw Gaussian derivative estimator is involved.
