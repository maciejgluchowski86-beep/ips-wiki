# Heat-reference fully nonlinear terminal PDE

Fix a terminal time \(T>0\) and an integer \(n\geq0\). The one-dimensional equation considered by Nguwi, Penent, and Privault has a fixed backward heat operator and a smooth nonlinearity depending on a finite spatial jet. Writing the equation in jet notation separates exact heat propagation from the nonlinear dependence later encoded by a branching construction.

**References.** Jiang Yu Nguwi, Guillaume Penent, and Nicolas Privault, *A fully nonlinear Feynman-Kac formula with derivatives of arbitrary orders*, arXiv:2201.03882; see [References](../meta/references.md).

## Definition

For a sufficiently differentiable function \(v=v(t,x)\), its spatial \(n\)-jet at \((t,x)\) is

$$
J_n v(t,x)
=
\bigl(v(t,x),\partial_xv(t,x),\ldots,\partial_x^n v(t,x)\bigr)
\in\mathbb R^{n+1}.
$$

The coordinates of a generic jet vector are denoted by

$$
z=(z_0,z_1,\ldots,z_n),
$$

so that \(z_j\) represents the \(j\)-th spatial derivative. For terminal data \(\phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R\), write

$$
J_n\phi(y)
=
\bigl(\phi(y),\phi'(y),\ldots,\phi^{(n)}(y)\bigr).
$$

Given \(f:\mathbb R^{n+1}\to\mathbb R\), the heat-reference PDE is

$$
\partial_tu(t,x)
+\frac12\partial_x^2u(t,x)
+f\bigl(J_nu(t,x)\bigr)=0,
\qquad
(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb R.
\tag{1}
$$

It is equipped with the terminal condition \(u(T,x)=\phi(x)\) for \(x\in\mathbb R\).

The adjective *heat-reference* records that the linear propagation is generated by \(\frac12\partial_x^2\). It is a descriptive convention on this wiki, not an additional PDE hypothesis.

## Terminal-time convention

Equation (1) is posed backward from the known value at time \(T\). If \(s=T-t\) and \(v(s,x)=u(T-s,x)\), then

$$
\partial_sv(s,x)
=
\frac12\partial_x^2v(s,x)+f\bigl(J_nv(s,x)\bigr),
\qquad
v(0,x)=\phi(x).
$$

Thus a positive increment of branching or heat-flow time corresponds to moving from \(t\) toward the terminal time \(T\) in the original variables.

## Convention on the phrase fully nonlinear

Nguwi--Penent--Privault use *fully nonlinear* for the class (1), which allows a general smooth functional dependence on the listed derivatives, including derivatives of arbitrary order. This usage should not be confused with the narrower second-order PDE classification in which *fully nonlinear* specifically means nonlinear dependence on the Hessian. For example, when \(n=0\), equation (1) is semilinear in the standard second-order classification even though it lies in the Nguwi--Penent--Privault coding framework.

The [mild formulation and branching-diffusion representation](mild-formulation-and-branching-diffusion-representation.md) keeps the heat part of (1) as an exact semigroup transfer and treats the nonlinear term through Duhamel's formula.
