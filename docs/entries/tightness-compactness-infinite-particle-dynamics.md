# Tightness and compactness construction of infinite-particle dynamics

## Criterion

A finite-to-infinite construction can be based on **compactness of stationary finite-volume laws and path measures**, rather than on a direct construction of the infinite generator. Conrad--Grothaus implement this for interacting Langevin particles in continuum.

Take cubes \(\Lambda_{\lambda_n}=(-\lambda_n,\lambda_n]^d\) with periodic boundary conditions and finite-particle Langevin dynamics with \(N_n\) particles, where
\[
\lambda_n\uparrow\infty,
\qquad
\frac{N_n}{(2\lambda_n)^d}\longrightarrow\rho.
\]
Assume the pair potential satisfies their repulsiveness, tempering, lower-bound, weak-differentiability and force-decay hypotheses. Uniform canonical Ruelle bounds control local particle moments independently of n.

Theorem 4.13 then proves that the induced path laws \(P^{(n)}\) are tight on
\[
C([0,\infty),\Gamma^v),
\]
the continuous-path space of velocity-marked locally finite configurations. Hence subsequential weak limits exist. Theorem 4.17 identifies **every** such accumulation point P as a solution of the martingale problem for the infinite-particle Langevin generator.

At the invariant-law level, Theorem 5.1 proves that the finite-volume canonical Gibbs measures are relatively compact in the topology generated by tame local observables and that every accumulation point is a tempered grand-canonical Gibbs measure. These measures are the initial and invariant one-time laws of the limiting dynamics.

## Mechanism

The proof has three logically distinct compactness steps.

First, a uniform Ruelle bound controls local correlation functions of the canonical finite-volume Gibbs laws. This supplies volume-independent moment bounds for the number of particles and velocities seen in a bounded spatial window. Those estimates feed tightness criteria for the finite-volume path laws, producing subsequential limits without first solving the infinite stochastic equation.

Second, weak convergence alone does not identify the limiting process. The generator applied to a local cylinder function contains unbounded interaction sums. Section 3.4 develops uniform-integrability and approximation lemmas under the Ruelle bound. They permit passage to the limit in the finite-particle martingale identities. Theorem 4.17 therefore turns an abstract weak accumulation point into an infinite-particle martingale solution.

Third, stationarity must survive together with a meaningful equilibrium law. The invariant finite-volume canonical measures are themselves compact. Theorem 5.1 uses entropy/energy compactness and equivalence-of-ensembles arguments to identify all accumulation points as grand-canonical Gibbs measures. Since each finite process starts from its invariant canonical law, the weak limit inherits that Gibbs law as an invariant initial distribution.

The reusable architecture is
\[
\text{uniform local estimates}
\Rightarrow \text{tightness}
\Rightarrow \text{subsequential path limit}
\Rightarrow \text{martingale-problem identification}
\Rightarrow \text{invariant-law identification}.
\]

## Representative IPS use

The source constructs an equilibrium infinite-particle Langevin dynamics in \(\mathbb R^d\) for broad repulsive Ruelle-type pair potentials, including Lennard--Jones-type interactions, in every dimension and at arbitrary fixed temperature and density covered by the stated assumptions. The finite approximants are ordinary interacting \(N\)-particle Langevin systems in growing periodic boxes.

This is useful precisely when a direct infinite-dimensional Dirichlet-form or generator construction is unavailable or awkward. The Langevin generator is non-sectorial; the paper instead obtains existence by taking a thermodynamic limit of well-defined finite systems.

## Limitations

Compactness proves **existence of subsequential limits**, not uniqueness of the limiting dynamics. Theorem 4.17 shows that every accumulation point solves the same martingale problem, but unless that martingale problem is known to be unique, the argument does not imply convergence of the entire approximating sequence or ergodicity from arbitrary initial states.

Uniform local moment/Ruelle estimates are load-bearing. Without them, particles can accumulate locally or interaction terms can fail to be uniformly integrable, defeating both tightness and martingale-problem passage. The Gibbs identification also uses substantial equilibrium statistical-mechanics input and periodic finite-volume canonical ensembles.

This method differs from the live finite-volume-coercivity/exhaustion page. There one estimates \(P_t-P_t^{\Lambda}\) and lets the volume grow **inside a semigroup relaxation argument**. Here the main object is the family of probability laws themselves: Prokhorov-type compactness gives subsequential path/invariant-law limits, and the remaining work is to identify those limits.
