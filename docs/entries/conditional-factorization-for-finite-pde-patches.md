# Conditional factorization for finite PDE patches

A finite patch-first randomization can be organized so that the combinatorial and temporal patch data are exposed while the Gaussian spatial marks inside distinct patches remain independent. Conditional expectation then replaces every side patch by its conditional mean field before the parent heat or Hermite kernel is applied. The conditioning sigma-field must not contain the Gaussian mark whose centering is needed for the derivative cancellation.

This is a finite-depth statement for the quadratic Hessian equation. It is a probabilistic form of the exact [finite-depth Duhamel patch regrouping](finite-depth-duhamel-patch-regrouping.md) and does not by itself imply an infinite-depth \(L^1\) result. Its place in the larger programme is summarized in the [PDE branching-representations overview](../pde-branching-representations.md).

**References.** None: the statement below is the finite-level conditional-expectation form of the patch regrouping proved in this project. Conditional means and fluctuations are defined in [Conditional expectation and fluctuations of random fields](conditional-expectation-and-fluctuations-of-random-fields.md). The missing absolute-integrability step for every fixed canonical raw tree is proved separately in [Canonical raw signed measures for finite quadratic-Hessian trees](canonical-raw-signed-measures-for-finite-quadratic-hessian-trees.md).

## Setup

Fix \(T>0\), \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), and \(\phi\in C^\infty(\mathbb T)\), where

$$
\mathbb T=\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z).
$$

For

$$
\partial_tv
=
\frac12\partial_x^2v
+\lambda(\partial_x^2v)^2,
\qquad
v(0)=\phi,
$$

write \(z=v_{xx}\). The finite Picard recursion is

$$
z^{[0]}(t)=P_t\phi'',
$$

and

$$
z^{[N+1]}(t)
=
P_t\phi''
+
\lambda\int_0^t
\partial_x^2P_{t-s}
\left[(z^{[N]}(s))^2\right]ds.
\tag{1}
$$

For every finite \(N\), expanding (1) produces finitely many planar binary Duhamel trees. The [finite patch theorem](finite-depth-duhamel-patch-regrouping.md) decomposes each such tree uniquely into decorated maximal left-spine patches. No infinite-depth convergence assumption is used on this page.

## Patch skeleton data

Fix a finite Picard depth \(N\). For a patch-first randomization, expose the following data:

- the finite patch genealogy and the incidence relation between patches;
- the length of each patch and the ordered side-attachment slots along it;
- the ordered branch times or edge lengths inside each patch;
- any discrete mechanism or proposal choices used to select the finite Duhamel term.

Let \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\) be the sigma-field generated by these data.

The Gaussian increments, Hermite marks, and independent descendant seeds used to realize the spatial semigroup transfers are **not** included in \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\).

This exclusion is essential. If a centered Gaussian mark \(Z\) were already revealed, then

$$
\mathbb E[He_2(Z)\mid\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}]
$$

would no longer produce the cancellation \(\mathbb E He_2(Z)=0\). Here \(He_2(z)=z^2-1\) is the probabilists' second [Hermite polynomial](hermite-polynomials-and-gaussian-chaos.md).

## Random fields rather than pre-evaluated values

A side patch rooted at a branch is generated as a random field

$$
Y_P(x),
\qquad x\in\mathbb T,
$$

using a seed \(\omega_P\) independent of the seeds of the other side patches, conditionally on \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\). The field is defined as a function of a possible starting position \(x\); it is not generated only after the parent random location has been realized.

Its conditional mean field is

$$
m_P(x)
=
\mathbb E\left[
Y_P(x)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}
\right].
\tag{2}
$$

For the canonical centered quadratic-Hessian construction with \(\phi''\in C^\alpha\), \(\alpha>0\), the [canonical raw-measure theorem](canonical-raw-signed-measures-for-finite-quadratic-hessian-trees.md) proves the required finite-tree \(L^1\) integrability. Thus (2) is an ordinary conditional expectation at every fixed finite depth. The warning against conditioning an unresolved non-\(L^1\) object applies only to the infinite-depth object, not to these now-proved finite raw trees.

If a parent patch later reaches a random branch position \(X\), independent of the side-patch seeds conditional on the skeleton and the parent seed, then the same field is evaluated at \(X\).

## Proposition: side-patch factorization

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\) be distinct child patches attached at one branch point. For every deterministic spatial point \(x\),

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{j=1}^kY_{P_j}(x)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}
\right]
=
\prod_{j=1}^k m_{P_j}(x),
\tag{3}
$$

for the finite canonical raw construction. More generally the same identity holds for any finite patch randomization for which the displayed product is integrable.

If \(X\) is the parent branch location and is generated independently of the child-patch seeds after conditioning on the parent information, then conditioning first on \(X\) gives

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{j=1}^kY_{P_j}(X)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}},X
\right]
=
\prod_{j=1}^k m_{P_j}(X).
\tag{4}
$$

The parent heat or derivative kernel then averages the right side of (4) over the law of \(X\).

## Proof

Conditional on \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\), the seeds \(\omega_{P_1},\ldots,\omega_{P_k}\) are independent by construction. For fixed \(x\), each \(Y_{P_j}(x)\) is a measurable function of its own seed and the exposed skeleton data. Conditional independence therefore gives (3).

For (4), condition additionally on the parent branch position \(X\). The value of \(X\) changes the common argument at which the child fields are evaluated but does not reveal their independent seeds. Applying (3) pointwise at \(x=X\) gives (4).

## One centered Hessian edge

Let a parent derivative edge have deterministic elapsed time \(r>0\), and let its fresh Gaussian mark be \(Z\sim N(0,1)\). For an input field \(f\) independent of \(Z\) conditional on the exposed data, define

$$
\widehat K_r f(x,Z)
=
\frac{He_2(Z)}r
\left[
 f(x+\sqrt r Z)-f(x)
\right].
\tag{5}
$$

Because \(Z\) is not exposed in \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\), conditional expectation gives

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\widehat K_r f(\cdot,Z)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}},f
\right]
=
\partial_x^2P_rf.
\tag{6}
$$

If \(f\) is itself a random side field, condition first on the side field and then use the tower property. Under the corresponding conditional independence,

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\widehat K_r f(\cdot,Z)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}
\right]
=
\partial_x^2P_r
\mathbb E\left[
 f
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}
\right].
\tag{7}
$$

Thus the deterministic [Holder cancellation estimate](holder-cancellation-for-heat-semigroup-derivatives.md) acts on the conditional mean field after the fresh Gaussian mark has been averaged.

## Compensators and finite-tree integrability

If patch lengths, branch times, or mechanism types are sampled from positive proposal laws, their reciprocal [importance-sampling compensators](importance-sampling-compensators.md) cancel those proposal probabilities exactly at finite depth. This cancellation is independent of the spatial conditional factorization above.

For the canonical centered construction, the resulting signed object is the finite measure \(\mu_\tau^{t,x}\) constructed in the [canonical raw-measure theorem](canonical-raw-signed-measures-for-finite-quadratic-hessian-trees.md). If a probability proposal \(Q_\tau\) dominates that measure, then its exact canonical weight is the Radon--Nikodym derivative

$$
\frac{d\mu_\tau^{t,x}}{dQ_\tau},
$$

and automatically belongs to \(L^1(Q_\tau)\), with

$$
\int
\left|
\frac{d\mu_\tau^{t,x}}{dQ_\tau}
\right|dQ_\tau
=
\|\mu_\tau^{t,x}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\infty.
$$

Thus finite-tree integrability is no longer an open input for the canonical raw construction. The genuinely infinite question is whether these finite total variations are summable over the countable tree family.

## What follows at infinite depth

There are several distinct continuations of the finite identity.

If all continuous branch-time and Gaussian/Hermite variables inside a decorated skeleton are integrated out before the skeleton is sampled, the resulting deterministic profiles are the objects summed in [Theorem C-prime](skeleton-averaged-l1-representation-for-quadratic-hessian-pde.md). Under its explicit Catalan smallness condition, that skeleton-only estimator is unbiased and belongs to \(L^1\).

If the full canonical raw state is retained as the conditional barycenter, the [raw-barycenter obstruction](raw-marked-l1-obstruction-for-quadratic-hessian-pde.md) gives one fixed smooth datum for which the sum of finite raw total variations diverges. Between these endpoints, the [time-spine theorem](time-spine-coarsening-for-quadratic-hessian-patches.md) and the [residual signed variation characterization](residual-signed-variation-characterization-for-coarsened-patches.md) describe partial coarsenings. The remaining [\(L^1\) random-patch conjecture](l1-random-patch-conjecture-for-quadratic-hessian-pde.md) is a structured target-uniform problem on the full C-prime regime.
