# Conditional factorization for finite PDE patches

A finite patch-first randomization can be organized so that the combinatorial and temporal patch data are exposed while the Gaussian spatial marks inside distinct patches remain independent. Conditional expectation then replaces every side patch by its conditional mean field before the parent heat or Hermite kernel is applied. The conditioning sigma-field must not contain the Gaussian mark whose centering is needed for the derivative cancellation.

This is a finite-depth statement for the quadratic Hessian equation. It is the probabilistic form of the exact [finite-depth Duhamel patch regrouping](finite-depth-duhamel-patch-regrouping.md) and does not by itself imply an infinite-depth \(L^1\) result. Conditional means and fluctuations are reviewed in [Conditional expectation and fluctuations of random fields](conditional-expectation-and-fluctuations-of-random-fields.md).

**References.** None: the factorization below is proved here.

## Setup

Fix \(T>0\), \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), and \(\phi\in C^\infty(\mathbb T)\). For

$$
\partial_tv
=
\frac12\partial_x^2v
+\lambda(\partial_x^2v)^2,
\qquad
v(0)=\phi,
$$

write \(z=v_{xx}\). At finite Picard depth \(N\), the mild recursion produces finitely many planar binary trees, each decomposed uniquely into maximal left-spine patches by the finite patch theorem.

## Exposed patch skeleton

For one finite patch-first randomization, expose:

- the finite patch genealogy and incidence relation;
- the length of each patch;
- the ordered branch times or edge lengths inside each patch;
- any discrete mechanism or proposal choices selecting the finite Duhamel term.

Let \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\) be the sigma-field generated by these variables.

The Gaussian increments, Hermite marks, and independent descendant seeds used to realize spatial transfers are **not** included in \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\). This exclusion is essential: revealing a centered Gaussian mark before conditioning would destroy the mean-zero cancellation which produces the deterministic derivative operator.

## Random fields rather than pre-evaluated values

A side patch \(P\) is generated as a random field

$$
Y_P(x),
\qquad x\in\mathbb T,
$$

from a seed \(\omega_P\). Conditional on \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\), different side patches use independent seeds. Define

$$
m_P(x)
=
\mathbb E[
Y_P(x)
\mid
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}
].
\tag{1}
$$

Equation (1) is ordinary conditional expectation and therefore requires the finite-cutoff field to be integrable for the chosen finite proposal. Nothing here conditions an unresolved nonintegrable infinite functional.

If the parent later reaches a random location \(X\), the same random field is evaluated at \(X\); the field is not generated only after \(X\) has been sampled.

## Proposition: side-patch factorization

Let \(P_1,\ldots,P_k\) be distinct child patches attached at one branch point. For every deterministic \(x\), whenever the product is integrable,

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{j=1}^kY_{P_j}(x)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}
\right]
=
\prod_{j=1}^k m_{P_j}(x).
\tag{2}
$$

If \(X\) is the parent branch location and is generated independently of the child-patch seeds after conditioning on the parent information, then

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\prod_{j=1}^kY_{P_j}(X)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}},X
\right]
=
\prod_{j=1}^k m_{P_j}(X).
\tag{3}
$$

### Proof

Conditional on \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\), the seeds \(\omega_{P_1},\ldots,\omega_{P_k}\) are independent. For fixed \(x\), each \(Y_{P_j}(x)\) is a measurable function of its own seed and the exposed skeleton data, so conditional independence gives (2). Conditioning additionally on the parent location changes only the common spatial argument and does not reveal the child seeds; applying (2) at \(x=X\) gives (3).

## One centered Hessian edge

Let \(r>0\) be deterministic and let \(Z\sim N(0,1)\) be a fresh Gaussian mark. For an input field \(f\) independent of \(Z\) conditional on the exposed data, define

$$
\widehat K_r f(x,Z)
=
\frac{He_2(Z)}r
\left[
f(x+\sqrt rZ)-f(x)
\right].
\tag{4}
$$

Because \(Z\) is not exposed in \(\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}\),

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\widehat K_r f(\cdot,Z)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}},f
\right]
=
\partial_x^2P_rf.
\tag{5}
$$

If \(f\) is itself a random side field, conditioning first on \(f\) and using the tower property gives

$$
\mathbb E\left[
\widehat K_r f(\cdot,Z)
\,\middle|\,
\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}
\right]
=
\partial_x^2P_r
\mathbb E[f\mid\mathcal G_{\mathrm{skel}}].
\tag{6}
$$

Thus deterministic [Hölder cancellation](holder-cancellation-for-heat-semigroup-derivatives.md) acts on the conditional mean field after the fresh Gaussian mark has been averaged.

## Compensators

If patch lengths, branch times, or mechanism types are sampled from positive proposal laws, their reciprocal [importance-sampling compensators](importance-sampling-compensators.md) cancel those proposal factors exactly at finite depth. This proposal cancellation is separate from the spatial conditional factorization above. Integrability of the chosen finite random variable is still required before the conditional expectations are ordinary \(L^1\) objects.

## What follows at infinite depth

The finite factorization is the common starting point for several different infinite-depth coarsenings.

- [Theorem C-prime](skeleton-averaged-l1-representation-for-quadratic-hessian-pde.md) averages every continuous interior variable and is \(L^1\) under \(4a<1\).
- The [raw-barycenter obstruction](raw-marked-l1-obstruction-for-quadratic-hessian-pde.md) shows that leaving the canonical raw signed contribution as the conditional barycenter is non-\(L^1\) for one fixed smooth datum.
- The [time-spine theorem](time-spine-coarsening-for-quadratic-hessian-patches.md) retains genuine branch-time randomness and is \(L^1\) under its stronger two-condition small-data regime.
- The [residual signed variation characterization](residual-signed-variation-characterization-for-coarsened-patches.md) gives the exact criterion for arbitrary skeleton-preserving coarsenings: the first-moment cost is the total variation of the pushed-forward signed measure, equivalently the \(L^1\) norm of the conditional raw signed density.

At each fixed target in the C-prime regime, sparse full-state retention yields nonconstant integrable coarsenings. The remaining [random-patch conjecture](l1-random-patch-conjecture-for-quadratic-hessian-pde.md) is a structured target-uniform problem, not an unresolved question about whether any nonconstant retained-randomness coarsening exists.
